# Playtest 10 (2026-11-26): the same fight a third time, and the three together

Lucas ran the fight of playtests 8 and 9 again, solo, on the released v0.0.519 (the Log still says "Trivial (<20%)"): the four level 1 pregens against the Ogre Chieftain, the Orc Warrior, the Grave Wisp and two Goblin Sneaks (CV 410). 1. **The party's damage is what the record estimated:** 16.1 a turn over 28 turns, against 17. The paper turn (23) is not what a table deals.
2. **The fight is half a round short of the plan.** It ended in round 2 once and early in round 3 twice. 130 enemy Health against about 64 a round is two rounds; nothing carries it far into round 3.
3. **The enemies' damage is right when they get their turns, and they often don't.** Twice the raw was the plan's 50. In playtest 10 the goblins rolled four Tier 1s, the wisp and both goblins were dead in round 1 and the orc never acted in round 2: 27. Three of the five enemies get one turn or less in every fight.
4. **So the normal fight costs about 1 Rally Point a PC, not 1½,** and it has never put a PC Down. By the notes' edges all three are Easy.
5. **The class margin, measured three times: about a fifth** of the enemies' damage (Judge, Shield of Faith). No class card was priced into the enemies, as decided; this is the size of what that leaves out.
6. **Nobody has kept an AP for Guard or spent a Rally Point in a fight.** The fights are too short and too cheap for either to be a choice.
7. **Smite decides the ogre:** 59, 27 and 50 damage for 4 AP. The Basic Attack is the next best button; the other class cards are far behind in damage.
8. **Kaela deals 15 a fight** (twice 15 in three rounds): Tier 1s, and Strafe and Harrying Shot's small numbers.
9. **Not seen in three fights:** Hurled Goblin, Cornered, Shriek and Scatter, the Javelin's Lingering. Crack the Ground: 25, 0 and 8.

**What it means for the numbers (Claude's reading, nothing decided):**
- The estimates hold (the party's 17, the Standard's 8 a turn when it acts). What is off is the **number of enemy turns**: the plan counted on about 6½ full-strength turns; the small creatures die before they add theirs.
- **For the three rounds already decided**, the gap is small: about a quarter more enemy staying power puts the last enemy in the middle of round 3 and gives the enemies the turns the cost was built on. That quarter is about the class margin plus the lost half round.
- **For 4 to 5 rounds** the gap is not a tune: at 64 a round it needs about twice the enemy Health, or half the party's damage.
