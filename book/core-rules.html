Part I: Core Rules

Chapter 1: The Roll

Roll 2d12 and add the relevant Modifier. Compare the total to the DC to find the outcome tier.

Many other effects can add extra dice to the roll.

There are four Attributes: Body, Mind, Appeal and Instinct. They start at 0, and your Species and Class change them when you build your character.

Your Attribute Modifier for an Attribute equals its value: with Body 2, your Body Modifier is +2. Weapon, Power and Signature rolls use their own Modifiers instead (see Chapter 5).

Outcome Tiers

Tier Result Total
Tier 1 Failure Below the DC
Tier 2 Marginal Success From the DC to DC + 6
Tier 3 Full Success DC + 7 or more

A Tier 2 or Tier 3 result is a success; rules that care about it say "when you get a Tier 2 or 3 result." The names of the tiers are flavor: the rules use Tier 1, 2 and 3.

For improvised rolls, such as during roleplay, you don't always need three results: coming up with them every time can take a toll on the GM. When you just want a yes or no, Tier 1 is a failure and Tier 2 or 3 is a success.

Exploding Dice

When a d12 shows a 12, it explodes: count that die as 11, then roll it again and add the result. If the new roll is a 12, it explodes again.

Example

You roll a 12 and a 5. The 12 explodes: you count it as 11 and reroll it, getting an 8. The total is 11 + 8 + 5 = 24, plus your Modifier.

Doubles

If both d12s show the same number (before explosions), the result moves up one tier: Failure becomes Marginal Success, and Marginal Success becomes Full Success. Full Success can't go higher: it becomes a Critical instead (see below).

A Doomed roll can't produce Doubles (see Favored and Doomed Rolls).

Critical

A result is a Critical when you roll Doubles on a result that is already Tier 3, or when your total is 20 or more above the DC (33 or more against DC 13). Doubles that raise a Tier 2 to Tier 3 are not a Critical.

A Critical only matters for the few Actions that have a Critical row, written under Tier 3 and above the Effects. On a Critical, you get the Tier 3 result and the Critical effect as well. For any other Action, a Critical is simply a Tier 3.

Boons and Banes

  • Each Boon adds 1d3-1 to your total (0, 1 or 2).
  • Each Bane subtracts 1d3-1 from your total.
  • Boons and Banes don't cancel each other. Roll all of them.

Favored and Doomed Rolls

When a roll is Favored, you roll 3d12 and keep the highest Tier possible: usually that's the two highest die results, but sometimes it can be a Double (see below). When a roll is Doomed, you roll 3d12 and keep the two lowest. A roll that is both Favored and Doomed is a normal roll.

Roll three dice, then see if there are any explosions, calculated per die.

Example

You are Doomed and roll three different-colored dice: 10 (blue), 12 (green) and 12 (red). You reroll the two 12s and get 10 (green) and 5 (red): green is 11 + 10 = 21 and red is 11 + 5 = 16. You keep the two lowest, blue and red, so your result is 26 (10 + 16).

For Favored rolls you calculate the same way, but if keeping a lower die would give you a higher Tier, the Favored creature chooses which dice to keep.

Example

You are Favored and roll 1 (blue), 1 (green) and 5 (red). The two highest are a 1 and the 5, but keeping the two 1s gives you Doubles, which can be the better result. You choose which two to keep.

  1. Roll 3d12.
  2. Each die explodes on its own (a 12 counts as 11 and is rolled again; further 12s explode again). After all explosions, each die has its own total.
  3. Keep the two highest totals (Favored) or the two lowest totals (Doomed). A Favored creature can instead keep lower totals if they give a higher Tier.
  4. Favored only: for Doubles, compare the faces the two kept dice showed before exploding. A Doomed roll can't produce Doubles.

Rolls

A Roll is anything you roll. There are six kinds, and every roll is exactly one of them:

Check One Attribute Modifier against a DC, outside an Action: a "Body Check" (see Checks).
Save A roll to resist something: an "Appeal Save" (see Saves).
Weapon roll An Action with the Weapon trait. It rolls your Weapon Modifier.
Power roll An Action with the Power trait. It rolls your Power Modifier.
Signature roll An Action with the Signature trait. It rolls your Signature Modifier.

A Split Action is a Weapon roll or a Power roll, depending on the mode you choose when you use it.

Checks

A Check is a roll of one Attribute Modifier against a DC, written "Body Check" or "Instinct Check". The default DC is 13 unless something says otherwise, like "DC 12 Body Check".

Saves

Some effects make a creature roll a Save, written like "Appeal Save: 1d6 / 1d4 / 0 [R] Holy". The creature that must save rolls the named Attribute Modifier against the DC of the effect.

The DC is usually 11 + the Weapon, Power or Signature Modifier that sets it (or 11 + ⌈Attribute ÷ 2⌉ + 1 for an Attribute-based Save); if no DC is listed, it is usually 13.

The three values are what happens on its Tier 1 / Tier 2 / Tier 3 result: here 1d6 Holy Mind damage on a Tier 1, 1d4 on a Tier 2 and nothing on a Tier 3. Boons, Banes, Doubles, Favored and Doomed work on Saves like on any roll.

Save Actions

An Action with the Save trait doesn't roll: each target rolls the Save the card names (its Vs. line says, for example, Instinct Save). The DC comes from the Action's other traits: 11 + your Weapon Modifier for a [Save, Weapon] Action, 11 + your Power Modifier for a [Power, Save] Action, 11 + your Signature Modifier for a [Save, Signature] Action, and for a Save Action that rolls an Attribute (such as [Body, Save]), 11 + ⌈Attribute ÷ 2⌉ + 1.

The tiers are the target's result: Tier 1 is the worst for them. So everything that follows your result on an attack follows theirs here, reversed: a [Save, Weapon] Action deals its [Mod] on Tier 1 and 2, and its Effects row happens on Tier 1 and 2.

A Save Action has no Critical, and it is not a Strike. Boons and Banes on your own rolls don't apply to it; effects on the target's Saves do. Vulnerable gives the target that many Banes on the Save, and Cover gives them Boons, counted from the center of the area's Point of Origin to the target.

Roll Effects

Each Tier of a roll says what happens: damage, a condition, a movement, healing. This is how the most common effects are written. Conditions are in Conditions, Heal and Rally Points in Rally Points, and Forced Movement in Forced Movement.

Damage on a Card

The damage of an Action is what its card says: it adds a Modifier or a weapon's damage only when the card writes [Mod] or [W]. A Weapon Action writes the weapon's damage and its Modifier on the card; a Power Action writes its dice.

Modifier Damage [Mod]

When an Action deals damage equal to your Modifier, it is written [Mod]. The Modifier is always the one you used to roll for that Action. You never add a Modifier to the same damage twice.

When a Weapon Action adds your Modifier to its damage, the Modifier joins the weapon's damage and keeps its damage type and subtype: if the weapon deals Mind damage, so does the Modifier.

Power Actions don't add their Modifier to damage on any Tier: their damage is the dice on the card.

Damage Types

All damage a creature takes is either Body or Mind damage, though each of those has different subtypes. Blunt damage will almost always be Body damage, but not always. Damage is written this way:

  • [V]: the damage is applied to Body.
  • [R]: the damage is applied to Mind.
  • [C]: the origin chooses which type (Body or Mind) the damage deals.

Some Actions include two types of damage. For example, "[V] 1d4+1d6+3 Sl; [R] 1d8 Ps" means Body takes 1d4+1d6+3 Slash damage and Mind takes 1d8 Psychic damage.

Cleave

When you Cleave N, you deal the Modifier used as damage (with the same damage type and subtype as your weapon) to up to N creatures that are adjacent to the original target.

Several Targets

When one Action affects several creatures (an Area, or a Strike that says "all creatures"), you roll once, after the targets are chosen (for an Area, after you place it). The dice are shared, but every target is calculated on its own:

  • The DC is calculated against each creature.
  • Each creature has its own Boons, Banes, Favored and Doomed: cover, Prone, Vulnerable, range and so on can apply to one target and not to another.
  • The Tier, Doubles and Critical are decided separately for each creature.
  • Damage is rolled per Tier: once the Action is rolled and you see which creatures landed on which Tier, roll that Tier's damage once and apply it to every creature on that Tier.

For instance, if the Action deals 1d4 / 1d6 / 1d8, and two creatures get Tier 1 and one gets Tier 2, you roll 1d4 once and apply it to the two creatures on Tier 1.

All damage is rolled per die. If the Tier 2 damage of a Save is [V] 1d8 Co + [R] 1d6 Ho, the creature causing the Save rolls both 1d8 and 1d6 and deals that damage to all creatures who got a Tier 2. If the creature causing the Save is Empowered or Enfeebled, the value is added to all the damage rolls.

You roll every die that any of the targets needs, and each target uses only the ones that apply to it. If any target has Favored or Doomed, roll 3d12; for a target without it, use the first two d12 you rolled.

Example

You attack Peter and Will with one Action. You have 1 Boon against Will but not against Peter. You roll 2d12 and 1 Boon: the Boon is only added to the total you compare with Will's DC.

Example

You attack one Favored target and one non-Favored target with the same Action. You roll 3d12 (Favored) and keep the two highest for the Favored target; for the non-Favored target, use the first two d12 in the order rolled. Each target checks its own kept dice for Doubles.

Rounding

Round up by default. When a rule divides or multiplies a number and the result isn't a whole number, round it up, unless the rule says otherwise.

Rules Language

A single creature is referred to as they: "the creature is Hindered until they use a Move Action."

Allies. Your allies are the creatures on your side. You are never your own ally.

Origin. The origin of an effect is the creature that uses the Action, or the Point of Origin of an Area. When a rule says "the origin chooses", the origin decides, not the creature affected.

Since your last turn, this turn. A feature that says "since your last turn" counts from the end of your last turn. One that says "this turn" counts from the start of your turn.

Up to. A number of things you can use, gain or move is a maximum: you can use fewer, or none, unless the rule says you must. "The target can use 2 Rally Points" lets them use 0, 1 or 2.

Specific beats general. When a card or a feature says something different from a general rule, the card or feature wins for that case. The Evade Action, for example, doesn't trigger Reactions although movement normally does.

When a rule names a trait before the word "Action," it means an Action with that trait. For example, a Divine Action is an Action with the Divine trait. The same goes for a Weapon Action, a Power Action or a Nonlethal Action.

  • Traits inside one pair of square brackets, such as [Ranged, Strike, Weapon] Action, mean an Action with all of those traits: [Trait A, Trait B] is Trait A and Trait B. It doesn't mean only, it just must include the trait: a [Melee] Weapon Attack (1A) is the Weapon Attack Action that uses 1 AP and has the Melee trait, so it doesn't apply to a [Ranged] Weapon Attack (1A).
  • Separate brackets joined by "and" mean either: "[Strike] and [Weapon] Actions" are Actions with the Strike trait, the Weapon trait, or both. Alternatives can also be written with "or" inside the brackets: [Melee or Ranged].
  • A Body Action is an Action with the Body trait, and so on; an Action can be more than one (Trip is both a Body and an Instinct Action).

Weapons by property. When a rule names a weapon property before the word "weapon," it means a weapon with that property. A Two-handed weapon is a weapon with the Two-handed property, and a Projectile weapon is a weapon with the Projectile property, such as a bow or a crossbow.

Summary

  1. Roll 2d12 and add the relevant Modifier.
  2. Any 12 counts as 11 and is rolled again, adding the result.
  3. Add Boons (1d3-1 each) and subtract Banes (1d3-1 each).
  4. Compare to the DC: below it is a Failure, from the DC up to DC + 6 is a Marginal Success, and DC + 7 or more is a Full Success.
  5. If both d12s match, move the result up one tier.
  6. A Favored roll is 3d12, keeping the two highest. A Doomed roll is 3d12, keeping the two lowest.

Chapter 2: Turns and Actions

Encounters

An encounter is a fight or a similar scene: it starts when Initiative is rolled and ends when the GM says it's over. It can't end while a creature is Dying or Shattered: every creature must be unharmed, stabilized or dead. If a creature becomes Dying or Shattered outside of an encounter, it rolls on the Dying or Shattered Table, then Initiative is rolled and that creature goes last.

Designer's Tip

Sometimes, rolling Initiative when a single creature becomes Dying or Shattered and there is no obvious danger around, and an ally can stabilize them easily, is just going to slow down the game. In these non-threatening situations, feel free to roll on the Dying or Shattered Table once, and then the creature stabilizes.

Initiative

At the start of combat, every creature rolls Initiative. Initiative is a Check, normally an Instinct Check, but the GM can call for a different Attribute. The total is the creature's initiative.

Initiative is a zipper. The creatures are split into however many different groups exist. Usually it is the PCs against a single group of enemies, but three-way combat is possible. Each group puts its creatures in order from the highest result to the lowest. The group with the highest single result goes first. Then the groups take turns, one creature at a time, in the order of their highest result, each taking its highest remaining creature. A group with no creatures left is skipped.

Example

Victor rolls 14, Paul 13, Wes 10, Goblin Warrior 12 and Goblin Scoundrel 10. The PCs' highest result is 14 and the enemies' is 12, so the order is: Victor, Goblin Warrior, Paul, Goblin Scoundrel, Wes.

In a three-way fight, Mark rolls 13 and Nick 9, the enemies Goblin Warrior 12 and Goblin Scoundrel 8, and a third group Kobold Mage 13 and Kobold Shaman 5. The order is Mark, Kobold Mage, Goblin Warrior, Nick, Kobold Shaman, Goblin Scoundrel.

Ties are broken by the higher Attribute Modifier used for the Initiative roll. If they are still tied, the Friendly creature (a PC or an ally of the PCs) goes first.

Swapping Initiative

On your turn, if you haven't taken any Actions yet and aren't Down, you can spend 1 AP to swap places in the turn order with an ally who hasn't had a turn this round. The ally takes the turn now, at your place in the order, and gains its AP as normal.

You act at the ally's old place. Until then, your remaining Actions are frozen: you can't use them. When your new turn arrives, you carry on with the AP you had left instead of gaining new AP. You can't swap again that round.

Example

PC1 (first in the order) swaps with PC3 and keeps 4 AP. The order becomes PC3, NPC1, PC2, NPC2, PC1. When PC1's turn comes, PC1 has 4 AP.

Turns and Rounds

A turn is the time one creature acts, from the start of their turn to the end of it. A round is one full cycle of turns. Limits that say "per turn" count each turn separately, including other creatures' turns: you can make up to 3 Strikes in your turn, and up to 3 more (as reactions) in another creature's turn.

A turn-cycle is the period from the start of a creature's turn to the start of that creature's next turn. In the order "Victor, Goblin Warrior, Paul, Goblin Scoundrel, Wes", Wes's turn-cycle runs from the start of Wes's turn to the start of Wes's next turn.

Action Points

At the start of your turn in combat, you regain AP: you lose any AP you didn't use and gain 5 Action Points (AP). You spend AP to do things on your turn.

If you end your turn without spending all your AP, you can keep the rest to use outside your turn, for Reactions.

Losing and Gaining Action Points

When something gives or takes Action Points, it says whether it happens now or when you regain AP.

  • Now. "The target loses 1 AP" means they lose 1 AP they have right now; if they have none, it has no effect. "The target gains 1 AP" means they gain 1 AP right now. Unless otherwise stated, you can't have more than 5 AP.
  • When you regain AP. Changes to the AP you regain at the start of your turn are the Slowed and Quickened conditions: Slowed 1 means you regain 1 AP less, Quickened 1 means you regain 1 AP more.

Reactions

A Reaction is an Action you use in response to something another creature does. Its card has an Interrupt or a Trigger line that says what it responds to, and which of the two it is decides when it happens. Its cost is written with an R, such as 1R: it uses that many Action Points, but only as a response. A 0R Reaction uses no AP; its Frequency still limits it.

  • The same AP: Reactions use your Action Points: the AP you didn't spend on your turn stay available until you regain AP at the start of your next turn.
  • Interrupt: An Interrupt happens before the Action that triggers it. "Interrupt: A creature would Advance" means the Reaction happens before the creature uses the Advance Action.
  • Trigger: A Trigger happens after the Action that triggers it. "Trigger: A creature deals damage to you" happens after you take the triggering damage.
  • Once per triggering Action: You can react only once to the same Action, but several creatures can react to it. Some Actions, mainly Move Actions, can happen throughout different squares. It is still a single Action, and you decide when you take the Reaction: for instance, you can wait for the creature to move into a more advantageous position before taking your Reaction, instead of taking it as soon as the creature moves.
  • Multiple creatures reacting to the same trigger, at the same time: You must decide to react before making the rolls: when a creature triggers a Reaction from several creatures, they all decide whether they will react before making any rolls. The creatures taking the Reactions decide the order; if they are opposed, the GM decides or asks for a simple Check to decide who goes first, similar to how Initiative is rolled. If the triggering creature becomes an invalid target after a creature reacts (such as being killed), the remaining creatures still lose the AP as if they had used the Reaction.
  • On any turn: A Reaction can happen during any turn, including your own: if another creature's Action moves them away from you during your turn, you can react to that.
  • You can't use an Action outside your turn unless it is a Reaction.

While the name "triggering" is similar to "Trigger", it isn't only for Trigger Reactions: it also applies to Interrupt Reactions. The triggering creature is the creature who caused the Reaction to happen; the triggering Action is the Action that caused the Reaction to happen, and so on.

Designer's Tip

The clarification above might make you think you need to move one square at a time during a game, but this slows the game down a lot. You can just make the full movement and let the creature reacting decide when it happens during the movement. You might need to move the token back to where the Reaction happened if the movement is disrupted (see below), but that doesn't happen often. Doing it this way removes the suspense of "When will this creature start moving? Should I use my Reaction now, or wait and see if it moves into a more favorable position, with the chance that it stops moving before I react?" The table should decide how to run it.

Disrupting Actions

When an Action is disrupted, it doesn't happen, and the resources and AP spent on it are wasted. Some Actions can disrupt another Action, usually with a Critical, for example Opportunity Attack when it is used out of your turn.

An Interrupt disrupts the Action before it happens. For example, a Reaction with "Interrupt: A creature uses a Move Action" triggers when a creature uses an Action that Stands and then Advances (1 AP): the creature doesn't Stand, and the whole Action is disrupted (because you can't Advance while Prone).

A Trigger that names a Move Action fires at any point during the Move Action (after the first square it moves), but it is a single trigger: you can use the Reaction only once per Move Action, even with AP to spare. If the Action does something before moving (Stand, then Advance), the Stand happens first; the Trigger fires during the Advance.

When the disrupted Action is only a Linked Action, the Action stops only if that part is necessary for it. What already happened stays, and a disrupted movement ends in the square the creature was in. For example, a Charge disrupted before the creature has moved the 3 squares it needs is cancelled, and they make no Weapon Attack. If it is disrupted after those 3 squares, the Charge ends there, and they make the Weapon Attack if there is a valid target.

Moving Closer

A creature moves closer to you when the distance between you, counted in squares (every second diagonal counts as 2, as always), is smaller after their movement than before. An Interrupt checks this before the creature enters the square; a Trigger checks it if the creature entered any square nearer to you during the movement. The creature moves closer when it would enter a square that is nearer to you than the square it came from. A step to the side that leaves the distance the same is not closer. Evade, Forced Movement and Reposition don't trigger Reactions, so a Reaction to a creature moving closer can't react to any of them.

Actions

An Action is something you spend AP on. Every Action is written as a card; see Reading an Action.

Actions that roll have three tiers.

Whenever an Action is mentioned in the rules, its traits appear next to it. For example, in "You must use 1 AP [Move] to get up from Prone," Move is a trait.

Setup

Some Actions have a Setup entry before the Action. It describes what happens before the dice are rolled.

Targets

An Action that targets something can only be used if it has a valid target. An Action with no valid target can't be used. Most Actions target creatures only, but the GM can allow one to target an unattended object, such as a barrel, a crate or a door. Usually the Action rolls against DC 13 for the object, but the GM can set a different DC depending on the circumstances.

Willing and Unwilling

Some Actions target a willing creature, and some rules change for an unwilling one (passing through its square, for example). A creature is willing when it accepts the effect: you choose for yourself, and each creature chooses for itself when the effect would happen. An Unconscious creature counts as willing. Any other creature that doesn't accept is unwilling. A creature doesn't have to know what the effect does to accept it.

Requirements

Some Actions have Requirements, written under the Frequency line (and above the Interrupt or Trigger of a Reaction). To use the Action, you must meet them; otherwise you can't use it at all. They are checked when you start to use the Action, before you pay its cost and before any Setup. Charge requires at least Speed 3: with Speed 2, you can't Charge, and a Speed bonus that the Charge itself gives doesn't count toward it.

0 AP Actions

A 0 AP Action costs no AP, but it is still an Action: you can use it only on your turn, and its Frequency still limits it. An Action that costs no AP and is used in response to something is a Reaction, written 0R (see Reactions). Linked Actions cost no AP either, because they are part of another Action.

Linked Actions

An Action that another Action lets you use as part of it is a Linked Action. It is written with the AP of the Version you use, such as "Weapon Attack (1A)", or "(1A/2A)" when you choose between two Versions.

  • It is part of the original Action, so it costs no AP.
  • It keeps its traits and requirements, unless the Action says otherwise: you must still be able to use it. If an Action lets you Evade (a Linked Action) while you are Immobilized, you can't.
  • It affects the target of the original Action, unless it says otherwise. If the original Action doesn't list a target, you can choose the target per Linked Action.
  • It doesn't count toward its own uses: not toward its Frequency, the 3 Strikes of the turn, or At-will Banes, unless the Action says otherwise (a Charge's Weapon Attack counts toward At-will Banes).
  • If it isn't optional and you can't make it, the whole Action is disrupted. A Linked Action is optional when the Action says you can make it ("you can Evade 2"), and required when it says you do it ("you Advance 5, make a Weapon Attack (1A) and Advance 5 again").

If a Linked Action has no valid target, the rest of the Action is disrupted (that Linked Action and anything after it don't happen); what already happened stays. By default, you can't change the target of a Linked Action. For example, if a player asks to change the target from a creature to an object so they can continue the Action, the GM should probably accept it only if it makes sense, such as attacking a door on the way, and not "attacking the ground".

Example

Double Shot lets you make two Weapon Attacks (1A). Afterwards you can still use Weapon Attack as its own Action, and that first use has no At-will Bane.

Targets of Effects

Unless something says otherwise, the effects of an Action, and the Actions it lets you use (Linked Actions), affect the target of the Action. Remember, if the Action doesn't have a target, you choose who to target on each individual Linked Action. If you use Shove on an enemy, the enemy is the one who is Pushed.

Effect Timing

Effects written in a tier happen only on that tier. General Effects, written on the Effects row at the bottom of the Action, happen on a Tier 2 or 3 result, never on a Tier 1. An effect written directly in the Tier 1 row ("Tier 1: [V] 1d4 Ho; Prone") does happen on a Tier 1.

Actions with no roll have no tiers: their effects simply happen.

The effects of an Action happen after its damage is applied to the target.

When an effect applies only to some tiers, it says so with the tiers in parentheses: "Immobilized (T3)" is only Immobilized on a Tier 3 result, and "(T2/T3)" is Tier 2 or Tier 3.

Buffs

A Buff is an effect that modifies another Action, such as Rimebrand's extra damage on your Weapon Actions.

  • A Buff that gives a single value ("+[R] 1d6 Co") applies on every Tier, Tier 1 included.
  • A Buff that gives a value per Tier ("+[R] 1d4/1d6/1d8 Co") applies its Tier 1 value on a Tier 1, its Tier 2 value on a Tier 2 and its Tier 3 value on a Tier 3.
  • A Buff that only applies from Tier 2 onward is written with a 0 on Tier 1: "+[R] 0/1d6/1d8 Co".
  • On an Action with several targets, a Buff or a Debuff is rolled once per Tier, not per creature (see Several Targets).

Raising Defenses. "+1 Defense" raises every Defense of the creature: Armor Defense, Ward Defense and every Vs. Attribute. "+1 AD", "+1 WD" or "+1 Defense vs Body" raises only that one. A feature can narrow it further, such as "+1 Defense vs Actions that Push". These bonuses don't add up with each other (see Stacking).

Stacking

The same effect never stacks with itself, and bonuses from different effects don't add up either: for each value (Empowered, Enfeebled, Vulnerable, a Defense, Guard...), a creature uses only the highest bonus and the highest penalty that apply. If a creature gets the same effect again, it keeps the higher value and the longer duration: two effects that make it Enfeebled 1 do not make it Enfeebled 2.

Three things do add up:

  • An effect that says it increases a value: "increase your Enfeebled value by 1" (an Action that says "increase your Empowered value by 1" adds to the Empowered you have).
  • Boons and Banes.
  • An effect that increases your Slowed value (being Desperate, Dying or Shattered).

Equipment is not a bonus. Your armor gives your Armor value and your charm your Ward value; effects add to those. With +1 AD and +1 Defense, your Armor Defense rises by 1 (not 2) and every other Defense by 1.

Example

Divine Surge makes the next ally who deals damage to its target deal an extra [R] 1d8 Holy. If two Paladins use Divine Surge on the same creature, the next ally still deals only one extra 1d8: it is the same effect.

Frequency

Some Actions have a Frequency, written in the title bar of the card, such as At-will, Turn (once per turn), Round (once per round), Encounter (once per encounter) or Daily (once per Sleep). Actions with no Frequency can be used as often as you have AP.

Some traits can change the Frequency or expand the Frequency rules, such as the Strike trait, which limits you to a maximum of three Strikes per turn (see Chapter 7).

At-will

An At-will Action can be used any number of times, but each use after the first in a turn adds 1 Bane to the roll. For example, if you use Weapon Attack three times in one turn, the first roll is normal, the second has 1 Bane, and the third has 2 Banes. This doesn't apply to Linked Actions.

Each use after the first in a turn adds 1 Bane per specific At-will Action. Different At-will Actions each have their own first-use count.

On an Action with no roll, At-will has no mechanical effect: it is a stylistic default for Actions that can be used freely on your turn.

Turn

A Turn Action can be used once per turn: once on your turn, and once on each other creature's turn, as long as you have the AP. A Reaction with the Turn Frequency can answer one creature on its turn and another on theirs.

Round

A Round Action can be used once per turn-cycle, not once per round number: if you are at the end of the Initiative order, use a Round Action, end your turn and a new round begins, you still can't use it again until your whole turn-cycle completes.

Encounter

An Encounter Action can be used once during a whole encounter. It resets when the encounter ends, which is the GM's call.

Daily

A Daily Action can be used once, and again only after you complete a Sleep.

Versus

An Action that rolls uses a Modifier and, if it targets a creature, rolls against a Defense or DC. The Modifier is your Weapon, Power, Signature or Attribute Modifier (see Chapter 5). Every Action that rolls has a Vs. entry naming its Defense: Armor Defense (AD), Ward Defense (WD), a Vs. Attribute such as "Vs. Body", or a specific DC.

Defenses

An Action against a creature rolls against one of its Defenses. A creature has three kinds:

Defense DC
Armor Defense (AD) the creature's Armor Defense
Ward Defense (WD) the creature's Ward Defense
Vs. an Attribute 13 + half the target's Attribute, rounded up (−1 and 0 give +0, 1 and 2 give +1, 3 gives +2)
Vs. DC A set DC, such as "Vs. DC 13": some Actions have a set DC, and others target something that has no Defenses, such as a stationary object.
  • Armor value is the value provided by the armor a creature wears. Armor Defense is 11 + Armor value + other items + bonuses − penalties.
  • Ward value is the value provided by the ward a creature carries or is, such as a holy symbol, a charm, a ring or a feature. Ward Defense is 11 + your Power Modifier + Ward value + other items + bonuses − penalties. A creature without a ward normally has Ward value 0; items and features can increase this value.

Rounds and Durations

Until

When an effect says it lasts until something happens, it lasts through that something, and ends once it has been resolved. For example, a creature that is Hindered until they use a Move Action is still Hindered during that Move Action, and stops being Hindered afterwards. "Until you leave your square" ends after any movement, Forced Movement and Reposition included (see Moving and Leaving Your Square).

Your Next Action

When an Action says an effect happens on your next Action and gives no duration (unlike "For 1 round"), you must make that Action before the end of your current turn. If you don't, the effect is wasted.

Rounds

A round is one full cycle of turns. It starts with the first creature's turn and ends when the last creature ends their turn.

An effect that lasts a number of rounds counts down by 1 at the end of its origin's turn (the creature that created the effect), starting with the origin's next turn, meaning the first "end of turn" is ignored if the effect is created during the origin's turn. An effect that lasts 2 rounds is written "for 2 rounds".

Example

On the first round of combat, Victor (the first creature in the turn order) causes an effect on Wes that lasts 2 rounds. The effect ends at the end of Victor's turn on the third round.

End of Turn

When an effect lasts until the end of the turn of the affected creature, it is marked EoT.

An EoT effect on yourself still ends at the end of the current turn. A duration in rounds is different: it does not count down at the end of the turn in which the effect was created, because it has to last a whole round.

Each position in the turn order is an initiative slot. Effects end at the end of an initiative slot, not of a creature. If a creature swaps initiative, an EoT effect still ends at the end of the slot the creature was in when the effect was applied, and a duration in rounds still counts down at the end of the slot of its origin.

Escalation

Escalation is a character's adrenaline: the longer a fight goes on, the more of it you have, and you spend it on your class's Escalation abilities. You start combat with 0 Escalation, and each creature has their own Escalation. At the start of every round after the first, if you aren't Unconscious, you gain 1 Escalation, up to 3. While Unconscious, you keep the Escalation you have. You can't have more than 3 Escalation at once, unless you have some feature or ability that changes that.

You spend it on Escalation Actions. Their card shows the Escalation they cost on top of their AP, as in "1 Escalation". You can spend Escalation on more than one Action in the same turn. When the encounter ends, your Escalation goes back to 0, and every effect that spending Escalation created ends too.

Some Escalation abilities are passive: they work while your Escalation is at least the number they name ("while your Escalation is 2 or more"). Spending Escalation can turn them off until you gain it back.

Example

Rounds 1, 2, 3 and 4 start: you have 0, 1, 2 and 3 Escalation if you spend none. On round 3 you spend 1 on Divine Surge and have 1 left; at the start of round 4 you have 2. The fight ends on round 5: your Escalation goes back to 0, and a Divine Surge you haven't used yet is lost.

Chapter 3: Movement and the Environment

Playing on the Grid

Fights are played on a map divided into squares, on a table or in a virtual tabletop. Every creature is a token that stands on one or more squares, and every distance in these rules is counted in squares.

You move around the grid using Actions. Some allow you to move up to your Speed (like Advance); others have special rules, like the Evade Action, which lets you move without triggering Reactions.

When calculating distances and movement, each square counts as 1, but every second diagonal counts as 2 squares (1, 2, 1, 2...). Areas and ranges are measured the same way. Exception: Forced Movement and Reposition count each diagonal square as 1 (see Forced Movement and Reposition). If you are calculating your movement on your turn, the count continues through your whole turn, across every Action that moves you. If you move when it isn't your turn, you calculate it from the start: you don't keep track of the movement from your previous turn.

  • Counting squares: count the squares from one creature to the other, not the one they stand on. A creature next to you, even diagonally, is 1 square away.
  • Elevation: count the squares up or down as you count the squares on the map, then add them together like a diagonal: the larger count, plus half the smaller one (rounded down). A creature 4 squares away and 4 squares up is 6 squares away. How tall a creature is: see Size.

Size

Every creature has a Size: a rank from 1 to 7, its SR (Size Rank), written with its name first, such as Medium (3). Size says how many squares a creature occupies, and it is used to compare creatures.

Size Rank Occupies Squares
Tiny 1 1 × 1 1
Small 2 1 × 1 1
Medium 3 1 × 1 1
Large 4 2 × 2 4
Huge 5 3 × 3 9
Gargantuan 6 4 × 4 16
Titanic 7 5 × 5 25

The side of the area a creature occupies is its rank minus 2, and at least 1. An Action can compare SR: "The target's SR is at most 1 higher than yours" means the target's rank is no more than one above yours. Any smaller creature qualifies.

A creature is as tall as it is wide: one that occupies more than one square also occupies more squares height-wise, so imagine it as a cube. A Large creature (2 × 2) is 2 squares tall, and a Gargantuan one (4 × 4) is 4 squares tall. This might feel odd for some creatures, such as a horse, but the simplification is necessary for the game to work.

Size doesn't change Reach by itself: a Large creature still has Reach 1. Anything that makes a creature bigger says whether its Reach changes too.

Squeezing

Creatures can normally squeeze to reach places smaller than themselves, but not to the point that wouldn't make sense. A creature can squeeze through a space one rank below theirs (a Large creature, 2 × 2, can squeeze through a gap 1 square wide), and each square they move this way counts as Difficult Terrain. If a creature stops moving while squeezing to do another Action, meaning they don't fit completely in the space they are in, all their Rolls are Doomed and they are Vulnerable equal to half their level (rounded up) until they move to a space where they fit completely. Some creatures have features that change this, like a Gargantuan (4 × 4) snake with "The snake can squeeze through 1 square", but this doesn't mean the creature can safely end its turn while squeezing: unless otherwise noted, the Doomed penalty and the Vulnerable condition above still apply.

Forced Movement and Reposition never squeeze a creature: they stop in the last square where it fits completely (see Collision).

Movement

An Action that lets you move gives a distance, written "Advance 5" or "Evade 2". You can always move less than the specified amount: "Advance 5" means up to 5 squares, not exactly 5. But each time you move with an Action, you can't move more than your current Speed, unless an ability says otherwise: with Speed 2, an Action that lets you Evade 3 lets you Evade only 2. Forced Movement and Reposition don't use your Speed.

Advance

Advance is the normal movement, and the Action every creature uses to move. You only move with an Action that moves you: Advance, Evade, or another Action that says so (someone else moving you, with Forced Movement or Reposition, is not you moving). An Advance can trigger reactions.

You can split an Advance: move part of it, use other Actions, then move the rest later in the same turn. Movement you don't use is lost at the end of your turn. You can only split the movement of the Advance Action itself. If another Action moves you as part of a sequence (such as Charge), you can't split that movement. For instance, when you Charge, you can't Advance 3, make your Weapon Attack (1A) and then Advance the rest of your movement.

When you split an Advance, it is still one Action. A Reaction that triggers on your movement can fire only once for that Advance, no matter how many segments you move in.

Evade

Evade is a type of movement that doesn't trigger reactions. You can't Evade into or pass through Difficult Terrain. What an Evade costs is decided by the Action that lets you Evade, such as the Evade Action. The Evade Action doesn't have the Move trait.

You can't split an Evade the way you can split the Advance Action. If an Action lets you Evade 3 and you Evade 1 square, you can't Evade the other 2 squares later for free.

When an Action allows you to Evade, you can use the movement as an Advance instead. By itself this brings no benefit, but it lets a creature move willingly through reactions, or move at all if they are unable to Evade.

Moving and Leaving Your Square

Rules ask three different questions about movement, and the answers are not the same:

Movement A Move Action You moved You left your square
Advance, Charge, Crawl Yes Yes Yes
Evade No Yes Yes
Stand Yes No No
Forced Movement (Pushed, Pulled and Slid) No No Yes
Reposition No No Yes
  • Move with a capital letter always means a Move Action: an Action with the Move trait. "Until you use a Move Action" ends only with a Move Action.
  • "You haven't moved" is about your own movement: moving with an Action, Evade included. Forced Movement and Reposition are not you moving, and standing up is not moving.
  • "You haven't left your square" is about any change of position, Forced Movement and Reposition included. A creature that occupies several squares leaves its square when any square it occupies changes.

Corners

You can't cut solid corners when you move. Moving diagonally takes you through the corner point where four squares meet. If a wall or anything solid occupies that point, the corner is solid and you can't move diagonally past it: you must go around. A wall that ends exactly at the corner counts, and so does a pillar that stands on the corner or an object that fills a square beside your path, because each of them reaches the point. A small object in the middle of a square doesn't reach the corner, so it doesn't block the diagonal. This applies to every movement of yours (Advance, Evade, Charge) and to Forced Movement and Reposition. A creature that occupies several squares follows the rule with each square it occupies.

Creatures aren't solid corners: you can move diagonally between two creatures. If both are unwilling, this is Difficult Terrain, as if you were passing through the squares of an unwilling creature; a willing creature lets you pass. If a Forced Movement would move a creature diagonally between two creatures, it doesn't pass through them: it stops in the last legal square and Collision applies as normal. A Reposition can't pass between two creatures either: it stops in the last legal square, without Collision damage.

You (Y) move diagonally. A red X is a solid corner: you must go around. A green dot is an open corner.

Straight Line

When an Action, effect or rule moves a creature (yourself included) in a straight line, like the Charge Action, draw a line from the center of the creature's square to the center of the destination square. The creature moves along the same squares as a Line: one square per step along the line's longer direction, at each step the square whose center is nearest to the line, ending in the destination. Where the line passes exactly between two squares, choose a side once, left or right (looking toward the destination), for the whole movement: every tie takes that side. The creature that moves chooses for its own movement; the origin chooses for a Forced Movement. If a square on the line isn't a legal move (for Charge: Difficult Terrain, a creature in the way, or a solid corner), the movement stops in the last legal square before it.

In the figures, Y is you. Blue squares are the squares the token passes, the green square is where it ends, and red squares are not legal. The line is what counts: the path of the token on the grid does not have to be exactly straight, because a token moves from square to square.

Forced Movement

Forced Movement is movement you impose on another creature, choosing where it goes. There are three types: Pushed, Pulled and Slid. Reposition also moves a creature of your choice, but it isn't Forced Movement. Every Forced Movement follows these rules:

  • It doesn't trigger reactions.
  • It ignores Difficult Terrain.
  • It can move a creature into Hazardous Terrain or a Deadly Hazard.
  • It doesn't use the creature's Speed, and each square counts as 1, even when it is diagonal.
  • Its value is reduced by the creature's Steadiness.
  • Its value is a maximum: you can always move the creature less. It can never make a creature end its movement inside another creature or an object: this is Collision, and it only happens when the destination you chose is blocked.

Pushed

Being Pushed is a Forced Movement. Pushed has a value: up to that many squares you are moved. The origin chooses the destination. Each square you are pushed into must be farther from the origin than the previous one (measured in a straight line between the centers of the squares), and the path must stay within a cone spreading from the origin through you.

That cone is every step going in a direction within 45° of the straight line from the origin to you. Each square counts as 1 toward the value, even when it is diagonal. The 45° edge counts for Pushed. It doesn't for Pulled, whose cone is narrower.

If something blocks the way to the destination the origin chose, Collision applies.

Example

A goblin stands directly below you and you use Pushed 3 on them. They can end in any square in the cone shown below: 1 square to the side after 1 step, 2 after 2 steps, and 3 after 3 steps. They can't be moved sideways or back toward you. If the goblin were diagonal from you, the cone would point diagonally.

The numbered squares are where G can end, and the number is how many steps it takes.

Pulled

Being Pulled is a Forced Movement, the opposite of Pushed. Pulled has a value: up to that many squares you are moved. The origin chooses the destination. Each square you are pulled into must be closer to the origin than the previous one (measured in a straight line between the centers of the squares), and the path must stay inside a cone that has its apex on you and points toward the origin.

That cone is every direction less than 45° from the straight line from you to the origin: the 45° edge is not part of it. Each square counts as 1 toward the value, even when it is diagonal. A creature that is next to the origin can't be pulled any closer, so it stops there.

You can't be pulled into the origin's square. The origin can end the movement next to it, or choose its own square as the destination: then Collision applies, with the origin as the creature in the way, and both take damage for the unused squares.

Example

A goblin stands directly above you, 3 squares away, and you use Pulled 3 on them. They can end in any square in the cone shown below: straight toward you after 1 step, or any of the 3 squares next to you after 2 steps. Then they stop. You can leave them there, with no damage, or choose your own square as the destination: the 3rd square is then unused, Collision applies and you and the goblin take 1 damage each.

The numbered squares are where G can end, and the number is how many steps it takes. You can stop G next to you, or name your own square as the destination: G stops next to you and Collision applies.

Slid

Being Slid is a Forced Movement. Slid has a value: up to that many squares of path you are moved. The origin chooses the destination and the exact path the creature takes: a Slid can curve, moving the creature closer to the origin, farther from it, or sideways.

Each square of the path must be next to the previous one, and each counts as 1 toward the value, even when it is diagonal. The path can't cross a wall or pass through an object, and it can't pass through another creature's square or the origin's: if the path the origin chose does, Collision applies at that point. The origin must have Line of Effect to the creature at every square of the path; if it would lose it, the movement ends there (that is not a Collision, so there is no damage).

Example

A goblin stands 2 squares east of you, with a brazier (Hazardous Terrain) in the square between you. You use Slid 3: the goblin goes 1 square northwest, then 1 west, ending in the square directly north of you. It used 2 of its 3 squares, passed beside the brazier without entering it, and you had Line of Effect to it all along. The path curved: the value counted squares of path, not straight-line distance.

The numbered squares are the path, in order. The thin green lines are the Line of Effect from you (Y): it must reach G at every square.

Collision

Collision happens when something is in the way of the destination the origin chose. For Pushed and Pulled, calculate it from the center of the creature: draw a line from where the creature started to where the Forced Movement was meant to end. For Slid, follow the path the origin chose. If something is in the way, the creature stops in the last legal square the line or the path passes through: a square where the whole creature fits and that has no object or creature in it.

A creature that stops short takes Lethal Blunt Vigor damage for its unused squares: the squares between where it stopped and the destination the origin chose.

  • An object (a wall, a closed door, etc.): 2 damage for each unused square.
  • Another creature, or the origin: 1 damage for each unused square, to each of the two creatures.

The origin decides how far to aim. If it chooses a destination the creature can reach, there is no Collision and no damage: moving a creature less than the value never hurts it.

Example

Pushed 4 aimed at a destination 4 squares away, with a wall right behind you, deals 8 damage. Aimed through a creature after 1 square, it deals 3 damage to each. Pushed 1 square, with nothing in the way, deals no damage.

A Reposition stops at an object or a creature without taking Collision damage.

Reposition

Reposition moves a creature of your choice along a path, but it isn't Forced Movement. Reposition has a value: up to that many squares of path. Your Reach and the creature's Size don't limit it. The path follows the rules of Slid (each square next to the previous one, each counting as 1 even when it is diagonal, and the origin must have Line of Effect to the creature at every square), with these rules:

  • It doesn't trigger reactions.
  • It ignores Difficult Terrain.
  • Its value is reduced by the creature's Steadiness.
  • It can't move a creature into Hazardous Terrain or a Deadly Hazard, though it can move the creature to a less advantageous position, for example into a flanked square.
  • It can't pass through creatures or objects: it stops in the last legal square before one, without Collision damage. It can never make a creature end its movement inside another creature or an object.
Example

Dave stands between you and a goblin, in a line, with an empty square between you and Dave. Reposition 2 can't move the goblin through Dave's square, but the path can go around him: 1 square diagonally over Dave, then 1 square diagonally down, into the empty square next to you.

The numbered squares are the path, in order. The red line is the straight way through Dave: a Reposition can't pass through a creature.

Steadiness

Some creatures are harder to move than others. Every creature has a Steadiness: normally 0, and never negative. When you are moved by Forced Movement or Reposition, reduce its value by your Steadiness: a Pushed 3 against Steadiness 1 is a Pushed 2, and a value of 0 or less moves you nowhere. The value is reduced before the origin chooses the destination, so a creature with Steadiness also takes less damage from Collision (the origin can aim less far). You can choose not to apply your Steadiness, for example when an ally helps you.

Difficult Terrain

Each square of difficult terrain counts double the squares it would normally count. The GM decides where it is (rubble, deep snow...). Darkness is Difficult Terrain for creatures that can't see in it. Passing through the squares of an unwilling creature is Difficult Terrain too.

Hazardous Terrain

Hazardous Terrain is any terrain that deals damage or inflicts a harmful condition on the creatures in it. The effect that creates it is marked [HT] in its text and declares one or more trigger patterns.

[HT] is notation, like [W] and [Mod]: it is written in the text of the effect that creates the area, not among the Action's traits (a Weapon Attack whose Tier 2 leaves caltrops is not a hazard itself). It declares its pattern(s) after a colon, such as [HT: Entering] or [HT: Entering, Turn]: "The area lasts 2 rounds [HT: Entering, Turn]." The three patterns are:

  • Entering: "When a creature enters this area." A creature in the area when it appears is treated as entering it.
  • Turn: "When a creature ends its turn in this area."
  • Movement: "When a creature moves within this area." Triggers once per Movement (Advance, Evade, or Forced Movement) that crosses any square of the area.

An [HT] effect with no declared pattern uses Entering and Turn by default.

Turn-cycle limit. Entering and Turn both count against the same per-creature turn-cycle limit. A creature that enters and ends its turn in the same area is affected at most once per turn-cycle. The Movement pattern is exempt: it can hit the same creature multiple times per round, once per Movement.

Size. An [HT] area affects a creature once regardless of its Size.

Example

A 2 Emanation of fire (Distance 10 sq.) has [HT: Entering, Turn]. A creature that walks into it takes 1d8 Body Fire damage on entering; a creature already inside takes the same damage when it ends its turn there. A creature that enters, leaves and re-enters with a different Movement in the same turn-cycle is affected at most once.

Deadly Hazards

Some hazards kill at once: a fall into a chasm, a pit of lava, a crushing trap. These are Deadly Hazards. A Forced Movement into one kills Minor enemies, and a Reposition can't move a creature into a Deadly Hazard at all (see Reposition). Any other creature, Player Characters included, isn't killed: they barely avoid it and are Imperiled, for example hanging from the edge of the chasm.

A creature that enters a Deadly Hazard voluntarily (by its own movement, or by choosing not to escape) also chooses: it is either Imperiled or outright killed.

Reach

Reach is how far your body reaches: how far you can interact with things in the world. By default, every Species has a Reach of 1: you can reach 1 square from your position.

When an Action shows "Reach" as its distance (touching an ally, grabbing an object, tripping a creature), it means your Reach. Weapons don't change your Reach: they have a Range of their own (see Weapon Range, where a Reach above 1 adds to the Range of your melee weapons).

You can't target a creature or an object beyond the distance of an Action (its Reach or its range).

The numbers are the distance in squares from the creature in the middle.

Weapon Range

Every weapon has a Range, melee weapons included. An Action whose distance is Weapon uses the Range of the weapon you use for it. Weapon Actions have that distance unless they say otherwise.

  • How it is written. A Range is written like the ranges of ranged weapons: normal / far (1 Bane) / maximum (2 Banes). A single number is a normal range with no far or maximum range. Most melee weapons have Range 1; a lance has Range 1 / 2.
  • Reach above 1. Your Reach above 1 adds to every number of the Range of your melee weapons: with Reach 2, a lance attacks at 2 / 3. It doesn't change the Range of a ranged or thrown attack.
  • Long. A weapon with the Long property takes 1 Bane against adjacent creatures. A pike with Range 3 and Long is at its best 2 or 3 squares away.

Weapon Actions and Your Weapon

A Weapon Action takes its distance and its Melee or Ranged nature from the weapon you use for it, and from how you use it. With a Ranged weapon, or a weapon with the Thrown property when thrown, it uses the weapon's ranged Range and counts as a Ranged Action (Prone's Bane applies to it). With a Melee weapon used in hand, it uses the weapon's melee Range and counts as a Melee Action (Prone's Boon applies). An Action that has the Melee or Ranged trait itself needs a weapon used that way. While you have a free hand, you can use your Fists as your weapon.

Engaged

You are Engaged if you are adjacent to an enemy (next to you, even diagonally) and both of you can be Engaged. Being Engaged does not depend on what you perceive: an enemy next to you that you cannot see still Engages you.

A condition can say that you can't be Engaged, as Unconscious does. A creature that can't be Engaged is never Engaged, and the creatures next to it are not Engaged with it.

By itself, being Engaged does nothing. Other rules use the word, such as Ranged Combat While Engaged below.

Ranged Combat While Engaged

Using a [Ranged] Action while you are Engaged is hard: you suffer 2 Banes.

When you use a [Ranged] Action against a creature that is Engaged with a creature other than yourself, you suffer 1 Bane.

Flanking

A creature threatens every enemy within the normal range of a Melee weapon, for as long as it qualifies to use that weapon's Weapon Attack (1A). The 3-Strike limit doesn't remove the threat: it prevents using the Action, not qualifying for it. Some conditions, such as Unconscious, cause a creature to threaten no one, and a weapon with the Non-threatening property doesn't threaten, unless a feature says otherwise. The far range of a Melee weapon doesn't count, and a Melee weapon with the Long property doesn't threaten adjacent enemies.

Like Engaged, threatening does not depend on what you perceive, but it uses range and not adjacency: a creature with a Range 2 weapon threatens an enemy 2 squares away, which is not Engaged with it.

Creatures that threaten the same enemy flank it. A creature that flanks an enemy gains 1 Boon on its [Melee] Actions against that enemy for each other creature that flanks it, up to 3 Boons. But each ally of that enemy that threatens the flanker cancels 1 of those Boons, and a creature never gains fewer than 0.

Blue tokens are allies and red-brown tokens are enemies. A badge is the Boons that creature gets on [Melee] Actions against the creature in the middle (G, John in the fifth picture) or against D in the last one.

Example 1: Two flankers. Philip and John both threaten a goblin: each has 1 Boon on [Melee] Actions against it.

Example 2: Three flankers. A third ally threatens it too: 2 Boons each.

Example 3: Four or more. A fourth ally: 3 Boons each, which is the limit. A fifth adds nothing.

Example 4: Range 2. Philip holds a weapon with Range 2 and stands 2 squares from the goblin. He threatens it, so he flanks with John, who is next to it: 1 Boon each.

Example 5: Three goblins on John. Three goblins threaten John, and Philip stands next to two of them. The goblin on the left has the other two goblins flanking with it and no ally of John's next to it: 2 Boons. The other two also have 2 goblins flanking with them, but Philip threatens each of them and cancels 1: 1 Boon each.

Example 6: Two lines of fighters. Two lines of fighters face each other, all of them against D, the second fighter on the left. Each fighter has allies beside it that threaten the enemies in front of them, so almost every Boon is cancelled: D's neighbors cancel the Boons, and only a fighter at the very end of a line can still gain 1, against the second fighter of the other line.

Line of Effect

To target a creature with an Action, you need Line of Effect to them: a straight line from the center of your square to any corner of the target's space that nothing blocks. Walls and similar solid obstacles block a line. If all four corners are blocked, you have no Line of Effect and can't target the creature, even if you perceive it.

An area effect checks Line of Effect from its Point of Origin to each creature in the area (from the center of the space, when the Point of Origin is a creature's space).

A Large creature (2x2 squares or bigger) uses the four corners of its whole space. A Large creature that is the one targeting picks any square it occupies as its starting square.

A line that only touches the end of a wall isn't blocked.

Cover

Cover is how well a target is protected from you. Count the corners of the target's space that are blocked from the center of your square:

Blocked corners Cover Effect on your Actions against it
0 None None
1 Low 1 Bane
2 Medium 2 Banes
3 High 3 Banes
4 Total No Line of Effect: you can't target them

The number of Banes is the number of blocked corners.

  • Creatures in the way. If another creature is on the line from the center of your square to the center of the target's space, the target has Low cover (1 Bane). Creatures never count as blocked corners, and cover doesn't add up: you take the highest cover that applies.
  • Cover and the rest. Cover applies to melee and ranged Actions alike. Its Banes add to other Banes, and to the penalties of Obscured and Hidden creatures.

Leaning

A creature behind a wall can lean out to attack. While you are Leaning, you calculate cover from one of your corners instead of the center of your square, and your own cover against other creatures' Actions is reduced by 1 level (Total → High → Medium → Low → none). A creature leaning out of Total cover becomes High cover (still targetable). Leaning lasts until the end of your turn, so Reactions can use it. You can stop Leaning at any time on your turn, at no cost. See the Leaning Basic Action.

Areas

Some Actions have an Area, written on its own line, for example Area: 1 Emanation. These Actions have the Area trait. An Area Action can also have a Distance, on the next line: how far from the creature using the Action its area can be placed, for example Distance: 10 sq. With no Distance, an Emanation is around the creature that uses the Action.

Every area spreads from a Point of Origin: for an Emanation, its target (a creature's space or a square); for a Line, your square; for other areas, the point the Action says they start from (for example, the tip of a cone). The Point of Origin is a place, not the creature that uses the Action, which is still called the origin.

Emanation

An Emanation, written with a value like 1 Emanation, extends a number of squares around its target: a creature or a square. It affects every creature in its area unless the Action says otherwise. If its target is a creature, the origin decides whether that creature is affected too. For example, the Paladin decides whether the target of a 3 AP Smite also makes the Appeal Save, and a creature using Whirlwind (an Emanation around itself) decides whether it is affected.

An Emanation around a creature extends that many squares from every square the creature occupies, so it follows the creature's Size. A 1 Emanation around a Medium (3) creature covers 3 × 3 squares (its square and the 8 around it); around a Huge (5) creature, which occupies 3 × 3, it covers 5 × 5. A Distance of 10 sq. means the Emanation is placed up to 10 squares from the creature using the Action; with no Distance, it is around that creature.

Aura

An Aura is an area that follows its origin. It extends a number of squares from every square the origin occupies (the same footprint as an Emanation around that creature) and moves with them. It is written with a value, like Aura 2, and its Point of Origin is always the creature it belongs to. The origin decides whether they are affected themselves. A creature gains an Aura's effect as soon as it enters the Aura, and loses it when it leaves. An Emanation, by contrast, is placed at a target and stays there.

Burst

A Burst, written with a value like 2 Burst, is centered on a corner, the point where four squares meet, never on the center of a square or on a creature. It affects every square whose center is within that many squares of the corner, measured in a straight line: a 1 Burst covers the 4 squares around the corner, and a 2 Burst covers 12 (a 4 × 4 block without its corners). Otherwise it works like an Emanation: it affects every creature in its area unless the Action says otherwise, and its Point of Origin is the corner, placed within the Action's Distance.

The dot is the corner the Burst is centered on.

Line

A Line, written with a value like Line 10, is a row of squares that always starts from you: its Point of Origin is the center of your square, as for an Aura. It has no Distance: its value is how far it reaches.

Aim it at any square. Draw a segment from the center of your square through the center of that square, and take one square per step along the segment's longer direction: at each step, the square whose center is nearest to the segment. The first square is the one next to you. The Line goes on past the square you aimed at until the next square would be farther from you than its value (counted like any distance: every second diagonal counts 2). Only those squares are the Line, never every square the segment touches. A Line 10 is 10 squares straight, 8 squares at a slope like 2 to 1 or 5 to 2, and 7 squares on a diagonal.

  • Ties. Where the segment passes exactly between two squares, choose a side before rolling: left or right, seen from above looking from you toward the square you aimed at. Every tie in that Line takes that side. A Line with no ties has nothing to choose. Both sides give the same number of squares, though not always the same shape.
  • Larger creatures. A creature bigger than Medium starts the Line from one of the squares of its space on the side it aims toward.
  • Walls. A Line stops at the first square it can't enter (a wall or a solid obstacle): the squares behind it are not part of the Line. This matters for a Line that stays on the map, or Hazardous Terrain.
  • Height. A Line is 1 square high unless the Action says otherwise: it doesn't reach a creature flying above its squares.
  • Targets and cover. A Line affects every creature in its squares unless the Action says otherwise, and the Action is rolled once for all of them. Line of Effect and Cover are checked from your square, as for any area, but creatures in the Line don't give cover to other creatures in it (walls still do).
  • No range Banes. The Line's value is its reach, so a weapon's far or maximum range adds no Banes inside it. Other Banes still apply: Engaged for a [Ranged] Action, Cover from walls.
Example

You use a Line 6 and aim at the square 2 squares east and 1 south of you. The segment passes exactly between two squares at the first, third and fifth steps. You choose left: the Line takes the square directly east of you, then goes 1 south for every 2 squares east, 5 squares in all (the 6th would be 7 squares away). Had you chosen right, it would start with the square diagonally southeast of you: a different shape, the same 5 squares.

Y is you. The number in each square is its distance from you, counted as for any distance. The dashed line is the segment from the center of your square through the square you aim at (outlined).

Orange squares are ties (the segment passes exactly between two squares): every tie takes the side you chose. Dashed squares are the other side, not taken.

Y is you. The dashed line is the segment; the outlined square is the one aimed at.

Other areas

Other areas (cones) also affect every creature in them unless the Action says otherwise. Line of Effect and Cover are checked from the Point of Origin to each creature, and the Action is rolled once for all its targets.

Light

Light affects Sight (see Senses): the light where a creature is decides how well you see it.

A light source sheds Bright light in an Emanation of its size, and Dim light for the same number of squares beyond that. A torch with Bright light 4 gives Bright light in a 4 Emanation and Dim light from 5 to 8 squares away (an 8 Emanation). Smite's Bright light 2 gives Bright light in a 2 Emanation and Dim light from 3 to 4. Beyond that, the light of the scene applies (daylight, night, a dark cave...).

A light has the same shape as an area: around a creature or an object it is an Emanation, counted from every square it occupies, and a light placed on a corner is a Burst. So a Bright light 2 reaches the same squares as a 2 Emanation, and its Dim light works the same way with its own size.

Bright squares: strong color. Dim squares: pale. The torch is the dot in the middle.

Light never makes a square darker: where several lights reach, use the brightest one. A torch carried in daylight changes nothing. A creature is in one light level at a time, so its penalties never stack. A creature bigger than one square is in the brightest light of any square it occupies: you see the part of it that is lit.

Bright

You see creatures in Bright light Clearly, and you move normally. Bright light is daylight outside, or the Bright light of a light source.

Dim

When you target something that is in Dim light, the target is Obscured (see Senses). Dim light is twilight, a full moon, or the Dim light of a light source, such as the edge of a bonfire's light.

Darkness

When you target something that is in Darkness, you can't see it: it is Hidden if you perceive it some other way, and Unsensed if you don't (see Senses). Squares in darkness are Difficult Terrain for you.

Senses

You perceive creatures through your senses. Each sense lets you perceive some creatures as Clear, Obscured or Hidden. Use your best sense for each creature; a sense never makes a creature harder to perceive. A creature you perceive with none of your senses is Unsensed.

Perception Your Actions against it Its Actions against you
Clear Normal. Normal.
Obscured 1 Bane. 1 Boon.
Hidden Doomed. Favored.
Unsensed You can't target it. Favored.

Actions with the Area trait ignore Senses: they target the creatures in their area however well you perceive them.

A roll that is both Favored and Doomed is a normal roll (see Favored and Doomed Rolls). But you gain no Favored or Boon from a creature's poor perception of you if you perceive that creature as Hidden: two creatures that are each Hidden to the other are both Doomed against each other, and neither is Favored.

Every creature has Sight and Hearing:

  • Sight: You perceive creatures in Bright light as Clear and creatures in Dim light as Obscured. You can't see creatures in Darkness, or creatures you have no Line of Effect to.
  • Hearing: You perceive creatures that make noise within 10 squares as Hidden, even through walls. Creatures in combat make noise, even standing in the same square on their turn. A creature can Hide to stop making noise.

So in Darkness you usually still hear a creature, and it is Hidden to you; an invisible creature that you only hear is Hidden too.

To be decided

Hiding and sneaking are not written yet.

Sight Senses

Sight Senses change how light works for your Sight. They also remove the Difficult Terrain of Darkness.

  • Dim-light Sight: For your Sight, Dim light counts as Bright.
  • Dark Sight: For your Sight, Dim light and Darkness count as Bright.

Other Senses

Other senses work in any light, but only within their range and conditions, and only to perceive creatures: they don't help your Rolls that require sight. Some examples:

  • Lesser Heatsight (6 sq.): You perceive creatures within 6 squares as Obscured, if they give off heat or are much colder than their surroundings.
  • Heatsight (6 sq.): As Lesser Heatsight, but you perceive them as Clear.
  • Tremorsense (6 sq.): You perceive creatures within 6 squares as Clear, if they touch the same ground as you and no thick wall is between you.
Example

A Goblin with Dark Sight stands in Darkness, and Krysta, who has only Sight and Hearing, stands in Bright torchlight. Krysta can only hear the Goblin, so it is Hidden to her: her Actions against it are Doomed, and its Actions against her are Favored. The Goblin sees Krysta Clearly.

A Snake with Lesser Heatsight attacks Krysta in the same Darkness. Krysta is warm, so the Snake perceives her as Obscured (1 Bane on its Actions), while she only hears the Snake (Hidden: Doomed against it, and the Snake is Favored against her). In Dim light, the Snake's heatsight would give it nothing more than its Sight.

A Cave Worm with Tremorsense loses Krysta when she climbs a rope. If she is also quiet (she Hides) and it is dark, she is Unsensed: the Worm can't target her.

Krysta and a Goblin, neither with Dark Sight, fight in Darkness. Each only hears the other, so each is Hidden to the other: both are Doomed against each other, and neither is Favored.

The GM decides how each creature perceives each other when it isn't obvious.

Chapter 4: Health

Health Pool

Your Health Pool is the sum of two thresholds:

  • Body Damage Threshold (BDT) = ((Body + 2) × Body Health Multiplier + Species Body Health) × (Level + 17) ÷ 16
  • Mind Damage Threshold (MDT) = ((Mind + 2) × Mind Health Multiplier + Species Mind Health) × (Level + 17) ÷ 16

Round each threshold up. The Health Multipliers come from your class, and the Species Health values from your species.

Example: a level 4 Human Paladin has Body +2 and Mind +1. BDT = ((2 + 2) × 4 + 3) × 21 ÷ 16 = 24.9, rounded up to 25. MDT = ((1 + 2) × 4 + 4) × 21 ÷ 16 = 21. Health Pool = 46.

Body and Mind are not separate pools. All damage reduces the same Health Pool. The thresholds only decide which conditions you suffer.

Damage

Record every point of damage as:

  • Body or Mind
  • Lethal or Nonlethal

Damage is Lethal unless an Action or effect says it is Nonlethal. Lethal and Nonlethal damage both count toward the thresholds and toward your total damage. They never cancel or convert each other. Damage keeps accumulating after you are Down.

When you have Body damage equal to or more than your BDT, you are Bloodied. When you have Mind damage equal to or more than your MDT, you are Strained.

Instances of Damage

An Action's damage can come in several instances: each part with its own pool and damage type is one instance. "[V] 1[W] + [Mod] + [R] 2d8 Ho" deals two: the weapon's damage with your Modifier (Body, the weapon's type), and 2d8 Holy (Mind). Each [W] is its own instance (2[W] is two, see the Gear document), and extra damage from another effect, such as Divine Surge's 1d8, is its own instance. Each target takes its own instances. Rules that count instances, such as Hallowed Stance ("one instance of damage per Action"), count them this way.

Lethal and Nonlethal

Actions deal Lethal damage by default. Actions with the Nonlethal trait deal Nonlethal damage by default. A Nonlethal Action can deal Lethal damage by taking a Bane, and a Lethal Action can deal Nonlethal damage by taking a Bane as well.

How damage is written on a card ([V], [R], [W], [Mod] and the damage types) is in Roll Effects. Empowered and Enfeebled, which change the dice of damage, are in Conditions.

Going Down

When your total damage equals or goes above your Health Pool, you are Down. Follow these steps in order:

  1. Nonlethal damage ≥ 50% of your Health Pool: you fall Unconscious.
  2. Some Nonlethal damage, but under 50%: choose to fall Unconscious or keep fighting as Desperate.
  3. No Nonlethal damage: check your Dominant Damage and become Dying or Shattered.

Getting Back Up

When your damage drops below your Health Pool, you are no longer Down: Desperate, Dying and Shattered end, and if you are Unconscious, you wake up at once.

NPCs Going Down

When an NPC goes Down, they die if they have more Lethal damage, or fall Unconscious if they have more Nonlethal damage. If they are equal, the GM chooses.

Dominant Damage

Compare your total Body damage to your total Mind damage. The higher one is your Dominant Damage. On a tie, the creature that goes Down chooses.

Dominant Damage doesn't do anything by itself, but it determines the condition you gain when you go Down. Some abilities might also make use of it. You know which Dominant Damage a creature has if you perceive them (Hidden is enough).

  • Body is dominant: you become Dying.
  • Mind is dominant: you become Shattered.

Wounds and the Tables

Every result on the Dying or Shattered Table is a Wound. A Wound from the Dying Table is a Body Wound. A Wound from the Shattered Table is a Mind Wound.

Roll: 2d12 (no exploding dice) − your Body (Dying) or Mind (Shattered) + 1d6 per Wound of the same kind you already have (Body Wounds for the Dying Table, Mind Wounds for the Shattered Table).

Example: a character with 3 Body and 1 Body Wound rolls 2d12 − 3 + 1d6 on the Dying Table.

Rally Points

Each character begins with Rally Points equal to your class's Rally value + ⌈(Body + Mind + your level) ÷ 2⌉, where Body and Mind are your Attributes.

When a Rally Point is used, normally with Steel Yourself, the target removes ⌈their Health Pool ÷ 4⌉ damage. You choose which damage to remove.

Heal. When a feature says a creature heals a number, it removes that much damage. The creature chooses which damage, as with a Rally Point.

If you are Exhausted, use your Health Pool as if you weren't: Exhausted doesn't lower your Rally Points or the damage each one removes.

While Down (Desperate, Dying or Shattered) or Unconscious, you can't use Steel Yourself. Other Actions can still let you use Rally Points, such as Lay On Hands.

Nonlethal damage is removed like any other damage.

Only a Rest restores Rally Points. Sleep does not. A new encounter does not.

Guard

Some effects give you Guard, written with a value: Guard 6. When you take damage, your Guard takes it first (Body or Mind damage alike), and only the rest reaches your Health. Damage your Guard takes isn't damage you take: it doesn't count toward your thresholds or your Health Pool, and it doesn't trigger anything that happens when you take damage.

  • Damage reduction (such as Hallowed Stance) applies first; Guard takes what is left.
  • Guard isn't Health: it can't be healed, and Rally Points don't restore it.
  • It doesn't stack: if you gain Guard while you have some, keep the higher value (see Stacking).
  • It ends at the end of the encounter.
Example

Zack has Guard 6 and takes 8 Body damage: his Guard takes 6 and ends, and he takes 2 Body damage.

Recovery

There are three ways to recover, from shortest to longest: a Breather, a Sleep and a Rest.

Take a Breather

Taking a Breather is 10 minutes of rest. It removes all of your Nonlethal damage. It is not a Rest (see below).

Sleep

Sleep is 8 hours of non-strenuous activity, such as sleeping or keeping a quiet watch. You can complete a Sleep at most once every 16 hours. Completing a Sleep lets you use your Daily Actions again.

If you go 24 hours without completing a Sleep, you are Exhausted 1. The value increases by 1 for every further 24 hours without one.

Completing a Sleep reduces your Exhausted value by 2.

Rest

To complete a Rest, a character spends a day (24 hours) resting in a safe place. A Rest includes a Sleep.

A safe place is a place where you are sure you can rest safely, such as a town. The GM decides what is a safe place. Towns are almost always safe. Someone might still try to attack you while you rest and sleep, but it isn't the same as sleeping in the wilderness, where a beast could show up at any moment. A town with a lot of unrest might not be safe. The GM has the final say.

When you complete a Rest, you remove all the damage you took and you regain all your Rally Points. If you have Wounds, roll a Body Check for your Body Wounds and a Mind Check for your Mind Wounds. Each check removes Wounds of its own type only (Body Check → Body Wounds; Mind Check → Mind Wounds).

Roll Result
Tier 1 You remove 1 Wound.
Tier 2 You remove 2 Wounds.
Tier 3 You remove 3 Wounds.

You are Down and still fighting. You are Slowed 1, or increase your Slowed value by 1 (to a maximum of 3), while Desperate.

Unconscious

  • You can't take Actions, you are Prone, and you drop everything you hold.
  • You can't be Engaged, and you threaten no one (see Flanking).
  • While Unconscious, every creature is Unsensed to you.
  • All your Rolls are Doomed, whatever the Attribute. The only exceptions are the Checks you roll each turn while Dying (Body) or Shattered (Mind); those are not Doomed.

  • You can't wake up while Dying or Shattered, or while your damage is equal to or more than your Health Pool.
  • While Unconscious and not Dying nor Shattered, every hour you remove damage equal to what one of your Rally Points removes.
  • If you take damage while Down, you roll on the Dying or Shattered Table. If you weren't Dying or Shattered yet, check your Dominant Damage first and become one of them.

An Unconscious creature isn't a threat, so creatures normally don't attack them, even mindless ones (some very cruel creatures might). A creature that keeps fighting while Dying or Shattered is still a threat, and is attacked like anyone else.

Building a Character

  1. Attributes. All four Attributes (Body, Mind, Appeal, Instinct) start at 0.
  2. Species. Apply your species' Attribute changes (the species are in the Species document). It also gives you Species Body Health and Species Mind Health, a Speed, your Reach (1), your Steadiness and your Size (all current species are Medium (3)).
  3. Class. Apply your class' Attribute increases. It also gives you a Body Health Multiplier and a Mind Health Multiplier, a Rally value, a Speed Modifier, a Weapon Modifier and a Power Modifier. Your Speed is your species' Speed plus your class' Speed Modifier.
  4. Choices. When a species or class says "or", pick one. A class's choices between two Attributes must be different from each other (see Distinct Choices).
  5. Health Pool. Calculate it from your Attributes, the Health Multipliers and the Species Health values:
    • Body Damage Threshold = ((Body + 2) × Body Health Multiplier + Species Body Health) × (Level + 17) ÷ 16
    • Mind Damage Threshold = ((Mind + 2) × Mind Health Multiplier + Species Mind Health) × (Level + 17) ÷ 16
    • Health Pool = the two thresholds added together.
  6. Rally Points = your class' Rally value + ⌈(Body + Mind + your level) ÷ 2⌉.
  7. Features and Talents. Take the Features and Class Actions of your class for your level, the Picks its level list gives you, and the Talents they let you pick (the Talents are in the Talents document; weapons and armor in the Gear document).
  8. Escalation. Choose the Escalation abilities your class's level list gives you, from its Escalation abilities.

Classes

Your class gives you Attribute increases, a Body Health Multiplier and a Mind Health Multiplier (used for your Health Pool), a Rally value (used for Rally Points), a Speed Modifier, a Weapon Modifier and a Power Modifier, an Armor Training, and a list of Escalation abilities. Round modifiers up. A class's Weapon Modifier and Power Modifier use the higher of the Attributes it lists for each, and can't use the same Attribute.

When you pick your class, you choose its Attribute increases when they say "or". These choices can't be changed later.

Each class is written in its own document: Paladin and Archer.

Class Actions, Picks and the Repertoire

A class's Class Actions are the Actions it gives you at level 1. A class can give a Hallmark Action instead: a choice of two Actions that are the class's fantasy (the Paladin: Smite or Lay On Hands; the Archer: Double Shot or Rolling Shot); you take one, and the other is not yours. A Hallmark Action doesn't need the Signature trait: that trait only sets the Modifier an Action rolls (Smite has it, Double Shot makes no roll of its own). Its Repertoire is everything else it offers, listed by Frequency: Passive (it works on its own), Turn, Round, Encounter and Daily. A Pick is one choice from the Repertoire: the level list says how many Picks you take at each level and from which list (an Encounter Pick comes from the Encounter list; a Pick "from any list" can come from any of them). Escalation abilities are chosen the same way, from the class's Escalation list.

Leveling Up

Each class has a level list: what you gain at each level (Features, Class Actions, Picks and Escalation abilities). When you reach a new level, you take what its entry gives you. Your Health thresholds grow with your level too (see Building a Character, step 5).

Stages

The levels are grouped in Stages: Stage 1 is levels 1 to 4, Stage 2 levels 5 to 7, Stage 3 levels 8 to 10. A new Stage raises your Modifiers (see Weapon, Power and Signature Modifiers) and the NPCs of the Bestiary (their tables have one row per Stage).

Distinct Choices

When your class gives you a choice between two Attributes, you can't pick an Attribute you already picked from another choice of the same kind:

  • Attribute increases. Each one goes to a different Attribute. If a class offers "Body or Appeal +1" and "Appeal or Instinct +1" and you pick Appeal for the first, you must pick Instinct for the second.
  • Weapon and Power Modifiers. They never use the same Attribute (see below).

The two kinds don't limit each other: a Paladin who adds +1 to Body can still use Body for the Weapon Modifier.

Weapon, Power and Signature Modifiers

Each class lists two Attributes for its Weapon Modifier and two for its Power Modifier. Each Modifier uses the higher of its two Attributes; you never add them together:

  • Weapon Modifier = the higher of the class's two Weapon Attributes ÷ 2 + 1
  • Power Modifier = the higher of the class's two Power Attributes ÷ 2 + 1

The Attribute a Modifier uses is your Weapon Attribute or your Power Attribute.

Your Modifiers grow with your Stage: your Weapon and Power Modifiers are +1 at level 5 (Stage 2) and +2 at level 8 (Stage 3), but never above +4. A Modifier of +3 at level 1 is +4 from level 5 on.

If the same Attribute is the higher one for both Modifiers, one of them uses its other Attribute instead. You choose which, and you can change that choice when you level up.

Your Signature Modifier is the higher of your Weapon Modifier and your Power Modifier (if they are equal, choose one). Its Attribute is your Signature Attribute. Actions with the Signature trait roll it.

Example

A Paladin with Body 1, Mind 2 and Appeal 3. Both Modifiers want Appeal. If the Weapon Modifier keeps it, it is 3 (3 ÷ 2 = 1.5, rounded up to 2, + 1) and the Power Modifier uses Mind: 2 (2 ÷ 2 + 1); the Signature Modifier is 3, from Appeal. If the Power Modifier keeps Appeal instead, it is 3, and the Weapon Modifier uses Body: 2 (1 ÷ 2 = 0.5, rounded up to 1, + 1).

Escalation Abilities

Each class has a list of Escalation abilities: Passives, which work while you have enough Escalation, and Actives, Actions that cost Escalation on top of their AP. Your class's level list says how many of each you choose at every level. See Escalation for how you gain and spend it.

Chapter 7: Traits

All Actions have traits. Traits have their own rule interactions. Traits with no rule of their own (such as Divine) are used by other rules and features.

Area

These Actions have an Area (see Areas), and can have a Distance that says how far from you the area can be placed. They target every creature inside their area that they affect, and they ignore Senses: they target creatures you perceive as Obscured, Hidden or Unsensed, with no penalty for it, and light doesn't change their roll. You need Line of Effect to the Point of Origin to place the area, and Line of Effect and Cover are checked from the Point of Origin to each creature. A Line has no Distance: it always starts from your square.

Body, Mind, Appeal and Instinct

An Action with one of these traits rolls that Attribute Modifier. A Body Action rolls Body, and so on. Some effects care about them, for example "your Body Actions gain 1 Boon".

When an Action has more than one of these traits, you roll the one with the highest value. The Action still has all of them: Trip is a Body Action and an Instinct Action, whichever it rolls. If the Action has a Vs. entry that says "the one used to roll," that Attribute is also the Defense: if you roll Body, you roll against Body.

Fear

The Fear trait has no rules of its own. Other rules, Talents and Tags refer to it.

Focus

These Actions require concentration. This is not a mechanic by itself, but some features affect your ability to concentrate.

Gun

Gun Actions need a weapon with the Firearm property, such as a pistol or a musket, and use its Range as their Distance. A Weapon Action without this trait gets it when you use such a weapon (so a Weapon Attack with a musket is a Gun Action). A Firearm is not a Projectile weapon: Shot Actions can't use it.

Handle

To use an Action with the Handle trait, you must be able to use your hands (or similar appendages). You also need a hand free to make any Action with this trait. Releasing an object costs no AP (Release).

Melee

These Actions need a melee weapon used in hand, and use its Range as their Distance. A Weapon Action without this trait gets it when you use a melee weapon in hand (see Weapon Actions and Your Weapon).

Move

A Move is an Action with the Move trait.

To use an Action with the Move trait, you must have a Speed of at least 1.

You can't end your movement in a square with another creature. A creature can allow you to pass through their squares. Otherwise, passing through the squares of an unwilling creature is Difficult Terrain.

Nonlethal

These Actions deal Nonlethal damage by default. A Nonlethal Action can deal Lethal damage by taking a Bane.

Power

These Actions roll your Power Modifier.

Ranged

These Actions target creatures from a distance. The Action's Distance can be written like a ranged weapon's Range: normal / far / maximum, with 1 Bane at far range and 2 at maximum. A single number is a normal range only. A [Ranged, Weapon] Action needs a ranged weapon, or a weapon with the Thrown property used thrown, and uses that weapon's Range as its Distance. A Weapon Action without this trait gets it when you use a ranged weapon or throw a Thrown weapon.

Save

A Save Action: its targets roll a Save instead of you rolling against their Defense. See Save Actions.

Self

An Action with the Self trait always targets you, the origin of the Action. An [Area] Action with Self is centered on you: you are the target of its Emanation.

Shot

Shot Actions need a weapon with the Projectile property, such as a bow or a crossbow, and use its Range as their Distance. A Weapon Action without this trait gets it when you use such a weapon (so a Weapon Attack with a crossbow is a Shot).

Signature

These Actions roll your Signature Modifier: the higher of your Weapon Modifier and your Power Modifier, even when they also have the Weapon or Power trait. They keep their other traits: a [Signature, Weapon] Action still needs a weapon and gets every effect on [Weapon] Actions (see Rolls). Signature is used for a class's own Actions, such as its Daily Actions and some Hallmark Actions (Smite).

Split

A Split Action has two modes, Weapon and Power, and you choose one each time you use it. In that use, the Action takes the chosen mode's trait and is an Action of that mode for every rule: a Weapon Action in Weapon mode (it needs a weapon, takes Melee or Ranged from it, and uses its Range), and a Power Action in Power mode (no weapon). The card shows the two modes side by side; everything else (Frequency, target, trigger, Critical) is shared.

Strike

Strike is any Action that tries to hit a creature, such as an attack with a weapon or a spell. You can make at most 3 Strikes per turn.

Weapon

This uses a weapon you are wielding. Actions with this trait require a weapon, and their Distance is the weapon's Range unless they say otherwise.

These Actions roll your Weapon Modifier. A Weapon roll normally targets Armor. When a rule calls for a Weapon roll against a DC, use the DC instead.

A Weapon Action with the Melee or Ranged trait needs a weapon with that property. A Weapon Action without them gets the one that matches the weapon you use.

Tags

Creatures have Tags instead of traits: words that other rules refer to, such as Profane (the Paladin's Divine Power). A creature's Tags are listed in its stat block. Like traits with no rule of their own, a Tag does nothing by itself.

Chapter 8: Conditions

These conditions are in alphabetical order. A creature can't have the same condition twice: if they would gain a condition they already have, keep the longer duration, and for a condition with a value, such as Vulnerable 3, the highest value (see Stacking). The conditions that come from damage (Bloodied, Strained, Desperate, Dying, Shattered and Unconscious) are in Health States.

Empowered

Empowered N adds N to every die of the damage: with Empowered 1, a d6 deals 1 more, and 2d6 deals 2 more. Flat numbers, and damage with no dice, don't change.

Enfeebled

Enfeebled N is the opposite: it subtracts N from every die of the damage, but each die still deals at least 1. Enfeebled always has a value.

A creature uses one Empowered value and one Enfeebled value, the highest of each (see Stacking). Subtract the Enfeebled value from the Empowered value, and the result applies to every die of every instance of damage the Action deals. For example, a Reload Shot makes a Strike Empowered 2, and a Fear effect makes the same creature Enfeebled 1: the Strike is Empowered 1.

Exhausted

Exhausted always has a value, such as Exhausted 2. While you are Exhausted:

  • Your Speed is reduced by the value (to a minimum of 1).
  • All your Rolls take a penalty equal to the value.
  • Your Mind Damage Threshold is reduced by 4 times the value.

A lower Mind Damage Threshold lowers your Health Pool, but not your Rally Points: they use your Health Pool as if you weren't Exhausted.

If your Mind Damage Threshold reaches 0, you must make a Mind Save (DC 13). Mind Damage Threshold cannot go below 0.

Tier Result
Tier 1 You fall Unconscious and start a Sleep. You can't wake up while your Mind Damage Threshold is 0.
Tier 2 Your Exhausted value increases by 1, and you must make this Save again in 12 hours.
Tier 3 You must make this Save again in 12 hours.

Hindered

Your Speed is halved, rounded down.

Immobilized

You can't use Move Actions or Evade. Unless otherwise specified, you can remove Immobilized by spending 1 AP [Move]: the one Move Action you can use while Immobilized.

An Immobilized creature is also hard to move. An Action or effect that attempts to Force Move or Reposition you and doesn't roll is automatically disrupted: you aren't moved.

Example

Lana is Immobilized and her ally, Jared, has a feature that says "At the start of your turn, you can Reposition 3 an ally within Reach." If Jared attempts to use this on Lana, nothing happens and the effect is wasted.

If the Action rolls, the movement also needs the roll to beat the Immobilized DC: 11 + the Modifier the origin of the condition used (the Power Modifier for a Power Action, the Weapon Modifier for a Weapon Action, and so on; 13 if the condition has no origin; the highest if there are several). A Save Action doesn't roll, but it always has a Modifier: the origin rolls 2d12 + that Modifier against the Immobilized DC, once for all the Immobilized creatures it would move. If the result is below the Immobilized DC, you aren't moved. The rest of the Action works as usual. Steadiness applies after this check.

Imperiled

You barely avoided a Deadly Hazard: you are hanging from a ledge, balancing on the lip of a lava pit, or pinned at the edge of a crushing trap. While you are Imperiled:

  • All your Rolls are Doomed.
  • You are Vulnerable 3.

At the start of your turn you get back to safety, to a legal square next to the hazard, and stop being Imperiled. This takes no Action, but you are Slowed 1 that turn (or increase your Slowed value by 1), so no condition can keep you there. A creature can help you get to safety with the Interact Action (1 AP, Handle).

Prone

  • Melee Actions against you gain 1 Boon, and Ranged Actions against you suffer 1 Bane.
  • You can't Evade.
  • The only [Move] Actions you can use are Crawl and Stand.

Quickened

Quickened always has a value, such as Quickened 2. You increase your maximum AP, and the AP you regain at the start of your turn, by the value: if you are Quickened 2, you regain 7 AP when your turn begins. You don't gain AP when you become Quickened, only when you regain AP at the start of your next turn.

Slowed

Slowed always has a value, such as Slowed 2. You decrease the AP you regain at the start of your turn by the value: if you are Slowed 2, you regain only 3 AP when your turn begins. You also reduce your maximum AP by the value. You don't lose AP when you become Slowed, only when you regain AP at the start of your next turn.

Slowed and Quickened cancel each other out, but they don't reduce each other's value: if you are Quickened 2 and Slowed 2, you regain the normal 5 AP. If one of them ends before your turn comes around (you lose Slowed 2 but are still Quickened 2), you regain 7 AP.

Vulnerable

Vulnerable always has a value, such as Vulnerable 3. Actions against you gain a number of Boons equal to that value. If you would have Vulnerable twice, only the highest value applies.

Line of Effect A straight line from the center of your square to any corner of the target's space that nothing blocks.
Linked Action An Action another Action lets you use as part of it, written like Weapon Attack (1A). It costs no AP.
Long A weapon property: 1 Bane against adjacent creatures, and it doesn't threaten them.
Main hand, off hand Your two hands. Which one holds a weapon changes nothing, unless a feature says so.
Master 15 Mastery with a weapon: from level 8, your Actions that deal [W] with it deal one more [W].
Mastery How well you know a kind of weapon: +1 for a Tier 3 on a Weapon Action with it (at most 1 per Weapon Group an encounter). The weapon's Mastery (3, 4 or 5) unlocks its Weapon Technique; 8 Expert, 15 Master.
Minion An NPC role with CV 25, with 3 AP a turn and no Dying: at 0 Health it dies, or falls Unconscious if the GM prefers.
Minor enemies Minions and Grunts (CV below 100).
Move An Action with the Move trait.
Moved You moved with an Action that moves you, Evade included. Forced Movement, Reposition and Stand don't count.
Moves closer The distance between you is smaller after the movement; an Interrupt checks it square by square.
Next Action An effect on your next Action with no duration must be used before the end of your turn, or it is wasted.
Non-threatening A weapon property: the weapon doesn't threaten, so you can't flank with it, unless a feature says otherwise (Brawler). Fists have it.
Obscured Your Actions against the creature take 1 Bane; its Actions against you gain 1 Boon.
Origin The creature that uses an Action, or the Point of Origin of an Area.
Point of Origin The place an area spreads from: an Emanation's target, your square for a Line, the tip of a cone. Line of Effect and Cover for the area are checked from it.
Power Modifier The higher of the class's two Power Attributes ÷ 2 + 1. Power Actions roll it.
Power roll An Action with the Power trait (or a Split Action in Power mode).
Prerequisites What you need to choose a Talent.
Projectile A weapon property: the weapon shoots (a bow, a crossbow). Shot Actions need one.
Properties The properties of a weapon (Actions have traits, creatures have Tags): Melee, Ranged, Long, Projectile, Firearm, Thrown, Reload, Two-handed, Barehanded, Non-threatening.
Pulled Forced Movement toward the origin, up to its value, inside a 45° cone.
Pushed Forced Movement away from the origin, up to its value, inside a 45° cone.
Quickened A condition with a value: you regain (and can hold) that many more AP at the start of your turn.
Rally Point Removes damage equal to a quarter of your Health Pool. Steel Yourself can't be used while Down or Unconscious.
Range How far a weapon reaches, melee weapons included: normal / far (1 Bane) / maximum (2 Banes).
Ranged Combat While Engaged Using a [Ranged] Action while Engaged: 2 Banes. Against a creature Engaged with someone else: 1 Bane.
Rating How strong one of an NPC's numbers is for its role: Terrible, Low, Medium, High or Extreme (Minions have no Extreme).
Reach How far your body reaches: 1 square for every Species. Weapons have a Range instead.
Reaction An Action used in response to an Interrupt or a Trigger, with a cost like 1R (0R costs no AP).
Recovery The three ways to recover: a Breather, a Sleep and a Rest.
Regrip The Basic Action (1 AP, no Handle) to hold a weapon with both hands again, move it to the other hand or swap the weapons in your hands.
Release 0 AP: drop an object, or let go of it with one hand.
Reload A weapon property: a Reload Shot needs a reload afterwards.
Reload Shot A Favored and Empowered 2 shot with a Reload weapon.
Reposition Moving a creature along a path you choose, up to its value. Not Forced Movement: it never passes through creatures, never enters hazards, and needs Line of Effect.
Requirements What you need to use an Action.
Rest A day in a safe place. It includes a Sleep.
Role What an NPC is for in a fight: Minion, Grunt, Standard or Elite. It sets the NPC's CV, AP and the tables its ratings use.
Roll Anything you roll: a Check, a Save, a Weapon, Power or Signature roll, or an Attribute Action.
Round One full cycle of turns.
Save A roll a creature makes against an effect; its three values are for Tier 1 / 2 / 3.
Save Action An Action with the Save trait: its targets roll the named Save against 11 + your Modifier; Tier 1 is the worst for them.
Sense A way to perceive creatures: Sight, Hearing, Heatsight... Your best sense decides if a creature is Clear, Obscured, Hidden or Unsensed to you.
Setup What an Action does before its dice are rolled.
Several Targets One roll for every target of an Action, each calculated separately.
Shot A trait: the Action needs a Projectile weapon (a bow, a crossbow).
Signature Modifier The higher of your Weapon Modifier and Power Modifier. Signature Actions roll it.
Signature roll An Action with the Signature trait.
Size A creature's rank from 1 to 7, and how many squares it occupies.
Slid Forced Movement along a path the origin chooses (it can curve); the origin needs Line of Effect.
Slowed A condition with a value: you regain that many fewer AP at the start of your turn, and your maximum AP is that much lower. Desperate, Dying and Shattered give Slowed 1 (or 1 more, to at most 3).
Sleep 8 hours of non-strenuous activity, at most once every 16 hours; it renews Daily Actions.
Special Action An NPC Action beyond its Basic attack: its damage is the Basic attack's times 1.4 (1 AP), 2.2 (2 AP) or 3.5 (3 AP).
Specific beats general A card or feature that says something different from a general rule wins for that case.
Speed How far a creature can move with an Advance: species Speed plus class Speed Modifier.
Split A trait: the Action has a Weapon mode and a Power mode, and you choose one each time you use it.
Squeezing Moving through a gap one square narrower than you: Difficult Terrain, and Doomed and Vulnerable if you stop there.
SR Size Rank: a creature's Size as a number from 1 (Tiny) to 7 (Titanic). Medium is SR 3.
Stacking Bonuses don't add up: for each value (Guard included) use the highest bonus and the highest penalty. "Increase by N", Boons, Banes and AP losses do add up.
Stage A group of levels: Stage 1 is levels 1-4, Stage 2 levels 5-7, Stage 3 levels 8-10. It raises your Modifiers and sets the Bestiary's row.
Standard An NPC role with CV 100: about 56 Health at Stage 1, dies to about 2 to 3 PC turns.
Steadiness A creature's resistance to being moved: it reduces the value of Forced Movement and Reposition. Normally 0.
Steel Yourself The Basic Action that uses your Rally Points.
Strike An Action that tries to hit a creature. At most 3 per turn.
Success A Tier 2 or 3 result.
Tag A word on a creature (not an Action) that other rules refer to, such as Profane.
Threatens A creature that can make a [Melee] Action that deals damage threatens every enemy within that Action's normal range (not adjacent ones with a Long weapon).
Thrown A weapon property: the weapon can be used in hand or thrown, with a Range for each.
Tier The outcome of a roll: 1, 2 or 3.
Trigger A Reaction that happens after the Action that triggers it.
Turn The time one creature acts.
Turn A Frequency: once per turn, on your turn and on each other creature's turn.
Unsensed You can't target the creature; its Actions against you are Favored.
Vs. An entry that names an Attribute: the Action rolls against half of it (rounded up) instead of Armor Defense or Ward Defense.
Vulnerable A condition with a value: Actions against you gain that many Boons.
Ward Ward Defense (WD) — 11 + Power Modifier + Ward value + other items + bonuses − penalties. The Defense against Power Actions.
Weapon Group Blades, Axes, Hammers, Polearms, Bows, Crossbows, Firearms, Unarmed. Your Mastery with a weapon is at least a third of your best in its group.
Weapon Modifier The higher of the class's two Weapon Attributes ÷ 2 + 1. Weapon Actions roll it.
Weapon roll An Action with the Weapon trait (or a Split Action in Weapon mode).
Weapon Technique A weapon's own Action, unlocked with Mastery; used only with that weapon, while you wield it.
Willing, Unwilling Willing: a creature that accepts the effect (an Unconscious creature counts as willing). Unwilling: one that doesn't.
Wound A result of the Dying or Shattered Table (Body or Mind).
