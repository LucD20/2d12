# The Expedition Loop (candidate proposal, from 2026-11-09; nothing decided, nothing in rules-src)

Lucas (2026-11-08/09): Momentum should matter more, so "filler" fights are worth it, without copying Draw Steel's Victories; the enemies in the next room shouldn't just wait while the party rests (a common PF2e complaint); fleeing enemies get chased to death and the fight drags. Built in a back-and-forth with Lucas; **[Lucas]** = his idea or decision, **[agreed]** = proposed by Claude and approved by him. To check with the ruler before any rule is written (below).

## 1. Escalation and Momentum
1. **[Lucas]** **Escalation runs 1 to 6**: the Escalation Passives at rungs **2, 4 and 6**, the Apex at 6.
2. **[Lucas]** **You start each encounter at Escalation 1 + Momentum** and gain **+1 at the start of each round after the first**. **[agreed]** The Critical and the class hook stay at +1 once an encounter each (half a rung on the new scale).
3. **[Lucas]** **Momentum +1 after each encounter**; **[agreed]** after each encounter **overcome**, fight or not (a fight won, a negotiation or a sneak that gets you past, a Challenge succeeded; never a bag of rats, never a fight you fled), so the clever route isn't punished.
4. **[agreed]** **Momentum is the party's, at most 3** (a start of at most 4): the Apex comes in round 3 at best (round 2 with a Critical or a hook), never in round 1 (2d20's "escalation spiral" avoided).
5. **[agreed]** **-1 Momentum at each Sleep** (the answer to "why doesn't a Sleep reset it": in the wild you sleep with one eye open, the edge dulls but doesn't vanish), **-1 when a PC goes Down**, **all of it lost on a Retreat**, **cleared at a Rest** (as Rally Points).
6. When each rung comes (no Critical or hook; a normal fight is about 3.5 rounds): Momentum 0: rung 2 in round 2, rung 4 in round 4, rung 6 in round 6; Momentum 1: 1 / 3 / 5; Momentum 2: 1 / 2 / 4; Momentum 3: 1 / 1 / 3. The first fight of an expedition is a warm-up (no Passive in round 1 at Momentum 0: lower than today's start: the Bestiary's fit must be checked); the third or fourth is where the Apex shows up.
7. Why not Draw Steel's Victories (Lucas: too close): no XP from wins, no resource pool; Momentum only moves the start of a ladder that already exists. Research: 2d20's Momentum pool (cap 6, bleeds between scenes; criticized for spirals), 13th Age's escalation die (resets every fight on purpose; "start it higher" as a variant), Daggerheart's capped Hope / Fear carry-over, snowballing and comeback design (a strong brake and a weak positive loop), the 15-minute adventuring day (count every draining moment, make fights optional, story pressure).

## 2. Alert (the site's answer to Momentum)
8. **[agreed]** **One Alert number per inhabited site (a dungeon, a fort, a dangerous district), 0 to 6, visible to the players.** Momentum is the party's edge, Alert is theirs. Wilderness and empty ruins don't need it.
9. **[agreed]** **Raised by:** a fight in the site +1; **each enemy that escapes +1, exactly** (nothing else: never "the whole dungeon comes"); a loud event (fire, an explosion, a raised alarm) +1; **a Sleep inside the site +2** (they regroup); a failed stealth or infiltration Challenge +1. **Lowered by:** a stealth Challenge succeeded -1; silencing a messenger or the alarm; a prisoner's shortcut or password -1; **leaving the site: -1 per Sleep spent outside**, but on return cleared rooms may be re-manned or fortified (the Alexandrian's advice).
10. **[agreed]** **The site's sheet** (a few lines, like the town stat block), for example: **Goblin Warren** · Alert 0-1 Calm · 2-3 **Wary** (patrols; fights draw the next room) · 4-5 **Alert** (guards doubled, the chieftain wakes, you can't Surprise them) · 6 **Lockdown** (gates barred, the shaman starts her ritual). Thresholds change the world (gates, rituals, enemies fleeing with the treasure), not only the number of enemies.
11. **[agreed]** **Resting in a site:** a Breather is safe at Calm and Wary; at Alert and Lockdown even a Breather risks a patrol; **the first Breather right after a fight never rolls** ("let them breathe"). **A Sleep inside the site: the GM rolls 1d12; equal to or less than the Alert, you are found** before morning (0% at Alert 0, 50% at Alert 6; the Sleep's +2 applies after the roll). A watch means no Surprise (Senses: a waking PC perceives the patrol); barricading and hiding the camp (a short Challenge) subtract from the roll; an interrupted Sleep doesn't complete (no Daily Actions until it is finished; the 16-hour rule still applies).
12. **[agreed]** **Fights draw neighbors:** at Wary or higher, from round 3 of a fight, the next room's creatures (the site's roster) can join: long, noisy fights are dangerous, quick quiet ones pay.
13. Why 2d12 avoids PF2e's worst version: a Breather removes only Nonlethal damage, a Sleep heals nothing, Rally Points come back only at a Rest in safety; what is left is the verisimilitude problem ("the goblins next door just wait?"), which Alert answers. The two loops balance: each fight raises both Momentum (+1, at most 3) and Alert (+1); the late fights are when the party is strongest and the site readiest (Alert's reinforcements count toward a fight's CV). Research: the Alexandrian's adversary rosters and "Resting in the Dungeon" (cleared rooms that stay empty make resting free; Normal / Alert states; monsters regroup), the Angry GM's Tension Pool (visible dread; criticized as a complicated random-encounter roll), the overloaded encounter die (one roll for many hazards; "let them breathe" after a hard fight), Torchbearer's camps and Darkest Dungeon's ambushed camps, stealth games' alert meters with thresholds and a Lockdown (Invisible Inc.'s time-based alarm).

## 3. The decided fight and the Parting Beat
Lucas: players almost never let an enemy escape, even when told no reinforcements will come ("they'll tell the tale"); a morale rule falls into the same trap; chasing drags. No game has fixed it fully; the research names the causes: fear of the unknown (a runner might bring a total party kill: Schwalb), lost rewards (loot, XP), unclear consequences, and the mop-up grind (the Alexandrian: call the fight when its outcome is clear, each player describes one thing they do).
14. **[agreed]** **The fight is decided** when the enemies' side has **lost half its CV**, or **its leader is Down**, or the GM calls it (Mordheim's rout trigger, as a number). Never while a PC is Down at an enemy's feet. Then the remaining enemies flee (fanatics who fight on mean the fight wasn't decided).
15. **[Lucas]** **The GM points each runner to an exit** (usually up to 4: doors, a window, a hole; some rooms have 2 or 1). Minor enemies take the nearest, a leader the smartest. **[agreed]** **The room does the work** (no Bar action): a **crowded exit** (two or more runners) gives 1 Boon on Run Down and Call Out against them; **with one exit only**, everyone is crowded **and the runner farthest from it surrenders at once** (cornered). A creature with no way out (Immobilized, Grabbed) surrenders.
16. **[Lucas]** **Each PC takes one Parting Action, in the order the players choose**; **[agreed]** each resolves at once (later players react), still a single pass:
    - **Run Down:** attack one runner within your Speed + your weapon's Range: caught on a Tier 2 or better, or **any hit on a Minor enemy**; it dies, or surrenders if you prefer.
    - **Cover an Exit** (Lucas's "make an escape difficult"): the other PCs' Run Down and Call Out against runners at that exit gain **2 Boons**. Pure teamwork: nothing alone.
    - **Call Out:** an Appeal Check against one runner's Instinct: it **surrenders** (a prisoner).
    - **Offer Terms:** **one PC only**: one Appeal Check against the highest Instinct among all the runners: on a Tier 3 all of them surrender, on a Tier 2 the Minor ones; on a Tier 1 nobody, and the Action is spent. The big swing: the order matters (Offer Terms first, then the rest clean up).
    - **Shadow:** let one runner go on purpose: it escapes (+1 Alert), but you learn where its exit leads and gain **the preparation bonus for the next encounter** (+1 Escalation: you know what you'll face).
17. **[agreed]** **Every runner not caught, surrendered or Shadowed escapes: +1 Alert each, exactly.** **Runners drop their loot** (they flee light: their lots stay). **The encounter counts as overcome for Momentum** whether anyone escaped or not. **Important runners** (the lieutenant with the key) can instead get one round of a Chase Challenge (book/roleplay-skills.md proposal 9).
18. **[agreed]** **Prisoners pay:** a captured or surrendered enemy can be questioned (an Appeal or Mind Check, or roleplay): the site's next room or patrol (**the preparation bonus**), a shortcut or password (**Alert -1**), or a ransom (SC). **A bound prisoner never raises Alert.**
19. **[agreed]** **Escalation at the end:** at Escalation 4 or more, **two** Parting Actions; at 6, a Doubles on a Parting roll gives the party +1 Momentum (at most 3).
20. **[agreed]** **Class hooks:** Archer "Long Shot" (Run Down at twice the weapon's Range; your Marked Quarry can't escape your Run Down); Paladin "Oath Binds" (Call Out and Offer Terms with 2 Boons, and they work on Profane creatures, who never surrender to anyone else). **Talents:** Sweep the Field (Run Down catches two runners at the same exit), Blood Hound (Shadow without the +1 Alert), Interrogator (your prisoners always talk).
21. **Why nothing dominates:** Run Down is the default but one runner within reach; Cover only pays with teammates (and at the crowded exit); Call Out beats Run Down when you want prisoners (they pay); Offer Terms is the gamble that can end the beat; Shadow is the only option that pays for letting someone go; the room sets the context (one exit: catching is easy; four: choices). Counters and complements, not one best answer (the design rule: a dominant option removes the choice; fix it with counters and situational value, not by rebalancing numbers). Research: Warhammer's break / flee / pursue (one roll each side, caught = destroyed: dramatic, no step-by-step chase), Mordheim's rout test (the whole side, at 25% out of action).
22. **Dropped on the way (and why):** Tend (Round Actions can be used out of combat, so anyone can Steel Yourself for free right after; encounter-length conditions end out of combat: it gave nothing); Grab It (dominated by Run Down and Call Out, and runners now drop their loot anyway); Bar an Exit (Lucas: it is outright the best option, everyone barring blocks every exit, and choosing a new exit needs a second pass: the room's crowded / cornered rule does its job).

## 4. In Foundry
A "Decided" button (it checks the half-CV or leader trigger); the GM clicks the exits on the map, arrows show each runner's exit, crowded exits are marked; each player picks a Parting Action from a sysCard and one card shows all the results. A Momentum counter (the party's) and an Alert track per site, visible; the Sleep button rolls the site's d12.

## 5. What the ruler must check first
- The first fight of an expedition (Momentum 0, a start at Escalation 1 below rung 2) against today's start (rung 1 from round 1).
- The expedition's last fight (Momentum 3) against the Bestiary (with Alert's reinforcements in the CV): the boss isn't trivialized.
- The half-CV trigger: how many rounds it saves (the last round or two of mopping up), and what that does to the Health and Rally Point numbers.
- Offer Terms' odds (Appeal against the highest Instinct): a gamble, not a sure thing.

## 6. What it would touch
