## 4. Where the gap comes from

| The four PCs | The table | SIM (pillars, 300 fights) | SIM over the table |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Damage a PC turn | 15.5 | 22.6 | +46% |
| Attack rolls a PC turn (its Reactions' rolls too) | 2.4 | 2.5 | +3% |
| Damage an attack roll | 6.4 | 9.0 | +41% |
| ... counting only what an enemy's Health could take (no damage past its last point) | at most 6.4 | 6.7 | +5% |
| ... a PC's own attack roll against the Ogre or the Orc | | 9.1 | |
| Moves a PC turn (an Advance or an Evade) | 0.9 | 0.5 | |

**The PCs roll as often in SIM as at the table; the gap is in the damage counted for a roll.** SIM counts every point rolled, also past an enemy's last point of Health: its PCs kill the three Goblins (5 Health each) and the Thug with whole hits and the Orc by round 3, and about a quarter of what they roll is lost that way. The table killed one Goblin and the Thug and put the rest into the Ogre and the Orc, so nearly all it rolled counted. Counted against Health, a roll is worth the same in both. What is left is real and is a decision, not a rule: against the Ogre and the Orc a SIM roll is bigger (the builds take the larger Versions and their Escalation Passives every turn: Rolling Shot at 2 AP with its extra die where Kaela used the 1 AP one), and 34 rolls are too few to say how much (one roll's damage varies by about 6: the table's average is known to about 1 either way).
