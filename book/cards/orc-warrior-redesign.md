## What every idea stands on

- **The Default Standard:** Health 36 (Vigor 18, Resolve 18), Armor Defense 13, Ward Defense 13, Weapon +2, Power +2, 4 AP and the Reaction Point, Speed 5. A Tier 1 deals nothing unless the card says so.
- **The numbers:** a melee Basic attack of nothing / 3 / 6 with the Critical's 1d10!; a Special of nothing / 7 / 11 on one creature or 5 / 8 on each of two; about 9 damage on paper in a turn that moves, 12 in one that doesn't (8 to Health at the table).
- **It already has every Basic Action** (Chapter 14): Charge, Shove, Trip, Grab, Demoralize, Feint. So its own cards don't push, knock Prone, charge or give Vulnerable: those are a click away already.
- **No number assumes a class** (P11).
- **Prices are [judgment]:** there is no rider price list yet (G11 comes later). The rule of thumb used: a Mild condition on Tier 3 only is free (a Tier 3 or a Critical is 27% of rolls at +2 against 13); an effect on Tier 2 and 3 costs 1 damage on each; a ranged Basic attack is 1 lower on each Tier than the melee one.
- **From the research:** one thing of its own the players must answer; a visible change in the fight; a behaviour players can learn; a block that runs from one glance (`research/enemies-at-the-table.md`).

## The ideas

### The melee Basic attack (1 AP, At-will; [Weapon, Melee, Strike]; Reach (1); one creature; Weapon vs. Armor Defense)

**M1. War Axe** (the plain one). *The orc brings its axe down in a short, heavy arc.*
- Tier 2: [V] 1d4! Sl. Tier 3: [V] 2 + 1d6! Sl.
- The reference Basic attack: nothing to remember. 3.2 a use.

**M2. Hamstringing Axe.** *The orc cuts low, at the leg that would carry you away.*
- Tier 2: [V] 1d4! Sl. Tier 3: [V] 2 + 1d6! Sl; Hindered (EoT).
- It keeps what it reaches: a PC hit hard has half its Speed for its next turn. Free [judgment]. The decision it gives: step away now, or stand and trade.

**M3. Shield-Splitter.** *The orc hammers at your guard until it gives.*
- Tier 2: [V] 1d4! Sl. Tier 3: [V] 2 + 1d6! Sl. **Special:** this damage ignores Guard.
- An answer to a PC that keeps AP for Guard, which every PC can do. Risk: it switches off a core rule (and the Paladin's Lay On Hands with it): the kind of cancelling players dislike. Claude would not take it.

### The ranged Basic attack (1 AP, At-will; [Weapon, Ranged, Strike]; one creature; Weapon vs. Armor Defense)

It shares the At-will count with the melee one. Like any ranged Strike it is Doomed while the orc is Engaged, so it is what the orc does on the way in, or at a PC it can't reach.

**R1. Throwing Axe** (the plain one). *The orc pulls a hand axe from its belt and hurls it.* Distance 5 sq.
- Tier 2: [V] 2 Sl. Tier 3: [V] 2 + 1d4! Sl.
- 2.5 a use. A short range on purpose: it closes the last gap, it is not a duel with the Archers.

**R2. Sling.** *The orc whips a stone at your head.* Distance 8 sq.
- Tier 2: [V] 2 Bl. Tier 3: [V] 2 + 1d4! Bl; Staggered (EoT).
- A stone that rattles: no Reactions until the end of your turn, so the orcs that follow it walk in untroubled. Farther than R1, the same damage; the rider is free [judgment]. A learnable behaviour: one orc slings, the others close.

**R3. Barbed Javelin.** *The orc throws a short spear that stays in the wound.* Distance 6 sq.
- Tier 2: [V] 2 Pi. Tier 3: [V] 2 Pi; Lingering 1 (Bo/In).
- It taxes a back-liner 1 AP to Shake Off, or 1 damage a turn for the rest of the fight. Lingering is strong for a Basic attack even on Tier 3 only (four orcs throwing): the Tier 3 loses its die for it [judgment].

### The Special (2 AP, Round)

**S1. Wide Sweep.** [Weapon, Melee, Strike]; Reach (1); up to two creatures; Weapon vs. Armor Defense. *The orc swings the axe flat through everyone in front of it.*
- Tier 2: [V] 5 Sl. Tier 3: [V] 8 Sl. (Each creature its own roll.)
- The default two-creature Special (G6): the same turn's damage on two PCs, so less on one. 4.4 a creature, 8.8 when it catches two. The decision it gives the players: don't stand side by side next to an orc.

**S2. Breaking Blow.** [Weapon, Melee, Strike]; Reach (1); one creature; Weapon vs. Armor Defense. *The orc puts its whole weight behind one stroke.*
- Tier 2: [V] 7 Sl. Tier 3: [V] 11 Sl.
- **Bloodied** (after the Tiers): if this makes the target Bloodied, the target is Daunted of the orc (EoT).
- The single heavy hit, with one of the new Bloodied lines (C4): the blow that bloodies you shakes you. 6.1 a use; the line is free [judgment] (it happens once a PC a fight at most). Against one PC it concentrates damage: the opposite of S1.

**S3. War Howl.** [Power, Save, Fear]; Area: 2 Emanation; all enemies in the Area; Mind Save against its Power DC (13). *The orc throws its head back and howls, and the sound gets into your bones.*
- Tier 1: [R] 6 Ps; Daunted of the orc (EoT). Tier 2: [R] 3 Ps. Tier 3: no effect.
- The orc's Resolve damage: it gives Strained and Ward-side play a share of an orc fight (Chapter 14 wants about one creature in four to press Resolve). About 3.3 a PC in the area; worth the Special when it catches two or more. It needs no reach, so it is also something to do while Immobilized. Risk: a Save for each PC in it is slower than S1's two rolls.

### The Reaction (1R)

**X1. Blood for Blood** (Encounter). *The wound only makes it angrier.*
- Trigger: a creature's Action makes the orc Bloodied. Target: the triggering creature.
- Effect: the orc Advances up to 2 toward the target, then makes a Basic attack against it.
- **The visible change at Bloodied**, which the research names among the most praised features, and it fires once an orc: little disruption (rule 16). The players can answer it: Bloody it from far away (the Advance 2 may not reach, and it throws instead), or wear its Resolve down and never Bloody it. About 2 damage a fight for each orc [judgment].

**X2. Dying Swing** (Encounter). *It is already falling when the axe comes round one last time.*
- Interrupt: the orc would go Down. Target: one creature within its Reach.
- Effect: the orc makes a melee Basic attack against the target, then goes Down.
- "Don't finish it next to you": the killing blow is better thrown, shot, or struck by someone who can take it. One use an orc, always at the same moment. About 2 damage a fight for each orc [judgment]. Risk: it rewards the players for not being adjacent, which the Archers already want.

**X3. Run Down** (Round). *You step back; it is already there.*
- Trigger: an enemy within the orc's Reach leaves its Reach by its own movement. Target: the triggering creature.
- Effect: the orc Advances up to 3 toward the target.
- No damage: it keeps the orc on its target, so stepping away costs the PC more than it gains unless it Evades far. It fires often (most rounds something moves away): more interruptions than X1 or X2, the reason Claude ranks it last.

### Features (optional)

**F1. Blood Fury.** While the orc is Bloodied, it is [[Empowered]].
- The same idea as X1 as a standing state: once Bloodied its first damage each turn adds 1d6!. A book word, nothing new to learn. About +1 damage a turn over the fight [judgment]. **With X1 it would be two Bloodied effects on one creature: one of them, not both.**

**F2. Blood Scent.** The orc's [Strike] Actions against a Bloodied creature gain 1 Boon.
- A behaviour the players can learn and answer with the core alone: spend a Rally Point to get an ally back above Bloodied and the orcs lose interest. Risk: it pulls every orc onto the hurt PC, which is how a PC goes Down (the record's P6). Claude would keep it for a creature meant to be frightening, not the default.

**F3. No Feature.** The Default Standard as the plainest block: four Actions and nothing else.

## Claude's pick (not decided)

**War Axe (M1), Throwing Axe (R1), Wide Sweep (S1), Blood for Blood (X1), no Feature (F3).**

- It is the reference creature, so its two Basic attacks are plain and every other creature can be read against them.
- Its one thing of its own is the moment it is Bloodied: visible, once, answerable without any class.
- Wide Sweep is the spread the record asked of a Standard's Special.
- Paper turn: moving, Wide Sweep on one creature and a War Axe, 7.7 (12.1 when the Sweep catches two); standing, 10.5 (14.9 with two); three War Axes and a move, 8.6. So against a PC standing alone it simply strikes, and it sweeps when two stand together. Blood for Blood adds about 2 a fight.

**The second set, if the orc should press harder:** Hamstringing Axe (M2), Sling (R2), Breaking Blow (S2), Dying Swing (X2), no Feature.

### Claude's review (2026-11-25)

1. **War Howl's two Tiers are almost the same damage:** 1d6! averages 4, 1 + 1d4 averages 3.5. The gap between failing and failing badly is then Vulnerable alone. Fine if meant; a Tier 2 of 1d4 would separate them.
2. **About 2.9 [R] a PC in the Area** (a Mind of 0 to +2 against DC 13). On one PC it is worth less than the two axes it replaces (5.4); on two it is level and adds Vulnerable; on three it is the better turn. **So the orc howls when two PCs are within 2 squares of it and strikes otherwise:** a behaviour players can learn. Paper turn with two PCs in the Area: moving, 9.0; standing, 11.9 (decision 14 wanted 9 and 12).
3. **Vulnerable 1 (EoT) under the zipper:** it lasts to the end of that PC's turn, so it helps the howling orc's own axes that turn and any orc that acts before that PC does; if the PC's turn is next, only the howler's. The orc also has Demoralize (the same condition, one creature, 1 AP): the two don't add up (highest value only).
4. **Four orcs can mean four Mind Saves a PC a round.** A lot of rolling when Lucas rolls for everyone; the alternative is one line ("a creature that has rolled this Save this round is not affected again").
5. **Barbed Javelin's Lingering 1 is a sting, not a tax.** It doesn't add up from several javelins, and over a two-to-three-round fight it is about 2 damage, against 1 AP and a Save to Shake Off: most players will leave it in. (Claude called it strong when it was shown: wrong.) Lingering 2 or 1d4 would make shaking it off a real choice.
6. **The javelin and Shove both need a free hand** (Handle): hence the one-handed axe and the Setup that draws the javelin.
7. **The Shove Feature: 1 Boon, not 2.** His own card rule: an unnamed Boon is 1, rarely 2. Shove rolls Body, so the orc needs its Attributes. A Shove against a wall is already worth an axe (2 Collision damage a square not moved: 4 / 6 for 1 AP; the 2 AP Shove averages 6.0 with no Boon, 6.4 with one, 6.8 with two), so the Feature shows near walls and other creatures, and does nothing in the open. A visible change: a Bloodied orc starts shoving.
8. **Two things at Bloodied** (Blood for Blood once, the Shove Boon standing). They read as one idea, the orc changes when Bloodied. With Vigor 18 that is after about two landed hits; Bloodied also takes 2 from its Speed and 1 from its Steadiness, so it is slower and easier to push while it pushes harder.
9. **Cost:** Blood for Blood adds about 2 damage a fight for each orc, roughly a sixth on top of the normal fight's cost. Left for the table to judge.
