Decision 17: the hook, and the rule for answering a moment

Answering a moment. A Reaction's Interrupt or Trigger, and a feature that says "when...", wait for a moment. To answer a moment you must perceive every creature it names (Hidden is enough; Unsensed is not), unless the card says otherwise.

Then the card does something. Whatever it does to a creature targets that creature, and targeting needs Line of Effect as always. A Reaction never reaches through a wall you have no Line of Effect past.
  • Perceiving is the default, as you asked. An ally who has Hidden from everyone, his own side included, can't be answered for.
  • Rebuke through a wall: no. It targets the triggering creature, so he needs Line of Effect to the enemy. The same goes for Shield of Faith and Bulwark of Faith.
  • The ally in the moment must be perceived: seen, or heard. In a fight you hear every creature within 10 squares, through walls.
  • Kindled Wrath has no target, only a moment: he must perceive the ally who becomes Bloodied or goes Down.
  • "Moment" is already the book's word (Chapter 2: "An Interrupt or a Trigger names a moment the rules define"). I dropped "an effect that only reads something that happens".
Kindled WrathPaladin Feature · level 2
An ally's blood on the ground, and your god's anger is yours.
Once an encounter, when an ally becomes Bloodied or goes Down, your Escalation increases by 1.

"Would damage" or "damages"?

I'd keep "would". Chapter 2 writes every Interrupt that way ("A creature would Advance", "would leave a square", "would damage someone") and every Trigger in the present ("makes a [Strike] Action", "goes Down"). The word says the thing hasn't happened yet, even to a reader who skips the label. "Interrupt: ... damages an ally" reads as if the damage were already done.

Decision 18: the Encounter Picks' themes (decided, in this order)

Level 2 gives one Encounter Pick from three. An Encounter Action is worth double its AP and can carry two riders. Smite is already the class's burst, so these are three other moments.

  1. The great shield. Today's Bulwark of Faith: an ally would take damage; before it, they gain Guard equal to what one of their Rally Points heals; after it, they are Slid 1. The big version of "Guard before the hit", once a fight.
  2. Holy ground. Today's Consecrated Ground and Beacon: a Zone he plants for a few rounds, where the line holds.
  3. The word against the Profane. Today's Word of Radiance; 4e's Turn Undead: an area that throws enemies back and does more to the Profane.

Decision 16: Hallowed Stance, at Escalation 6 (decided: A)

Hallowed StancePaladin Escalation Passive · Escalation 6
Your god's light carries you: wherever you are needed, you are already there.
While your Escalation is 6, once on your turn, a [Divine] Action you use gains "Setup: You Advance 3."

Shown as A. "Once on your turn" is your wording: his own turn only, so no move on an enemy's turn.

Your question: can a move given by an Action pass your Speed?

The rule is already written, in Chapter 3: "each time you move with an Action, you can't move more than your current Speed, unless an ability says otherwise: with Speed 2, an Action that lets you Evade 3 lets you Evade only 2." Nothing to add, and the card needn't repeat it.

Why the "increase it by 3" clause gains almost nothing

A Paladin's Speed is his species' Speed; the class gives +0.

SpeciesSpeedLay On Hands' Setup (Advance 5), + 3Reckoning's Setup (a full Advance), + 3
Human58, cut back to 5: no gainno gain
Dwarf4cut back to 4: no gainno gain
Elf6cut back to 6: 1 square gainedno gain
  • A: drop the clause. Hallowed Stance gives its Advance 3 to the [Divine] Actions that have no Setup: Smite, Binding Light, Judge, Lantern of Faith, Commandment. On Lay On Hands and Reckoning he keeps the card's own Setup, which is the better one. One sentence, no special case.
  • B: add "this movement can exceed your Speed". Lay On Hands reaches 8 squares and Reckoning his Speed + 3. A real gain, and a second place in the book where a move passes Speed.

The Setup rules, as decided

  1. A Setup happens before the rest of the Action. It is optional unless it says you must.
  2. The Action's Distance and its targets are checked after the Setup.
  3. If the Setup names the Action's target ("toward the target"), you choose that target before the Setup, and it stays the Action's target.
  4. If an Action in your Setup is disrupted, the rest of the Action still happens. Only then, if the Setup had named a target, you can choose another target for the rest of the Action.
  5. When something gives a Setup to an Action that already has one, you choose which of the two to use. They don't combine unless a card says so.

An example for the book: a Paladin uses Lay On Hands on an ally 5 squares away and Advances toward him. An enemy's Reaction stops him after 2 squares. Lay On Hands still happens, and he can touch another ally within his Reach from where he stands. Had nothing stopped him, he couldn't have walked toward one ally and then touched a different one.

Named DCs (decided)

Yes, with one guardrail.

NameWhat it is
Power DC11 + your Power Modifier
Weapon DC11 + your Weapon Modifier
Signature DCthe higher of the two
Body DC, Mind DC, Appeal DC, Instinct DC11 + half the Attribute, rounded up, + 1 (today's formula)
  • It is common practice. 5e's spell save DC and Pathfinder 2e's class DC are numbers on the sheet that cards point to.
  • The guardrail: what confuses Pathfinder 2e's players is having two DCs that overlap (class DC and spell DC), and asking which one an ability uses. So a named DC only where it is one Modifier the character already has. Never a "class DC" beside them.
  • Why it helps us: today the DC is read from the card's traits and summed at the table, and the same sum is done again for the Immobilized DC and inside Ward Defense. A name removes the sum and makes the card say what it means.
  • Talents that raise a DC become possible. Keep them rare: you set +1 on a Modifier as a full step of difficulty, and a DC bonus is that strong on every Save he forces. 5e hands them out very sparingly.
  • Not disruptive: only four cards are Save Actions today. The places to change are Chapters 1, 5 and 8, the Glossary, and the sheet in Foundry.
  • On cards: the traits stay the default, so a [Power, Save] Action is against his Power DC with nothing more written. A card with no such trait writes "vs your Power DC".

Your question: "(T1/T2)" or "Tier 1 or 2:", is the space worth it?

No. On Rebuke the parentheses save two words, about eight characters. And they cost clarity twice: they are an abbreviation to learn, and they pile onto the duration's parentheses ("Prone (EoT) (T1/T2)").

So I've kept "Tier 1 or 2:" and marked the book's "(T3)" notation to be retired in the same pass that changes the Effects row. One way to write a Tier. Say if you'd rather the reverse.

"Save ends": decided, no

My answer: no, because we already have a better version of it.

What it is

A roll at the end of the affected creature's turn; on a success the condition ends. 4e had it; Draw Steel keeps it (28 times in its classes, read and counted). It ends at the same moment as EoT, so it adds no new end point.

What players say

  • For: one ability can't shut down a whole fight; strong conditions can exist without a fixed long lock; the afflicted player gets a roll. (Search summaries.)
  • Against: you "can't plan around it". It is weaker than it looks: "(save ends) have a 50/50 chance of ending sooner than that anyway and very little chance of lasting more than 2 or 3 rounds" (read on EN World). When saves keep failing it becomes the "Stun-Lock Wizard" (a 4e blog that proposes removing it; search summary). And it is a roll every turn for every condition.

Why it doesn't fit us

  • The odds don't suit our roll. A creature with +2 against DC 13 gets a Tier 2 or better 72% of the time. A save-ends condition would last 1.4 turns on average: barely more than EoT, for an extra 2d12 roll each turn. Make it "Tier 3 ends" and it lasts 4.5 turns, longer than our fights.
  • Our fights are short. Save ends earns its place in fights of 6 to 8 rounds. In 3.5 rounds, EoT and "(2 rounds)" cover the range.
  • We already have the better kind: the creature pays to end it. Break Free (Immobilized, 1 AP), Stand (Prone, 1 AP), Shake Off (Lingering, 1 AP and a Save). The afflicted creature gets a choice instead of a die, and the price is AP.

So where a condition should outlast a turn without a fixed count, I'd write "until it pays", as Binding Light's Immobilized already does.

Two things I found in the rules on the way

  • A dangling reference: Chapter 8 lists "until a Save: see Effect Timing" as a duration, and Effect Timing has no such rule. To fix.
  • A notation that already exists: Effect Timing says an effect that applies only on some Tiers is written with the Tiers in parentheses: "Immobilized (T3)", "(T2/T3)". So Rebuke could read "its Move Action is disrupted (T1/T2)" and Kneel "Prone (T1/T2)". My "Tier 1 or 2:" line duplicates this. One of the two should go.

Durations: decided, only EoT and rounds, counted as today

My answer: keep only EoT and rounds, don't move the count, and write "(1 round)".

What it means for us

  • The trouble everywhere is the number of end points. There are four possible ones (start or end, the origin's turn or the target's). We have two: EoT ends with the target's turn, rounds end with the origin's. A third is the thing to avoid, so your instinct is right.
  • Don't count at the start. Counting at the end guarantees that every creature takes one full turn under the effect, including the one whose turn it was made in. Shield of Faith's Taunt is made on the enemy's turn: counted at the end, it covers that enemy's next turn. Counted at a start, it would cover only the rest of this one, or a length set by Initiative order alone.
  • What "(1 round)" costs on something he holds up: it also covers his own next turn. For Brace and Courage that is harmless.
  • One spot in the book already runs start to start: the unused AP's Guard. Chapter 2 defines it in full, so it isn't ambiguous; I noted it.

The Senses rule in two steps, with pictures (proposed)

The table was still two things to cross. Here it is as two questions, asked in order, and four scenes.

1. Your eyes: how do you perceive your target? That sets your penalty, from the line above.

2. Its eyes: do you perceive it better than it perceives you? Only then you get a bonus: 1 Field Boon if you are Obscured to it, Favored if you are Hidden or Unsensed to it. If you don't perceive it better, there is no bonus.

Green is good for the one the arrow starts from, orange is bad. Dashed, curved arrows: how each one perceives the other. Solid, straight arrows: what that does to its attack. The square under each token is the light it stands in.

What changes from today's rule: one case. Two creatures each Obscured to the other take 1 Field Bane each (today they cancel to a normal roll).

Your question about hearing through walls: nothing changes there. In combat you still hear every creature within 10 squares, through walls, and it is Hidden to you. The odd case I listed is the reverse one: a creature that can't sense you at all. In a fight that only happens after a Hide, which isn't written. So I've taken it out of this change and left it for the Hide session: today's rule stands (you are Doomed against a creature you only hear, with no bonus).

For the book, the four scenes would be drawn as one diagram in the book's own style (Y is you).

The new condition is named Half-Blinded (decided); Dazed stays

  • "Stunned" is hated for losing a whole turn. EN World has a thread titled "Stun: The Fun-Killer". The usual line: if you still have options, you're still playing.
  • Pathfinder 2e's Stunned 1 means "lose 1 of your 3 actions". That is our Dazed 1 exactly. So the rename would be accurate, and it would bring the word's bad name with it.
  • Our Dazed takes a counted number of AP. At 1 it is what the genre calls dazed: you lose part of your turn and still play.
  • Renaming Dazed is the expensive path: Chapter 8, the Glossary, the Archer's cards, the Bestiary's tables, Foundry's condition item and the SIM. Naming the new condition differently costs nothing.

Your question: should Obscured be 2 Banes?

I'd keep it at 1. You're right that one Bane is modest: it is -1 on 2d12, about 7 points off the chance to hit. But:

  • Obscured is two-sided. A Field Bane on your Actions against the creature and a Field Boon on its Actions against you. Between two creatures fighting each other that is already a swing of 2. At 2 and 2 it would be a swing of 4, close to Doomed.
  • The Field limit is 2. Obscured at 2 would fill it alone, so cover or shooting into a melee would add nothing in Dim light.
  • The ladders match today: Low cover 1, Medium cover 2, High cover Doomed; Obscured 1, Hidden Doomed.
  • Dazzled would double in price, too much for a 1 AP use of an Art.

Found on the way: two creatures that are each Obscured to the other cancel out. Dim light only matters when it is one-sided.

Names for the condition

Dazzled is the plain word, and Pathfinder 2e players know it with the same meaning. Its one flaw: it sits one letter from our Dazed, which takes AP. Others: Half-Blinded (explains itself, pairs with Blinded), Glare-Struck, Bleary, Sunstruck, Blurred.

The new condition (decided; its name is open)

Yes to the condition. Glare's sentence was an effect invented for one card. A named condition is defined once and reused: another light card, a monster's flash, a creature of the dark dragged into Bright light. Chapter 8 has Blinded and nothing between it and normal sight, and the Senses table already has the middle state (Obscured), so the definition is one line.

Half-Blinded. Your Sight perceives no creature better than Obscured: a creature that would be Clear to you is Obscured. Your other senses aren't affected.

By the Senses table that means 1 Field Bane on its Actions and 1 Field Boon on Actions against it. I'd price it as a Moderate condition, about 12 to 16 points a landing (judgment).

Rules set along the way (decided)

  • The system's purpose: fun, engaging, tactical, with the least ambiguity. A card that needs an undefined word gets the rule written.
  • "Effects happen on Tier 2+" is removed, for the whole book. An Effects entry is an extra thing the Action does, whatever the roll. What happens only on some Tiers is written on those Tiers. Applied in one pass over every card, with the classes.
  • Each Tier's line holds its whole result: the damage, a semicolon, then what else that Tier does. You roll, find your Tier, read one line.
  • A Tier 3 visibly beats a Tier 2: a bigger die or one more thing on its line.
  • A Tier line can name two Tiers, and a Tier that isn't written does nothing ("Tier 1 or 2: ..."), for yes-or-no effects. Proposed.
  • "Moving away from you" needs its ruling in Chapter 2, the mirror of "Moving Closer": eight questions it must answer are logged.
  • The full Advance and Evade rules move onto their Action cards. Decided; to do with or after the pack that changes Advance.
  • A Setup is optional unless it says you must. For Chapter 2.
  • If an Action in your Setup is disrupted, the rest of the Action still happens. For Chapter 2.
  • A Passive is a standing sentence; a "moment" ability is a 0 AP Action with an Escalation line. No rule note needed.
  • Named DCs: Power DC, Weapon DC, Signature DC, and one per Attribute. Never a second DC that overlaps them.
  • Targets are named before the Setup; the Distance is measured after it. For Chapter 2.
  • A straight line toward a creature goes to the center of its space; you can stop in any square on it and can't enter its space. Closer, never around. For Chapter 3.
  • His Hallmark Strike costs 2 AP, so he doesn't become a striker. His turn is one main Strike, one small thing and a move (2 + 1 + 1). Two conditions: the Strike's damage is about 35 of its 50 points with the rest in its rider, and its Tier 1 still does the rider, because at Weapon +1 one roll now carries half his turn.
  • The two Hallmark Actions are a Strike and a Help. The Help is Lay On Hands: Guard before the hit, on his own turn. Shield of Faith stays the name of a Hallmark Reaction (the smaller hit, on the enemy's turn).

Why this shape

  • The decision it gives: whom to judge. The biggest hitter, or the enemy next to the Archer. Moving it costs 1 AP, so the first choice matters; when the Judged creature goes Down it moves for free.
  • Certain, with a number. No roll, no Reaction AP. A Standard's Basic attack is 3.5 on a Tier 2: minus 2 is more than half of it. Against its Special it is a quarter.

The three ways to shape it:

A
A menu of up to 3 uses (my default). One pick gives a whole small toolbox on one theme. A level 1 Paladin has more to do, and the card is still short because each line is one thing. The risk: three lines to read.
B
At most 2 uses, like every other Action. Simpler; each Art is narrower.
C

Question 6: does the Paladin track a number of his own? (decided: A)

You asked: could Escalation be the pool, spent on abilities as it once was? I think it would hurt, as you suspected. Spending it switches off the Passives that need "Escalation 2 or more", which is exactly why it stopped being spent. Every class and the Elites share that ladder, and Momentum would turn into a starting purse. What works without spending: an ability can read Escalation ("1d8 more for each Escalation above 1"), so it grows through the fight and nothing is tracked.

The brief says he "uses all he has" and explodes. The question is what "all he has" is made of. Three ways, each with an example line (examples, not proposals).

A
Nothing new to track (my default). The burst spends what is already on the sheet: AP, the Reaction AP, Escalation.
"3 AP. You can also spend your Reaction AP: 2d8 more."
or: "1d8 more for each Escalation above 1."
Nothing to remember. The cost is real: no Reaction that round, so for a round he can't protect anyone.
B
One charge, on or off. A single token.
"When you use a Reaction to protect an ally, you are Charged. Your next divine Strike spends it: 2d8 more."
Protecting visibly loads the burst, so a round spent shielding is never a wasted round. Never more than one, so nothing to hoard. Nimble's paladin works this way; its designer moved to one charge from a stack of dice.
C
A pool of points.
"Wrath: 2 at the start of each turn, 1 more when the enemy you judged damages you. 1 Wrath protects an ally; 5 pays the big Strike."
This is Draw Steel's Censor. The deepest choice each round (protect now, or save for the blow), and a number to track every round. Its known weakness is a slow first round; a guide says of turn 1: "Not enough to do anything powerful." Our fights last about 3.5 rounds.

My default: A, with B allowed if a card needs it. C only if you want the Paladin to be the class with a resource.

The research in ten points

Other games' rules and other players' opinions, not ours. Every quote below was found on its page.

  1. Your three "softs" are not words players use. They would say off-tank, emergency healer, capped nova. A 2008 poster describes the 4e Paladin almost as you did:
    "single target tank, he grabs a guy and duels that guy, however he has some party benefit abilities so he helps via heals/temp HP for his party while he holds one guy on himself."brislove, EN World
  2. A tank that only survives is not a tank.
    "A Tank doesn't actually have to take aggro, a tank has to reduce the incoming damage."The-Magic-Sword, Paizo forums
    That fits "can't take hits for you". He needs a real way to protect, with a small count.
  3. Nothing in our rules makes "they stay in front" real. No Opportunity Attack at Stage 0 and no zone of control: an enemy walks past him. Other games' strongest answer is a move that ends next to the front-liner (Lancer, 13th Age). The opportunity attack is the weakest:
    "the absolute floor of stickiness... unless the enemy is worried about your damage output, that cost may be minor compared to the benefits of eating that tasty wizard over there."RPGBot, The Tank Fallacy
  4. One line of your brief pulls against another. A protector by Reaction works on an ally close enough to be hit:
    "Champions work best if someone else is in melee range or at least in that 15 foot range."Deriven Firelion, Paizo forums
    "The squishies stay back" puts them far. He needs two tools: a Reaction for the ally beside him, and a stop for the enemy heading to the one who stayed back.
  5. A protector with no threat gets ignored, unless his protection is certain. A Pathfinder 2e Guardian playtester:
    "piddly damage, which means there was not really a reason for the monsters to attack me."VampByDay, Paizo forums
    The 4e Swordmage also did little damage and was obeyed:
    "preventing 8 damage from my marked target is enough to draw them to me."renau1g, EN World
    No roll, and a number the table hears. This matters more now that his Weapon Modifier is +1.
  6. Every modern paladin hangs on one enemy he marks. 4e's Divine Challenge, 13th Age's Paladin's Challenge, Pathfinder 2e's Smite, Draw Steel's Judgment. The marked enemy is the one he punishes, protects against and explodes on: three roles, one job. The 4e mark failed while it asked the slowest class to stay adjacent, and worked once it didn't.
  7. One Reaction a round is the protector's whole budget.
    "one enemy can blow your reaction and then another can target a squishy without consequence."Witch of Miracles, Paizo forums
    In our rules, AP he keeps from his turn pays for more Reactions. So "hold an AP back to protect twice" already exists.
  8. He has no burst today. Holy Wrath at 2 AP is worth the same as Smite at 2 AP, and a level 1 Paladin has none. The one designer's table found (13th Age) sets a per-battle attack at 1.5 times a normal one and a daily at 2 times. Ours should sit at 1.5 to 2.5 times, deal damage even on its worst Tier, and work best as a finisher.
  9. A burst that builds slowly has a price in short fights. The drama essays want the explosion late:
    "the suspense curve is inverted... all the big cool stuff happens at the beginning."M_Natas, EN World
    A Draw Steel guide answers: "doing the same action early in a fight is better than doing it later." A burst with no gate that grows with Escalation leaves the choice to the player.
  10. "Against the profane" fails when it is a creature type. It is worth nothing or too much, depending on the campaign. In Pathfinder 2e only 7% of monsters are weak to holy. The pattern that works: it always works, and a little more against the Profane. One GM's fix was by behaviour: those who "committed crimes against their kingdom" count as unholy. For us: the enemy he judged could simply count as Profane for him.

Not checked: Giant in the Playground, RPG.net and Reddit refuse automated readers; nothing above rests on them.
