What I need from you now

One decision: the first Feature, the mark. Say "Proposed", or change it. The three things most worth your eye:

  1. What the mark does: a smaller hit (my proposal), or B or C below it.
  2. The size: your Power Modifier (2), once a round.
  3. Moving the mark costs 1 AP, as the Archer's does.

Why this shape

  • The decision it gives: whom to judge. The biggest hitter, or the enemy next to the Archer. Moving it costs 1 AP, so the first choice matters; when the Judged creature goes Down it moves for free.
  • Certain, with a number. No roll, no Reaction AP. A Standard's Basic attack is 3.5 on a Tier 2: minus 2 is more than half of it. Against its Special it is a quarter.
  • It asks nothing the enemy controls. No "stay adjacent to it" (the 4e Paladin's flaw). The only distance is the ally's from him: he stands where his allies are.
  • It is your support direction, small. The Hallmark Reaction will be the big version, once, on any enemy; this is the trickle on one.
  • Profane in every campaign. Later "against a Profane creature" lines work on the enemy he chose, and on real Profane creatures too. Divine Power is retired.
  • Its sibling: the Archer's Mark Quarry has the same frame (0 AP at the start, 0R when it goes Down, 1 AP to change). The Archer marks to hurt; the Paladin marks to shield.
  • The risk: the GM must remember "once a round" for that creature. Foundry has the pieces for it (the mark and the damage reduction); not tried.

If you'd rather the mark did something else

B
It pays for ignoring him. "The first time each round it damages an ally within 5 squares of you, it takes [R] 2 Ho." About 3 points a turn. This is 4e's Divine Challenge, which was called "barely a deterrent" at this size.
C
A soft taunt. "It is Taunted by you" (1 Bane against anyone else). About 2 to 5 points. The kind that was ignored on the Pathfinder 2e Guardian.

Not in this card, on purpose: the burst (a later Strike that is better against the creature he Judged), and anything that stops an enemy walking past him (a Reaction or an Art).

What the Modifiers do: his plain Weapon Attack rolls +1, a step under the Archer's. His divine Actions roll +2, and a divine Strike with the [Signature] trait rolls +2 as well. So the everyday swing is modest and the burst hits like a striker's.

His support: a smaller hit, or Guard before the hit. A step out of reach is a skirmisher's thing; ending conditions and stopping the drop at 0 are a healer's.

Your direction on Rally Points: letting allies use them is more a Cleric's thing; the Paladin can have some, not much. Lay On Hands as it is today (a Rally Point every round) doesn't fit him. At most one of his cards will touch an ally's Rally Points.

Question 3: what an Art looks like (decided: A)

An Art is the card you look at when attacking isn't the answer. It never deals damage. My default was "up to 3 Versions". What I meant: a small menu of different uses on one card, and you get all of them when you pick the Art. Two sketches to show the shape. These are not proposals; the real Arts come later, one at a time.

2 AP
You Advance. A Prone or Down ally you pass moves with you and ends adjacent to you.

The three ways to shape it:

A
A menu of up to 3 uses (my default). One pick gives a whole small toolbox on one theme. A level 1 Paladin has more to do, and the card is still short because each line is one thing. The risk: three lines to read.
B
At most 2 uses, like every other Action. Simpler; each Art is narrower.
C
Each use is its own Art. "Lantern of Faith" would be only the first line. The simplest cards; with one Art at level 1, he gets exactly one small trick.

Question 6: does the Paladin track a number of his own? (decided: A)

You asked: could Escalation be the pool, spent on abilities as it once was? I think it would hurt, as you suspected. Spending it switches off the Passives that need "Escalation 2 or more", which is exactly why it stopped being spent. Every class and the Elites share that ladder, and Momentum would turn into a starting purse. What works without spending: an ability can read Escalation ("1d8 more for each Escalation above 1"), so it grows through the fight and nothing is tracked.

The brief says he "uses all he has" and explodes. The question is what "all he has" is made of. Three ways, each with an example line (examples, not proposals).

A
Nothing new to track (my default). The burst spends what is already on the sheet: AP, the Reaction AP, Escalation.
"3 AP. You can also spend your Reaction AP: 2d8 more."
or: "1d8 more for each Escalation above 1."
Nothing to remember. The cost is real: no Reaction that round, so for a round he can't protect anyone.
B
One charge, on or off. A single token.
"When you use a Reaction to protect an ally, you are Charged. Your next divine Strike spends it: 2d8 more."
Protecting visibly loads the burst, so a round spent shielding is never a wasted round. Never more than one, so nothing to hoard. Nimble's paladin works this way; its designer moved to one charge from a stack of dice.
C
A pool of points.
"Wrath: 2 at the start of each turn, 1 more when the enemy you judged damages you. 1 Wrath protects an ally; 5 pays the big Strike."
This is Draw Steel's Censor. The deepest choice each round (protect now, or save for the blow), and a number to track every round. Its known weakness is a slow first round; a guide says of turn 1: "Not enough to do anything powerful." Our fights last about 3.5 rounds.

My default: A, with B allowed if a card needs it. C only if you want the Paladin to be the class with a resource.

The research in ten points

Other games' rules and other players' opinions, not ours. Every quote below was found on its page.

  1. Your three "softs" are not words players use. They would say off-tank, emergency healer, capped nova. A 2008 poster describes the 4e Paladin almost as you did:
    "single target tank, he grabs a guy and duels that guy, however he has some party benefit abilities so he helps via heals/temp HP for his party while he holds one guy on himself."brislove, EN World
  2. A tank that only survives is not a tank.
    "A Tank doesn't actually have to take aggro, a tank has to reduce the incoming damage."The-Magic-Sword, Paizo forums
    That fits "can't take hits for you". He needs a real way to protect, with a small count.
  3. Nothing in our rules makes "they stay in front" real. No Opportunity Attack at Stage 0 and no zone of control: an enemy walks past him. Other games' strongest answer is a move that ends next to the front-liner (Lancer, 13th Age). The opportunity attack is the weakest:
    "the absolute floor of stickiness... unless the enemy is worried about your damage output, that cost may be minor compared to the benefits of eating that tasty wizard over there."RPGBot, The Tank Fallacy
  4. One line of your brief pulls against another. A protector by Reaction works on an ally close enough to be hit:
    "Champions work best if someone else is in melee range or at least in that 15 foot range."Deriven Firelion, Paizo forums
    "The squishies stay back" puts them far. He needs two tools: a Reaction for the ally beside him, and a stop for the enemy heading to the one who stayed back.
  5. A protector with no threat gets ignored, unless his protection is certain. A Pathfinder 2e Guardian playtester:
    "piddly damage, which means there was not really a reason for the monsters to attack me."VampByDay, Paizo forums
    The 4e Swordmage also did little damage and was obeyed:
    "preventing 8 damage from my marked target is enough to draw them to me."renau1g, EN World
    No roll, and a number the table hears. This matters more now that his Weapon Modifier is +1.
  6. Every modern paladin hangs on one enemy he marks. 4e's Divine Challenge, 13th Age's Paladin's Challenge, Pathfinder 2e's Smite, Draw Steel's Judgment. The marked enemy is the one he punishes, protects against and explodes on: three roles, one job. The 4e mark failed while it asked the slowest class to stay adjacent, and worked once it didn't.
  7. One Reaction a round is the protector's whole budget.
    "one enemy can blow your reaction and then another can target a squishy without consequence."Witch of Miracles, Paizo forums
    In our rules, AP he keeps from his turn pays for more Reactions. So "hold an AP back to protect twice" already exists.
  8. He has no burst today. Holy Wrath at 2 AP is worth the same as Smite at 2 AP, and a level 1 Paladin has none. The one designer's table found (13th Age) sets a per-battle attack at 1.5 times a normal one and a daily at 2 times. Ours should sit at 1.5 to 2.5 times, deal damage even on its worst Tier, and work best as a finisher.
  9. A burst that builds slowly has a price in short fights. The drama essays want the explosion late:
    "the suspense curve is inverted... all the big cool stuff happens at the beginning."M_Natas, EN World
    A Draw Steel guide answers: "doing the same action early in a fight is better than doing it later." A burst with no gate that grows with Escalation leaves the choice to the player.
  10. "Against the profane" fails when it is a creature type. It is worth nothing or too much, depending on the campaign. In Pathfinder 2e only 7% of monsters are weak to holy. The pattern that works: it always works, and a little more against the Profane. One GM's fix was by behaviour: those who "committed crimes against their kingdom" count as unholy. For us: the enemy he judged could simply count as Profane for him.

Not checked: Giant in the Playground, RPG.net and Reddit refuse automated readers; nothing above rests on them.
