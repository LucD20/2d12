The Hunting Bow's Technique: Wounding Shot proposed

The old Wounding Shot was a Weapon Attack with more on it, so it was the first shot of every round with nothing to decide. You, on Skirmish Shot: "a Technique must feel good, almost unique". The research adds: a rider that happens by itself on every hit is one more thing to track, players want a choice made on the attack, and a second roll is what slows the table. So: one roll, the bow's own thing seen on most uses, and a choice attached to using it.

Analysis

  • The decision it gives. Shoot from where your turn started, or move first and lose it this round. For the Archer it is a question of order against his own Feature: Shifting Aim pays for moving before each shot, and Wounding Shot is only there before the first step. So a turn is "Wounding Shot from here, step, Quick Shots with the Boon", or "step first for cover and the Boon, and no Wounding Shot". Starting the turn Engaged (the shot is Doomed), with no line, or at far range makes it a real choice.
  • A second decision every round: Bleed doesn't add up (the higher stays), so the shot goes to a foe that isn't bleeding yet.
  • Why not 2. It is the best first shot of every round, and on 73% of its uses it is a Weapon Attack: the bow's own thing is seldom seen.
  • Why not 3. A shot for [Mod] and a rider is Trick Shots' shape. An Archer with that Art would hold a fourth Trick Shot.
  • The rules it touches. Bleed (Chapter 8): damage at the end of each of the creature's turns; it ends with an Interact (1 AP) and a Body or Instinct Save against DC 13. Nothing to count down. "Bleed 1 (Bo/In)" is the book's notation, flat on purpose: your direction for damage, and nothing to roll at the end of each turn. "Left your square this turn" is Shifting Aim's own phrase, so the two read as a pair.
  • Its siblings. No Archer card bleeds or asks you to stand still. Pin and Snap Shot stop a foe; this one costs it Health or an AP. A Paladin with a Hunting Bow gets a reason to shoot first and walk after.
  • Table feel. One roll. The bow's thing happens on 72% of uses. One number on a bleeding creature, said at the end of its turn. The GM gets a choice too: staunch for an AP, or bleed.
  • The research. D&D 2024's weapon riders are liked for making same-die weapons differ, and disliked where they are automatic or need a second roll. This is neither. Pathfinder 2e puts its weapon tricks on a critical only: the "seldom seen" problem of way 2.
  • Left off on purpose: a second Version (the old 2 AP one was only bigger) and a Critical line. The old "only a Tier 3 on the Save ends this Bleed" can come back as this card's own Critical if you want it to be one of the few.

A
Three rungs, one moved. Basic opens in Stage 0, Advanced with Stage 1 (Group Mastery 4, level 4, in place of 6), Exotic with Stage 2 (Group Mastery 10, level 8). Stage 3 brings no new bow: it has both Modifiers, and each bow's last unlock is placed there. Six bows, as decided. my pick
B
Four rungs, one a Stage. A fourth rung above Exotic for Stage 3 (Group Mastery 16, level 12). Every Stage has a bow to want. With eight weapon groups it is 8 to 16 more weapons, each with a Technique and unlocks, for levels nothing else is written for yet.
C
Four rungs, a new one at the bottom. A plain bow for Stage 0 with no unlocks, and the Hunting and Recurve Bows open with Stage 1. The simplest level 1; but Stage 0 then has one bow and no choice, and the first playtests are Stage 0.

Why A. The tree works because deeper is not stronger: a rung is a different way to play, paid by fewer unlocks. A fourth rung is paid by nothing until it is designed. A also keeps B open: a rung can be added on top later without touching a bow below it. C takes away the one choice Stage 0 has.

In all three the two Basic bows open at Group Mastery 0 and their first unlock is at 3. The gates' numbers are settled with the Advanced bows. If you meant a bow's own unlocks: the table's fourth row is the answer (they need to reach further up, not further down), and each bow's are spread over its Stages when it is built.

What I would keep decided

  1. The six bows in two lines. Hunting Bow and Recurve Bow (Basic), Longbow and Composite Bow (Advanced), Warbow and Flight Bow (Exotic). History as flavor, the game decides the numbers. Only the two Basic bows are built now.
  2. Two Basic bows, the same damage, two ways to play. The Hunting Bow stands and wounds; the Recurve Bow moves. They replace Chapter 15's Shortbow and Longbow.
  3. Strung. A bow held in one hand, the other free to draw. It fits the Archer better than Two-handed did: the hand that draws the string draws the knife. Darting Blade's "stow your bow" is read again with Light.
  4. Bleed as the Hunting Bow's own thing. It is in the book (Chapter 8), it has no duration to track (the creature pays an Interact to end it), and no Archer card bleeds.
  5. The ranges: normal 8 and 6, far twice that, maximum three times, for maps of about 25 squares.
  6. What an unlock is: the Technique, a utility, or a step of the Technique; never extra dice. A Technique writes 1[W]. An Advanced or Exotic bow has its Technique from the day you can use it.
  7. For the later bows, as ideas only: the Arcs rule ("can I shoot over it?" should have a rule), the Warbow's Line (one roll, several creatures: the new Archer's own shape), the Flight Bow's range that covers the map.

  • Flurry of Arrows and its steps (Rider's Stride, Unbroken Rhythm, Storm of the Steppe).
  • Parthian Shot and its steps (Turn and Loose, Second Wind, Gone Before They Land).

  • Light Feet, Close Quarters, Quick Nock.

  • Every second Version that is only bigger.

What is left of the old Basic bows after this: the Hunting Bow keeps its row, its range and Wounding Shot's core (a shot that bleeds). The Recurve Bow keeps its row, its range and its idea; its Technique starts again.

Research

The project's research folder had nothing on weapons' own abilities or on a Light property. One pass was made on what it lacked. What other games and their players say:

  • A trick for each weapon is liked because weapons with the same die finally differ. The trick itself is called small. In D&D 2024 the Shortbow has Vex (your next attack on that target has Advantage) and the Longbow has Slow.
  • What slows the table is a second roll, not a rider. "only one that really slows down the game is topple as it requires extra rolls".
  • A rider that happens by itself on every hit is called a stumbling block for beginners and one more thing to track. Several posters wanted a choice made on the attack.
  • One trick a weapon plus a free draw gives the "golf bag": players swap weapons every attack to use each trick. Free draw is nobody's Light property; where drawing is cheap, it is cheap for every weapon.
  • "Light" is a pairing rule in five of the seven games read: it says what a second weapon in the other hand does. The rapier and the club are the edge cases.
  • Short bow against long bow: a bigger die and more range alone make the short bow a "dumbed down" long bow. Pathfinder 2e's Volley works as balance and is disliked as fiction ("makes no sense"). The other ways found: a different rider on each bow, or mobility.
  • Draw Steel gives the trick to the kit, not the weapon, and has one "bow".

The note: book/research/weapons-bows-light.md. One pass, not re-checked by a second. Reddit, Giant in the Playground and RPG.net refuse readers and were left alone. No game with a ladder of unlocks for each weapon was read at its source.
