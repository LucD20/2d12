  • Against. The two Basic swords don't deal the same, as the bows did; they can't, because hands are priced. And "Longsword" will mean two hands, against what most games taught players.

2. By blade, the bows' shape: a straight line and a curved line

Straight: Arming Sword, Longsword, Montante; it grows from one hand to two. Curved or single-edged: Messer, Sabre, Shamshir; all in one hand. The two Basic swords are both one-handed and deal the same, played two ways, exactly like the bows.

Against: no two-handed sword at level 1 in this group. That is true to the sources and a surprise to players. And the curved line's order of difficulty rests on nothing I could read.

3. The familiar names

Longsword (one hand, as games use the word), Bastard Sword and Greatsword as one line that grows; a second line from the curved swords.

Against: it contradicts the sources you asked for, and the three are one sword at three sizes.

Research: what the fencing texts say

  • Meyer (1570) names two foundations. The long sword, "as a foundation of all fencing". The dusack, "a beginning and foundation for all weapons that are used in one hand". The dusack is a wooden trainer; its steel relatives are the messer and the sabre.
  • Fiore (about 1400) teaches the sword in one hand before the sword in two, "to make my system easier to learn". Another copy of his book gives the reverse order.
  • The big two-hander is the specialist's weapon in every source read. Di Grassi (1594): "as a galleon among many galleys". Figueyredo (1651): the montante has "sixteen simple rules, and sixteen composed", each a drill for a situation: hold a street, a narrow street, "Guarding a Lady", against shieldsmen.
  • The rapier is "a newly discovered practice for the Germans" for Meyer, and he teaches it last. Silver (1599) calls it "the worst weapon", for its length.
  • A Chinese saying: "a hundred days for the dao, a thousand for the spear, ten thousand for the jian".
  • The names are traps. The historical longsword has a two-handed grip; what games call a longsword is an arming sword. "Bastard sword" is the period word for the sword of one or two hands. "Claymore" is two weapons. "Flamberge" is wrong for the wave-bladed two-hander.

What the swords inherit from the bows

  • Mastery: a point with one weapon for a won encounter that was normal or harder; never lower than half your best in the group; unlocks at 10 (the Technique), 20, 30, 45, 60; an Advanced weapon Doomed below 10 with it, an Exotic one below 20.
  • A row is a flat number and one exploding die. Today's placeholders: Longsword (one hand) 0 / 2 + 1d6! / 4 + 1d6!; Greatsword (two hands) 0 / 5 + 1d6! / 8 + 1d8!.
  • Hands: 2 Banes from the off hand, 1 for a Light weapon. What pays for fighting with two weapons was parked for this group.
  • A Technique: a Round card, one roll, the weapon's own thing seen on most uses, a choice attached to using it; no copy of a Paladin or an Archer card.
