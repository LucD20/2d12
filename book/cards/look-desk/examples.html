A. The rubricated book

No frame: a rule above and below. Black for what you read, one dark red for what you act on (the name, the labels, a Version's heading). Real small caps. No colored terms; the example is an indented passage.

B, with the research only

B without your two words. The panel stays (the research's findable block: 5e's flat panel, Old-School Essentials' tint). What changes: the card's text in a plain sans that sets it apart from the serif page, as 5e does; the name in the accent and in bold, not small caps; an accent rule above and below the panel and one under the traits, 5e's way of carrying structure in one color; frequency and cost as plain bold words; Distance and Target with bold labels. No colored terms, no rounded corners, no saturated bar.

D. The research alone

Without your two words, weighing only what the sources praise for use at the table: the block found by a pale band behind its name line (Old-School Essentials, 5e), its text in a plain sans that sets it apart from the serif page (5e's Scala Sans; the research found a sans inside blocks common and not what reads as an app), bold labels, one accent, the frequency as a word. No colored terms, no rounded corners, no saturated bar.
