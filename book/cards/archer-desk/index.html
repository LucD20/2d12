The brief (your words, 2026-11-16)

I want to remove the quarry part of the archer. Later a ranger class will get it.

Archer are a damage dealers that deals damage by hitting a lot of times, not one big hit. They are very mobile, running away or moving side to side to distract enemies. They are about shooting fast and precise, then moving away. They are fast to change to melee for a quick strike then moving back to shooting arrows. Because they are ranged, they keep on the back lines since they are fragile, and a single action of damage doesn't do lots.

Decision 2: the mechanic, many hits from one roll

The number of hits grows with the Tier, written in the book's own notation (your catch: my word "arrow" was just [W]):

Tier 1: [V] [Mod] Pi.
Tier 2: [V] 2[W] + [Mod].
Tier 3: [V] 3[W] + [Mod].
  • One roll, up to three hits, and a Tier 1 that still lands something: he never whiffs and never lands one big number.
  • Resist is taken once from an Action, so three hits inside one Action lose it once; three separate Weapon Attacks lose it three times.
  • Left out, with reasons: an "Arrows" pool of dice (tracking; pools moved per hit are what players call fiddly); no Bane on repeated shots (the Twin Strike trap); a meter cashed in one blow (the nova, the opposite of the brief); Weapon +3 (yours: "wouldn't be good").
  • To watch: at 15 Mastery 1[W] becomes 2[W]; a class whose cards say 3[W] meets that step differently. For the Stages work.

What the research says about this brief

  • What bores is one best button, repeated. On 4e's ranger: "Every. Single. Ranger. Uses. Twin-Strike. Almost. Every. Round. [...] They're so boring." Your own playtest said the same of Double Shot. The hits must come from several different cards.
  • The shots that move are the ones the same players liked. "I like Hit And Run/Nimble Strike. They are basic attacks, yes, but they also let you move."
  • Range is paid for by less damage, danger up close, or things in the line of fire. Ours are in place; no Archer card erases them for free.
  • Small hits are liked when they cost one roll and never whiff. Jonathan Tweet (13th Age): "A disadvantage of a single large attack compared to two small attacks is that the single attacks are more likely to produce an unlikely result."
  • A token for each hit is what players call fiddly: keep it to "no more than 1-3".
