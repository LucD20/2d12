Versions and Criticals: the search is back

My answer to both: we use them less on purpose, and the research supports it, with one thing worth adding for the Critical.

  • Versions are not underused. Where Versions differ only in size, players use one and ignore the rest: "our wizard never casts anything but three-action magic missiles". The ones that read as a choice differ in kind (Pathfinder 2e's Heal: touch, range, area). Ours are of that kind where we have them (Judge, Lay On Hands), and the Arts' Modes are the same idea at one cost.
  • The rule I would keep: a second Version only when it does a different thing, never a bigger one. A shared second cost kills the cheap Version, and Frequency is ours: an Encounter card with a cheap Version would almost never be used cheap.
  • A Critical line on each card is the wrong place at 2.8% of rolls. Games with criticals that rare (Draw Steel: 3%) write the critical once, for everything, and big, and put each ability's excitement on a result that comes often. We already do both halves: a Critical raises Escalation by 1, and a Tier 3 must visibly beat a Tier 2.
  • What is worth adding: one Critical line a class, on a Feature. For the Archer, something like "When you get a Critical with a [Strike] Action, you can make a Weapon Attack (1A) against any creature in range": one more arrow. Rare, loud, and nothing to read on every card.

One quick research pass, not re-checked: book/research/versions-criticals.md. A Critical is Doubles on a result that is already Tier 3: about once in 36 rolls at Weapon +2.

Opening Shot, your version, checked

An ally's Weapon Attack is worth 25 points. It costs that ally their Reaction AP, which is worth 10 to 15 when they have a Reaction to spend it on and nothing when they don't. So your version gives about 10 to 25 points a round from Escalation 4, two to three times a Passive's 5 to 10. It would also fire nearly every round: an extra attack roll outside anyone's turn, the thing you found disruptive in Overwatch.

It is the best teamwork on the list, so I kept it and gated it on his Tier 3:

A Tier 3 is 21% of his shots; with two or three shots a turn it fires in about half the rounds: about 6 to 12 a turn, and half the extra rolls. Other gates: once an encounter, or moving it to Escalation 6.

The check on Clear Lines

One Field Bane moves his Tiers from 28 / 51 / 21% to 35 / 48 / 17%, which takes Quick Shots from 9.6 to 8.7 damage (9%). If about two thirds of his shots are at a target Engaged with an ally, lifting those Banes is worth about 4 to 5 points a turn: a little under a Passive's 5 to 10. The brief (your words, 2026-11-16)

I want to remove the quarry part of the archer. Later a ranger class will get it.

Archer are a damage dealers that deals damage by hitting a lot of times, not one big hit. They are very mobile, running away or moving side to side to distract enemies. They are about shooting fast and precise, then moving away. They are fast to change to melee for a quick strike then moving back to shooting arrows. Because they are ranged, they keep on the back lines since they are fragile, and a single action of damage doesn't do lots.

Decision 2: the mechanic, many hits from one roll

The number of hits grows with the Tier, written in the book's own notation (your catch: my word "arrow" was just [W]):

Tier 1: [V] [Mod] Pi.
Tier 2: [V] 2[W] + [Mod].
Tier 3: [V] 3[W] + [Mod].
  • One roll, up to three hits, and a Tier 1 that still lands something: he never whiffs and never lands one big number.
  • Resist is taken once from an Action, so three hits inside one Action lose it once; three separate Weapon Attacks lose it three times.
  • Left out, with reasons: an "Arrows" pool of dice (tracking; pools moved per hit are what players call fiddly); no Bane on repeated shots (the Twin Strike trap); a meter cashed in one blow (the nova, the opposite of the brief); Weapon +3 (yours: "wouldn't be good").
  • To watch: at 15 Mastery 1[W] becomes 2[W]; a class whose cards say 3[W] meets that step differently. For the Stages work.

What the research says about this brief

  • What bores is one best button, repeated. On 4e's ranger: "Every. Single. Ranger. Uses. Twin-Strike. Almost. Every. Round. [...] They're so boring." Your own playtest said the same of Double Shot. The hits must come from several different cards.
  • The shots that move are the ones the same players liked. "I like Hit And Run/Nimble Strike. They are basic attacks, yes, but they also let you move."
  • Range is paid for by less damage, danger up close, or things in the line of fire. Ours are in place; no Archer card erases them for free.
  • Small hits are liked when they cost one roll and never whiff. Jonathan Tweet (13th Age): "A disadvantage of a single large attack compared to two small attacks is that the single attacks are more likely to produce an unlikely result."
  • A token for each hit is what players call fiddly: keep it to "no more than 1-3".
