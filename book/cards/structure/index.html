Card Structure Desk

The Action card's new structure, built one part at a time. Structure only: what a card holds and where. The look comes later. Nothing here is in the book or in Foundry. Updated 2026-11-16.

What I need from you now

Part 1, the header, is decided: the name with the frequency and cost on the bar; the Flavor; the traits on their own line; Distance and Area at the left, the Target at the right; the roll as a labeled line above the Tiers; a Strike always writes its Defense.

  1. Your question, the category ("Paladin Escalation Action"): I would take it off the card. Everything in it is already on the card (Escalation 6, Encounter, the cost) or in the chapter heading above it, and no rule in the book reads it. It stays as data, so the book and Foundry can still group cards by it. Say yes, or keep it.
  2. Part 2, the body: 1 (plain sentences), 2 (every line labeled) or 3 (two zones). Next section.

Part 2: the body, three structures

What the body can hold: Requirements, an Interrupt or Trigger, a Setup, a named value (Smite dice), the roll line, the Tiers, a Critical, something that happens with no roll, and standing rules about the card. This part also settles part 5 (text with no label), because the two can't be separated. Shown on Smite (a named value and a rule after the Tiers), Rebuke (something that happens before the Save) and Judge (standing rules).

The headers are the one you chose, drawn without the category line (see the question at the top). Judge's two costs are drawn as lines; Versions are part 3.

1. Play order, plain sentences

Every line sits where it happens. Only the fixed parts carry a label (Requirement, Trigger, Setup, Attack or Save, the Tiers, Critical). Anything else is a plain sentence.

  • Shortest to write; closest to how the redesign's cards were drafted.
  • A plain sentence doesn't say what it is: "The target is Hindered (EoT)" happens when the card is used; Judge's last paragraph is always true. The reader must tell them apart.

2. Play order, every line labeled (my pick)

The same order, and two more labels: Effect for what happens when the card is used, with no roll; Special for a rule about the card itself. A defined word is its own label (Smite dice, Judged).

The roll line: three ways, after the research

Your doubt holds. No published game found opens an ability with "Each target makes an X Save". Elsewhere the roll is a labeled line in the body (4e, D&D 2024's monsters), a labeled field in the header (Pathfinder 2e's spells), or a sentence in the prose (5e's spells).

What else the research said

  • For a dedicated line: "the first question is always 'what do I roll?' so the attack / save / etc needs to be called out" (a Paizo forum poster, 2018; the final book added the line).
  • For a sentence: "you don't need to change your gaze from the text to the save or target line" (EN World, 2014). Putting the line next to the Tiers answers it.
  • On the grid: one player called Draw Steel's format "difficult to parse on first reading", and a fan site reprints its grid as labeled rows. So our grid keeps "Distance:", "Area:" and "Target:". The evidence here is thin: nobody compares a grid with stacked lines.

One research pass, not re-checked. Reddit, Giant in the Playground and RPG.net refuse readers and were left alone. The note: book/research/card-layout.md.

My pick: B, the grid

  • It serves the system's purpose. A fixed place for each fact is the least ambiguous, and the roll line states who rolls against what in words.
  • It fits the fullest and the emptiest card. Word of Radiance's header is three lines; Armor of Faith's is two.
  • Its cost: the most work to build, in the book's builder and later in Foundry's card. A is the easiest to read cold; C is the cheapest.

The two models (Pathfinder 2e's block and Draw Steel's block) are named from the rules texts read earlier in this redesign; I did not search for players' opinions of the layouts.

The order of the nine parts

  1. The header (this page).
  2. The body's order: Requirements, Setup, Interrupt or Trigger, lines before the roll, the Tiers, Critical, lines after.
  3. Versions: columns with "As X AP", or one full line a cost.
  4. Modes (an Art's named choices).
  5. Text with no label: one element instead of four.
  6. The Target line when a card touches two creatures (waits for the targeting research).
  7. Passives and Features: a card like an Action's, or lighter.
  8. Level and Escalation on the header or not.
  9. Critical: an optional row, or dropped.
