## Research

`book/research/` had nothing on swords. One pass was made: **`book/research/swords.md`** (2026-11-16; 30 takeaways, a table of 27 swords; one pass by an agent, not re-checked; [read] there means read on Wiktenauer). What it found, for the first decision:

- **Meyer (1570) names two foundations** [read]: the long sword, "as a foundation of all fencing", and the dusack, "a beginning and foundation for all weapons that are used in one hand". The dusack is a wooden trainer; its steel relatives are the messer and the sabre.
- **Fiore (about 1400) teaches the sword in one hand before the sword in two** [read], "to make my system easier to learn" (another copy gives the reverse order). A modern instructor (Clements, ARMA) [checked]: the single sword first, and skills carry upward by family.
- **The big two-hander is the specialist's weapon in every source read.** Di Grassi (1594) [read]: "as a galleon among many galleys", for men "mighty and big to behold". Figueyredo (1651) [read]: the montante has "sixteen simple rules, and sixteen composed", each a drill for a situation (hold a street, a narrow street, "Guarding a Lady", against shieldsmen). ARMA [quoted]: "its use was considered as special skill, often meriting extra pay".
- **The rapier is "a newly discovered practice for the Germans" for Meyer, taught last** [read]. Silver (1599) [read] calls it "the worst weapon", for its length, not its difficulty. **Not verified:** that the sabre was quick to teach and the rapier a long schooling.
- **The Chinese saying** [checked, one school's blog]: "a hundred days for the dao, a thousand for the spear, ten thousand for the jian".
- **The names are traps.** The historical longsword has a two-handed grip; what games call a longsword is an arming sword. "Bastard sword" is the period word for the sword of one or two hands. "Claymore" is two weapons. "Flamberge" is wrong for the wave-bladed two-hander (Flammenschwert). "Broadsword" belongs to basket hilts.
- **What each was good at** (for the Techniques, later): the longsword's bind and winding, half-sword and the pommel strike; the montante's rules against many and for guarding a person; the Zweihänder's lugs that catch a weapon; the messer's free hand that seizes; the sabre's cut drilled as a pattern and the curved blade's draw cut; the estoc's point for the gaps of armour.

## Decision 1: the six swords (DECIDED 2026-11-16: way 1, by hands)

**The group.** Swords become their own group, without the Dagger (his answer of 2026-10-31: "Daggers (light) and Swords become their own groups"): the half-Mastery rule then joins swords with swords only. A shortsword goes with the daggers, as he thought.

**1, by hands (Claude's pick): Meyer's two foundations.** The bows' two lines were two materials; the swords' are one hand and two.

| | **The one-handed line** (a free hand, speed) | **The two-handed line** (power, reach, control of the other blade) |
|---|---|---|
| **Basic** | **Arming Sword:** the knight's straight sword, cut and thrust; the sword Fiore teaches first | **Longsword:** the two-handed grip of the German school; Meyer's "foundation of all fencing" |
| **Advanced** | **Sabre:** the curved blade, the cut drilled as a pattern, the draw cut | **Zweihänder:** the Landsknecht's great sword, with lugs to catch a weapon |
| **Exotic** | **Rapier:** the long thrusting sword, "newly discovered", taught last | **Montante:** the Iberian great sword with its thirty-two rules, one man against many |

- **For:** it follows the sources at both ends (the two foundations first, the specialist's weapons last); a level 1 character has a one-handed and a two-handed sword to choose from, which is the choice the rules already price (a free hand against +3 damage); and each rung up is a real step in what the sword asks of its user.
- **Against:** the two Basic swords don't deal the same, as the bows did (they can't: hands are priced); and "Longsword" will mean two hands, against what most games taught players.

**2, by blade (the bows' shape): a straight line and a curved line.** Straight: Arming Sword → Longsword → Montante (it grows from one hand to two). Curved or single-edged: Messer → Sabre → Shamshir (all in one hand). The two Basic swords are both one-handed and deal the same, played two ways (cut and thrust against the chop), exactly like the bows. Against: no two-handed sword at level 1 in this group (true to the sources; a surprise to players); and the curved line's order of difficulty rests on nothing read.

**3, the familiar names (his issue 2).** Longsword (one hand, as games use the word), Bastard Sword, Greatsword as one line that grows; a second line from the curved swords. The names players know. Against: it contradicts the sources he asked for (the longsword), and the three are one sword at three sizes.

### Analysis

- **The decision it gives.** Where the foe goes. One square, any direction: off an ally it was Engaged with (a friend's bow is no longer Doomed), into the reach of a second ally for a flank, out of a doorway, out of its cover, or into Hazardous Terrain (Forced Movement can: Chapter 3). And whether to use it at all: its Tier 2 hits with the die alone, so a Weapon Attack hits harder when the foe is already where you want it. The same honest trade as Circling Shot.
- **The Tier 3.** Grabbed: the foe is Immobilized and only you can move it, until it pays 1 AP to Break Free, you let go (no Action), or you leave its reach. Your hand is then busy (the rule: "the holder can't use the hand or limb that holds you for anything else"), so **you can't use Seizing Cut again while you hold**: letting go is the next decision. A simpler Tier 3, his to choose: "Slid 2".
- **Its pair, ahead of time.** The one-handed sword moves the foe's body. The Longsword, in two hands, is left the other blade (the bind: the research's "control of the other blade").
- **The Paladin with it.** He has no everyday way to move one enemy (Word of Radiance and Reckoning push a crowd, once a fight). With Hold the Line's Brace (the squares beside him are Difficult Terrain) and Judge (it protects allies within 5 squares), placing one foe each round is what a protector wants. His Weapon Modifier is +1: he sees the Slid on 65% of uses.
- **Against the classes' cards.** Binding Light (2 AP) Immobilizes for a round on a Tier 2 or 3 with more damage: Seizing Cut's Grabbed is a quarter as likely, ends for 1 AP, and costs you your hand. Trick Shots' Knock Back pushes 1 or 2 for [Mod] damage at range; this slides 1 with the sword's die, in reach, with an empty hand.
- **The rules it touches.** Slid (Chapter 3: the origin chooses the path; no Reactions; Collision if the path is blocked; Steadiness resists it, so no size limit is written on the card). Grabbed (Chapter 8). "A free hand" is the book's phrase (Handle Actions need one). Line of Effect: on the Target line.
- **Table feel.** One roll; a token moves on two uses in three; a Tier 3 is a picture (you have him by the arm).
- **The research.** Fiore's sword in one hand is taught after grappling and dagger, and his plays with it seize the arm or the hilt with the left hand (from memory: not in the pass that was read; Lecküchner's messer has the same and was read: "you should fence with one hand with the Messer and you should have the other one on the back. If you want to fight with the empty hand..."). Other games: a rider that needs a second roll is what slows the table; this has none.
- **Not on the card, on purpose:** a Tier 1; a size limit; a second Version.

## Decision 5: the Longsword's Weapon Technique (DECIDED 2026-11-16: way 1, written with Pressed in decision 5b)

**What it has to do.** The same four things (one roll; its own thing seen on most uses; a choice attached; no copy of a class card), and be the Arming Sword's pair: that one moves the foe's body; this line was given "control of the other blade" in decision 1. The research: the Liechtenauer school's longsword is fought **from the bind**, blade on blade; its Zwerchhau, the "thwart-hew", "breaks all hews which are hewn from above down" [read, Ringeck]. The rules have a word for what a bound blade suffers that no class card uses: the **Pressure Bane** (Chapter 1: "Banes an effect of another creature puts on you... a blow that leaves you reeling"; at most 2 count).

**1, the bind (Claude's pick): your blade stays on theirs.**

> **Thwart Cut** · Round · 1 AP
> *You cut across your foe's blade and stay on it, and every blow it tries has to come through your steel.*
> Weapon · Melee · Strike
> Distance: Weapon | Target: One creature
> **Attack:** Weapon vs. Armor Defense
> **Tier 1:** Bound, on its Strikes against you only.
> **Tier 2:** [V] 1[W]; Bound.
> **Tier 3:** [V] 1[W] + [Mod]; Bound, with 2 Pressure Banes.
> **Bound:** The target's [Strike] Actions take 1 Pressure Bane (EoT). It ends early if you stop threatening the target.

**2, the half-sword:** one hand on the blade, the point driven into the gaps (the same school, against armour [read]). Pierce; "this Strike counts the target's Armor Defense as 2 lower"; a Tier 3 adds Prone. A reason to pick the armoured foe. Against: a number on the roll (the kind of bonus nobody sees at the table), and it is the natural thing of an Estoc if one ever comes.

**3, the murder-stroke:** the sword turned round and the hilt brought down like a hammer ("strike him with the pommel to the other side of his head" [read]). Blunt; Tier 1 [V] [Mod], Tier 2 [V] 1[W] + [Mod], Tier 3 the same and Dazed 1 (the target loses 1 AP now). The big moment. Against: its own thing is seen on a quarter of the uses, and losing an AP is the largest everyday rider in the game.

### Analysis

- **The decision it gives.** Whom to bind: the foe that hits hardest, not the one nearest to dying. And then to stay: the bind lasts only while you threaten the target (Chapter 3: it is within your weapon's Range and you could attack it), so walking away to another fight lets its blade go. The foe has a way out too: step out of your Range, and pay the movement.
- **Its pair.** Seizing Cut: one hand free, move the body, a light hit. Thwart Cut: both hands on the sword, hold the blade, a light hit from a heavy sword. Position against protection.
- **The Paladin with it.** Judge takes his Power Modifier off the first damage a Judged foe deals to an ally each round; Shield of Faith takes damage off one hit as a Reaction. Thwart Cut works one step earlier and on another number: the foe's roll, against anyone. The three add up without repeating each other, and it gives a two-handed Paladin a protector's play that costs no Reaction.
- **Against the classes' cards.** Taunted (Shield of Faith, Commandment) is 1 Bane on its Actions against anyone but you; Bound is 1 Bane on its Strikes against everyone, you included, and needs your sword in reach. No card uses a Pressure Bane; the Bestiary does (NPC zones), and the cap of 2 keeps them from piling up.
- **The rules it touches.** Named Boons and Banes (Chapter 1). EoT (the end of the affected creature's turn). Threatening (Chapter 3, Flanking). "Bound" is a defined word on the card, as "Judged" is on Judge: it is written once, under the Tiers that use it. Line of Effect: on the Target line.
- **Table feel.** One roll; something on every Tier (as he asked of Seizing Cut); a marker on the bound foe that the GM feels on its next attacks.
- **The name.** Thwart Cut is the Zwerchhau in English (the translations read say "thwart-hew"). His liking for the rarer names: the card could carry the German, **Zwerchhau**, with the Flavor explaining it.

## Decision 5b: a new condition for "Banes on your Strikes" (DECIDED 2026-11-16: Pressed)

> ### Pressed
> Pressed always has a value, such as **Pressed 1**. Your [Strike] Actions take that many **Pressure Banes** (see [[Named Boons and Banes]]: at most 2 count). If you would be Pressed twice, only the highest value applies.

- **The name, candidates:** **Pressed** (Claude's pick: it says which Bane it is, so "Pressed 2" explains itself and the cap of 2 comes with it) · Reeling (the rule's own example: "a blow that leaves you reeling") · Hampered · Off-balance · Rattled (reads as fear) · Checked · Fettered (reads as Immobilized) · Harried (close to "Harry", an Archer idea he kept for later) · Weakened (reads as the removed Enfeebled).
- **Why Strikes only:** it is a thing done to a weapon arm or an aim; Saves, Power Actions that force a Save and skill rolls are untouched, which keeps it clear of Frightened.
- **Why a value:** like Vulnerable (the same sentence shape and the same stacking rule), with the Pressure name capping it at 2.
- **Who else can use it:** the Bestiary's "1 Pressure Bane on its next roll" could become "Pressed 1 (EoT)" when its tables are next touched; a later class or weapon (a net, a whip, a hail of arrows).

**Thwart Cut written with it** (shorter: the defined word leaves the card):

> **Thwart Cut** · Round · 1 AP
> *You cut across your foe's blade and stay on it, and every blow it tries has to come through your steel.*
> Weapon · Melee · Strike
> Distance: Weapon | Target: One creature
> **Attack:** Weapon vs. Armor Defense
> **Tier 1:** Pressed 1 (EoT).
> **Tier 2:** [V] 1[W]; Pressed 1 (EoT).
> **Tier 3:** [V] 1[W] + [Mod]; Pressed 2 (EoT).
> **Special:** This Pressed ends if you stop threatening the target.

Two changes from the first draft, both to keep the condition plain: **the Tier 1 is a full Pressed 1** (a condition "against you only" would need a "by" like Taunted's; the card then mirrors Seizing Cut: the same effect on a Tier 1 and a Tier 2, the damage added on the second); and **the early end is a Special line of the card**, not part of the condition (other sources of Pressed won't have a blade to keep on it).
