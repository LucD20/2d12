## The rule in one line

A weapon's damage at a Tier is **a flat number plus at most one die**. Tier 1 stays 0. Nothing else changes: `1[W] + [Mod]` is still how an Action writes it, and 2[W] is still two instances (two flat parts, two dice).

## What it does

- **One die a hit, never two** (2[W]: two dice where there were up to four).
- **A narrower range.** The Greatsword's Tier 3 goes from 2 to 12 to 5 to 10 (column A) or 5 to 12 (column B).
- **The same averages** (column A): no number of the ruler moves, the Bestiary stays as it is. Where today's damage is two dice with a whole average (2d6 = 7, 2d8 = 9), one die can't give it exactly: those go up half a point.
- **It is not Draw Steel's damage:** the roll is still 2d12 with Boons and Banes, and a die is still rolled for damage.

## How weapons stay different

Today the dice tell weapons apart. In the draft the **shape** does:

| Kind | Shape | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| Blades, Polearms | a small flat part, a d6 | the middle |
| Axes, Firearms | little or no flat part, a big die at Tier 3 | swingy, the highest top |
| Hammers, Crossbows, the Longbow | a big flat part, a d4 | steady |
| Light and Thrown weapons | a d4, flat only at Tier 3 | low |

## How big can the die be

Lucas: "people like big die, so make sure if d8 is the real limit without unbalancing the game". **It is not a limit: the die's size does not move the balance** when the average stays the same. Measured (`node book/ruler/sim/weapondice.mjs`; today's level 1 party, 1,500 fights a cell; rounds, Health lost with its spread, a PC Down, won):

| Weapons | Normal (4 Standards) | Deadly (6 Standards) |
|---|---|---|
| Today | 3.64 r (± 0.81) · 14% (± 18) · Down 22% · won 99% | 5.57 r (± 1.81) · 47% (± 38) · Down 68% · won 72% |
| A (same averages) | 3.59 r (± 0.70) · 13% (± 18) · Down 22% · won 99% | 5.65 r (± 1.85) · 45% (± 37) · Down 67% · won 73% |
| B (d8 the biggest) | 3.67 r (± 0.69) · 14% (± 19) · Down 25% · won 99% | 5.56 r (± 1.79) · 46% (± 37) · Down 67% · won 72% |
| B, the Greatsword's Tier 3 as 3 + 1d10 | 3.66 r (± 0.73) · 13% (± 18) · Down 23% · won 99% | 5.59 r (± 1.74) · 46% (± 37) · Down 68% · won 73% |
| B, the Greatsword's Tier 3 as 2 + 1d12 (Tier 2: 1d8) | 3.64 r (± 0.77) · 13% (± 18) · Down 22% · won 99% | 5.53 r (± 1.79) · 46% (± 37) · Down 67% · won 72% |
| The same, and the Crossbow as 0 / 1 + 1d6 / 2 + 1d8 | 3.64 r (± 0.74) · 14% (± 19) · Down 23% · won 99% | 5.65 r (± 1.84) · 47% (± 37) · Down 69% · won 72% |

Every row is the same within the runs' noise (about 2 points), B included. So the die is a choice of feel: a bigger die on a smaller flat part is a wider range (the Greatsword's Tier 3: 5 to 12 with 4 + 1d8, 4 to 13 with 3 + 1d10, 3 to 14 with 2 + 1d12), which is what the idea set out to narrow. **A way to have both:** big dice only where the weapon's story is the big hit, the two-handed melee weapons' Tier 3 (Greatsword 3 + 1d10, Greataxe 4 + 1d12 as in B, Pike 2 + 1d8), and small dice everywhere else (Tier 2s, one-handed and ranged weapons, the Maul as the steady two-hander).

**Decided (Lucas, 2026-11-12): small dice.** "Lets go back to small die yeah, that was the point of the whole thing": column B as written (a d8 at most, and only on a two-handed melee weapon's Tier 3; the Greataxe's 4 + 1d12 in column B becomes 6 + 1d8, the same 10.5).

## 2[W]

No card deals 2[W] or more today (checked: the card files have none), **but Mastery does (Lucas, 2026-11-12): Chapter 15, 15 Mastery (Master), from level 8: 1[W] becomes 2[W]**. That is the weapon's own step of damage growth, so 2[W] has to keep working: way (a) below keeps it exactly as the book has it. With this draft it can still exist in three ways: (a) two instances as now (Greatsword Tier 3: 8 + 2d8), two dice; (b) **roll once, double the total** (one die, the same average, a wider range); (c) double the flat part and roll one die (lower: it is no longer twice the weapon). Or no Action writes more than 1[W] and a bigger hit is 1[W] plus a flat bonus. Open, for Lucas.

## What to know before choosing

- **A changes no balance number**; it can go in without the ruler. **B does:** melee Tier 3s up about 1, ranged Tier 3s down 1. From the Stage 0 run (candidate A there, a ranged die one size smaller), a step this size moves the fights by a few points only: B is for the feel, it does not by itself make melee as good as ranged (the Guard for an Engaged melee PC did that).
- **Cards that write their own dice** (Smite and the like) are not touched here; the same idea can be applied to them card by card.
- **Still to do if adopted:** Chapter 15's tables and its 2[W] example, gear.json and the Foundry weapons, the ruler's class files, the Weapon Techniques that quote a weapon's dice.
