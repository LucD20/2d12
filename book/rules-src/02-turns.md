# Chapter 2: Turns and Actions

## Encounters

An **encounter** is a fight or a similar scene: it starts when Initiative is rolled and ends when the GM says it's over. It can't end while a creature is Dying or Shattered: each one must first stabilize, have its damage brought back below its Health Pool, or die. If a creature becomes Dying or Shattered outside of an encounter, it rolls on the Dying or Shattered Table (this is its roll for the encounter that follows), then Initiative is rolled and that creature goes last.

> [!tip]
> Sometimes, rolling Initiative when a single creature becomes Dying or Shattered and there is no obvious danger around, and an ally can easily get them back up, is just going to slow down the game. In these non-threatening situations, feel free to roll on the Dying or Shattered Table once, and then the creature stabilizes.

## Initiative

At the start of combat, **every creature rolls Initiative**. Initiative is a Check, normally an Instinct Check, but the GM can call for a different Attribute, for everyone or for some creatures only. The total is the creature's initiative.

Initiative is a **zipper**. The creatures are split into however many different **groups** exist. Usually it is the PCs against a single group of enemies, but three-way combat is possible. Each group puts its creatures in order from the highest result to the lowest. The group with the highest single result goes first. Then the groups take turns, one creature at a time, in the order of their highest result, each taking its highest remaining creature. A group with no creatures left is skipped.

> [!example]
> Victor rolls 14, Paul 13, Wes 10, Goblin Warrior 12 and Goblin Scoundrel 10. The PCs' highest result is 14 and the enemies' is 12, so the order is: Victor, Goblin Warrior, Paul, Goblin Scoundrel, Wes.
>
> In a three-way fight, Mark rolls 13 and Nick 9, the enemies Goblin Warrior 12 and Goblin Scoundrel 8, and a third group Kobold Mage 13 and Kobold Shaman 5. Mark and the Kobold Mage tie at 13 and the PC goes first (see Ties, below): the order is Mark, Kobold Mage, Goblin Warrior, Nick, Kobold Shaman, Goblin Scoundrel.

**Ties** are broken by the higher Attribute Modifier used for the Initiative roll. If they are still tied, the Friendly creature (a PC or an ally of the PCs) goes first.

### The Start of the Encounter

Some abilities happen **at the start of an encounter**, such as the Paladin's [[Judge]]. After Initiative is rolled and before the first turn, each of them waits in a **stack**, in the turn order. The GM can change that order: an ally can go later, for example to act after another ally's ability. Then they resolve one at a time, in the stack's order, and the first turn begins.

> [!example]
> Chris and Krysta are Paladins. Chris rolls 15 and Krysta 13, so the stack is Chris's Judge, then Krysta's. Chris wants to know which enemy Krysta Judges before choosing his own, so the GM puts Krysta's first.

### Swapping Initiative

On your turn, if you haven't taken any Actions yet and aren't Down, you can spend **1 AP** to swap places in the turn order with an ally who hasn't had a turn this round. The ally takes the turn now, at your place in the order, and gains its AP as normal.

You act at the ally's old place. Your turn doesn't end when you swap: it waits, so you don't regain AP until your new turn ends. Until then, the AP you had left are **frozen**: they pay for nothing, not even a Reaction. When your new turn arrives, you carry on with those AP, and you regain AP at its end as usual. You can't swap again that round.

> [!example]
> PC1 (first in the order) swaps with PC3 and keeps 3 AP. The order becomes PC3, NPC1, PC2, NPC2, PC1. When PC1's turn comes, PC1 has 3 AP.

## Turns and Rounds

A **turn** is the time one creature acts, from the start of their turn to the end of it. A **[[Rounds|round]]** is one full cycle of turns. Limits that say "per turn" count each turn separately, including other creatures' turns: you can make up to 3 Strikes in your turn, and up to 3 more (as reactions) in another creature's turn.

A **turn-cycle** is the period from the start of a creature's turn to the start of that creature's next turn. In the order "Victor, Goblin Warrior, Paul, Goblin Scoundrel, Wes", Wes's turn-cycle runs from the start of Wes's turn to the start of Wes's next turn.

## Action Points

You spend **Action Points (AP)** to do things on your turn, and to pay for [[Reactions]] on any turn. Every creature starts an encounter with **4 AP** (a [[Minions|Minion]] with 3).

At the **end of your turn**, after everything else that happens then, you **regain AP**: you lose any AP you didn't use and gain **4 AP**. Those AP are your next turn's. A Reaction is paid with the AP you have when it happens: on your own turn, with that turn's AP; on another creature's turn, with the AP you regained for your next turn, so whatever you spend reacting then, you don't have on your next turn.

### Unspent AP

AP you haven't spent when your turn ends are lost: you regain up to 4, never 4 more. Nothing else comes of them.

> [!example]
> Drak ends his turn with 1 AP left: he loses it and regains 4. During the goblins' turns he uses Shield of Faith (1R) on an ally, so his next turn starts with 3 AP. At its end he regains 4 again.

### A Turn With 0 AP

If you have 0 AP when your turn starts, the turn still starts and ends as usual (what happens at its start and at its end still happens, and you regain your AP at its end), but you can't take an Action in it.

### Losing and Gaining Action Points

When something gives or takes Action Points, it says whether it happens **now** or **when you regain AP**.

- **Now.** "The target loses 1 AP" means they lose 1 AP they have right now; if they have none, it has no effect. A card that wants the loss to carry over to the next turn writes [[Dazed]]. "The target gains 1 AP" means they gain 1 AP right now. Unless otherwise stated, you can't have more than 4 AP. Between your turns, the AP you have are the ones you regained at the end of your last turn: a loss then comes out of your next turn.
- **When you regain AP.** A change to the AP you regain at the end of your turn is the [[Slowed]] condition: Slowed 1 means you regain 1 AP less. [[Quickened]] gives you AP at the start of your turn instead: Quickened 1 means you gain 1 AP more when that turn starts.

## Reactions

A **Reaction** is an Action you use in response to something another creature does. Its card has an **Interrupt** or a **Trigger** line that says what it responds to, and which of the two it is decides when it happens. Its cost is written with an R, such as **1R**: it uses that many Action Points, but only as a response. A **0R** Reaction uses no AP; its Frequency still limits it.

- **Paying:** A Reaction is paid with your **AP**: on your own turn with that turn's AP, on another creature's turn with the AP you regained at the end of your last turn (see [[Action Points]]). There is no other point to pay with, and no rule limits how many Reactions you use: each card's Frequency and its Interrupt or Trigger limit it.
- **No Reaction by default:** No creature has a Reaction of its own: every Reaction comes from a class, an item or a creature's entry. Moving away from an enemy doesn't let it attack you, unless it has a Reaction that says so.
- **Interrupt:** An Interrupt happens **before** the moment it names: "Interrupt: The target would enter a square adjacent to you" happens before the creature enters that square (see [[Entering a Square]]). An Interrupt can say that part of its effect happens after the triggering Action ("after the damage, they are Slid 1"): that part waits for it. An Interrupt on a creature that **would damage** someone happens once the triggering Action's roll is made and its damage is known, before that damage is dealt: the Reaction can't change that roll.
- **Trigger:** A Trigger happens **after** the moment it names, which can be in the middle of an Action: "Trigger: The target moves away from you" happens after the first square that takes it farther, while it is still moving; "Trigger: A creature deals damage to you" happens after you take that damage.
- **Interrupts before Triggers:** When an Interrupt and a Trigger respond to the same Action, every Interrupt resolves before any Trigger.
- **At its moment:** An Interrupt or a Trigger happens at the moment it names, as above, not at the end of the triggering Action. When that moment comes in the middle of an Action (its damage, with an Effect still to come), the Reaction is resolved there, whole, and everything the Action hasn't done yet waits for it: what follows the damage in the Tier's line, an Effect line written after the Tiers, a later target. Then the Action goes on with what it has left, if it still can.
- **Once per triggering Action:** You can react only once to the same Action, but several creatures can react to it. Some Actions, mainly Move Actions, can happen throughout different squares. It is still a single Action, and you decide when you take the Reaction: for instance, you can wait for the creature to move into a more advantageous position before taking your Reaction, instead of taking it as soon as the creature moves.
- **Multiple creatures reacting to the same trigger, at the same time:** You must decide to react before making the rolls: when a creature triggers a Reaction from several creatures, they all decide whether they will react before making any rolls. The creatures taking the Reactions decide the order; if they are opposed, the GM decides or asks for a simple Check to decide who goes first, similar to how Initiative is rolled. If the triggering creature becomes an invalid target after a creature reacts (such as being killed), the remaining creatures still lose the AP as if they had used the Reaction.
- **On any turn:** A Reaction can happen during any turn, including your own: if another creature's Action moves them away from you during your turn, you can react to that.
- You can't use an Action outside your turn unless it is a Reaction.

> [!example]
> Lonny, an Archer, stabs a wisp with Darting Blade, whose Effect line, written after its Tiers, says "You Evade 2". The damage puts the wisp Down, and the wisp has Dying Flare: "Trigger: The wisp's damage reaches its Health Pool", a 1 Emanation around it. The Flare happens at that moment, before Lonny's Evade: he is still next to the wisp, so he is in the Area and makes the Save. Only then does Lonny Evade 2.

> [!tip]
> You don't need to move one square at a time: make the full movement and let the reacting creature say where along it the Reaction happens. If the Reaction disrupts the movement, move the token back to that square.

### What a Reaction Responds To

An Interrupt or a Trigger names a **moment** the rules define, and the Action that holds the moment is the triggering Action.

"Triggering" is used for both kinds of Reaction: an Interrupt has a triggering creature and a triggering Action too.

### A Reaction's Target

Every Reaction has a Target line, and its Interrupt or Trigger is written about **the target**. The creature whose Action sets the Reaction off is the **triggering creature**.

**To use a Reaction** you need [[Line of Effect]] to its target, as for every Action. You must also **perceive** the target and the triggering creature (see [[Senses]]): Hidden is enough; Unsensed is not.

A Reaction can also do something to the triggering creature when it isn't the target: Shield of Faith's target is the ally, and it Taunts the attacker. That needs Line of Effect to the triggering creature too, like anything a card does to a creature (see [[Targets of Effects]]); without it, that part doesn't happen and the rest does.

A feature that says "when..." waits for a moment: you must perceive the creatures it names. What it then does to a creature needs Line of Effect like anything else.

> [!example]
> Zack, a Paladin, has Shield of Faith: "Target: One ally. Interrupt: The creature you Judged would damage the target with a [Strike] Action." The orc Zack Judged, around a corner from him, shoots Nari, who stands 3 squares from Zack in the open. Zack has Line of Effect to Nari and hears the orc: he uses Shield of Faith, and Nari takes half of the damage. He has no Line of Effect to the orc, so the orc isn't Taunted. Had Nari been the one behind the wall, Zack couldn't have used it at all.

> [!example]
> Zack has Rebuke: "Trigger: An enemy within 3 squares of you moves. Target: The triggering creature." A goblin 2 squares from him, on the other side of a wall, runs off. Zack hears it, but Rebuke targets the goblin and he has no Line of Effect to it: he can't use Rebuke.

> [!example]
> Zack has Kindled Wrath: "when an ally becomes Bloodied or goes Down, your Escalation increases by 1". Nari becomes Bloodied behind a wall, 6 squares from Zack. Zack hears him (within 10 squares), and Kindled Wrath does nothing to Nari, so it needs no Line of Effect: his Escalation increases. At 12 squares behind that wall, out of sight and hearing, it wouldn't.

### Disrupting Actions

When an Action is **disrupted**, what is left of it doesn't happen, and the resources and AP spent on it are wasted. Some Actions can disrupt another Action, usually with a Critical.

A Reaction disrupts an Action only when its card says so. When an Interrupt does, the Action never starts and nothing of it happens. When a Trigger does, the Action stops where it is (see below).

A Trigger that names a Move Action fires at any point during the Move Action (after the first square it moves), but it is a single trigger: you can use the Reaction only once per Move Action, even with AP to spare.

When the disrupted Action is only a Linked Action, the Action stops only if that part is necessary for it. What already happened stays, and a disrupted movement ends in the square the creature was in. For example, a Charge disrupted before the creature has moved the 3 squares it needs is cancelled, and they make no Basic Attack. If it is disrupted after those 3 squares, the Charge ends there, and they make the Basic Attack if there is a valid target.

### Entering a Square

A creature **enters a square** when its own movement brings it into that square. A moment about movement is always about the creature it names: "an enemy enters a square adjacent to you" happens when that enemy moves into such a square, never when you move next to it. **When you are the one who moves, the other creature has entered nothing**, has not moved closer to you and has not moved away from you, and nothing that waits for one of those happens.

- Only the creature's own Move Action counts: Evade, Forced Movement and Reposition don't trigger Reactions from movement (see [[Moving Closer]]).
- A creature already adjacent to you that moves to another square adjacent to you enters a square adjacent to you.
- For a creature larger than one square, a square is adjacent to it when it is adjacent to any square of its space.

> [!example]
> Steven, an Archer, has Snap Shot: "Interrupt: The target would enter a square adjacent to you." Jared, an enemy, stands 3 squares from him. On his turn Steven Advances and stops next to Jared: Jared entered no square, so Steven can't use Snap Shot. On Jared's turn he Advances from one square beside Steven to another square beside him: he would enter a square adjacent to Steven, and Steven can use Snap Shot.

### Moving Closer

A creature **moves closer to a creature** (to you, to an ally: whoever the card names) when it enters a square that is nearer to that creature than the square it came from, counted in squares (every second diagonal counts as 2, as always). An Interrupt checks this before the creature enters the square; a Trigger checks it if the creature entered any square nearer to you during the movement. A step to the side that leaves the distance the same is not closer. Evade, Forced Movement and Reposition don't trigger Reactions from movement, so they are never moving closer or moving away. The rest of this section, and [[Moving Away]], say "you" for that creature.

### Moving Away

A creature **moves away from you** when it enters a square that is farther from you than the square it came from, counted in squares (every second diagonal counts as 2, as always). Everything else mirrors [[Moving Closer]]:

- An Interrupt checks before the creature enters the farther square; a Trigger checks whether it entered any square farther from you during the movement.
- A step that leaves the distance the same is neither closer nor away.
- A movement that comes closer and then goes away (a creature running past you) moves away from its first farther square.
- For a creature larger than one square, count from its nearest square to your nearest square.
- "Within 3 squares of you" on such a Reaction is checked in the square the creature is in when you take the Reaction.
- If the movement is disrupted, it ends in the square the creature was in when the Reaction was taken.

### Moving Within N Squares

A creature **moves within N squares of you** when, with its own Move Action, it leaves a square that is within N squares of you. So a step that starts inside the N squares counts, wherever it goes (closer, away or to the side), and a step from farther away into them doesn't: 4 squares to 3 doesn't count for "within 3", 3 to 2 does, 3 to 4 does.

- Standing up from Prone leaves no square, so it isn't moving. Neither Break Free, Evade, Forced Movement nor Reposition counts (they don't trigger Reactions from movement).
- It is a single trigger for each Move Action, as for any Move Action: the reacting creature chooses the square along it where the Reaction happens.
- Count the squares as always; for a creature larger than one square, from its nearest square to your nearest square.

### Ends Its Turn

A moment that a creature **ends its turn** ("Trigger: An enemy ends its turn adjacent to you") happens **after everything at the end of that turn**: its [[Lingering]], its effects that end (EoT), and its AP regained. It comes just before the next creature's turn starts, so it is outside every creature's turn. Where the creature stands then is what counts. Several Reactions to it are resolved as for any trigger (see [[Reactions]]).

## Actions

An Action is something a creature does in a fight. Most cost AP. Every Action is written as a card; see [[Reading an Action]].

Actions that roll have three tiers.

### Setup

Some Actions have a **Setup** line. It says what happens before the rest of the Action.

- **A Setup is optional**, unless it says you **must**. You can do part of it: "You Advance up to 5 squares" lets you move fewer, or not at all.
- **The Action's Distance and its targets are checked after the Setup.** You can start out of reach, and the Setup brings you there. If no valid target is within the Distance after the Setup, the rest of the Action doesn't happen, and its cost is still paid.
- **A Setup that names the Action's target fixes it.** If the Setup says "toward the target", you choose that target before the Setup, and it stays the Action's target.
- **If an Action in your Setup is disrupted, the rest of the Action still happens.** Only then, if the Setup had named a target, you can choose another target for the rest of the Action.
- **Setups don't stack.** When something gives a Setup to an Action that already has one, you choose which of the two Setups to use.

### Targets

An Action that targets something can only be used if it has a valid target. An Action with no valid target can't be used. An Action that names a number of targets needs that many: "Two creatures" can't be used against one. "Up to" means any number from one to that many. When an Action targets a creature, you can target an object instead, such as a barrel, a crate or a door. Usually the Action rolls against DC 13 for the object, but the GM can set a different DC depending on the circumstances (the rest of targeting objects is not written yet: see [[Line of Effect]]).

### Willing and Unwilling

Some Actions target a **willing** creature, and some rules change for an **unwilling** one (passing through its square, for example). A creature is **willing** when it accepts the effect: you choose for yourself, and each creature chooses for itself when the effect would happen. An **Unconscious** creature counts as willing. Any other creature that doesn't accept is **unwilling**. A creature doesn't have to know what the effect does to accept it.

### Requirements

Some Actions have a **Requirement** line, the first of the card's labeled lines. To use the Action, you must meet it; otherwise you can't use it at all. It is checked when you start to use the Action, before you pay its cost and before any Setup. Charge requires at least Speed 3: with Speed 2, you can't Charge, and a Speed bonus that the Charge itself gives doesn't count toward it.

### 0 AP Actions

A **0 AP** Action costs no AP, but it is still an Action: you can use it only on your turn, and its Frequency still limits it. An Action that costs no AP and is used in response to something is a Reaction, written **0R** (see [[Reactions]]). [[Linked Actions]] cost no AP either, because they are part of another Action.

### Linked Actions

An Action that another Action lets you use as part of it is a **Linked Action**. It is written with the AP of the Version you use, such as "Basic Attack (1A)", or "(1A/2A)" when you choose between two Versions.

- It is part of the original Action, so it **costs no AP**.
- It keeps its traits and requirements, unless the Action says otherwise: you must still be able to use it. If an Action lets you Evade (a Linked Action) while you are Immobilized, you can't.
- It affects the target of the original Action, unless it says otherwise. If the original Action doesn't list a target, you can choose the target per Linked Action.
- It doesn't count toward its own uses: not toward its Frequency, the 3 Strikes of the turn, or the At-will count, unless the Action says otherwise.
- If it is required and you can't make it (no valid target, a requirement you don't meet), the Action stops there: that Linked Action and everything after it don't happen, what already happened stays, and the cost is still paid. An optional one you can't make is skipped, and the Action goes on. A Linked Action is optional when the Action says you **can** make it ("you can Evade 2"), and required when it says you **do** it ("you Advance 5, make a Basic Attack (1A) and Advance 5 again").
- **A movement you can't make.** When an Action has you move ("You Evade 2") and you can't (you are Immobilized or Prone, or no square is open to you), you move 0 squares and the rest of the Action happens. If the Action says you **must** move ("you must Evade 2 first"), the movement is necessary: the whole Action is disrupted.

By default, you can't change the target of a Linked Action. For example, if a player asks to change the target from a creature to an object so they can continue the Action, the GM should probably accept it only if it makes sense, such as attacking a door on the way, and not "attacking the ground".

> [!example]
> A card lets you make a Basic Attack (1A) as part of it. If you then use Basic Attack as its own Action in the same turn, that is your first use: it isn't Doomed.

### Targets of Effects

Unless something says otherwise, the effects of an Action, and the Actions it lets you use (Linked Actions), affect the **target of the Action**. If you use Shove on an enemy, the enemy is the one who is Pushed.

**Line of Effect, always.** Whatever a card or a feature does to a creature (damages it, moves it, heals it, gives it a condition, Guard, a Boon or a Bane, or lets it act), you need [[Line of Effect]] to that creature at that moment, whether the creature is on the Target line or named in the text. In an Area, the line is from the Point of Origin. A creature you have no Line of Effect to isn't affected; the rest of the card still happens. A card that works without it says so: "(no Line of Effect needed)".

An effect on one creature that others gain or lose from (Vulnerable, Taunted, a creature made easier to perceive) is done to that creature alone: you need no Line of Effect to the others.

### Effect Timing

Every line of a card says when it happens (see [[Reading an Action]]). Two cases need more:

- A **Bloodied** or **Strained** line happens only against a target that is [[Bloodied]] or [[Strained]]. **Where it is written decides when it counts:** above the Tiers, the target must already be Bloodied (Strained) when the Action is used; after the Tiers, it counts too if this Action's damage makes the target Bloodied (Strained). Like an Effect line, it happens whatever the roll unless it names the Tiers it needs ("on a Tier 2 or 3, ...").

Inside a Tier's line, the damage is written first and applied first; what follows the semicolon happens after the damage is applied ("[V] 1[W] + [Mod]; Prone").

### Buffs

A **Buff** is an effect that modifies another Action, such as the 2 Boons that Feint's Tier 3 gives your next [Strike] Action against its target. A Buff that harms the Action it modifies is a Debuff.

- On an Action with several targets, a Buff or a Debuff is rolled once per Tier, not per creature (see [[Several Targets]]).

**Raising Defenses.** "+1 Defense" raises **every** Defense of the creature: Armor Defense, Ward Defense and every Attribute DC. "+1 AD", "+1 WD" or "+1 Body DC" raises only that one. A feature can narrow it further, such as "+1 Defense vs Actions that Push". These bonuses don't add up with each other (see [[Stacking]]).

#### Stacking

Unless a card says otherwise, the same effect never stacks with itself, and bonuses from different effects don't add up either: for each value (Vulnerable, Slowed, a Defense, [[Guard]]...), a creature uses only the **highest bonus** and the **highest penalty** that apply. If a creature gets the same effect again, it keeps the higher value and the longer duration: two effects that make it Vulnerable 1 do not make it Vulnerable 2. Lingerings of different subtypes are different effects (see [[Lingering]]). A condition with a source, such as Daunted of a creature, comes from one source at a time (see [[Chapter 8: Conditions]]).

Four things do add up:

- An effect that says it **increases** a value: "increase your Slowed value by 1" adds to the Slowed you have.
- **Boons and Banes**, up to the limit of their name (see [[Named Boons and Banes]]).
- **Named bonuses.** A bonus to a value such as a Defense or a Speed can have a name, as Boons and Banes do: Field, Pressure or Support. Bonuses with different names add up; with the same name, only the highest counts. An unnamed bonus never adds to another bonus: it counts only when it is higher than the named ones together.
- **Damage reductions** from different effects (see [[Guard]]).

For example, Jacob has a +1 Support bonus to every Defense and a +1 Field bonus to Armor Defense: his Armor Defense rises by 2. A second +1 Support bonus changes nothing, and an unnamed +1 bonus doesn't add to them either: he keeps +2.

**A card can say otherwise.** [[Judge]] does: multiple Paladins can Judge the same creature. For example, Chris and Krysta are Paladins and both Judge the same Orc. It is Judged by Chris and Judged by Krysta: it counts as Profane for each of them.

**Equipment is not a bonus.** Your armor gives your Armor value and your charm your Ward value; effects add to those. With +1 AD and +1 Defense, your Armor Defense rises by 1 (not 2) and every other Defense by 1.

> [!example]
> Hindered halves a creature's Speed. If two Actions make the same creature Hindered, its Speed is still halved once, not twice: it is the same effect.

### Frequency

Every Action has a **Frequency**, written in the title bar of its card, such as **At-will**, **Turn** (once per turn), **Round** (once per round), **Encounter** (once per encounter) or **Daily** (once per [[Sleep]]).

Some traits can change the Frequency or expand the Frequency rules, such as the Strike trait, which limits you to a maximum of three Strikes per turn (see [[Strike|Chapter 7]]).

#### At-will

An **At-will** Action can be used any number of times, but each use after the first in a turn adds **1 Bane** to the roll. **An At-will [Strike] Action used again is Doomed instead** (see [[Strike]]): if you use Basic Attack three times in one turn, the first roll is normal, and the second and the third are Doomed. This doesn't apply to Linked Actions.

Each At-will Action has its own count, except At-will [Strike] Actions, which share one (see [[Strike]]).

An At-will Action with no roll has no Bane to take: use it as often as you can pay for it.

#### Turn

A **Turn** Action can be used once per turn: once on your turn, and once on each other creature's turn, as long as you have the AP. A Reaction with the Turn Frequency can answer one creature on its turn and another on theirs.

#### Round

A **Round** Action can be used once per turn-cycle, not once per round number: a Round Reaction you use late in one round can't be used again early in the next, before your own turn has started. A card that says **"once a round"** or **"once each round"** means the same: once per turn-cycle.

#### Encounter

An **Encounter** Action can be used once during a whole encounter. It resets when the encounter ends, which is the GM's call.

#### Daily

A **Daily** Action can be used once, and again only after you complete a [[Sleep]].

## Versus

An Action that rolls uses a **Modifier** and, if it targets a creature, rolls against a **Defense** or DC. The Modifier is your Arms, Power, Signature or Attribute Modifier (see [[Arms, Power and Signature Modifiers|Chapter 5]]). Every Action that rolls says so on its **Attack** line, which names the Modifier and the Defense: "Attack: Arms vs. Armor Defense". The Defense is Armor Defense (AD), Ward Defense (WD), an Attribute DC of the target ("vs. Body DC") or a specific DC.

### Defenses

An Action against a creature rolls against one of its three **Defenses**; some Actions roll against a set DC instead:

| Defense | DC |
|---|---|
| **Armor Defense (AD)** | the creature's Armor Defense |
| **Ward Defense (WD)** | the creature's Ward Defense |
| **An Attribute DC** | 11 + the target's Attribute: its Body DC, Mind DC, Appeal DC or Instinct DC (see [[Attribute DCs]]) |
| **Vs. DC** | A set DC, such as "Vs. DC 13": some Actions have a set DC, and others target something that has no Defenses, such as a stationary object. |

- **Armor value** is the value provided by the [[Armor|armor]] a creature wears. **Armor Defense** is 11 + Armor value + other items + bonuses − penalties.
- **Ward value** is the value provided by the ward a creature carries or is, such as a holy symbol, a charm, a ring or a feature. **Ward Defense** is 11 + your [[Arms, Power and Signature Modifiers|Power Modifier]] + Ward value + other items + bonuses − penalties. A creature without a ward normally has Ward value 0; items and features can increase this value.


## Rounds and Durations

Each position in the turn order is an **initiative slot**. Effects end at the end of an initiative slot, not of a creature: if a creature swaps initiative, an EoT effect still ends at the end of the slot the creature was in when the effect was applied.

### Until

When an effect says it lasts **until** something happens, it lasts through that something, and ends once it has been resolved. For example, a creature that is Hindered until they use a Move Action is still Hindered during that Move Action, and stops being Hindered afterwards. "Until you leave your square" ends after any movement, Forced Movement and Reposition included (see [[Moving and Leaving Your Square]]).

### Your Next Action

When an Action says an effect happens on your **next Action** and gives no duration (unlike "For 1 round"), you must make that Action before the end of your current turn. If you don't, the effect is wasted.

When an effect gives **another creature** its next roll or its next Action ("its next roll has 1 Boon"), it lasts until that creature makes one. It is wasted if the creature hasn't made one by the end of its next turn (given during that creature's own turn, by the end of that turn).

### Rounds

A **round** is one full cycle of turns. It starts with the first creature's turn and ends when the last creature ends their turn.

An effect that lasts a number of rounds counts down by 1 at the end of the initiative slot in which it was applied (the origin's own slot when it was applied on its turn, the current slot when it was applied as a Reaction or out of turn), starting with that slot's next turn: the end of the turn in which it was applied doesn't count. An effect that lasts 2 rounds is written "for 2 rounds", or with the rounds in parentheses after it: "Taunted by you (1 round)". A duration is one of these: [[End of Turn|EoT]]; a number of rounds; [[End of Encounter|EoE]] (until the encounter ends); or a condition's own end (Prone lasts until you Stand). Nothing else lasts until the start of your next turn.

> [!example]
> On the first round of combat, Victor (the first creature in the turn order) causes an effect on Wes that lasts 2 rounds. The effect ends at the end of Victor's turn on the third round.

### End of Turn

When an effect lasts until the end of the turn of the affected creature, it is marked **EoT**.

An EoT effect you gain on your own turn ends at the end of that turn; one you gain on another creature's turn lasts until the end of your next turn. A duration in rounds is different: it does not count down at the end of the turn in which the effect was created, because it has to last a whole round.

### End of Encounter

When an effect lasts until the encounter ends, it is marked **EoE**.

## Escalation

**Escalation** is a character's adrenaline: a level from **1 to 6** that rises as a fight goes on. Each PC has their own. It is never spent: the cards that need it or grow with it read it.

- **Start:** you start every encounter at **Escalation 1 + your party's [[Momentum]]**.
- **Rounds:** at the start of every round after the first, if you aren't Unconscious, your Escalation increases by 1. While Unconscious, you keep the Escalation you have.
- **Critical:** once an encounter, when you score a [[Critical]] on one of your Actions, your Escalation increases by 1.
- **Your class's hook:** each class names one more event that, once an encounter, increases your Escalation by 1 (the Paladin: an ally becomes Bloodied or goes Down; the Archer: an enemy you have damaged goes Down). You gain your class's hook at level 2.
- **At most 6:** you can't have more than 6 Escalation, unless a feature says so. A Critical or a hook that happens while your Escalation is already 6 does nothing, and it isn't used up for the encounter.
- **The end:** when the encounter ends, your Escalation goes back to 1, and the Actions that need it can't be used.

An Action whose card says **Escalation N** can be used only while your Escalation is at least N. Your class's **Apex** is an Encounter Action that needs **Escalation 6**; it comes at level 4, the first level of Stage 1 (see [[Escalation Abilities]]). Your Escalation also feeds your cards that grow with it, such as the Paladin's Smite.

> [!example]
> Nari's party has Momentum 1, so he starts a fight at Escalation 2. Round 2 starts: he is at 3. In round 2 he scores a Critical: he is at 4. Round 3 starts: 5. Round 4: 6, and he stays there. When the fight ends, he is back at 1.

#### Escalation Die

**dE**, the **Escalation die**, is a die with twice your Escalation's sides, **at least a d4**: a d4 at Escalation 1 and 2, a d6 at 3, a d8 at 4, a d10 at 5 and a d12 at 6. It never explodes. A card writes how many to roll: "2dE" at Escalation 3 is 2d6. It is the Escalation of the creature using the card, when it rolls.

### Momentum

**Momentum** is the edge your party builds over an expedition: a number from **0 to 4** that the whole party shares. Each of you starts an encounter at Escalation 1 + Momentum, so at 5 at most.

- **Gaining:** +1 after each encounter the party overcomes. Until scenes outside a fight have rules, the GM says which of them count (a negotiation, a sneak past a guard post).
- **Losing:** -1 each time a PC goes Down, and -1 at each [[Sleep]]. If the party retreats from an encounter, it loses all of it.
- **A [[Rest]]** clears it: you leave a Rest with Momentum 0.
- It is never under 0 or above 4.

> [!example]
> Jacob's party has Momentum 2 after two fights, so the third starts at Escalation 3. In it Jacob goes Down: Momentum 1. They win: Momentum 2 again. They sleep in the ruins: 1 in the morning.

> [!tip]
> The GM's valve: after a bad turn of the dice for the whole table, the GM can give every PC +1 Escalation. It is the only raise outside the rules above.
