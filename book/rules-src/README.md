# How the rules are written (rules-src)

The rules are built from the numbered chapter files in this folder into **six documents**: Core Rules (`core-rules.html`), Species (`species.html`), Gear (`gear.html`), Paladin (`paladin.html`), Archer (`archer.html`) and Bestiary (`bestiary.html`). Only files named `NN-name.md` are chapters: `INDEX.md` (generated) and this file are not. **Set aside (2026-11-12, Stage 0):** the Talents document is `set-aside/16-talents.md`, kept and not built, and its cards and Opportunity Attack are listed in `book/cards/set-aside.txt` (kept in the card files, placed in no chapter; the build checks the list). To bring the document back: move the file here again and add `('talents', 'talents.html', 'Talents')` to `DOCS` in build_rules.py.

## The documents
A chapter file says which document it belongs to on its **first line**: `<!-- doc: species -->` (no tag means Core Rules).

| Files | Document |
|---|---|
| `01-roll.md` to `09-glossary.md` | **Core Rules** (Chapters 1 to 9; the shared glossary is here) |
| `11-species.md` | **Species** |
| `12-paladin.md` | **Paladin** |
| `13-archer.md` | **Archer** |
| `14-bestiary.md` | **Bestiary** |
| `15-gear.md` | **Gear** (weapons, properties, Mastery, armor) |
| `set-aside/16-talents.md` | **Talents** (set aside, not built) |

- One build makes all of them. **A link to a place in the same document scrolls by the page's own script** (v0.0.427), so it works inside another page's frame (the Claude app's viewer, a published page); a link to another document needs the files side by side. `[[Links]]` work across documents: a link to a heading in another document becomes a link to that file (`core-rules.html#...`). If a title exists in two documents (each class has its own Features by Level, Locked Actions...), a link goes to the one in **its own document**, else to the first.
- Core keeps its chapter numbers ("Chapter 5" in the text). The other documents have no chapter numbers: their file starts with a plain `# Title`, and their sections are `##`.
- To move a section to another document, move its text to that document's file (or a new `NN-name.md` with the `doc` tag) and change its heading levels so the document's title is the `#`. Rebuild: the checks list every link that broke.
- **Document titles:** the builder shows a title block (the document's title and "Draft") only when the first chapter's heading is a different text (Core Rules, Bestiary); when the chapter heading already is the title (Species, Gear, Talents, Paladin, Archer) there is no title block and no "(Draft)" in the page title. The class documents have no tagline.
- **Stage 0 (2026-11-12):** the Stages are 0 (levels 1-3), 1 (4-7), 2 (8-11), 3 (12-15), and only Stage 0 is written. Escalation runs 1 to 6 (Passives at 2, 4 and 6; the Apex at 6, from level 4); Momentum is 0 to 4. **Weapon damage is a flat number plus at most one small die** (`5 + 1d6`, a d8 at most; 2[W] is two instances). **An NPC's damage is written whole** (`1 + 1d4`: no `[Mod]`), and its Vigor and Resolve are ready numbers by rating. No creature has a Reaction by default (Opportunity Attack is set aside). Minions and Grunts have 3 AP; a Minion has 6 + 6 Health, no Reaction AP, and up to 3 act in one turn slot; a ranged NPC attack deals one damage rating lower; an NPC's CV moves with its Health and damage ratings (5 / 10 / 15 / 30 a step by rank; a Defense or Modifier step is half); an NPC's Defense goes up 2 points a rating (a Standard: 10 / 11 / 13 / 15 / 17); an Elite's Escalation is on the 1-to-6 ladder (its abilities at 2, 4 and 6). **Rank** is Minion / Grunt / Standard / Elite (it was called "role" until 2026-11-12); **role** is what an NPC does (Striker, Soldier, Skirmisher, Artillery, Controller, Leader: a set of ratings, no rules). An NPC Action's Defense, Modifier and pool are three separate choices (Weapon / AD / [V] and Power / WD / [R] are the usual sets).
- **Weapons (the Bow remake and the Swords, 2026-11-16):** only the Hunting Bow, the Recurve Bow, the Arming Sword and the Longsword are designed (the Swords' four later names, Sabre, Rapier, Zweihänder and Montante, are in `cards/swords-redesign.md`, not in the book); **every other weapon of `15-gear.md` is a placeholder** (Lucas: made to playtest and fill space): don't argue from their rows, properties or Techniques, and rewrite them with their Weapon Group. A weapon table's last column is the rung (Basic, Advanced, Exotic). A redesigned Technique is a Structure card in `cards/stage0/gear.json`, placed under a bold line that names its weapon. Mastery: +1 for a won encounter (normal or harder), unlocks at 10 / 20 / 30 / 45 / 60 for every weapon.
- **Unnamed bonuses (decisions-s1.md item 20):** a source that gives an unnamed bonus gives **+1, rarely +2, and never "+1 per X"**; the one exception is a bonus that grows with the Stage ("+1 per Stage").
- **The book's look (2026-11-17; `cards/card-look.md`; decided by research only):** the page in Source Serif 4, the cards in Source Sans 3, both embedded from `rules-assets/` (full files: real small caps and old-style figures); one accent, a dark bronze, only for the cards' structure; game terms are not colored (only [V] and [R] damage keep their colors); a defined term is bold where it is defined; examples and open boxes are indented passages with an italic lead-in; tables have horizontal rules only; light by default, dark by the reader's system or the contents column's switch, print always light. The rules are the last section of `rules.css` ("THE BOOK LOOK"); Foundry keeps its own look.
- **Class documents by level (2026-11-17, replaces the order below):** the intro, the at-a-glance box, "The <Class> by Level" as a table (Level · You gain · You choose) with a sentence for level 4, then `## Level 1`, `## Level 2`, `## Level 3`, each holding its parts under headings that carry the level (`### Level 1: Features`, `### Level 1: Hallmark Action`, `### Level 1: Hallmark Reaction`, `### Level 1: Art`, `### Level 1: Escalation 2 Ability`; `### Level 2: Escalation Hook`, `### Level 2: Encounter Pick`, `### Level 2: Escalation 4 Ability`; `### Level 3: Second Art` (a pointer to Level 1's Arts), `### Level 3: Escalation 6 Ability`). **Every choice** opens with "**Choose one.** *Quick build: X.*", then one line per option (name, frequency and cost, what it does), then the cards.
- **Class documents (the redesign, 2026-11-16; both classes have it):** the stat box (with an `Escalation hook` row), the level list (`Features`, `Hallmark Action`, `Hallmark Reaction`, `Art`, `Picks`, `Escalation`, `Talents`), `Level 1` (`Features`, `Hallmark Action Selection`, `Hallmark Reaction Selection`), `<Class> Arts`, the `Repertoire` (`Encounter`) and the `Escalation` (`The Escalation Hook`, `Escalation 2`, `Escalation 4`, `Escalation 6`). The redesigned class's cards are in `book/cards/stage0/` (the book reads them over the old cards of the same name; Foundry and the ruler read the old files until the Foundry pass).
- **Class documents (pack R-classes, 2026-11-04):** the stat box (with an `Escalation hook` row), the level list (`Features`, `Hallmark Action`, `Hallmark Reaction`, `Picks`, `Escalation`, `Talents`; a Talent at every level), `Level 1` (`Features`, `Hallmark Action Selection`, `Hallmark Reaction Selection`, `Picks`), the `Repertoire` listed by Frequency (`Encounter`, `Daily`; `Passive`, `Turn` and `Round` when a class has them) and the `Escalation` (`Escalation 1 Passives`, `Escalation 2 Passives`, `Escalation 3 Passives`, `Apex`); no intro lines: Core defines Class Actions, Picks and the Repertoire (Characters, "Class Actions, Picks and the Repertoire"). A Passive card writes its rung in `escalation` (the card shows "Escalation N"); an Apex card has `escalation: 3` and the category `<Class> Apex`; the Hallmark Reactions' category is the Feature that grants them (`Divine Power`, `Sharpshooter`), like the Hallmark Actions'. **Numbers that step by Stage** (healing, Guard dice, blocks: decision sheet item 18) are written in the text as "2d6 + your Power Modifier (3d6 at Stage 2, 4d6 at Stage 3)" or with the Stage ("twice your Power Modifier + your Stage"). A level-by-level explanation of each class is still to be written (B1).
- **Core structure (2026-10-25):** **Roll Effects** (Chapter 1, before Several Targets) holds how damage is written ([Mod], [W], the damage types and subtypes) and Cleave; Empowered is in Conditions (Enfeebled left the game, 2026-11-13); an Action's Area and Distance are two lines (`**Area:** 2 Emanation`, `**Distance:** 10 sq.`); Light has the shape of an area (an Emanation around a creature or object, a Burst on a corner), and only the light where the TARGET is matters (Dim: Obscured, Darkness: Hidden or Unsensed): there is no penalty for standing in Dim light or Darkness yourself.
- **Names in the examples (Lucas):** use only these names, he/him: Dave, Jared, Zack, Nari, Drak, Wes, John, Steven, Mark, Jacob, Chris, Lonny, Nick, Will, Philip, Peter, Paul, Victor; she/her: Krysta, Lana. Don't repeat a name until all have been used, and vary the combinations; the exception: examples (and figures) that explain the same or a very similar thing keep the same name. In diagrams, **"you" is Y** (never O or K); a token for a named creature carries the initial of its name (two names in one figure must not share an initial); extra allies are numbered tokens (3, 4, 5). The builder's check lists the names in use and the ones never used. **Used so far (2026-10-24):** Peter and Will (Several Targets); Victor, Paul and Wes (Initiative, Turns and Rounds); Mark and Nick (the three-way initiative); Dave (Reposition); Philip and John (Flanking); Krysta (the Senses examples, one name for all four); Lana and Jared (Immobilized). **Not used yet:** Zack, Nari, Drak, Steven, Jacob, Chris, Lonny.
- **Colors (every document):** the builder (`build_rules.py`, `class_colors` and `CLASS_TOKEN`) colors, in the text of every chapter (not in headings, links, trait chips or card titles): a damage expression (a [V] or [R] tag with its dice and numbers, or a [W] / [Mod] expression for Vigor; the damage code keeps its own color; what follows, like "Pushed 1", is never colored), the four attribute words, the Weapon, Power and Signature Modifiers (not Weapon as gear or as a trait), Bane, Boon and Rally Point with their number, the states that tilt a roll or damage (**red**: Doomed, Vulnerable, Down; **blue**: Favored, Empowered), costs (`1 AP`, `(1A)`, `1R`: the color of the cost chips), a movement with its value (`Evade 2`, `Pushed 1`), and `[HT: ...]` (the Bane red). The colors are CSS variables in `rules.css` (`--c-body`, `--c-mind`, `--c-instinct`, `--c-appeal`, `--c-wmod`, `--c-pmod`, `--c-smod`, `--c-bane`, `--c-boon`, `--c-rally`, `--c-ap`, `--c-mv`): very dark on the light page, lighter on the dark one. **Write the Body tag on Body damage ([B] 1[W] + [Mod]); there is no default pool. Write [Strike] Action, [Melee] Actions (a trait in brackets) when you mean the trait. Bane and Boon are capitalized.**
- **Figures side by side** (`diagrams.py` `wrap`): the diagrams of a row share one height, so their captions start on the same line. Colors in the Straight Line figures: blue = squares passed, green = where the movement ends, red = not legal.
- **Trait groups:** a card lists its traits by group, in this order: **Roll** (Appeal, Body, Instinct, Mind, Power, Save, Signature, Split, Weapon), **Requires** (Firearm, Handle, Melee, Projectile, Ranged; Shot and Gun were renamed Projectile and Firearm on 2026-11-16: the old card files keep the keys `shot` / `gun` until the Foundry pass and `cards.py` prints the new names), **Kind** (Area, Move, Self, Strike), **Damage** (Nonlethal), **Keywords** (Divine, Focus, Fear and any trait with no rule of its own), alphabetical inside a group; each trait is a plain word in its group's color (`--g-roll`, `--g-requires`, `--g-kind`, `--g-damage`, `--g-keywords`), separated by a middle dot (no chips, no group labels since 2026-11-16). A new trait goes in `GROUPS` in `build_rules.py`. **Weapon properties (Barehanded, Long, Reload, Two-handed...) are not traits and have no color.**
- **The class style:** a chapter file whose first lines have `<!-- style: class -->` has the book page look (small-caps headings with an ornament, a drop cap, the stat box, the level list) and its Action cards **open and close** (closed at first; the Contents has "Open all cards" and "Close all cards"). Used by `12-paladin.md` and `13-archer.md`. The HTML pieces of the page (`.intro`, `.statblock`, `.levels`) are written in the Markdown: copy them from `12-paladin.md`.
- Each document has its own Contents, a bar to jump between the documents, and an Open Items appendix when it has open (to be decided) boxes.

## Build and look things up
Run from the folder that holds `rules-src/` (it also needs `build_rules.py`, `rules_tools.py`, `rules.css`, `rules-assets/` and `diagrams/`).

- `python3 build_rules.py` builds the six documents, rewrites `INDEX.md` and prints the checks. `python3 build_rules.py /tmp/test/core-rules.html` builds them in another folder (Core at that path, the others beside it).
- `python3 build_rules.py --where Engaged` lists every mention of a term (several terms may be given): its heading, where it is defined in bold, its glossary entry, and each line that uses it with the section it is in. **Run it before changing a rule**, to see every place that must change.
- `INDEX.md` lists each chapter's sections with line numbers, its Action cards, its open (to be decided) boxes and the glossary entries. Use it to jump to a place without reading the file.
- The checks never stop the build. They warn about: broken `[[links]]`, headings with the same title in different files, links that land on a repeated title, glossary entries no chapter mentions, rule headings without a glossary entry (many are fine), unknown callout kinds, Action cards with the same name or without a Frequency, and placeholders left in the built book.

## A chapter file
```
<!-- part: Part I: Core Rules -->
# Chapter 3: Movement and the Environment

## A section
### A subsection
```
The first line says which Part it belongs to. Headings (`##`, `###`) are the targets of cross-references and appear in the table of contents.

## Cross-references
- `[[Heading title]]` links to the heading with that title; `[[Heading title|label]]` shows another label. The match ignores case and punctuation, and a title that only starts the same also works.
- If two headings have the same title, the link goes to the **first** one in the book. Use a different title for a heading you need to link to.
- Inside a table, a pipe in a link must be written `\|`: `[[Areas\|**Area**]]`.
- **Don't repeat a rule that is already in place (Lucas, 2026-11-08).** Say it once, where it belongs; elsewhere add "(see [[Chapter N]])" only when it helps. No reminders of why a rule doesn't apply, and no lists of exceptions that could limit future Actions.
- **Named Boons and Banes (2026-11-08):** the name is part of the text, never deduced: an Action gives a Field, Pressure or Support Boon or Bane only when it writes the name ("1 Pressure Bane"), and then it always is one. Give a name only when several sources must share a limit: a Bane an effect puts on enemies is **Pressure**, a Boon an effect gives several allies at once (an aura, a zone, "allies within N") is **Support**, one from position or sight is **Field**. An unnamed Boon or Bane is 1, rarely 2, never "1 for each X" (anything that scales gets a name, so it gets a limit). The rule: Chapter 1, Named Boons and Banes.

## Boxes
```
> [!example]
> A goblin stands directly below you and you Push them 3. ...
```
`[!example]` is an example, `[!note]` a designer note, and `[!tbd]` an open question: every `[!tbd]` box is collected in the appendix "Open Items" under the heading it is in, so write what is missing in one sentence.

## Placeholders and automatic coloring
- `{{dt:Holy}}` is a damage type with its icon and color: Acid, Blunt, Cold, Electricity, Fire, Force, Holy, Pierce, Psychic, Slash, Thunder, Unholy.
- `{{dc:Ho}}` is a two-letter code in the type's color (Ac, Bl, Co, El, Fi, Fo, Ho, Pi, Ps, Sl, Th, Un). A code written after a number or a `]` ("1d6 Ho", "[Mod] Bl") is colored by itself.
- `{{pool:V}}` and `{{pool:R}}` are the heart (Vigor) and the brain (Resolve); `{{pool:B}}` / `{{pool:M}}` still work.
- `@@CARD:Name@@` places a card of `book/cards/` (see "An Action card" below). `@@SAMPLE_CARD@@` is drawn by the builder. `@@DIAGRAM:name@@` puts a diagram in the text: cover, leaning, flanking, engaged, reach, emanation, light, burst, straight (charge), line1 to line3 (the Line area), difficult, push, senses (the four scenes of the two questions), movingaway and the rest. They are drawn by `diagrams/diagrams.py` (the names are listed in `book_diagram` there); a new one needs a drawing function and a name. Every number in them is computed from the rule. `python3 diagrams/build_diagrams_draft.py` builds a page with all of them to look at. The builder needs the `diagrams/` folder beside it.
- Trait words (Strike, Weapon, Melee, Ranged, Move...) and damage type names are colored by the builder wherever they appear: write them as plain words.
- Notation in the text: `[V]` and `[R]` (Vigor and Resolve damage; pack R-core, 2026-11-04: they were `[B]` and `[M]`, which the builder, Foundry and the ruler still read until R-Foundry's second version: write the new ones), `[Mod]` (Modifier), `[W]` and `1[W]` (weapon damage). Vigor and Resolve are the Health thresholds, never the Body and Mind Attributes.

## An Action card
**The cards are not written in the chapters:** a chapter writes `@@CARD:Weapon Attack@@` where a card goes and the card lives in `book/cards/` (the format: `book/cards/README.md`). **Since 2026-11-16 the builder draws every card in the Structure** (`book/cards/card-structure.md`, "THE STRUCTURE, WHOLE"), straight from the card's data (`cards.py structure()`, `build_rules.py card()`); the old Markdown block (`<div class="action">`) is no longer read, and the build warns when a chapter writes one. A card of `book/cards/stage0/*.json` (written in the Structure) takes the place of the old card of the same name; every other card is adapted (same words, new places: `book/cards/README.md`, "The Structure"). The build's card check lists a marker with no card and a card no chapter places.

A card, top to bottom (a part the card doesn't have is not printed):
1. **The bar:** the name; at the right "Escalation N" (when it needs one) · the Frequency · the cost ("2 AP", "1 or 2 AP", "1, 2 or 3 AP", "1R", or "Passive" for a card with no cost). Its color is the Frequency's: At-will green, Turn teal, Round blue, Encounter red, Daily purple, none grey. **The category and the level are not printed** (they stay in the data and in `INDEX.md`).
2. **The Flavor** (italic).
3. **The traits,** one line of plain words in the color of their group.
4. **The grid,** one row: "Distance: ..." and "Area: ..." at the left, "Target: ..." at the right (two lines on a phone).
5. **The labeled lines, in play order:** Requirement · Interrupt / Trigger · Setup · a defined word · Effect · Attack / Save · Tier 1 / Tier 2 / Tier 3 (or "Tier 1 or 2") · Critical · Effect · Special · Passive. The roll line is "Attack: Weapon vs. Armor Defense" or "Save: Appeal".
6. **Blocks,** one per Version or Mode: a heading (the cost, or the Mode's name with the traits only it has), its own Distance / Target row when it has one, its labeled lines. What the blocks share comes before them.

In a class-style chapter the card opens and closes (the bar is the `summary`). Every card is an anchor (`card-weapon-attack`), so `[[Weapon Attack]]` links to it. `@@SAMPLE_CARD@@` (Chapter 1, "Reading an Action") draws two of the book's cards with numbered markers and no legend (the chapter's text explains the numbers): Smite with 1 the name, 2 Escalation · Frequency · cost, 3 the Flavor, 4 the traits, 5 Distance and Area, 6 the Target, 7 the roll line, 8 the Tiers, 9 another labeled line (its Special); Lay On Hands with 10 on a block's heading (`SAMPLE` and `sample_card` in build_rules.py).

## The glossary
Each row is `| [[Heading\|**Term**]] | Meaning in one sentence. |` and its link goes to where the rule is written. **Every new term gets a glossary entry**, and a rule that changes updates its entry too (`--where Term` shows the entry).

## For each change
1. `--where Term` for every term the change touches.
2. Edit the chapter files (small replacements, not rewrites).
3. Rebuild and read the checks.
4. Update the glossary and the notes of what is open (`rules-backlog.md`).
