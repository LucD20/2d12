Gear

Weapons

Your class determines your Weapon Modifier. Each weapon belongs to a Weapon Group and has its own Weapon Technique, which you unlock with Mastery. A Weapon Technique can only be used with the weapon it belongs to, while you wield it: its Weapon Actions use that weapon, never another one.

Example

A Paladin with a Weapon Modifier of +2 deals 5 + 1d6! + 2 Slashing damage on Tier 2 with a Greatsword Weapon Attack: 8 to 13.

Weapon Damage [W]

[W] is the damage of your weapon: on each Tier, a flat number plus at most one small die. A weapon's die explodes, so it is written with ! (see Exploding Dice): on its highest face, count it as one less and roll it again. An Action that deals 1[W] adds one instance of the weapon's damage. 2[W] is two instances: the flat number twice and two dice. A weapon's damage has its own type, written in its table ([V] for every weapon so far): 1[W] deals that type, whatever the rest of the Action deals. Later weapons, such as wands and staves, may deal Resolve damage or both.

Example: an Action that deals 2[W] deals 0 / 10 + 2d6! / 16 + 2d8! with a Greatsword, and 0 / 4 + 2d4! / 8 + 2d4! with a Crossbow.

Reading the Weapon Tables

  • Group: its Weapon Group.
  • Range: in squares. Melee weapons have a Range too: 1 for most of them. For a ranged weapon, the first number is the normal range, the second the far range (1 Bane) and the third the maximum range (2 Banes).
  • Damage: Tier 1 / Tier 2 / Tier 3, all [V]. Tier 1 is 0 for every weapon. A Thrown weapon's damage is for the weapon in hand.
  • Mastery: the Mastery its Weapon Technique needs: 3 for common weapons, 4 for specialist ones, 5 for exotic ones.

A melee weapon deals more than a ranged one, which strikes from safety. Among melee weapons, one that takes both hands deals the most and one that leaves a hand free less: what the weapon can do pays for the difference. The die tells weapons apart: a d4 on a big flat number is steady (Hammers, Crossbows, the Longbow), a d8 swings (Axes, Firearms), a d6 is in the middle.

Hands

You have two hands: your main hand and your off hand. Which hand holds a weapon changes nothing in the rules, unless a feature says so. Each weapon you hold uses one or two hands. A weapon with the Two-handed property must be held with both hands: holding it with one hand gives 3 Banes on [Strike, Weapon] Actions with that weapon. A weapon or your Fists in the other hand don't take them.

You need a hand free to use Handle Actions. A creature with both hands full has to Release something first (0 AP: drop an object, or let go of it with one hand), and can Regrip (1 AP) to hold a weapon with both hands again, move a weapon to the other hand or swap the weapons in your hands (Regrip doesn't need a free hand).

Properties

Weapons have properties instead of traits: Melee, Ranged, Long, Projectile, Firearm, Thrown, Reload, Two-handed, Barehanded and Non-threatening. Every weapon has the property of its kind: a melee weapon has Melee, a ranged weapon has Ranged.

An Action with the Weapon trait and the Melee or Ranged trait needs a weapon with that property. A Weapon Action without them takes the property of the weapon you use.

Barehanded

A Barehanded weapon can't be disarmed and doesn't occupy a hand (or a similar appendage), but you must still have a free hand to use it. Fists are Barehanded. A hidden blade, a Knuckle Dagger or a spiked buckle could be too.

Firearm

A Firearm shoots with powder, such as a pistol or a musket. It is not a Projectile weapon: Shot Actions can't use it. Gun Actions need a Firearm, and a Weapon Action with a Firearm is a [Gun] Action.

Long

A weapon with the Long property is made to keep enemies at a distance: you take 1 Field Bane on Weapon Actions with it against adjacent creatures, and it doesn't threaten them (see Flanking). A Pike has Range 3 and Long.

Projectile

A Projectile weapon shoots something, such as a bow or a crossbow, instead of being swung or thrown. Shot Actions need a Projectile weapon.

Thrown

A weapon with the Thrown property can be used in hand or thrown, and it has a Range for each, written like "Range 1, Thrown 4 / 8 / 12". Used in hand, a Weapon Action with it is a Melee Action; thrown, it is a Ranged Action, uses the thrown Range and deals 1 less damage on Tiers 2 and 3 (a thrown Dagger: 0 / 1 + 1d4 / 3 + 1d4). A thrown weapon leaves your hand.

Non-threatening

A weapon with the Non-threatening property doesn't threaten: you can't flank with it (see Flanking), unless a feature says otherwise. Fists have it.

Reload

A weapon with the Reload property doesn't need to be reloaded every time you use it: you can reload quickly between shots. But you can make a more powerful shot that does require a reload.

Before you roll a Weapon Strike with the weapon, you can choose to make a Reload Shot: the Strike is Favored and Empowered. After the shot, you must Reload before you can use that weapon again. See the Reload Action in Chapter 6: Basic Actions.

Weapon Groups

Every weapon belongs to one of eight Weapon Groups: Blades, Axes, Hammers, Polearms, Bows, Crossbows, Firearms and Unarmed (Fists and the Knuckle Dagger). Weapons of a group are used the same way, so what you learn with one helps with the others: see Mastery.

Mastery

Anyone can use any weapon; you get better with the ones you use. In an encounter, when you get a Tier 3 on a Weapon Action with a weapon, you gain 1 Mastery with that kind of weapon. You gain at most 1 Mastery per Weapon Group in each encounter: the first Tier 3 with a weapon of a group gives it, and no weapon of that group gains more until the next encounter (a weapon of another group still can).

Mastery belongs to you, not to the weapon you hold: it is for a kind of weapon (a Greatsword, a Crossbow), so a better or different Greatsword uses the same Mastery, and you keep it when you lose the weapon.

Your Mastery with a weapon is at least a third of your highest Mastery with a weapon of its group (rounded down). The Mastery you gain with it adds to that.

Example

Zack has 15 Mastery with the Greatsword. His Mastery with a Longsword and a Dagger, both Blades, is at least 5, so their Weapon Techniques are unlocked. When he gets his first Tier 3 with the Longsword in an encounter, it has 6 Mastery.

Fists

Every creature counts as having Fists. They are a weapon like any other, with the properties Barehanded, Melee and Non-threatening, in the Unarmed group. While you have a free hand, your Fists count as a weapon you are wielding, alongside any other weapon you hold: a Weapon Action can use them. Fists have no Weapon Technique: Unarmed Talents give their Actions.

Armor

The armor a creature wears gives its Armor value, and its Armor Defense is 11 + that value (see Defenses).

Armor Armor value Armor Defense
Unarmored 0 11
Light 1 12
Medium 2 13
Heavy 3 14

Armor Training

Each class has an Armor Training: Light, Medium or Heavy. Each one includes the lighter ones (Heavy covers Medium and Light), and every creature is trained in Unarmored. The Paladin has Heavy and the Archer Medium.

If you wear armor you are not trained in, all your Rolls are Doomed, and Actions that roll against your Armor Defense or Ward Defense are Favored.
