# Talents: the design record

> **Status (2026-11-20): CURRENT, the Talents record (T1 to T17 and the lessons; section 9 is the restart for the playtest PCs). Its summary is `decided.md` section 4; the current cards are `cards/talents-playtest.md`.**

**Opened 2026-11-19.** Lucas: "Talents are the meat and likely the most important part. Research has to be thorough [...] Let's prepare as well as we can. Start." **Status: preparation. Nothing here is decided and nothing is in the book.** This file is the record of the Talents' design, the way `book/magic-design.md` is magic's: his words, what is already fixed, everything that waits on Talents, the research, the problems, the steps, then each decision as it is taken.

---

## 1. Lucas's words

- **2026-11-19, opening this piece:** "The talents is going to be the actual meat of the game, so far we have been doing the skeleton. Talents is going to be the main thing, how things work, how all of it comes together. How a diplomatic paladin differs from a quiet, ranged paladin, with similar strengths and capabilities. I don't want to fall into the trap of skill feats from 2e, or skill tax from 1e and 3.5, or competing talents like edges from Swade (why nobody really gets social edges instead of combat edges)."
- **2026-11-19:** "Talents are the meat and likely the most important part. Research has to be thorough, as many systems that uses this type of mechanic as possible: savage worlds, Star Wars (fantasy grounds) etc."
- **2026-11-15, the class redesign** (`class-charter.md`, rule 11): "the build is more Talents than class".
- **2026-11-18, magic** (magic record, decision 44): the base rule carries the limit, and a Talent is where a character grows past it. "Remember, progression is horizontal."
- **2026-11-18, magic** (decision 49): the classes got hard limits because they are beginner classes, "to start out"; "talents and such, will likely be 'rules altering' like spells as well in the long run."
- **2026-11-09, the structure question:** categories for each level (today's rule) or Talent points, "with the fear of powergamers hoarding".
- **2026-11-07, roleplay** (`roleplay-skills.md`): "it is a Roleplaying game, so this is a big part of the system." His dislikes there: Draw Steel's over-the-top out-of-combat features, Pathfinder 2e's skill feats.
- **2026-11-19, skills:** the system has no "trained" tick, "it was just modifiers"; the Attribute a skill uses can't be locked ("You want to intimidate with strength sometimes").

### His three traps, as tests for every Talent and for the structure

| The trap | From | The test |
|---|---|---|
| **Permission** | Pathfinder 2e's skill feats | Does this Talent grant leave to try something anyone should be able to try? |
| **Tax** | D&D 3.5 and Pathfinder 1e | Is this Talent paid only to keep a number usable, or to reach the one that was wanted? |
| **Competition** | Savage Worlds' Edges | Does a pick ever set a combat option against a non-combat one? |

### The system's standing tests (CLAUDE.md)

Fun, engaging, tactical, **the least ambiguity possible**; growth is **horizontal** (a level gives options, never a number on a roll or a DC); complex is fine, intuitive is required.

## 2. What is already fixed

### In the rulebook (`rules-src/05-characters.md`)

- **The level list's slots.** Level 1: a Skill Talent. Level 2: a Talent. Level 3: a Skill Talent. Level 4: a Stage Talent (and at the first level of Stages 2 and 3). **Every slot is empty:** "The Talents are set aside for now."
- "A Talent is a feature any character can take, apart from their class."
- "Levels give options; Stages change the numbers." Attributes don't grow with level or Stage. A Modifier is never above +4.
- **Swapping on a Rest:** every choice of the level list, Talents included, can be changed on a Rest for one that could have been made at that level.
- Effects of the same kind don't stack.

### In the set-aside chapter (`rules-src/set-aside/16-talents.md`; out of the book since the class redesign)

- A Talent has **a category** (Combat, Mobility, Magic, Skill), **a level** and sometimes **Prerequisites** (a Talent, a level, "an Action with a trait you have", an Attribute). Chains existed (Brawler, then Kickboxer).
- **"Talents That Modify Actions":** a Talent names the Actions it changes by their traits (Heavy Blows: "[Melee, Strike, Weapon] Actions with a Two-handed weapon").
- Twenty cards: twelve Combat, two Mobility, four Magic (all Divine), Class Expertise. Deprecated with the old classes; their prices and reasons are in `../archive/book-drafts/options-talents.md`.

### In the class charter (`class-charter.md`)

- A class is a fixed shape (about 18 cards at Stage 0): 2 Features, Hallmark cards, Arts, Escalation abilities, Picks, the Apex.
- **Rule 11:** a card that counts "your Actions" names a trait, never all of them, because the build is more Talents than class. The Paladin's glue is [Divine]; "a later Talent can carry the trait to work with the class's cards".

### The earlier proposal (rules-backlog, "TALENT STRUCTURE: PROPOSAL", 2026-11-09; never answered)

Two tracks that never compete (a Talent and a Skill pick each level; Skill Talents never give combat numbers); Minor and Major Talents (a level's Talent is one Major or two Minor); ranks I to III; Stage Talents as milestones; retraining one pick at each level-up; to avoid: one currency for skill and combat, trees with filler. A short research pass stood behind it.

### The frame of play outside a fight (magic record, decisions 113 to 118; 2026-11-19)

- Anyone can attempt anything plausible; a skill makes you better and never grants permission (`roleplay-skills.md`, proposals 1 and 2).
- **Routine:** if 11 + your total skill modifier is at least the DC, with no threat and no time pressure, you do it without rolling. No "trained" tick.
- One guide for a failed Check. No help Action outside a fight; in a fight, help gives 1 Support Boon.
- Five kinds of outcome belong to a Check: getting past, swaying people, finding out, enduring and travelling, making and mending.
- **For the Skills pass:** how a skill's number is made (his five directions) and that its Attribute can't be locked.

### Magic's side (magic record)

- A Magic Talent or a class **opens** a Path and a School (27). Talents grant spells and change spells by trait; **they never give ranks** (17).
- Paths and Schools are traits a Talent can key on ("your [Fire] spells...", "your [Evocation] spells...").
- Every PC has Surge; only spells spend it for now; the classes get their uses "at their next pass" (68).

## 5. The problems to design against

### Lucas's three traps, with their causes on record

| Trap | Its causes, by the research | What 2d12 already has against it |
|---|---|---|
| **Permission** | The sentence "anyone can try; this makes you better" not printed on the pick; **a list so long each pick needs ground, taken from the base rule**; a GM made to remember a penalty for everyone without the pick | `roleplay-skills.md` proposal 1 (the sentence Pathfinder didn't print); Routine is a base rule, not a Talent |
| **Tax** | A pick that restores a number the math assumes; a rung used only to reach the next; a pick everyone of a kind takes first; buying back a penalty; a forced order of upgrades | Flat numbers, the +4 cap, no "trained" tick, no rising DCs |
| **Competition** | One budget, and the fight is where you die; **the non-combat pick is a number while the combat pick is an action**; the skill alone already does the job; the circumstance never comes up; the leak: actions usable in a fight in the non-combat slot | Typed slots in the level list; separate spell picks (magic, 120) |

### Four more, from the research

- **Memory.** Conditional and off-turn triggers are what players forget; a wider sheet nobody can remember is the risk of horizontal growth. The class charter already says "no silent bonuses, no tracking across turns".
- **Filler.** More picks than good ideas, a fixed number of boxes, a single node everybody races to. "We don't think it's humanly possible to have 40-50 fun, interesting and balanced talents in a tree" (Street).
- **Dominated options.** Two picks that can be put on one scale; one of them wins.
- **Sameness.** Two characters of a class end up alike (Genesys's recorded worry). Lucas's own test: a diplomatic Paladin against a quiet, ranged one.

### Conflicts already on file

- **The set-aside chapter is Savage Worlds' Edge structure almost item for item** (categories, a level, Prerequisites, chains); its example Heavy Blows is a number.
- **Retraining:** any Rest (Chapter 5) against one swap at each level-up (the 2026-11-09 proposal).
- **"Skill Talents never give combat numbers"** (the proposal) is narrower than the leak the research found (actions used in a fight).
- **"No certainty"** (`roleplay-skills.md`) against the research's finding that loved non-combat options are reliable (Routine is one answer already taken).

## 7. Decisions

| # | Decided | Lucas's words |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | **What a Talent may contain: never a bare number.** A Talent adds a verb (a new Action, or a new way to use one), changes a rule, or lifts a limit of the base rules. A number may appear only as part of a verb (a new Action's damage), never as "+1 to X". **A fallback, not the default:** a Boon in a very circumstantial situation ("a boon when haggling") may be allowed from time to time, only if Talents without it don't hold up in play; he would rather not. | "A, but I think it can grant boons to very circumstantial situations from time to time, though I'd like it not to. This is just an idea in case Talents without it doesn't really hold up, like a boon when haggling." |
| T2 | **The budgets: a wall one way, kept with B in mind.** A Skill Talent gives nothing usable during an encounter: no Action, no Reaction, no Boon, no Guard, no condition. It may change what happens before a fight through the preparation rules (`items-economy.md` Part 2, items 15 and 16: the circumstances, never the numbers). A combat Talent may also have uses outside a fight, **but those uses follow the same principle: nothing simply lets you bypass.** His examples of what to avoid, from Pathfinder 2e: the Animist's Garden of Healing "simply broken out of combat and trivializes it"; Treat Condition, which "makes conditions not last anything out of combat, since each attempt takes actions (so less than 6 seconds) and has no limit, you can attempt non stop". **So no out-of-combat use may be repeatable without limit,** and a cost meant to last (a condition, damage, a Wound) is never made to vanish by quick, free attempts. Research: `research/bypass-and-repeat.md` (its 8-line checklist is T2's test). | "A with B aware. Same principle as before: Things shouldn't simply allow you to bypass." |
| T3 | **The class fixes the role; Talents decide the style; any character can take any Talent.** No Talent is written for one class. A Talent keyed on a trait reaches the class cards that carry it (class charter rule 11: one keyed on [Divine] works with a Paladin's cards). A class's own cards are never replaced by a Talent. **Lucas's plan for the classes, beside it:** a class has **its own progression at each Stage**, "Select X upgrades" from a list of upgrades to its own cards. His skeleton examples, nothing set: "Smite's dice; Smite deals some area damage; Smite gains a new 2AP version; Judge can apply to two targets". So a class grows by upgrades to its own cards, and Talents are the part shared by everyone. | "A for sure. I don't want Talents for each class. I plan classes to have progression per stage on their own (like 'Select X upgrades: Smite's dice; Smite deals some area damage; Smite gains a new 2AP version; Judge can apply to two targets etc. Nothing set, these are simple, skeleton examples)." |
| T4 | **The size of a pick: one size, with up to three ranks.** Every Talent pick is the same size. A Talent card may hold up to two further ranks, each a new rule written on the same card; taking a rank is a pick, and rank II needs rank I. **Guards, from the research:** every rank is worth a pick on its own (never only a step to the next: 13th Age 2e removed forced order for that reason); no rank is a bigger number (T1). It fits the sequences already on file (the summoner's chain; the counterspell tree: recognize, weaken, cancel). The 2026-11-09 proposal's Minor and Major sizes are not taken. | "A" |
| T5 | **How Talents are gated and found: named groups, gated only by Stage and by what they work on.** Talents sit in small groups named for a way of playing (examples: Archery, Diplomacy, Shadows, Faith), perhaps five to eight in each, so a player reads a short list for their idea. A Talent may require only a minimum Stage and the trait it works on (a Talent that changes your [Divine] cards needs you to have one). **No Talent requires another, except its own ranks** (T4 holds the sequences). A group is a place to look, not a cage: any character takes from any group (T3). Not taken: drawn trees, one sorted list. | "A is good." |
| T6 | **The schedule: alternate, one pick a level.** A Skill Talent on odd levels and a Talent on even levels (the pattern levels 1 to 3 already have, continued); the Stage Talent at level 4 becomes an ordinary Talent, and reaching a Stage opens the Talents that require it. By level 15: eight Skill Talents and seven Talents, plus the class's upgrades (T3). A rank counts as a pick of its kind (T4). **Fallback:** both kinds every level (B), if playtests show the growth to be too little. **His standing aim, with it: "It is very important we try to make all talents appealing, so people just don't go for the 'Whirlwind' (likely high stage) to hit everyone or similar."** No Talent is to be the obvious pick of its Stage: the must-have test of `research/talents.md` section 6, held against every Talent. | "A with a chance of B in case play test proves it to be little." |

## 8. Review after T6 (2026-11-19; Lucas: "Just to make sure all is going well: review what we have done so far, see if you are taking all into consideration, including the magic brainstorm")

Claude reread T1 to T6 against the magic record, `magic-out-of-combat.md`, `roleplay-skills.md`, `items-economy.md`, `ideas/expedition-loop.md`, the class charter and Chapter 5. No independent reader this time. **Nothing below is decided.**

### Holds up

- **T1 matches magic's own rule** (decision 18: a rank is never a number; 17: Talents never give ranks) and Chapter 5's "levels give options, Stages change the numbers".
- **T2 matches the frame of play outside a fight** (113 to 118) and the bypass checklist (`research/bypass-and-repeat.md`).
- **T3 fits magic's opening rule:** a Magic Talent opens a Path and a School (27) like any other Talent, for any class; a caster class starts with one open (21).
- **T4's ranks hold the magic seeds that were described as trees** (the counterspell tree; the summoner's chain): they become one Talent with ranks, as T5 allows no Talent to require another.
- **T5's trait gate is the one magic already needs** (a Talent on "[Fire] spells" requires a [Fire] spell).
- **Paired spell picks (120) don't touch the Talent schedule:** opening magic gives two battle spells and two spells outside a fight.

### Problems found

1. **Skill numbers have no home (the largest).** T1 forbids a Talent to raise a number, so a Skill Talent can't raise a skill. But four of Lucas's five directions for skills use skill points, and the 2026-11-09 proposal let a Skill pick be "a skill rank or a Skill Talent". **If one slot offers either a skill point or a Skill Talent, it is 5e's feat against +2, the swap the research found the number always wins** (`research/talents.md`, section 2). So skill points need their own source, apart from the Skill Talent slots. For the Skills pass, but it bounds what a Skill Talent can be now.
2. **(Answered, Lucas, 2026-11-19: a lower cost is not a changed rule allowed by T1, "Not really, no"; those seeds are out as written.)** **Some magic seeds change a cost or a die:** a cheaper Sustain (29), a safer Overreach with a smaller die (30), lifting armor's Surge cost (80). Is a lower price a "bare number" under T1? Claude's reading: a cost changed is a rule changed, allowed; but each is a candidate must-have for every caster (T6's aim), so each needs the must-have test when written.
3. **Two seeds sit near T2's wall:** Sudden Strike (an Appeal Check in a conversation to Surprise: `items-economy.md` Part 2, item 3) changes how a fight starts; haggling "sell for +X" (`items-economy.md` open list) is a number, Lucas's own fallback example. Sudden Strike reads as a combat Talent (it decides a fight's first round), haggling as the T1 fallback if ever needed.
4. **"A skill's outcome by magic" (114, his lockpicking with the Power Modifier)** works outside a fight, so it belongs in the Skill Talent slot, not the Talent slot; otherwise a caster pays a combat pick for it.
5. **The classes' Surge uses (68)** were assigned to "their next pass": T3's class upgrades by Stage are the natural home (a rider chosen at the moment of the hit), not Talents. Worth saying so.
6. **A caster's sheet is the heaviest:** seven Talents and the class's upgrades, plus two spells at opening and a pair at each new rank in each Path and School (two battle, two outside). Step 8 (the memory budget) must count a caster, not only a martial.
7. **Retraining has two answers on file** (any Rest, Chapter 5; one at a level-up, the 2026-11-09 proposal): step 7, next.
8. **T6 and the high Stage.** T5 gates by Stage, and Lucas fears the high-Stage pick everyone takes ("Whirlwind to hit everyone"). The research's cause: later picks priced as "bigger", not "different". Pricing (step 11) must keep a high-Stage Talent sideways.
9. **The set-aside chapter says Stage Talents at levels 5 and 8;** Chapter 5 says 4, 8, 12. Moot after T6, but the set-aside file should not be brought back as written.
10. **The skeleton files still disagree on the skill count** (8 to 12 in `roleplay-skills.md`, 16 in MASTER-PLAN): for the Skills pass.

**Lessons from Lucas's review of the first branches (2026-11-20; `cards/talents-first-pass.md` section 8), standing for every Talent:**
- **No shutting down a boss.** "We don't want a Disruptive Stance (Pf2e) situation [...] people shutting down enemy bosses." An effect that disrupts or stops an enemy must not land often enough to lock a major enemy down (a Tier 3 is a quarter of attacks).
- **A rare trigger can't carry a whole rank.** A Critical (about 4%) is too rare to be worth a pick: such an effect sits inside a rank that does something else as well.
- **Use the rules' own reach words.** "Threatened" over "adjacent" where a Long weapon should work.
- **Stopping all of a creature's Reactions by moving is too strong; a trigger that rarely happens, or a rider too small, is too weak.**

**Lucas's direction, 2026-11-20 (after `research/famous-picks.md`; the questions it raises are being asked):** "an error of my design mind was trying to go around penalties like ranged while engaged and such, what we can do is go around them with actions, so it isn't just a flat bonus like the problems we found." **A penalty or a cost is lifted by an Action you spend, not by a passive.** His examples: **Point Blank Shot,** "1A ranged attack while engaged that isn't Doomed"; an Action, "1A Encounter, that your next Overcast costs less Surge". The price is paid in AP and a Frequency, in the moment. Its nearest printed relative: Pathfinder 2e's Power Attack, a separate Strike in place of a passive trade (`research/famous-picks.md`).

**Why the batch kept failing (the review Lucas agreed with, "Yeah"):**
1. **Nothing was priced.** The yardsticks exist (`ruler/pc-budget.md`: a turn of three Weapon Attacks = 100 points; Chapter 14's rider prices: Mild 3, Moderate 8, Severe 12, Extreme 18; `ruler/catalogue/talents.txt`), and the CLAUDE.md analysis format asks for them, but every number was "[not priced]" and the audits were told not to judge numbers. Power was judged only by Lucas, by feel, each round.
2. **Every lesson was a ceiling, none a floor** (the boss rule, no rare triggers, T14, no overlap), so each fix landed weaker: most of his notes were "too weak".
3. **Discounts stacked:** a trigger that comes rarely, a Tier 3 (about 27%), and Minor enemies only.
4. **The wrong comparison:** a rank was checked against its own Rank I and the class cards for overlap, never against what else the pick could buy (a weapon Technique already gives Evade 1 with a shot).
5. **Too many at once:** nine Talents in nine families, all revised every round, so none became a reference.

**The way it goes now (Claude's proposal, agreed in outline):** an anchor first (one rank = N points of the 100-point turn, in the situation it is built for; measured in the SIM, N set by Lucas); every rank priced in the analysis format (Chapter 14's prices × the Tier odds × how often its trigger comes); floors in the lessons as well as ceilings; one Talent calibrated first and used as the reference; the audits judge value too.

**Numbering (Lucas, 2026-11-20):** every list of cards and branches is numbered **Talent.Branch.Rank** so he can reference it (0 for a Talent's own Rank I).
