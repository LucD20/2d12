### 1.1 Mearls on boss fights
- **VERIFIED.** Wargamer, "Former DnD designer outlines why boss fights suck in fifth edition", Mollie Russell, updated Oct 9, 2025. https://www.wargamer.com/dnd/mike-mearls-boss-fight . Mearls's words as quoted there (from a Bluesky thread of Oct 8, 2025):
  - "The game was built around the assumption that characters would engage in about 20 rounds of combat between long rests".
  - "In 4e, we assumed a certain number of encounters per day"; "In each encounter, we assumed you used encounter powers, then at-will. We spread out the extra damage from daily powers across the day."
  - "We did the same thing in 5e, and time has shown that it does not work for boss monsters"; "Why? The gap between daily and at-will damage is far bigger in 5e than in 4e. Spreading that extra damage across every round of a day misses the real benefit of daily abilities - a massive damage spike."
  - "A party that unloads with their best powers simply breaks the system"; "Their output is off the scale when you assume that everyone fights for 20+ rounds per day and spreads their at-will powers out." "It's quite likely that a semi-optimized party can vaporize boss monsters in a round or even less."
  - His fix: "to design boss monsters with a different hit point scale, one that assumes the party is alpha striking"; "but I think it's a viable path to introducing boss monsters that can stick around". (So report F's "accept the spike and build bosses that survive it" is VERIFIED.)
  - The article's own gloss: 5e fights "are expected to last three to four rounds".
- **VERIFIED, but not on Wargamer:** "We were probably off by a factor of 5." ### 1.2 EN World, "Structural Flaw of the D&D Combat System"
- **VERIFIED.** The words are the thread's opening post: M_Natas, Feb 27, 2023, post #1. https://www.enworld.org/threads/structural-flaw-of-the-d-d-combat-system.696124/ (the report's post id 8951224 is a page-2 reply that quotes it).
  - "I've noticed a structural flaw in D&D combat that detracts from the excitement: the suspense curve is inverted. The optimal strategy for a D&D fight is to inflict as much damage as possible early on to quickly kill the monster(s), and the rules make it very easy to do so."
  - "This leads to front-loaded (boss) fights where all the big cool stuff happens at the beginning, and if the fight drags on, it devolves into a slugfest where the characters spam cantrips and make normal weapon attacks. The dragon, for example, often gets killed by a normal melee attack or an eldritch blast rather than the lightning bolt or the fighter's eight attacks with their action surge."
  - His proposal: charges gained on plain turns, spent on round 3, "so you don't start the fight with an explosion but end it in one."
- Escalation die as the closest fix: **VERIFIED.** mamba, post #2: "the closest thing to this I am aware of is the escalation die from 13th Age." James Gasik, post #4, names it too.
- Useful objections in the same thread (new):
  - mamba, #2: "I believe this is only more 'cinematic' if the PCs then kill the enemy in their charged-up round and not two rounds after that when they are slugging it out again, waiting for their superpowers to recharge."
  - James Gasik, #4: "Back loading PC damage will result in PC's taking more damage from monsters; this will have impacts on how many encounters PC's can handle per game day, as well as how challenging individual encounters are." He adds an idea: "having abilities that only come online when players are below 50% health (4e's "bloodied" condition). But this would necessitate making healing better too".
  - MarkB, #18 (page 2): legendary resistances already make experienced parties "hold off on using their really big guns until after those resistances are gone"; he suggests wearing down the target's defenses "rather than introducing charge-up mechanics for the PCs", so that "paladins may be tempted to hold off on some of their smites".

### 1.3 Xanthir, opening with the strongest move
- **VERIFIED.** "Avoiding Novas, or, Encouraging Dramatic Escalation in D&D", Tab Atkins-Bittner, Jan 3, 2018. https://www.xanthir.com/b4sE0
  - "in stories, combat has a dramatic arc, with an opening, a build-up, and a climax where the characters pull out their strongest abilities; in wargames, action economy rules everything, and you want to minimize overkill, so it's almost always best to blow your strongest moves immediately (the so-called "nova") and then mop-up whatever's left."
  - On the Escalation Die: "This means their big, flashy attacks are best saved for a few rounds, to maximize their chance of succeeding - instead of nova-ing at the start of battle, you spend the time setting yourself up and beginning the engagement."
  - His extra step: "you can't use your highest level of spells, or your X/day abilities, or what-have-you, until the Escalation Die is at 3+ or something." And he names the paladin: "He's already shown the power of the Paladin's smites, and so restricting that by the Escalation Die seems like it would be fun."
  - Note: he describes the die giving "+2 to their AC" to enemies as compensation; that is his house version.

### 1.4 Square Fireballs, combat pacing and resources
- **VERIFIED**, and much richer than the excerpt. "Game Design (III): Combat pacing, resource usage", by Perico, Nov 16, 2012 (4e). https://squarefireballs.blogspot.com/2012/11/game-design-iii-combat-pacing-resource.html
  - "From a strategic point of view, there is no incentive to save resources for the end of an encounter, so whatever special powers a player intends to spend in a given combat will be blown away as soon as possible. As a consequence, most characters will be dealing a disproportionate amount of damage right at the start of combat, and be left with subpar offense later on".
  - "Everybody starts off doing something truly awesome, then continues with more mundane movements, and ends up using their puniest attacks."
  - "It's much more exciting to have some back and forth, to have desperate heroes draw from inner reserves, to end with a bang, rather than a whimper."
  - His three rules: (1) an Action Point Check by round: "1 - Impossible (no check) / 2 - 15 / 3 - 10 / 4+ - Automatic"; (2) "A character that uses an encounter attack power becomes Fatigued until the end of his next turn" (no two encounter attacks in a row); (3) "A character can only use one daily attack power each encounter."
  - On hoarding: "there is no reason to hoard action points: a hard limit of 1 action point means that you don't need to save them for a hypothetical Last Big Fight"; against that, "the game encourages players to leave daily resources unused"; "the metric for victory in easy battles is, primarily, the ability to not use the most awesome powers available to a hero."
  - He admits the exception himself: "An optimized, coordinated party may prefer to delay their best powers, spending the first round setting up enhancements and positioning for a lethal turn 2."
  - First comment (Brian Slaby, same day) raises the objection: the alternating pattern "could arguably make combat even MORE same-y", and points to 13th Age's Escalation Die.

### 1.6 Nine Sides on combat length
- **VERIFIED.** "Balancing Combat for Length in D&D 5e", Neal Zupancic, Mar 27, 2025. https://ninesides.substack.com/p/balancing-combat-for-length-in-d
  - "1 round: The "flash" combat. A combat which lasts for one round or less often feels anticlimactic. Some players may have rolled initiative without even getting the chance to act."
  - "2 rounds: The "quick" combat. A quick combat is generally quite satisfying."
  - "The basic principle involved in designing for length is very simple: figure out how much damage your party does in a round and multiply it by the number of rounds you want your enemies to last."
  - "I would recommend designing almost all combat encounters to last for three rounds. The outcome of this is that players will often expend limited resources to make the combats go faster - and this is great, because trading resources for the feeling of power and satisfaction is one of the fundamental processes that makes the game fun."
  - Also: "On average, a player character should hit about 65% of the time".

### 1.7 ComicBook, Crawford's reason for the 2024 Divine Smite
- **VERIFIED.** "Dungeons & Dragons Explains Why the Paladin's Divine Smite Was Changed", Christian Hoffer, July 24, 2024. https://comicbook.com/gaming/news/dungeons-dragons-paladin-divine-smite-changes/
  - "So with Divine Smite in particular, we realized very early on in Fifth Edition that frankly the Paladin was too powerful, and that excess of power was largely because of how open-ended the use of Divine Smite was".

  - "And so we did a holistic change because we wanted Paladins to feel like they actually had a range of cool options and not be in the position of the right choice is always just to Divine Smite."

## 4. Corrections that change a conclusion

1. **The size of an accepted nova (report F, section 5 and suggestion 5).** F estimated "2 to 3.5 times a normal hit". The one designer's rule of thumb actually found, Heinsoo's own table, is **lower: a once-per-battle attack is 1.5 times an at-will, a daily is 2 times, at every level.** F's other measurements still stand (they are from rules text), but the only stated design target sits at the bottom of F's range.
2. **The Angry GM is cited for the opposite of his view.** He says the short workday is "a fundamental [...] game-design issue" that needs a built-in offset ("Dynamic Tension"), and he attacks the "it's an adventure design issue" answer. This supports a system-side fix (build in the fight, lose at the end), not GM policing.
3. **There is no conflict about the Censor's wrath.** 2 a turn at level 1, 3 at level 7. Report D's doubt can be removed.
4. **The Censor's slow round 1 is confirmed in a player-director's own words**, and he judges it a weakness, not a virtue: early actions are worth more, fights are short, and the Conduit reaches 7 on round 1 while the Censor reaches 6 at the start of round 2. For a game with 3.5-round fights this matters: a two-round build is over half the fight.
5. **"They want to be hit" is marketing by the designer, not a player's report.** The independent GM's words are milder: "jack of all trades", "a cool revenge mechanic", "always extremely useful".
6. **Report F's claim "the community accepted once per turn and rejected the action tax" rests on fewer quotes than it looked.** Two of the four critic/defender quotes are not on the pages. What is verified: Torvald99's post (once per turn would have solved "the paladin 'nova round' issue"; the bonus action is the complaint), ComicBook's writer saying the same in his own words, Pantagruel666's "sidegrade", and dumbestdnd's "Once per turn was overdue." The conclusion survives, on thinner evidence.
7. **"Nobody defends the nova as a routine opener" (F, section 1) loses one of its two supports** ("let them be awesome" is not on the page). The Gloomhaven quote stands and is stronger in full: use the loss cards late, because early use "costs you several turns on your clock".
8. **Lancer's community word is "nova"**, not "alpha strike" (on the one build page read). Mearls uses "alpha striking" for D&D.
9. **Report F's EN World post links are not citable as given**; use the thread and post numbers above.
