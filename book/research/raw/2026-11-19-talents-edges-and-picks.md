# Edges, advantages, stunts and other free-standing picks outside the d20 family (raw report)

**Date:** 2026-11-19

**Lucas's words:** "The talents is going to be the actual meat of the game, so far we have been doing the skeleton. Talents is going to be the main thing, how things work, how all of it comes together. How a diplomatic paladin differs from a quiet, ranged paladin, with similar strengths and capabilities. I don't want to fall into the trap of skill feats from 2e, or skill tax from 1e and 3.5, or competing talents like edges from Swade (why nobody really gets social edges instead of combat edges)."

**What was read first (2d12):** `book/rules-src/05-characters.md` (the level list, "Levels give options; Stages change the numbers", Swapping on a Rest), `book/rules-src/set-aside/16-talents.md`, the entry "TALENT STRUCTURE: PROPOSAL" in `book/rules-backlog.md`, `book/roleplay-skills.md` (the six failure modes), `book/magic-design.md` (decision 121, sections 3.2, 3.3 and 5l), `book/research/README.md`.

**How far to trust it.** Other games' rules and other people's opinions, never 2d12's. Nothing here designs a Talent.

## 1. Savage Worlds

### The rules

- **One budget for everything.** In the Deluxe-era free rules an advance (every 5 XP) buys one of: "Gain a new Edge", "Increase one skill that is currently equal to or greater than its linked attribute", "Increase two skills that are currently less than their linked attributes", "Buy a new skill at d4", "Increase one attribute by a die type" (once a Rank). [read] https://rulespal.com/savage-worlds/rulebook (a copy of the old free Test Drive rules)
- **Ranks gate Edges.** Novice 0-19 XP, Seasoned 20-39, Veteran 40-59, Heroic 60-79, Legendary 80+; an Edge lists a Rank and often an attribute or skill die: "Charismatic (N, Sp d8)", "Frenzy (S, Fighting d10)". [read, same page] The Adventure Edition (SWADE) drops XP: an advance every session or every few, by campaign length. [checked] https://nerdist.com/?p=878739 . Ranks are then every four advances; an advance may also remove a Hindrance. [memory]
- **Hindrances buy picks at creation.** "Your character gains an 'advance' for each Minor Hindrance she takes (up to two), and two advances for taking a single Major Hindrance. … For 2 advances you can either: Gain another attribute point. Choose an Edge." [read, rulespal]
- **Categories:** Background, Combat, Leadership, Power, Professional, Social, Weird, Legendary. [memory] "Background or Professional Edge—must be chosen during character creation or with GM approval thereafter." [read, rulespal]
- **What the picks are.** In that list the social-facing Edges are numbers: "Attractive* (N, V d6): Charisma +2", "Charismatic (N, Sp d8): Charisma +2", "Noble* (N): +2 Charisma", "Strong Willed (N, Intimidate d6, Taunt d6): +2 Intimidate and Taunt", "Investigator …: +2 Investigation and Streetwise". The combat Edges beside them change what a turn is: "Frenzy …: 1 extra Fighting attack at -2", "Sweep …: Attack all adjacent foes at -2", "First Strike …: Automatically attack one foe who moves adjacent per round", "Level Headed …: Act on best of two cards in combat". The one social Edge that is a capability is "Connections (N): Call upon powerful friends with Persuasion roll". [read, rulespal]
- **Professional Edges bundle:** "Thief* (N, A d8, Climb d6, Lockpick d6, Stealth d8): +2 Climb, Lockpick, Stealth, rolls made to trick or deceive, detecting or disarming traps". [read, rulespal]
- **Charisma.** Deluxe: "Charisma is equal to the total bonuses or penalties given by Edges and Hindrances." [read, rulespal] SWADE removed it. Richard Woolcock (Zadmar): "nothing more than a syntax change"; Charisma "was simply a modifier that applied to Persuasion and Streetwise rolls. That's it"; he adds "there are now fewer Edges and Hindrances with generic Persuasion modifiers that you can stack". [checked] https://savage-stuff.blogspot.com/2025/08/savage-worlds-removal-of-charisma-in.html
- **SWADE's Edges.** A review: "Edges that granted flat bonuses to rolls have been reduced or altered (for instance, each level of attractive gives +1 to persuasion and performance instead of +2, and charismatic gives you a free re-roll…)"; "Professional Edges have been towned down fairly significantly and a variety of new non-combat Edges are introduced along with Edges linked to the Test and Support rules introduced in this edition." [checked] https://z-toast.blogspot.com/2019/02
- **Support (SWADE), Pinnacle's preview:** "supporting characters roll whatever skill they feel is relevant (as an action if the game is in rounds). Success adds +1 to the lead character's applicable total, and a raise adds +2." "General encouragement, by the way, is a Persuasion roll." The Test rules "replaces Tests of Will and Tricks". [read] https://peginc.com/savage-worlds-kickstarter-support-rules-preview/ . So Persuasion, Taunt and Intimidation have a job inside a fight, and SWADE's new Social Edges (Humiliate, Menacing, Retort, Provoke, Rabble-Rouser, Bolster, Work the Room) mostly ride on Tests and Support. [memory] SWADE also gives every character five core skills at d4, Persuasion among them. [memory]
- **Savage Pathfinder:** each class is a Class Edge, a bundle of abilities, taken free at creation; skipping it is said to be worth "about equal to 2 edges" (a livestream, second hand). [lead: search summaries of a d20 Radio review and an EN World thread; d20 Radio refused]

### Why Social Edges lose to Combat Edges: what is documented

1. **It is a known complaint.** "One topic that now and then surfaces on forums and social media about Savage Worlds is how players end up picking the same Edges (Combat Edges, usually) and developing the same skills... aka 'all characters look the same'." [checked] Axian Spice (the blog of a Savage Worlds licensee author [memory]), https://axianspice.blogspot.com/2020/09/about-character-variety-in-savage-worlds.html
2. **Frequency.** Same post: "If they only have ONE meaningful, relevant Social Conflict every six sessions, no player is going to go beyond Persuasion d8. If you always set up four combats per session, everyone will end up picking Quick, Block, Dodge, etc... and so you would, too!" [checked]
3. **Stakes.** "As a player, I know that Combat Edges and skills may make a difference between life and death. If, after a few sessions, I feel that Charismatic, Scholar, Woodsman won't make a dramatic impact on my adventures, changing the course of events for the better, I'll keep stacking Combat Edge upon Combat Edge." [checked, same]
4. **The pick's shape.** A flat +2 to one roll against a new action, shown in the list above. [read]
5. **The skill already does the job, and the bonus stacks to certainty.** Zadmar's "Politician" reaches Persuasion d12+2 and Charisma +8, "ensuring 2+ raises except on snake eyes". [checked] https://savage-stuff.blogspot.com/2018/10/status-and-renown.html . One or two picks finish the job; a fifth is wasted, where a fifth Combat Edge is not.
6. **The GM caps what the roll can do.** Zadmar: high Persuasion makes characters "likable and convincing (particularly for Social Conflicts), but it doesn't let them control the actions of others." [checked] https://savage-stuff.blogspot.com/2016/04/persuasion-is-not-mind-control.html (a post that condenses the forum rulings of Pinnacle's Clint Black, which were not opened). A combat pick's effect is fixed by the rules; a social pick's is negotiated.
7. **Prerequisites point elsewhere.** Charismatic needs Spirit d8, Investigator two skills at d8: a fighter's attributes qualify for combat Edges, and Background and Professional Edges are closed after creation without the GM's word. [read, rulespal]
8. **More combat Edges exist.** "Combat characters can easily nab six Edges without overlap, scientists get about two." [lead: a search summary of a Giant in the Playground thread; the site refused]
9. **The game's weight.** Zadmar: "Although Savage Worlds places a strong emphasis on combat, it is perfectly possible to create viable and interesting characters who focus on social abilities, relying on their resources and minions (and perhaps Tricks and Tests of Will) when a fight cannot be avoided." [checked, status-and-renown]

### What was done about it

- **Pinnacle (SWADE):** fewer stacking bonuses; social skills given combat work through Tests and Support; new Edges that hang on those; free core skills. [read / checked / memory as marked above]
- **The licensee's own verdict on the two obvious house rules.** A cap ("A maximum of one Combat Edge during character creation, and/or a maximum of two Combat Edges per Rank") or a bribe (double value for an advance not spent on combat): "Both courses of action are not great, frankly. Adding limits to players' choices is always bad." His answer is the adventure: "Investing in a 'face' type of hero requires a return for the advances spent there." [checked, Axian Spice]
- **Zadmar's Savage Abilities (2014), on list size:** with too few options "you'll see characters begin to converge around the same group of Edges"; four ways to make a long list less overwhelming: Edge Trees ("the Leadership Edges, which all branch from the Command Edge"), Flexible Edges ("two characters with the same Edge will usually take a different 'version' of it"), Racial Edges, Ranked Edges. He proposes **Minor Edges**: "An advance can be used to buy one Major Edge or two Minor Edges, or buy a Minor Edge and raise one skill". [read, his own text] https://www.godwars2.org/SavageWorlds/SavageAbilities.pdf
- **Bundles and free picks:** Professional Edges (toned down in SWADE), Savage Pathfinder's free Class Edge. No reception found.

## 2. Point-buy with one currency: GURPS, HERO, Mutants & Masterminds, Shadowrun, World of Darkness, 7th Sea

- **GURPS.** One currency (character points) buys attributes, advantages, disadvantages (points back, up to a campaign limit), 1-point perks, skills, techniques (one use of a skill raised alone), Talents (+1 a level to a group of related skills) and wildcard skills (one broad skill at triple cost). [memory] **The documented cost is the menu.** Steve Jackson Games: "new players sometimes find themselves overwhelmed by the game before they even start to play. One way to make character creation less daunting is to use a 'quick-start' technique known as a template." A template "reduces the amount of work needed to create the character and guarantees its competence." [read] https://www.sjgames.com/gurps/books/Wizards/excerpts.html . Lenses are smaller packages laid over a template. [memory] Every point in Diplomacy is a point not in a weapon skill, so a template fixes the spread, and Dungeon Fantasy's templates are classes in all but name. [memory]
- **HERO System.** The same idea, more exact; reputation: heavy. [memory]
- **Mutants & Masterminds.** "Advantages cost 1 Character point per rank"; four types: Combat, Fortune, General, Skill; some are "Ranked" ("+1 bonus to close attack checks per rank"). [read] https://www.d20herosrd.com/5-advantages/ . The Power Level caps attack and defense totals, so once they are at the cap further points can only go sideways. [memory] A cap on combat numbers is itself a fix: it ends the competition at a known point.
- **Shadowrun.** Positive qualities cost karma, negative ones refund it, each side capped at creation; karma also buys skills and attributes. The usual complaints: negative qualities taken as free points, a short list of always-taken positives. [memory]
- **World of Darkness / Chronicles of Darkness.** Merits in dots (1 to 5); Style merits are chains where each dot adds a maneuver; social merits (Allies, Contacts, Status, Resources) are dots that the Storyteller turns into effect. [memory] Reception found: "Fighting Styles can be more powerful than actual supernatural abilities" (TV Tropes) and "Numerous people have told us that equipment and fighting Merits in God-Machine Chronicle were wasted space" (a comment on Onyx Path's site). [lead: search summaries] **The complaint in the brief, "social merits are either mandatory or ignored", was not found on any page.**
- **7th Sea 2e.** Two Backgrounds, each a bundle of advantages and skills, plus a few points of free advantages (1 to 5 points each); advances come from finishing a Story the player wrote. The Duelist Academy is the known must-take. [memory]

**What the family shows.** One currency gives the most freedom and the most documented pain: the menu (GURPS's own words), numbers competing with breadth, drawbacks as free points. Every game in it grew a bundle on top (template, lens, Background), and one grew a cap.

## 3. A pick written by the player: Fate, Cortex Prime, Daggerheart's Experiences, Lancer's triggers, Unknown Armies

- **Fate Core.** Three free stunts; more cost refresh: "If you pick four stunts, your refresh is 2. If you pick five stunts, your refresh is 1." [read] https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/stunts-refresh . A stunt is one of three things: a new action for a skill, "an automatic bonus under a particular, very narrow circumstance" ("The usual bonus is +2"), or "a single exception, in a narrow circumstance, for any other game rule". The book's own test: "If you don't think you'll use the stunt at least twice in most of your game sessions, change the condition", and to the GM: "it's on you to help the players make sure their stunts see use—look at the conditions they choose here as a 'laundry list' of stuff that you want to trend toward in your sessions." Too broad is also named: "You don't want a stunt replacing the skill it modifies." A stronger stunt pays: once a conflict or scene, or a fate point. **Stunt families:** a stacking stunt is "a single super-stunt that costs two slots"; "If you look at stacking effects as expanding a stunt or skill vertically, you can look at branching effects as expanding them laterally", used to "help characters who are highly ranked in the same skill differentiate from each other". [read] https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/building-stunts
- **Fate Accelerated** makes the bonus stunt a sentence: "Because I [describe some way that you are exceptional…], I get a +2 when I [approach][action] when [describe a circumstance]", and the exception: "once per game session I can [describe something cool you can do]." [read] https://fate-srd.com/fate-accelerated/stunts
- **The Fate System Toolkit:** "Having to tie an aspect to a new stunt can make for stunts players are more attached to, and sets a nice upper limit on the number of stunts they can have." [read] https://keeper.farirpgs.com/resources/evil-hat/fate-system-toolkit/4-stunts/
- **Reception.** Groups write the +2 stunt "with ease" and struggle with the once-a-session kind; "there's no way to really, confidently balance them". [lead: a search summary of r/FATErpg] No Fate author's essay on this was found.
- **Cortex Prime.** Three Distinctions at d8, each with the Hinder SFX (roll a d4 for a plot point); other SFX are a cost and a benefit, mostly written at the table; assets are temporary dice. [memory; the official compendium page was a 404]
- **Daggerheart's Experiences.** "An Experience is a word or phrase"; two at creation at +2; "There's no set list"; it "can't be too broadly applicable and it can't grant your character specific mechanical benefits"; spend a Hope to add one to a roll. [read] https://github.com/seansbox/daggerheart-srd
- **Lancer's skill triggers:** "a flat bonus from +2 to +6 to your narrative actions, and can be basically anything." [checked] https://startplaying.games/blog/posts/how-do-you-create-a-character-for-lancer
- **Unknown Armies 3e:** identities as sentences ("I'm a ___, of course I can ___"). **Call of Cthulhu:** none; a character is an occupation and percentages that grow by use. **Traveller:** none; a career history. [memory] These are the control group: with no picks, two characters of one type differ by history and play alone.

**What the family shows.** A written pick never fails to fit the character and costs no list. What it gives is almost always a flat bonus, because that is the one thing a table can write safely: the rulebooks' own templates say so.

## 4. A small list that belongs to you: Apocalypse World, Blades in the Dark, Cypher, Ironsworn, Heart, Dragonbane

- **Apocalypse World and its family.** A playbook has its own short list of moves; an improvement is +1 to a stat, another move of your playbook, or "a move from another playbook". Moves are fights, talk and oddities side by side, each a rule exception or a new trigger, almost never a bare number. [memory]
- **Blades in the Dark.** Two kinds of track that cannot be spent on each other: "When you take an advance from your playbook track, you may choose an additional special ability. When you take an advance from an attribute, you may add an additional action dot". XP is earned by what you did: "When you make a desperate action roll. Mark 1 xp in the attribute for the action you rolled." [read] https://bladesinthedark.com/advancement . A playbook lists about eight abilities plus "Veteran" (an ability from another source). [memory; the playbook pages reset the connection] The crew has a third sheet with its own abilities and XP. [read, same page]
- **Cypher System.** "I am a [descriptor] [type] who [focus]." The type is "the noun"; the descriptor "flavors everything you do. The differences between a Charming Explorer and a Vicious Explorer are considerable"; "Focus is what makes a character unique. No two PCs in a group should have the same focus." Type abilities are picked from a tier's list: "Choose four of the abilities listed below" at tier 1, then two or three a tier, and "you can replace one of your lower-tier abilities with a different one from a lower tier". The focus gives a set ability a tier. [read] https://callmepartario.github.io/og-csrd/ . **Praise:** "I love Cypher's concept of the 'character sentence'… it is very easy to create a character with a clear and communicable identity". [checked] https://tbr.bearblog.dev/on-generic-systems-cypher/ . **Costs:** the GM is told to vet the long focus list for each campaign, which one reviewer did by reading every one. [checked] https://gnomestew.com/decrypting-the-cypher-system-part-4-setup/ . "Some foci seem better than others". [lead: EN World]
- **Ironsworn.** "To start, select three assets." "Add a new asset by spending 3 experience. Upgrade an asset by spending 2 experience." "Ironsworn assets are formatted as printable cards… Each asset card includes three abilities." Four types: companions, paths, combat talents, rituals. "You can mix-and-match assets however you like. There are no designated assets based on character classes or roles. However, you should avoid picking the same asset as another player." A path is identity ("Paths… reflect who you are and how you interact with the world"), and competence is not sold: "Ironsworn characters are assumed to be skilled fighters. Even without a combat talent, you can wield weapons and perform combat moves." The abilities are small ("add +1 and take +1 momentum on a hit") but hang on a named move in a named circumstance (Storyweaver: "by sharing an inspiring or enlightening song, poem, or tale"). [read] the 2019 rulebook's text at https://dokk.org/library/ironsworn_starforged_rulebook_2019 . One budget for all four types, in a game with no fight mode: a fight uses the same moves as a journey.
- **Heart: the City Beneath.** The player picks "beats" from their calling (minor, major, zenith); hitting one gives an advance of that size; a zenith ability ends the character. The pick is earned by doing a named thing. **Spire:** advances when you change the city. [memory]
- **Dragonbane.** One heroic ability from the profession at creation; more when a skill reaches its top value or as a reward for a great deed; they cost Willpower to use. Few picks, earned by events. [memory]

**What the family shows.** A character has few picks (three assets; one focus; three or four moves), each from a short list that is theirs, and each named for who the character is. The praise is about identity and about how easy the choice is; the complaints are about uneven strength inside a list.

## 5. Picks with ranks inside

- **Lancer.** Every talent has exactly three ranks, each a separate effect, not a larger number (Ace: rank 1 "Any time an attack misses you, you may fly up to 2 spaces in any direction as a reaction"; rank 2 "When you Boost while flying, you may move an additional 1d6 spaces, but take heat equal to half that amount"). [read: the game's data file] https://github.com/massif-press/lancer-data (lib/talents.json) . A pilot starts with three talents at rank 1 and adds one rank a License Level, deeper or wider. [memory] "As you advance in License Levels, you also get a chance to redistribute any choices previously made." Talents are combat only; the other half is the triggers above, and a guide calls Bonds "an extra layer to the game's somewhat slim narrative framework". [checked, startplaying, above]
- **Ironsworn:** deepening costs 2, a new card 3. [read, above]
- **Stars / Worlds Without Number:** a focus has two levels: "each focus has 2 levels of ability, which you can increase by spending points on character progression". [checked] https://rpgworkshop.wordpress.com/2018/01/24/stars-without-number-revised-edition-character-generation-session-and-opinions/
- **Year Zero Engine (Forbidden Lands):** kin, profession and general talents in ranks 1 to 3; a rank costs XP by its number; a profession's talents are that profession's only. [memory]
- **Symbaroum:** abilities at novice, adept and master, each tier a new effect at a rising price; boons and burdens are cheap side picks. Known complaint: a few master-level combinations dominate. [memory]
- **Fabula Ultima:** a level is one point in a skill of one of your classes; skills have a few levels each; a class at 10 levels gives a heroic skill; classes mix freely. [memory]
- **Mutants & Masterminds** (ranked advantages) and **Savage Worlds** (Improved X) rank mostly by a bigger number: "Block: Parry +1 / Improved Block: Parry +2". [read, above]
- **L5R 5e** ranks the character, not the pick: a school's curriculum has six ranks; XP spent on the current rank's list counts in full toward the next, anything else half. [memory]

**Deepening or broadening?** No study or designer statement was found. What the rules show: where a rank is a new effect (Lancer, Ironsworn, Symbaroum) the choice is praised; where it is +1 more (Improved Block) it is the pick called filler; Fate prices a stacked stunt as two slots. Zadmar's warning cuts the other way: with too few things to broaden into, characters converge.

## 6. Two budgets, two sheets, typed slots

- **ICON (Massif Press, playtest).** Official page: "a robust narrative system for story driven play and a final fantasy inspired tactical combat system, with either or both systems usable for play." [read] https://massif-press.itch.io/icon . A fan summary: "The two modes have pretty much completely separate rules and character sheets"; "Your Bond has some Bond powers… You start out with one and gain more as you level up, eventually getting to take them from other Bonds as well as your own." [checked] https://lyssa-rpg-docs.neocities.org/icon/rules-summary . Reception on the page: "the idea to give players different classes for both narrative play and combat is an absolutely inspired concept"; the same commenter finds the Bonds uneven ("half of them are character motivations, while half of them are character traits"); another reports the job system being rewritten for a 2.0. [checked, the itch.io comments] Tom Bloom's own reason for the split was not found.
- **Lancer:** two halves, the narrative one thin (section 5).
- **Stars Without Number (revised).** "Every PC gets to pick one focus of any kind"; an Expert or Partial Expert gets "one level of a non-combat, non-psychic focus for free in addition to this"; a Warrior a combat one. [lead: a search summary quoting the free edition; the PDF refused] A review confirms the shape and gives the one play report found: "1 player chose a Warrior for his mainly Pilot character… and still have the benefits of the Warrior Class". [checked, rpgworkshop] Another: foci make "characters stand out from each other mechanically… without the overwhelming crunch of 3rd Edition or Pathfinder". [checked] https://stargazersworld.com/?p=18438 . Later focus levels come from one pool, so the forced pick shapes level 1 only. [memory]
- **Draw Steel perks (the split only).** "Perks are features you can use to customize your hero and give them heroic moments outside of combat." Six types; "Whenever a feature allows you to gain a perk, that feature tells you which type of perk to choose." And against the permission trap: "The existence of specific perks doesn't mean that a hero can't attempt the task related to a perk without having that perk… Perks are special because they allow a hero to attempt a specific task without a test". [read] https://steelcompendium.io/compendium/main/Rules/Chapters/Perks/ . That certainty is what Lucas disliked (`book/roleplay-skills.md`).
- **The One Ring 2e.** Skill points buy skills; adventure points buy combat proficiencies, Valour (a Reward: better gear) and Wisdom (a Virtue: a cultural trick). Two currencies, earned side by side. [memory]
- **Blades:** separate XP tracks (section 4).
- **L5R 5e's shūji.** Techniques come in categories (kata, kihō, invocations, rituals, shūji); a school lists which it may buy, and most schools, bushi included, list shūji. [memory] From the 2017 beta's feedback thread (FFG asked: "How do you feel about the different types of techniques?"): "Shuji, in general, are very popular, as they seem to be more impactful and 'needed' than Katas; Katas are generally only useful in fights, while Shuji can be used in any kind of a conflict or a scene, even including the martial combat ones." Another tester counted "Less than 30% of the Kata let characters do new things" against "22 of 42 (about 52%) of the Shuji grant new actions", and a third called the kata "a trash heap. It fixes the Opportunities into specific things you must buy to unlock which traps a lot of narrative space behind techniques." [checked] https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/264986-week-10-update-and-focus-topic-1282017 . Beta only: nothing on the finished game was opened. It is the one case found where the social picks won, and the testers name why: they work in every kind of scene and more of them are new actions.

## 7. The pick as a card, and a hand smaller than the collection: Daggerheart

"Your loadout is the set of acquired domain cards whose effects your PC can use during play. You can have up to 5 domain cards in your loadout at one time… the rest are considered to be in your vault." A level gives "a new domain card at your level or lower from one of your class's domains", and "You can also exchange one domain card you've previously acquired". Swapping is free at a rest, or costs Stress (the card's Recall Cost) at any other time. [read, the SRD above] A class has two domains, so the hand offered at a level is small. [memory]

**Reception.** Praise: "The card format excels during character creation, especially for newcomers. Physically comparing options side-by-side… creates an intuitive browsing experience that pure text lists can't match." Against, same essay: "At each rest, players must decide which five domain cards to keep active… This transforms character optimization into a physical sorting task"; "most Daggerheart domain cards provide independent abilities that don't generate emergent combinations. The few that do interact work through simple numerical bonuses". [checked] https://thaumavore.substack.com/p/daggerhearts-card-system-essential

## 8. No picks, or picks that are not picks

- **Mythras / RuneQuest.** A combat style is a skill carrying a set of weapons and one trait; special effects are chosen after the roll, by the margin, from one list everybody has. A fighter differs by style and by the effect chosen in the moment. [memory]
- **Into the Odd / Electric Bastionland.** No abilities: a failed career hands you odd gear, and gear is what you can do. [memory]
- **Wildsea.** An aspect is a name, a short track (which is also the damage it soaks) and one special line, taken freely from the lists of a bloodline, an origin and a post. Losing the track loses the ability for a while. [memory]
- **Break!!**, **Gubat Banwa**, **Beacon** (Lancer's descendant): nothing opened, and too little remembered to report. [memory]
- **FFG Star Wars / Genesys** (another report's ground): a specialization tree is a 5 × 4 grid bought with the XP that also buys skills; Genesys replaced it with a pyramid (more low-tier talents than high). [memory]

## 9. The five questions, from the evidence

### (1) Why non-combat picks lose in a shared budget, and each fix tried

The reasons are checklist (b) in section 10; the fixes, each with its game and cost, are list (c). How they went, in short:

- **Removed the competition, no complaint found:** separate tracks fed by play (Blades, The One Ring); typed slots (Draw Steel, though what fills the slot drew Lucas's own complaint); a cap on the combat number (Mutants & Masterminds).
- **Worked, with a side effect:** social picks that work in a fight (L5R's beta: the fight-only picks became the weak ones); a forced free non-combat pick (Stars Without Number: warm reviews, one report of a player routing around the class, level 1 only); a hand smaller than the collection (Daggerheart: the sorting).
- **Half worked:** two sheets or two halves (ICON's idea praised, its narrative content uneven and being rewritten; Lancer's narrative half "slim"); bundles (SWADE weakened its own); free written descriptors (always fit, always a flat bonus).
- **Rejected by its own proposer:** caps and bribes on Combat Edges ("not great, frankly"); his answer is the adventure.
- **Locates the cause:** games with no fight mode (PbtA, Ironsworn) run one budget without the problem. The competition exists because the fight is a separate, larger and deadlier game.

### (2) A flat bonus against a change in what the character can do

Every source that compares them prefers the second, and the bonus is what gets cut over editions. SWADE reduced or replaced flat bonuses [checked, z-toast]. L5R's testers scored a category by how many of its picks "let characters do new things". Fate's book treats the +2 as the easy case and spends its warnings on making it narrow enough to feel special and common enough to come up. Daggerheart's critic names "simple numerical bonuses" as the weak interactions. Lancer's flat triggers are its thin half. The Savage Worlds list shows the trap: its social Edges are the flat ones.

Two cautions from the same sources. A pick that is a new *permission* gets the opposite complaint: the kata "traps a lot of narrative space behind techniques". And a rule exception is harder to write and to balance than a +2 (the Fate lead).

### (3) Small but identity-defining

Cypher's focus (one phrase, an ability a tier, "No two PCs in a group should have the same focus"), Ironsworn's asset (a card with a noun for a name, three small abilities, "avoid picking the same asset as another player"), a Blades or PbtA playbook ability (one of about eight), Dragonbane's heroic ability, Daggerheart's Experience. What they share: **the pick has a name that is a statement about the character; it is one of very few; the table is told not to duplicate it; its trigger is a described circumstance.** The mechanical effect is often tiny (+1 and momentum). What is praised is that the character can be said in a sentence.

### (4) Ranks inside a pick

See the end of section 5: a rank that is a new effect is liked, a rank that is +1 more is filler, and no source measured which players prefer.

### (5) What makes a list easy to choose from

- **A short list that is yours:** a playbook's eight abilities; a type's tier list; two domains' cards at or below your level.
- **Cards:** good for comparing side by side when choosing (Daggerheart's critic grants this; Ironsworn ships its assets as cards); slower than a sheet in play.
- **Fewer open at once:** Zadmar's four devices (trees, an Edge taken in "versions", lists by ancestry, lists by Rank).
- **A package first, choices after:** GURPS's template, in the publisher's own words a cure for being "overwhelmed".
- **A sentence to fill in:** Cypher's "adjective noun who verbs"; Fate Accelerated's "Because I…".
- **The GM cuts the list for the campaign:** Cypher's worksheet; Fate's "laundry list" read the other way (the picks tell the GM what to run).
- **Being able to take it back:** Lancer's redistribution, Cypher's swap of a lower-tier ability, Daggerheart's exchange and vault.

## 10. What seems to transfer

### (a) The structures found

| Structure | Games | What it solved | What it cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| One currency for everything | GURPS, HERO, Savage Worlds, Shadowrun, CofD, FFG | Total freedom; one rule | The menu; combat numbers beat breadth; drawbacks as free points; needs templates |
| Bundles over the currency | GURPS templates and lenses, Professional and Class Edges, 7th Sea Backgrounds | A competent start; hides the competition | Less choice; SWADE had to weaken them |
| A cap on the combat number | Mutants & Masterminds; SWADE (bonuses to rerolls) | Past the cap, picks go sideways | The cap becomes the target everyone reaches |
| Separate tracks, fed by play | Blades, The One Ring | Combat and non-combat never compete | Needs an XP engine with triggers |
| A forced free non-combat pick | Stars / Worlds Without Number | Every Expert has one | Level 1 only; a class can be routed around |
| Typed slots | Draw Steel perks | No competition at all | The slot's content must earn its place (certainty, in Draw Steel) |
| Two sheets or two halves | ICON, Lancer | The cleanest split | Two games; the narrative half thin or uneven |
| Social picks that work in a fight | SWADE Tests and Support, L5R shūji | The social pick is taken | Taken for its combat use; L5R's fight-only picks became the weak ones |
| A written descriptor with a bonus | Fate stunts and aspects, Experiences, triggers, Cortex | Always fits; no list | A flat bonus; breadth policed by the GM |
| A short list that is yours | PbtA, Blades, Cypher | Easy choice; rule-bending picks | Uneven strength inside a list |
| A named card with ranks | Ironsworn assets, Lancer talents, Symbaroum, Year Zero | Identity in a name; deepen or widen | A few dominant combinations (Symbaroum) |
| A loadout smaller than the collection | Daggerheart | Narrow picks stay worth owning | Sorting at each rest |
| A pick earned by an event | Heart's beats, Dragonbane | The pick records the story | Not plannable; uneven pace |
| No picks | Call of Cthulhu, Traveller, Into the Odd, Mythras | Nothing to optimize | Difference lives in history, gear or the moment only |

### (b) Why social and other non-combat picks go untaken: a checklist

1. One budget, and the fight is where the character dies. [checked]
2. Fights happen several times a session; a social conflict that matters, rarely. [checked]
3. The non-combat pick is a flat bonus; the combat pick is a new action. [read]
4. The skill alone already does the job, and bonuses stack to certainty, so the second pick is wasted. [checked]
5. The GM decides what a social success does; the rules decide what a hit does. [checked]
6. A circumstance that does not come up ("Child of the Court will be useless if your campaign doesn't deal with the nobility"). [read, Fate Core]
7. Prerequisites and creation-only categories steer later picks to combat. [read]
8. The list has more combat picks than others. [lead]
9. One face is enough for a party; every character fights. [memory: not found on an opened page]
10. The picks cannot be taken back, so the safe choice wins (inferred from the games that allow swapping; no source says it).

### (c) The fixes

- **Separate budgets** (Blades, The One Ring, Stars Without Number, Draw Steel, ICON). The only fix that removes the competition outright. Cost: what fills the non-combat slot must be worth a slot, or it becomes the forgettable ribbon; pushed to two sheets it becomes two games.
- **Capabilities, not numbers** (L5R shūji, PbtA moves, SWADE's direction). Cost: harder to write and to balance, and each one must pass the permission test (the kata complaint).
- **Work in both arenas** (Tests and Support; shūji). Cost: the pick is judged by its combat half.
- **A cap on combat numbers** (Mutants & Masterminds). Cost: none found beyond everyone sitting at the cap.
- **Take-backs** (Lancer, Cypher, Daggerheart). Cost: bookkeeping; a loadout makes a sorting step.
- **Few, named, not duplicated** (Cypher, Ironsworn). Cost: the list has to be vetted, and uneven picks show.
- **The adventure pays for the pick** (Fate's "laundry list", Axian Spice). Cost: it is GM practice, not a rule.

### (d) What 2d12's files already resemble (a description, not a recommendation)

- **`book/rules-src/05-characters.md`:** the level list already has **typed slots** (a Skill Talent at levels 1 and 3, a Talent at 2, a Stage Talent at 4): Draw Steel's and Stars Without Number's fix, not Savage Worlds' one budget. "A Modifier is never above +4" and "Levels give options; Stages change the numbers" are the **cap on the combat number**. "Swapping on a Rest" is the **take-back**, wider than Lancer's or Daggerheart's.
- **`book/rules-src/set-aside/16-talents.md`:** categories, a level and Prerequisites (a Talent, a level, a trait, an Attribute) are Savage Worlds' Edge requirements almost item for item, with its chains (Brawler, then Kickboxer: an Edge Tree). "Talents That Modify Actions" by trait is Lancer's talent. Its example, Heavy Blows (+2 damage), is a number.
- **`book/rules-backlog.md`, "TALENT STRUCTURE: PROPOSAL":** Minor and Major Talents are Zadmar's Minor Edges, written independently; ranks I to III are Lancer's and Ironsworn's shape; "two tracks, never competing" is Blades and The One Ring; "Skill Talents never give combat numbers" is the opposite of the SWADE and L5R fix (social picks that work in a fight).
- **`book/roleplay-skills.md`:** Backgrounds as 1 Boon are Daggerheart's Experiences; "one grounded out-of-combat Feature per class, free" is the Expert's free focus; proposal 1 (a Talent never grants permission) is Draw Steel's sentence on perks; proposal 2 (no certainty) refuses what Draw Steel's perks then do.
- **`book/magic-design.md`, section 5l:** a seed is "a limit in the base rules that a Talent could lift": Fate's third kind of stunt ("a single exception… for any other game rule") and what a Blades or PbtA ability is. Section 3.2 (Paths and Schools as traits a Talent keys on) is Lancer's talents keyed on weapon types. Section 3.3 lists "Ribbon" among the kinds a class covers.
- **The Action card** (`book/cards/`, `foundry/twod12/module/cardmodel.mjs`): a pick already has a card face, as Ironsworn's assets and Daggerheart's domain cards do; an Art's three Modes is a three-part card, though its parts are alternatives, not ranks.
