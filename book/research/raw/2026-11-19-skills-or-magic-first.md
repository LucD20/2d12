# Skills first, magic first, or side by side: what the order did in other games

**Date:** 2026-11-19. **Asked by Lucas,** just before designing magic outside a fight: "Should we do skills side by side? Should we do skill first? Or good to start with magic? Research before answering."

**What was read, and how far to trust it.** One researcher, about 45 searches and page loads. Marks: [read] rules text or a designer's own words on a page opened; [checked] an opinion or commentary on a page opened; [lead] a search summary, or a page that refused to load; [memory] no page opened. [earlier] means read by the reports of 2026-11-19 (`2026-11-19-magic-vs-skills.md`, `2026-11-19-out-of-combat-magic.md`) and not reopened here.

- **Read in full, from the page's own text:** four Paizo playtest blogs of 2018 (Seifter twice, Radney-MacFarland once; the fourth, "All About Spells", for its rituals section), Draw Steel's Tests chapter (the fan-run Steel Compendium's copy), Blades in the Dark's rituals page.
- **Read through a tool that returns extracts** (recheck a quote on its page before printing it): DM David's two articles, Jon Peterson's blog post, Grognardia, Rascal's article on Draw Steel, one EN World thread.
- **No note in `book/research/` covers skill systems or design order.**
- **Could not be opened:** both RPG Site threads on "the order of design" (login wall); Archives of Nethys' rule pages (they need a browser), so Pathfinder 2e's Knock and ritual rules are [earlier]; the 13th Age SRD's rituals page (404 at the address tried), so [earlier]; Dice Exploder's "Design Your Character Sheet First" (404). Reddit was not tried (it refuses automated readers).
- **Not found on any page:** a designer of D&D 3e, 4e or 5e explaining in their own words how the spell list was or was not re-cut against the skill system; any MCDM statement on the order in which Draw Steel's tests, skills, perks and class abilities were written; any account, by anyone, of a magic system rewritten because the skills arrived later. **No search was run for DC20**; Daggerheart and Shadow of the Weird Wizard got one search each and gave nothing on order.

**The short version:** the question of order is almost never discussed in print. What exists is the history (what each game had on the table when it wrote its non-combat magic) and the rules texts (what a ritual actually cites). Both are below. **Other games' rules and opinions, never 2d12's. Nothing here is decided, and this report recommends no order.**

## 1. History: which came first, and what followed

### D&D, 1974 to 1975: spells first, then one class's skills

- The 1974 rules had the spells and no skills. Knock, Invisibility, Levitate, Fly and the cleric's Find Traps were on the first lists [memory].
- The thief came after. Jon Peterson [checked]: Gygax's "Thief Addition" "had first shipped in a fanzine called the *Great Plains Games Players Newsletter* back in May" 1974, with rules he stressed had "not been tested"; "nearly a year before *Greyhawk*, the Thief class was on display at GenCon VII." Its abilities: "They can open locks, remove traps, climb walls, steal, backstab, hide in shadows, listen for noises and move silently", "mostly resolved with a percentile roll". Source: https://playingattheworld.blogspot.com/2012/08/gygaxs-thief-addition-1974.html
- **What followed, in two directions** (DM David, [checked], https://dmdavid.com/tag/the-thiefs-strange-trip-from-non-combatant-to-battlefield-domination):
  - The spells already owned the outcomes: "Too bad wizards and clerics could now cast spells like Detect Traps, Invisibility, Levitate, and Fly." "Most anything the thief did, a spell did better."
  - The skills, arriving as one class's property, took the baseline from everyone else: "fighting men, clerics, and magic users implicitly—or sometimes by rule—lost the ability to try similar feats."
- James Maliszewski, Grognardia [checked]: the difficulty of a better thief is doing it "without fostering the inevitable diminishment of the ability of other classes to attempt" the same things; in a comment, "Most of what a thief does isn't in fact all that different from what all D&D characters could do". https://grognardia.blogspot.com/2008/12/building-better-thief-part-i.html
- Jon Peterson's two books, Delta's D&D Hotspot and The Alexandrian were searched for and **not opened**: nothing here rests on them.

**What this case is evidence for.** Not "magic first breaks skills" in general. Two narrower things: a skill written later inherits whatever outcomes the spells already reach, and nobody goes back to the spells; and a skill list that arrives without a stated baseline ("what can anyone try?") is read as a permission list.

### AD&D and 3e: skills generalised, the spell list inherited

- Non-weapon proficiencies came in 1985 (Oriental Adventures) and 1986; thieves kept their own tables until 2000 [checked, DM David, https://dmdavid.com/tag/the-twisting-tale-of-skills-in-dd/].
- 3e put every skill on the one d20 mechanic. Monte Cook, as quoted there [checked]: "We knew we wanted to make a more robust set of skills." Any character could now attempt to hide or climb.
- **The spell list was carried over, not re-cut** [memory]: 3e's Knock still opens with no check. The known outcome is the tier list and the "I just use knock" complaint (`magic-vs-skills.md`, section 1).
- **No designer was found saying why.** Searches for Cook, Tweet, Williams and Reynolds on re-cutting spells against the new skills returned nothing. This is a gap in the record, not a finding about intent.

### D&D 4e: skills fixed, then rituals keyed to them

- The skill list and the skill check were core; rituals were written on top: a ritual names a key skill (Arcana, Religion, Nature, Heal) and many read their effect off that skill check's result [memory]. One forum poster's description [lead]: Detect Secret Doors and Discern Lies "give the arcana (or religion) check of the caster as a bonus to his perception or insight check."
- What followed is in `out-of-combat-magic.md`: no overlap complaint, and rituals went unused (gold and casting time). **Skills first did not prevent disuse.**
- No statement by Heinsoo, Wyatt, Mearls or Collins on the order was found.

### D&D 5e: spells written while skills were nearly optional

- Mike Mearls during the design, as quoted by DM David [checked]: "We're making skills completely optional." Playtesters kept them in.
- The spell list was carried over; Knock opens with no check [earlier]. So the spells were written without a settled skill system to measure against, and mechanism 1 of `magic-vs-skills.md` (certain where the skill rolls) is in the final game. The link between those two facts is this report's inference, not a designer's statement.

### Pathfinder 2e: one frame first, then spells and skills inside it

The best-documented case, from the designers' own playtest blogs (all [read]):

- **The frame was published first.** Mark Seifter, 30 March 2018: "You can also critically succeed or critically fail at a variety of checks, from attack rolls, to saving throws, to skill checks and beyond... In the playtest, we have a unified mechanic." https://paizo.com/blog/critical-hits-and-critical-failures
- **Spells came second and already cited skills.** Seifter, 16 April 2018, on rituals: "these downtime spells don't take up your spell slots, and... martial characters who manage to attain a high enough proficiency rank in magic-related skills like Arcana can cast them!" They "involve some number of secondary casters, which can get the whole party involved", and "if you critically fail planar binding, you call something dark and horrible". https://paizo.com/blog/all-about-spells
- **Skills came third, cut with magic in view.** Stephen Radney-MacFarland, 4 June 2018: "we put the general functions of Use Magic Device into each of the various knowledge skills that focus on magical traditions". And the sentence nearest to a statement about order: skill feats let the game grow "without us having to pull out the wires of the underlying skill... we can expand skill options in an open-ended way, without invalidating the gateway mechanics." https://paizo.com/blog/learning-takes-a-lifetime
- **Skills were given magic-sized outcomes at the top.** Seifter, 8 June 2018: Assurance gives a fixed result "without rolling"; a legendary character can "fall an unlimited distance", or "remove an affliction or permanent condition with a medical miracle". https://paizo.com/blog/feats-of-skill
- Knock as "+4 status bonus" to the check is [earlier]. **No designer was found describing Knock's redesign.**
- What followed is in `magic-vs-skills.md`: the overlap is closed; casters are reported as weak and skill feats as permission.

**Caution:** blog dates show the order of publication of one playtest book, not the order of work at the desk. What they do show is that rituals, skills and skill feats all cite the same two things (the proficiency ranks and the four degrees), and that those two were stated before either.

### Others, more briefly

- **13th Age** [earlier]: a ritual is resolved by "a skill check with a magical background". The ritual rule has no meaning without the background rule. Design order: not found.
- **Worlds Without Number** [earlier]: "Spells should not substitute for other classes... or substitutes for an arbitrary selection of skills". The test needs a skill list to exist; it is applied spell by spell by the GM.
- **Fabula Ultima** [earlier; the nuance is memory]: a ritual may never replicate "the mechanical benefits of an existing spell or Skill". In that game "Skills" are the classes' abilities, not a list of trained skills: **the never-list presupposes the classes' catalogue, not a skill system.**
- **Blades in the Dark** [read, https://bladesinthedark.com/rituals]: rituals are an add-on built entirely from parts every scoundrel uses: a "long-term project" clock to learn one, "at least one downtime activity to perform", stress "according to its magnitude", and, if dangerous, "an action roll (usually Attune)". Design order: not found.
- **Draw Steel** [read, https://steelcompendium.io/v2/Read/heroes/tests/]: class abilities cite the skill list by name: the conduit's Blessing of Fortunate Weather "grants creatures who make tests using the Hide skill an edge on those tests", "regardless of whether they have that skill or not". Rascal's account of its development [checked, https://www.rascal.news/how-mcdm-designed-draw-steel-alongside-their-fans/] says nothing about order; it does record the core roll changing from 2d6 to 2d10 during 2024, with everything built on it following. Earlier packets and videos were not opened.

## 2. Stated advice on sequencing: thin

- **Designers do not discuss "skills or magic first" in print,** as far as this search reached. No essay, talk or design column was found that addresses it.
- What exists is the general "core first". An EN World thread [checked, https://www.enworld.org/threads/approaches-to-rpg-design.436815/], forum posters, not named designers: "A game needs to have a foundation that the other game mechanics revolve around"; "The traits and skills are different sub-systems that are still connected to the core mechanic"; "There's a *lot* of design work to be done here after choosing your core mechanic."
- Leads not opened: Vincent Baker's "concentric design" (layers that can be sheared off and leave a playable game) [lead]; Dice Exploder, "Design Your Character Sheet First" [lead, 404]; two RPG Site threads titled for exactly this question [lead, login wall].
- The nearest thing to a principle in a designer's own words is Radney-MacFarland's, above: fix the "gateway mechanics", leave the list of uses open to grow. It is said of skills, not of order.
- "Design the thing everyone uses before the thing some use" and "the baseline before the exception" were searched for as phrases and **not found stated by anyone**. The D&D thief history is the usual evidence people reach for; the principle itself is folk wisdom [memory].

## 3. Side by side: one documented case, no accounts of regret

- **Pathfinder 2e is the only case found with a paper trail.** In practice it looked like this: two decisions taken for the whole game (ranks of training; four outcomes on every check), then spells, rituals, skill uses and skill feats each written to cite them. The skill list (17) and the feats came months later in publication.
- **Blades in the Dark** has the same shape by construction: rituals contain no rule of their own beyond three questions.
- **Test scenes run with both, and regret:** no designer account of either was found, for either order. The nearest facts: 4e's Essentials line later dropped rituals as a separate system [lead]; one indie designer cut a magic system after three months because it did not fit the game's goals, unrelated to skills [lead].
- So "rewritten once skills arrived" is, on this evidence, something that did **not** happen in the big games: the spells stayed as they were and the skills took the loss (D&D 1975, 3e, 5e).

## What seems to transfer

### (a) What the history shows under each order

| Order | What followed | Strength of the evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Magic first, skills later, nothing fixed between them** (D&D 1974 to 1975; 3e and 5e carrying the old list into a new or unsettled skill system) | The spells keep the outcomes; nobody re-cuts them; the skill is the weaker road to the same place. Separately, skills arriving without a stated baseline read as permission | The facts of order are solid. The causal reading is historians' and bloggers' [checked], repeated for decades. **No designer's own account** |
| **Skills first, then magic keyed to them** (4e; 13th Age's backgrounds and rituals) | No overlap complaint. 4e's rituals went unused for other reasons (price, time). Magic that only modifies checks is the Pathfinder-style outcome Worlds Without Number objects to | Rules text for the dependency; [memory] and [lead] for 4e's; **no designer statement on order** |
| **A frame first, then both inside it** (Pathfinder 2e; Blades by construction) | Rituals, skills and skill feats cite the same ranks and outcomes; the list and the feats came after. Reception problems of its own (weak casters, permission feats), which come from the stance, not the order | **The strongest evidence here:** four designer blogs [read]. But one game, and publication dates rather than a diary |

**Honest summary:** three or four cases, no controlled comparison, and the designers themselves are silent on sequence. The history supports "magic written with no skill frame fixed does not get corrected later" better than it supports any claim that skills must be finished first.

### (b) The smallest set of skill decisions a magic system leans on

From section 4, with the list and the numbers left out because no ritual text cites them:

1. What a check outside a fight is, and how many outcomes it has.
2. What a failure costs.
3. What "trained" means (as a gate or as an effect), in one sentence.
4. Whether anything succeeds without a roll, and for whom.
5. What form help takes on someone else's check, and how it stacks.
6. How one character helps another, and how a group acts together.
7. The units of time outside a fight.
8. The subjects skills cover, as categories of outcome (not the final list).
9. Which roll, if any, a casting makes.
