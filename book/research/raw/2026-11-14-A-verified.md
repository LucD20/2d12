**CORRECTED. The page does not say this.** The only "eternal losers" in the 13 pages is a GM's anecdote about NPC paladins dying while protecting the party, nothing about being ignored:

### P3. itch.io essay "Tanking in TTRPGs" (three pillars / Bruiser)
**VERIFIED.** Wrong On Porpoise (the designer of PYRE), November 26, 2025, https://itch.io/blog/1125694/tanking-in-ttrpgs
- "The reason is simple: durability alone is not enough. A real tank requires three pillars: Durability, or the ability to withstand more damage than average. Control, or the ability to influence enemy actions or positioning. Protection, or the ability to safeguard allies or redirect harm."
- "Protection keeps allies alive. Without protection, the tank cannot meaningfully prevent harm to the party. Instead of being a tank, they fill a Bruiser or Brawler role."
- **NOT ON PAGE:** "become optional threats instead of unavoidable ones" (the phrase is not in the essay). The nearest real sentence: "If enemies can choose to attack weaker targets instead, durability becomes irrelevant. From a design perspective, tanks need incentives for enemies to attack them, or consequences for ignoring them."
- Also on the page and not in the report: "A character who merely soaks and deals damage is simply a durable striker, not unlike the Barbarian from D&D 5E." And two tank types: the Anchor ("reward staying near allies, redirecting attacks, and punishing enemies that attempt to bypass them") and the Ram.

### P4. "nice to have but not enough of a reason for the party to want you"
**VERIFIED text, thread now identified, and the context CORRECTS the reading.**
- Chess Pwn, Aug 16, 2017, 10:43 am, "List of Character Roles" (Pathfinder First Edition, General Discussion), https://paizo.com/threads/rzs2uiur : "So really a character should have this a Primary role, and Secondary role. Also pretty much able to be summed up as combat role, and out of combat role. Primary roles means you have a reason to be in the party, secondary roles are nice to have but not enough of a reason for the party to want you."
- His lists: "Primary roles: battlefield controller. buffer de-buffer melee damage ranged damage" / "Secondary roles: crafter face scholar scout healer (this is for status stuff, not HP healing)". And: "tank isn't a role, due to the fact that you can't draw agro, and you don't actually need to take a lot of damage."
- So "secondary" here means **out-of-combat** roles, not a class's second combat role. The line does not say "a secondary healer or secondary tank is not a reason to bring you". It should not be used as evidence about hybrid combat roles. (It is not in rzs8tewp, where the report surfaced it.)

### P5. John Hopson on hybrids (Isegoria)
**VERIFIED.** Isegoria, "MMO Class Design: An Economic Argument", April 28, 2011, https://www.isegoria.net/?p=24416 (quoting John Hopson):
- "At the heart of the hybrid problem is the fact that if a hybrid can perform a given role as well as a specialist while also having other abilities the specialist can never have, playing a specialist becomes pointless."
- "The standard solution to this problem can be summed up in the phrase "Jack of all trades, master of none". Hybrids are generally made less effective in each area than their parent classes, with the intent that they make up the deficiency with their abilities from other areas. The paladin mentioned above might not be able to survive as much damage as a knight, but they can heal other players and help them survive, something a knight could never do."
- The other half, missing from the report: "Conversely, if a hybrid is always inferior to a specialist in any given role, then it's always better to have a specialist fill that role."

### P6. EN World "Jack Of All Trades is a cursed archetype" (all 8 pages read)
- **Depth: VERIFIED, but the report's gloss is off.** Fanaelialae, Sep 7, 2021, #42 (jack, page-5): "Right. Another way to put it is that a jack of all trades needs sufficient depth in all of their primary roles, but their breadth can be distributed across multiple roles. Whereas a specialist should have the bulk of both depth and breadth in their primary role. A hybrid who lacks depth in their primary roles simply can't perform adequately at anything, and this is the most common mistake I see in hybrid designs."
