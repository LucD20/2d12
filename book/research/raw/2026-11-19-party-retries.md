# The whole party retrying a failed task: what games print

**Date:** 2026-11-19.

**The designer's words:** "Amazing point I completely forgot. This requires extensive search to prevent the 'I attempt this, I failed' another player 'I will try as well'. With roleplay, it is easy to solve, with stuff like locked doors and such, it is hard: First, failed attempts need to mean something or prevent something small, a failed attempt can never stop story from moving: A locked door that leads to the objective means nothing if there isn't a drawback, for example, failing means the BBEG gets to prepare, or that makes so much noise it alerts people far away, or lose time etc. But a locked chest for loot usually is just extra, so a failed attempt means nothing meaning someone else can just try it again. There are simple ideas such as group rolls but it doesn't fit opening a chest. The easiest but a bit boring way is to just make everyone roll for it if Tier 1 just means failure. If tier 1 means failure plus can't be attempted again by anyone, it is also decent. Like I mentioned before, in roleplay, attempting to persuade someone and failing is easy to justify in character: 'can I attempt to persuade?' the argument was already given, and just saying 'I attempt persuade with another argument' isn't enough, retrying like this requires some level of good faith and changing of scenes or situations."

**What was read, and how far to trust it.** One researcher, about 55 searches and page loads, building on `raw/2026-11-19-retries.md` (repeated here only where a page was reopened for the party question). **Rules text opened:** Draw Steel's Tests and Negotiation (Steel Compendium); D&D 5e 2014 Basic Rules and the 2024 glossary (D&D Beyond); Pathfinder 2e (Archives of Nethys) and 1e (d20pfsrd); Blades' Teamwork; Fate Core; the Year Zero, Daggerheart and Cypher SRDs (Old Gus' transcriptions); Old-School Essentials (a mirror; the official SRD returned errors); Call of Cthulhu 7e's free rules; Dungeon World's GM chapter. **Opinions opened:** the Alexandrian, DM David, Sly Flourish, Alphastream, the Angry GM, Rob Heinsoo, Bind RPG, Burning Wheel and EN World threads. **Only quoted by others:** the 2024 DMG, Mouse Guard, Torchbearer. **Leads or memory only:** Savage Worlds, Ironsworn, Shadowdark's book, Mothership, Genesys, 13th Age's core book. Reddit and Paizo's forum were not tried (both refused before). Several pages came through a reader that quotes only short fragments: the fragments are exact, the words around them are the reader's.

**Marks:** [read] rules text or a designer's own words on a page opened; [checked] an opinion on a page opened; [lead] a search snippet or a page that refused; [memory] no page opened. **Other games' rules and opinions, never 2d12's. Nothing here is a proposal.**

## 1. What each game prints about the party, not the person

**Draw Steel** [read]
- **Group tests,** for "a single, simple task together": each hero rolls, and "If half or more of the heroes making the group test succeed, then the group test succeeds". Also: "failure on a group test shouldn't bring a story to a halt."
- **"Can I Try Again?"** is written to "you"; it never says whether another hero may try.
- **Negotiation counts per party:** "If the heroes try to use the same argument without a pitfall or motivation twice, the test automatically obtains a tier 1 outcome." "Each motivation can be successfully appealed to only once during a negotiation"; a spent one costs 1 patience. Every hero may argue, but the NPC's patience is one budget the party spends.
- **Montages are per character:** "An individual character can't use the same skill more than once in a montage test. Though multiple heroes can use the same skill..."
- https://steelcompendium.io/v2/Read/heroes/tests/ ; https://steelcompendium.io/v2/Read/heroes/negotiation/

**D&D 5e**
- **2014** [read]: Working Together, "The character who's leading the effort--or the one with the highest ability modifier--can make an ability check with advantage"; a helper must be able to "attempt alone". Group Checks, "If at least half the group succeeds, the whole group succeeds" (for a group that succeeds or fails together: a swamp). Passive checks, "10 + all modifiers", "the average result for a task done repeatedly".
- **2024** [read]: Influence, "On a failed check, you must wait 24 hours (or a duration set by the DM) before urging it in the same way again", written to "you". DMG p. 28 [checked, an EN World poster's quote]: "If failure has no consequences and a character can try and try again, you can skip the ability check and just tell the player how long the task takes. Alternatively, you can call for a single ability check and use the result to determine how long it takes".
- https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/basic-rules-2014/using-ability-scores ; https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary ; https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=9697399

**Pathfinder** [read]
- **2e, Group Attempts** (GM Core p. 52): for "a task that only one person in the group needs to succeed at, but that everyone can attempt", set a very hard DC: "most of the party will probably fail, but someone might still succeed." The dogpile is allowed and priced into the DC. Follow the Expert gives a follower a bonus on "a recurring skill check".
- **1e, Disable Device:** "Yes. You can retry checks made to open locks"; a full-round action; Take 20 allowed.
- https://2e.aonprd.com/Rules.aspx?ID=2631 ; https://2e.aonprd.com/Actions.aspx?ID=2625 ; https://www.d20pfsrd.com/skills/disable-device/

**Burning Wheel: does Let It Ride bind the group?** The forum is split [checked]: "LiR applies to a single player's roll, not across multiple players' tests" (noclue); "One player can't make the same roll over and over, and every player can't make the same roll in turn" (Wayfarer); "you can't test for the same trap (or the duplicate trap the next room over) twice" (gunner6). Luke Crane's words on the party question were not found [lead only]. https://forums.burningwheel.com/t/letting-it-ride-example/15700 ; https://forums.burningwheel.com/t/dungeon-crawling-and-let-it-ride/15848

**Mouse Guard and Torchbearer** [checked]
- The account "luke" ("Luke Wheel"; taken to be Luke Crane, not confirmed): "There is no 'Let It Ride' rule in Mouse Guard or Torchbearer. The closest analog is Fun Once. But in TB, there are many other reasons why LIR is unnecessary." https://forums.burningwheel.com/t/let-it-ride/13234
- Fun Once, as a poster quotes it: "if not, a different stat must be used to bypass the obstacle". The Scholar's Guide's example: "Dro's thief Myrgan fails to pick the lock to the storeroom in time", and a patrol arrives as the twist. https://forums.burningwheel.com/t/twist-example-clarification/24820
- Turns: when "multiple characters are required to test individually, it takes one turn" (Scholar's Guide p. 40, quoted); "each test costs a turn"; helpers share a lesser condition. https://www.enworld.org/threads/torchbearer-2nd-ed-actual-play.686046/ . The grind gives a condition every fourth turn [lead]. **A second character's attempt is a new test that spends a party turn.**

**Blades in the Dark** [read]: in a group action, "Each PC who's involved makes an action roll", the team takes "the single best result", and the leader "takes 1 stress for each PC that rolled 1-3 as their best result". "Only one character may assist a given roll." https://bladesinthedark.com/teamwork

**Fate Core** [read]: in teamwork, "only the lead character rolls", +1 per helper, and "all of the participants share in the potential costs". "Roll the dice when succeeding or failing at the action could each contribute something interesting to the game." "The worst, worst thing you can do is have a failed roll that means nothing happens—no new knowledge, no new course of action to take, and no change in the situation. Do not do this." https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/teamwork ; https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/what-do-during-play

**Year Zero Engine** [read]: "you may not roll again to achieve the same goal. You need to try something different or wait until the circumstances have changed in a substantial way. Or let another player character try." Up to three helpers, +1 each; "Failure must not stop the story completely." **The only game read that prints "another may try" outright;** each try may be pushed, at a risk. https://callmepartario.github.io/og-yzesrd

**Daggerheart** [read]: "the party chooses someone to lead the action", and each cooperating reaction roll moves the leader's roll by +1 or −1; Help an Ally costs a Hope. "Every time you make an action roll, the scene changes—there are no such rolls where 'nothing happens'"; "if failure would be boring, they succeed automatically." https://callmepartario.github.io/og-dhsrd/

**Cypher System** [read]: a failed task may be retried, but "they must apply at least one level of Effort when retrying that task", and a retry "takes the same amount of time as the first attempt did". "Perhaps a character has one chance to convince the leader of a group of thugs not to attack". Another character's attempt is not addressed. https://callmepartario.github.io/og-csrd/ (Rulebook pp. 212, 226)

**Call of Cthulhu 7e** [read]: a push needs a justification, and a second failure brings "a dire consequence". **One roll for the group:** in a "group Luck roll", the investigator with "the lowest Luck score (among those present in the scene) should make the roll". https://cocwiki.chaosium.com/rules/game-system.html

**Old-School Essentials (B/X)** [read, a mirror]: "Each character may only make one attempt to listen at each door"; "Each character can only make one attempt to search a specific area or item"; a thief "can only try this skill once per lock" and not again "before gaining an experience level". "A failed attempt to force open a door eliminates any possibility of surprise." A search "takes one turn"; the wandering check comes "once every two turns", "1-in-6". **Per character and per object; another character may try.** https://digital-gm.pages.dev/docs/ose-rules/adventures/dungeon-adventuring/ ; https://digital-gm.pages.dev/docs/ose-rules/classes/thief/

**Others, briefly**
- **GUMSHOE** [checked]: a core clue is found whenever a character "uses an Investigative Ability relating to that clue". No roll, so no dogpile. https://pelgranepress.com/2017/09/29/gumshoe-rules-summary/
- **Dungeon World** [read]: the GM moves "When they roll a 6-", and the move "should always follow from the fiction". Nothing on a second character trying; the miss has already changed the fiction. https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/gamemastering/
- **Shadowdark** [checked, a fan summary]: crawling rounds; in total darkness, "Check for a random encounter every crawling round". https://ceschini.github.io/memcached/TTRPG/shadowdark/gameplay
- **13th Age** [read, Rob Heinsoo, 2016]: "One of you should give me a skill check". https://pelgranepress.com/2016/06/08/13th-sage-skill-checks-in-13th-age/
- **Savage Worlds** [lead, an older edition]: cooperative rolls, +1 per helper's success, "a normal maximum of +4". **Ironsworn, Genesys, Mothership:** no text found.

## 2. The design answers found

**The problem, in the commentators' words** [checked]
- **The odds:** the Alexandrian (2023): with everyone rolling, "that 70% chance of success suddenly becomes a 96% chance of success".
- **The pace:** also the Alexandrian: "Is it really interesting to have everyone sit there sequentially rolling the check? What a huge drag!"
- **Alphastream (2026):** ask first "Is it possible for more than one PC to make this check?", and "the answer should probably be no". Also: "Five people can't crowd around the lockpick."
- **Sly Flourish:** "When the players see another player fail a check, they want to leap in to make the same check."
- Pages: https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/49184/roleplaying-games/random-gm-tip-dogpiling-the-check ; https://alphastream.org/index.php/2026/05/02/how-many-pcs-can-try-a-skill-check/ ; https://slyflourish.com/ability_check_toolbox.html

**The answers found** (each with its games in table (a) below; only the quotes not given elsewhere are here):
- **The best-suited rolls once:** DM David, "Limit the check to the most knowledgeable character" (https://dmdavid.com/tag/why-bother-having-everyone-roll-a-check-when-it-just-guarantees-success/); Alphastream, "Choose one person to take the lead".
- **Everyone rolls once, read as the party's result:** the Alexandrian's "gradated group check" "interprets the dogpile as a retroactive group check".
- **One die for everyone** [checked]: Bind RPG, "RPGs should avoid asking people to roll dice more than once for any result". The roll stays on the table, each character who has a go reads it with their own modifier, and a low roll means the lock is rusty for everyone. https://bindrpg.gitlab.io/blog/one_roll
- **A per-party limit:** Sly Flourish, "We might argue that only the first attempt could work and subsequent attempts won't succeed."
- **Every failure changes the situation:** the Alexandrian, "the first character to attempt the check can't fail, they can only suffer a consequence".
- **Each attempt spends time and exposure:** DM David, "Often the price of failure comes from losing time against a ticking clock" and "When attempting to break a door, the price of failure is often noise" (https://dmdavid.com/tag/how-to-avoid-boring-rerolls-of-dds-ability-checks/); the Alexandrian, "start or tap a failure clock"; the Angry GM, time "usually doesn't carry a cost unless there's a time limit" or wandering monsters (https://theangrygm.com/gambling-on-action/).
- **The failure breaks the lock or the tool:** Pathfinder 2e's critical failures (a broken toolkit; a jammed door, −2 on later tries; earlier report); Old-School Essentials' lost surprise.
- **Don't roll when failure changes nothing:** Year Zero, "You only roll dice in challenging situations—period"; Vincent Baker's "Roll dice or say yes" [checked, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogs_in_the_Vineyard]; passive scores and Take 10 or 20 put a number on it.

**Is the result the party's attempt?**
- **The result is the party's in:** 5e Group Checks, Draw Steel's group tests and its shared patience, Blades ("covers everyone who rolled"), Daggerheart, Fate ("all of the participants share"), Torchbearer's single turn, Call of Cthulhu's group Luck roll, Bind's one die.
- **The limit is per person in:** Old-School Essentials, Cypher, Year Zero, Draw Steel's retry rule and montages, and 5e 2024's Influence.
- **No game read says "a failed attempt belongs to the party, and nobody may repeat it."** The closest are Draw Steel's negotiation and Sly Flourish's "only the first attempt".

## 3. Social retries and physical retries

**In a social scene, the target remembers, so the limit can sit on the target:** Draw Steel's per-party arguments and shared patience [read]; 5e 2024's "in the same way again" [read]; Cypher's "one chance to convince" [read]; Pathfinder 1e's Diplomacy, once a day per creature, "although other requests might be made" (earlier report) [read]; the Angry GM, "The guard not trusting anything else you say after you get caught in a lie, that's a risk" [checked]. The Alexandrian asks the designer's own question, "Can Samantha step in and smooth things over?", and answers with the gradated group check [checked]. **What they share:** the target carries the failure forward (it refuses, distrusts, loses patience), so a second speaker meets a changed situation; Draw Steel judges by the argument, not the speaker.

**With a lock, nothing remembers, so the limit must sit on the character** (OSE's one try per thief), **the method** (Fun Once's "a different stat"), **the clock** (OSE, Torchbearer, Shadowdark, 5e 2024) **or the object** (Pathfinder 2e's broken tools, OSE's lost surprise); Pathfinder 1e sets none. The two answers written for the lock itself: Alphastream's "Five people can't crowd around the lockpick" and Bind's one die for everyone.

## What seems to transfer

### (a) The shapes of a party-level retry rule

| Shape | Games | How it plays | Known cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The best-suited rolls once, the others help** | 5e Working Together, Fate, Daggerheart, Cypher, YZE, 13th Age (Heinsoo) | One roll; helpers add a bonus | Little for the others to do; a weaker character's retry stays open unless forbidden |
| **Everyone rolls once; read the party's result** | 5e Group Checks, Draw Steel group tests, Blades, Daggerheart, the Alexandrian's gradated check | One round of rolls stands for all | Odds skewed by party size; the designer: "doesn't fit opening a chest" |
| **Allow the dogpile, raise the DC** | PF2e Group Attempts | Everyone tries; the DC expects it | Only the specialist matters |
| **One die for everyone** | Bind RPG | One roll; each reads it with their own modifier | Unusual; the best modifier always decides |
| **Per character, others free** | OSE, YZE, Draw Steel montages, Cypher (as written) | Each may try once | The party tries in turn: the designer's loop |
| **Per party: spent for everyone** | Draw Steel negotiation, Sly Flourish, one Burning Wheel reading | One failure closes that way for all | The best-suited must go first; "the same" is a GM call |
| **Every failure changes the situation** | Fate, Daggerheart, PbtA, Torchbearer, PF2e | Nothing is the same attempt twice | A consequence to invent each time |
| **Each attempt spends the party's time** | OSE, Torchbearer, Shadowdark, 5e 2024 | Retry freely; the clock pays | Needs a clock that bites |
| **A paid retry** | Cypher, YZE, CoC, Blades | Retry if you pay | A plentiful currency brings the loop back |

### (b) For a task whose failure has no stakes (the chest)

- **Skip the roll and name the time:** 5e 2024 DMG p. 28 [checked]; Fate, "just give the [PC]s what they want" [read]; Daggerheart, "if failure would be boring, they succeed automatically" [read]; Baker [checked].
- **Roll once, and read the result as how long it takes:** 5e 2024 [checked]; Take 20, at twenty times the time [read].
- **Time as the only price:** Old-School Essentials and Shadowdark, where every attempt feeds the wandering check [read / checked]; the Alexandrian: "you're spending a lot of time on this lock… what does that mean in the context of the wider world?" [checked].
- **One roll for the party:** Alphastream, DM David, Sly Flourish, 5e's Working Together [checked / read].
- **Lost on a bad failure:**
  - Pathfinder 2e's broken tools [read]; Sly Flourish's "only the first attempt could work" [checked].
  - The Alexandrian: "failing to pick a lock on a door is meaningful because it means the PCs need to find another way to get through the door" [checked]. For a chest, the other way is breaking it or carrying it off.
  - No source read prints "the contents are lost".
- **Extra successes are worth something:** the Alexandrian, "think about how each additional success on a check could provide a benefit" [checked].

### (c) The designer's sentences beside the nearest printed rules

- **"a failed attempt can never stop story from moving":** Fate ("no change in the situation. Do not do this."), Year Zero ("Failure must not stop the story completely."), Draw Steel ("failure on a group test shouldn't bring a story to a halt").
- **"the BBEG gets to prepare, or that makes so much noise it alerts people far away, or lose time":** DM David (noise; "a ticking clock"); Old-School Essentials (a failed forced door loses surprise; turns feed the wandering check); Torchbearer's twist (the patrol arrives while the thief fails the lock); the Alexandrian's failure clock.
- **"a locked chest for loot usually is just extra, so [...] someone else can just try it again":** 5e 2024 ("If failure has no consequences and a character can try and try again, you can skip the ability check"); Fate ("If you can't imagine an interesting outcome from both results, then don't call for that roll.").
- **"group rolls [...] doesn't fit opening a chest":** Alphastream agrees ("Five people can't crowd around the lockpick"); 5e and Draw Steel print group checks only for tasks done together.
- **"make everyone roll for it if Tier 1 just means failure":** Pathfinder 2e allows it and raises the DC; DM David ("someone almost always rolls high"); the Alexandrian (70% becomes 96%).
- **"If tier 1 means failure plus can't be attempted again by anyone":** closest are Draw Steel's negotiation and Sly Flourish's "only the first attempt could work". No rule read for a physical task makes the ban party-wide; the nearest is a broken lock or tool (Pathfinder 2e).
- **"the argument was already given [...] requires some level of good faith and changing of scenes or situations":** Draw Steel ("If the heroes try to use the same argument [...] twice, the test automatically obtains a tier 1 outcome"); 5e 2024 ("in the same way again"); Pathfinder 1e ("other requests might be made"); the Alexandrian (an interruption is not a change).

### (d) What 2d12's files already provide (observations)

- **Routine** (115: 11 + the skill modifier meets the DC, "no threat and no time pressure", a Tier 2 at most) is the "say yes" and passive-score answer. A chest with nothing at stake has neither, so Routine covers it **when the best-suited character's number reaches the DC**; the open case is a chest above every character's Routine number.
- **The help rule** (117: "the one best suited makes the Check; what the others do changes the task or is its own Check") is already the "best-suited rolls once" shape. It says who rolls, not whether someone else may roll after a failure.
- **Tier 1, "no, or yes at a real cost"** (116) is the success-at-a-cost answer: where the cost lands, the next attempt is not the same attempt. A plain "no" leaves the dogpile open.
- **Alert** (`book/ideas/expedition-loop.md` item 9) rises with "a loud event (fire, an explosion, a raised alarm)" and a failed stealth Challenge, and a Sleep in a site is rolled against it. **Time alone raises nothing in the agreed items** (no step per turn or per attempt, unlike OSE's turns or Torchbearer's grind); whether a forced door or a smashed chest is a loud event is not written.
- **"Repeating an approach takes 1 Bane"** in a Challenge (`roleplay-skills.md` item 9) is a price per approach inside a scene, the nearest thing to Draw Steel's per-party negotiation rule.
- **The retry rule in principle** (`decided.md` section 3) names neither the character nor the party; `rules-src` has no retry, group check or group roll text yet.
- **Decision 118's "getting past" and "swaying people"** are section 3's physical and social cases, which the games above handle differently (the clock or the object; the target's memory).

**Could not open or not found:** the 2024 DMG's own page and its group check; Mouse Guard's and Torchbearer's books; Luke Crane's words on the party question; Savage Worlds Adventure Edition, Ironsworn, Genesys, Mothership and 13th Age's core text; the official OSE SRD (HTTP 500; a mirror was read).
