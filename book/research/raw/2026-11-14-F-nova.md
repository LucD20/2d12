## 1. What makes a nova feel good, and what makes it feel bad

**Feels bad: one character ends the climax while the table watches.**
- [EXCERPT] An EN World thread on nerfing smite (5e 2014) has the complaint that the paladin lets one character melt the climactic villain in a single round while everyone else watches; one poster proposes smite once per round "so the big bad boss gets time to act and other players can contribute". The counter-argument in the same thread: the paladin spent a little over half his daily resources to do it, a wizard doing similar damage about a third, so no nerf is needed. https://www.enworld.org/threads/smite-nerf-paladin-buff.551731/page-3
- [EXCERPT] Search summary of a DM David post: a nova can come "at the fun of other players by taking a disproportionate amount of the spotlight", yet "players relish the occasional nova" and it creates memorable moments. https://dmdavid.com/tag/tag/damage/
- [EXCERPT] Nine Sides (5e encounter length): "A combat which lasts for one round or less often feels anticlimactic." https://ninesides.substack.com/p/balancing-combat-for-length-in-d
- [EXCERPT] Mike Mearls (Bluesky, Oct 2025, as reported by Wargamer and EN World): "It's quite likely that a semi-optimized party can vaporize boss monsters in a round or even less." He says 5e assumed about 20 rounds of combat between long rests and "we were probably off by a factor of 5"; the gap between daily and at-will damage is far bigger in 5e than in 4e; the real benefit of a daily ability is "a massive damage spike". https://www.wargamer.com/dnd/mike-mearls-boss-fight and https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=9773794
  - [my reading] This is the cleanest statement of the root cause: a nova is only a problem in proportion to (burst size) / (target's hit points), and it gets worse when daily resources are spent over fewer fights than the designer assumed.

**Feels bad: the best turn comes first, the rest is filler.**
- [EXCERPT] EN World, "Structural Flaw of the D&D Combat System": "the suspense curve is inverted"; boss fights front-load the big moves and then "devolve into a slugfest where the characters spam cantrips and make normal weapon attacks". Posters point to 13th Age's escalation die as the closest fix. https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=8951224
- [EXCERPT] A Xanthir blog post states the logic the optimizer follows: you want to minimize overkill, so it is almost always best to blow your strongest moves immediately; the author suggests big attacks work better when the rules make them better in later rounds. https://www.xanthir.com/b4sE0
- [EXCERPT] Square Fireballs (2012, 4e) on combat pacing and resources makes the same point about the action economy. https://squarefireballs.blogspot.com/2012/11/game-design-iii-combat-pacing-resource.html

**Feels bad: hoarding.**
- [EXCERPT] RPG.net, "[4e] Why do people hate daily powers?": "your only real decision is deciding whether this encounter is worth burning a daily power on". https://forum.rpg.net/threads/4e-why-do-people-hate-daily-powers.524624/
- [EXCERPT] EN World smite threads: paladins "just aren't bothered about their spells. They save them for boss fights"; players stop casting spells "to save them for crits to use divine smite". https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=8952236 and https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=8814691
- [EXCERPT] Game-Wisdom, "Combating the Hoarding Syndrome": a scarce single-use resource is kept "in case they would need them later" until the fight is lost; the fixes listed are cheap and plentiful resources, a limit per battle instead of per item, and resources with short-term weight and no long-term punishment. https://game-wisdom.com/critical/hoarding-game-design
- [READ] Draw Steel's answer in the rules: the Heroic Resource is gained every turn and "You lose any remaining wrath at the end of the encounter." Nothing can be saved for tomorrow, so nothing is hoarded. (Censor, "Wrath in Combat".) https://github.com/SteelCompendium/data-rules-md/blob/main/Classes/Censor.md

**Feels bad: the big hit misses.** See section 6.

**Feels good (what defenders say).**
- [EXCERPT] "Crits are not that common and crits when it really matters are even less common, let them be awesome" (EN World, smite changes). https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=8952585
- [EXCERPT] Gloomhaven players on loss cards: "the big guns you break out to seal the deal, or to put a desperate situation back on track". https://forum.quartertothree.com/t/gloomhaven-tactical-combat-in-a-persistent-world/133841?page=14
- [my reading] The two things defenders name are **choosing the moment** and **the swing** (ending a threat, or turning a losing fight). Nobody in these excerpts defends the nova as a routine opener.

## 2. Pacing models

### Daily resources (5e slots and smites)
- [READ] 5e 2024 rules: Divine Smite is a level 1 spell, "Casting Time: Bonus Action, which you take immediately after hitting a target with a Melee weapon or an Unarmed Strike"; 2d8 Radiant, +1d8 against a Fiend or Undead, +1d8 per slot level above 1. Paladin's Smite (level 2): always prepared, one free cast per Long Rest. (SRD 5.2, copy at https://github.com/Mnehmos/The-Worlds-Largest-Dungeon/tree/main/Resources/markdown)
- [EXCERPT] Nimble's design updates describe the failure: optimized players "go nova" with their top slots in each fight and rest after every encounter. https://nimble5e.backerkit.com/hosted_preorders/project_updates?page=4
- [EXCERPT] The Angry GM argues the short workday is an adventure-design problem more than a system problem. https://theangrygm.com/the-gms-burden-and-other-stories
- [EXCERPT] Alphastream: 4e largely avoided it through encounter powers. https://alphastream.org/index.php/2015/10/17/interlude-extending-the-five-minute-workday/

### Encounter powers and the 4e nova round
- [READ] 4e glossary: adventurers start each day with 1 action point; spending one is a free action that gives "an extra action to use during its current turn"; a milestone (two encounters without an extended rest) gives another; an extended rest resets to 1. So the 4e nova round is daily + action point + encounter power, and the system pays you an action point for *not* resting. (Local copy of the 4e compendium; "once per encounter" for action points is from memory, unverified.)
- [EXCERPT] A community guide "The Big Bang: A Guide to Novas" existed for 4e; a blog on 4e solos says "a typical party's dailies could overwhelm nearly any monster in the first turn" and recommends adding other monsters, even minions. http://www.dyasdesigns.com/dragon_phoenix/The%20Big%20Bang%20%20A%20Guide%20to%20Novas%20_%20The%20Wizards%20Community.htm and https://dd4sign.blogspot.com/2012/03/solo-design.html

### Resources that build during the fight
- [READ] **Draw Steel, Censor (the closest thing to our Paladin).** Wrath: "you gain wrath equal to your Victories" at the start of a combat; "At the start of each of your turns during combat, you gain 2 wrath"; +1 "the first time each combat round that a creature judged by you deals damage to you"; +1 "the first time each combat round that you deal damage to a creature judged by you"; all lost at the end of the encounter. Heroic abilities cost 3, 5, 7, 9, 11 wrath; signature abilities are free. https://github.com/SteelCompendium/data-rules-md/blob/main/Classes/Censor.md
  - [my reading] Arithmetic: in the day's first fight (0 Victories) a Censor who marks a target and trades blows with it has 4 wrath in round 1, 8 in round 2, 12 in round 3. The 9-wrath attack is a round 3 event unless he skipped the 3 and 5 cost abilities. Later in the day, Victories pull the big turn earlier. So the build is paid for by doing the class's job (mark one enemy, hit it, be hit by it), not by waiting.
- [READ] Other Draw Steel classes use the same frame with a different trigger: Fury gains 1d3 Ferocity per turn, +1 the first time each round he takes damage, +1d3 the first time he becomes winded or dying (a comeback trigger). Shadow gains Insight and pays 1 less for a heroic ability when he has an edge on it (setup rewarded with a discount). Conduit can pray for more Piety and on a 1 takes 1d6 + level damage (gamble). https://github.com/SteelCompendium/data-rules-md/tree/main/Classes
- [EXCERPT] MCDM's own framing: characters build heroic resources "so battles get more interesting round by round"; a review: "as the fight goes on the heroes get stronger, not weaker". https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/draw-steel-heroes-review/ (also in our tactical-combat.md)
- [EXCERPT] An EN World critic reports that a very variable resource gain "was death when trying to plan your turn" (Draw Steel playtest feedback, as told by a poster). https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=9705911
- [EXCERPT] EN World, "Gaining spell slots (and other resources) during the fight": with a building resource players can "slowly use up their momentum as it is accumulated or save it for one big action several rounds in". https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=9778490
- [READ] **Lancer, Overcharge** (a nova that costs more each time): "Once per turn, you can OVERCHARGE your mech, allowing you to make any quick action as a free action... The first time you OVERCHARGE, take 1 heat. The second time... 1d3 heat. The third time, take 1d6 heat, and each time thereafter take 1d6+4 heat. A FULL REPAIR resets this counter." https://github.com/massif-press/lancer-data/blob/master/lib/actions.json
- [READ] Lancer, Nuclear Cavalier talent: the first attack each turn "while in the Danger Zone" (heat at half or more) deals +2 Heat, then +1d6 Energy. Bonus damage for sitting in a risky state. Duelist: "1/round, when you hit with a Main Melee weapon, you gain 1 Blademaster Die... up to a maximum of 3", spent on a parry, a deflect or a free grapple. A small capped pool built by hitting, spent mostly on defense. https://github.com/massif-press/lancer-data/blob/master/lib/talents.json
- [EXCERPT] Gloomhaven (loss cards): players split between "the primary threat in Gloomhaven is my character running out of cards", exhaustion as "a horrible foundation for a game design", and the defense that loss cards "if you use them wisely" finish scenarios faster. The Spellweaver draws the most complaints because most of her good powers are losses. https://forum.quartertothree.com/t/gloomhaven-tactical-combat-in-a-persistent-world/133841?page=12
  - [my reading] Gloomhaven's burn is the purest "use all they have" cost: the nova shortens your own clock. People who dislike it dislike that the cost is paid in *turns you no longer get to play*.

### Setup or cooldown novas
- [READ] **PF2e Magus, Spellstrike:** one activity that casts a spell and Strikes; "use your Strike's results to determine the effects of both the Strike and the spell"; then "you can't do so again until you recharge your Spellstrike as a single action". A nova every turn at the price of one action of setup, all on one roll. https://github.com/foundryvtt/pf2e/blob/master/packs/pf2e/actions/class/magus/spellstrike.json
- [EXCERPT] Paizo forums on the Magus: posters "don't like the feast/famine aspect"; a 2025 thread wants more ways to recharge; one poster rejects a recharge on a critical hit as "too feast-or-famine for an already feast-or-famine class". https://paizo.com/threads/rzs436ol and https://paizo.com/threads/rzs6qm09&page=2?Classes=
- [READ] **PF2e Swashbuckler:** panache is gained by performing a stylish (bravado) action; finishers "can be used only while you have panache and cause you to lose your panache". Precise Strike: +2 precision damage on a normal Strike, 2d6 on a finisher (scaling to +6 and 6d6). Confident Finisher on a failure still deals "half your Precise Strike damage". https://github.com/foundryvtt/pf2e/tree/master/packs/pf2e/class-features
- [EXCERPT] The remaster changed the gain: on a failed (not critically failed) bravado check "you gain panache but only until the end of your next turn"; Paizo's stated goal was to "increase the consistency of the class to allow for more stylish moments"; players like that immune enemies no longer shut the class down; some worry finishers now happen almost every round. https://paizo.com/community/blog/v5748dyo6uw72 and https://paizo.com/threads/rzs43u5o&page=2
  - [my reading] Paizo's lesson, twice: a setup that can fail before the payoff that can also fail is two chances to have a null turn. They softened the setup, not the payoff.
- [READ] **PF2e Investigator, Devise a Stratagem:** "Roll a d20, then decide"; if you Strike the target you "must use the result of the d20 roll"; if the roll is bad you take the skill option instead. The player knows the big hit's roll before committing the action. https://github.com/foundryvtt/pf2e/blob/master/packs/pf2e/actions/class/investigator/devise-a-stratagem.json
- [EXCERPT] **4e Avenger, Oath of Enmity:** "the Avenger's answer to Sneak Attack, Hunter's Mark... Instead of boosting damage, it boosts accuracy by giving you the better of two attack rolls." Fans call the double roll "fantastic"; critics say the class is the only striker "that gets nothing extra". https://www.tribality.com/2016/01/21/the-avenger-part-one-and-onlyish/ and https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=5654507
- 5e Battle Master dice, the rogue's Assassinate opener: (from memory, unverified) superiority dice are spent after the hit and recover on a short rest; Assassinate depends on surprise and initiative and is widely called unreliable for that reason. Not researched this session.

## 3. "Use all they have": stacking, and how designers cap it

**What the uncapped version looked like (5e 2014).**
- (from memory, unverified) Divine Smite 2014: no action, declared after a hit, any number per turn, one slot each, dice doubled on a critical hit.
- [EXCERPT] EN World: smite on a crit is "far more potent than it should be"; "smite and single classed sneak damage spikes are a problem with crits"; a proposed fix is to commit before the roll, which "simply prevents exploitation of crits". One poster calls deciding after the roll "a perversion of the gameplay loop where a player changes or waits to declare their action with a retcon after seeing the result". https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=8962246 and https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=6720384
- [EXCERPT] "Divine Smite: from class feature to bonus action tax" (2025 blog): the old smite was "the most efficient resource dump in the game". https://dumbestdnd.com/p/divine-smite-5e-from-class-feature-to-spell/

**The 2024 cap and the reaction.**
- [READ] The rule: bonus action, right after a hit (text in section 2). One bonus action per turn, so one smite per turn, none on an opportunity attack made in another creature's turn (a bonus action is taken only on your own turn; from memory, unverified), and it competes with every other bonus action (the 2024 Lay on Hands is a bonus action too, per the excerpt below).
- [EXCERPT] Jeremy Crawford to ComicBook: "with Divine Smite in particular, we realized very early on in Fifth Edition that frankly the Paladin was too powerful"; "When we originally designed Divine Smite, it was under the hood, basically a spell"; "we did a holistic change because we wanted Paladins to feel like they actually had a range of cool options." https://comicbook.com/gaming/news/dungeons-dragons-paladin-divine-smite-changes/ and https://www.cbr.com/dnd-paladin-divine-smite-5e-change-explained/
- [EXCERPT] Reaction, critics: "They could've just let it be once a turn. A bonus action AND a spell is just such a nerf"; make it "a once per turn ability... costing an appropriate level spell slot (think Sneak Attack, but with a spell cost)"; a guide regrets losing "the ability to go supernova on a BBG and blitz them with 3 Smites in a single turn". https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/class-forums/paladin/223052-2024-paladin-thoughts?comment=41 and https://dungeonsanddragonsfan.com/new-2024-paladin-dnd-5e-class-changes/
- [EXCERPT] Reaction, defenders: "undoubtedly a nerf to divine smite, but it's packaged with a number of buffs"; "a well designed nerf"; smite damage "really was the thing that set the Paladin SO much higher above the other classes". https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/d-d-beyond-general/general-discussion/199755-discussion-new-paladin-2024-players-handbook?comment=27 and https://lr.sudovanilla.org/r/onednd/comments/1e3ywu3/some_folks_here_are_underrating_the_new_paladin
  - [my reading] Across these excerpts the community accepted **once per turn** and rejected **the action tax**. The complaint is not "I can't stack three", it is "my smite now blocks my heal and my other tricks". For a class that is also meant to be "soft support", putting the burst and the aid on the same action slot is the thing to avoid, or to do on purpose.

**13th Age's cap.**
- [READ] Smite Evil: "once per battle, plus an additional number of times per day equal to your Charisma modifier. As a free action before you make a paladin melee attack roll, you can declare that you're using a Smite Evil attack. Add +1d12 to the damage roll AND deal half damage with the attack if it misses." Feats: +4 to hit on the smite; 2d12 then 4d12; and a talent line, "When one of your Smite Evil attacks drops a non-mook enemy to 0 hp, that use of Smite Evil is not expended." https://github.com/Obsidian-TTRPG-Community/13th-Age-SRD-Markdown/blob/main/Compendium/13A/Classes/Paladin.md
  - [my reading] Four ideas in one small feature: a floor of one per fight (never an empty fight), a small daily reserve on top (the "dump" decision still exists), declared before the roll but protected by half damage on a miss (no retcon, no whiff), and a refund on a kill (rewards the finisher use over the opener use).
- [EXCERPT] A 13th Age forum poster's summary of the trade: "Smite Evil does less face value damage in exchange for higher reliable damage." 13th Age 2e gives every paladin a Smite power "to augment their melee attacks at choice moments". https://www.tapatalk.com/groups/13thage/do-you-feel-the-paladin-is-a-tiny-little-bit-under-t4281-s10.html and https://pelgranepress.com/2026/05/28/13th-age-2e-pregenerated-character-builds-gallant-paladin/

**Draw Steel's caps (all [READ]).**
- The pool itself: you cannot spend what you have not built this fight, and the top costs take two or three rounds.
- The action economy: one main action a turn, so one heroic strike a turn, unless you roll a critical hit (natural 19 or 20 on a main action), which "allows you to immediately take an additional main action".
- Surges, the stackable part: "Whenever you deal rolled damage, you can spend up to 3 surges to deal extra damage... Each surge you spend deals extra damage equal to your highest characteristic score." A hard cap of three per damage roll, and surges are lost at the end of combat. This is the game's "dump" dial: a second, small pool poured onto one hit, capped at 3. https://github.com/SteelCompendium/data-rules-md/blob/main/Chapters/Classes.md
- The Talent can overspend: "You can spend clarity you don't have... to a maximum negative value equal to 1 + your Reason score. At the end of each of your turns, you take 1 damage for each negative point". A nova on credit, repaid in health. https://github.com/SteelCompendium/data-rules-md/blob/main/Classes/Talent.md

**What a capped nova keeps of the fun** [my reading of the above]: the choice of the moment and the size of the swing survive a cap; what the caps remove is multiplication (crit times smite times extra attacks) and the one-round boss kill. The designs people praise keep one loud stacking lever with a low ceiling (three surges; one smite a turn; one 13th Age smite a fight plus a few a day).

## 4. "Explode before they see it coming": opening burst or turn-around

- The optimizer's default is the opener: [EXCERPT] "it's almost always best to blow your strongest moves immediately" to avoid overkill and shorten the fight (Xanthir). https://www.xanthir.com/b4sE0 And the table's complaint is exactly that: the "inverted suspense curve" (section 1).
- Designs that push the burst later, all read as rules:
  - [READ] Draw Steel: resource grows each turn; later fights of the day start higher (Victories). The burst moves earlier only as the adventuring day goes on.
  - [READ] Draw Steel Fury: +1d3 Ferocity the first time he becomes winded or dying. A "bloodied" comeback trigger on the hero.
  - [READ] 13th Age paladin: the smite is refunded if it drops a non-mook, so the best use is as a finisher.
  - [READ] 4e: an action point for every second encounter without a rest; the nova is a reward for pressing on.
  - 13th Age escalation die: already in our escalation-and-guard.md.
- [EXCERPT] On comeback triggers: TV Tropes' warning that a very strong comeback mechanic makes "performance outside of this one mechanic... pointless"; an EN World thread on damage models notes players may "just stay bloodied at all times" if the state pays. https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/ComebackMechanic and https://www.enworld.org/threads/tink-tink-boom-vs-the-death-spiral-the-damage-mechanic-in-rpgs.665644/page-4
- [EXCERPT] Mearls's 2025 direction for bosses is on the monster side: accept the spike and build bosses that survive it. https://www.wargamer.com/dnd/mike-mearls-boss-fight
- **I found no essay that argues head-on "late bursts are better drama than opening bursts".** The claim is implied by the "inverted suspense curve" thread, by 13th Age's escalation die and by Draw Steel's marketing line, but I could not open a page that makes the argument in full. Gap.

**The cost side: exposed after the dump.**
- [READ] Lancer: each Overcharge costs more heat; heat leads to the Danger Zone and overheating.
- [READ] Draw Steel Talent: negative clarity deals damage to you each turn and makes you "strained".
- [READ] PF2e Swashbuckler: the finisher ends panache (and, from memory, unverified: no more attack actions that turn).
- [READ] PF2e Magus: after the Spellstrike, one action of the next turn goes to recharging.
- [EXCERPT] Gloomhaven: the burned card shortens your whole scenario.
  - [my reading] Two families of cost. "Empty" (you have nothing left: 5e slots, Gloomhaven) and "exposed" (you are worse at defending for a while: heat, strain). For a class whose normal job is to stand in front, "exposed" is the more interesting cost: the turn after the explosion the front line is thinner, and the party has to cover its own tank for a round. That is a table moment. "Empty" just makes the remaining turns dull.

## 5. Burst for a class that is not a consistent damage dealer

**The model that fits the brief best: Draw Steel's Censor** [READ].
- Normal turns are a mark and its upkeep. Judgment is a maneuver: one enemy is judged; when it uses a main action the Censor may deal holy damage equal to twice his Presence as a free triggered action; for 1 wrath he can stop it from shifting away, give its power roll a bane, lower a potency, or taunt it after a melee hit.
- The mark is also the generator: +1 wrath when the judged creature damages him, +1 when he damages it. The class is paid for standing in front of the worst enemy.
- The pool is spent either way: on small defensive triggers (1 wrath each) or on a big strike (9 wrath, 10/14/20 + Might). Tank, support and burst draw on **one pool**, so every round has the question "protect now or save for the blow".
- Its 9-wrath list is half support: "Shield of the Righteous" (the same 10/14/20 damage plus 10/15/20 temporary Stamina to him and adjacent allies) and "Congregation" (8/12/16 damage and an ally, or two, strike the target for free). The nova of a soft-nova class can be a *team* nova. https://github.com/SteelCompendium/data-rules-md/blob/main/Classes/Censor.md

**Other patterns seen in the rules read.**
- Lancer Duelist [READ]: dice gained on a hit (max 3), spent on parry or deflect. Counters that buy defense, with a hard cap.
- PF2e Swashbuckler [READ]: the setup action is itself useful (Tumble Through, Demoralize and the like), so a setup turn is a support turn.
- 4e Avenger [EXCERPT]: the mark buys accuracy instead of damage; steady, not spiky. Liked for feel, criticized as "nothing extra".

**What players say about dull off-turns.**
- [EXCERPT] Crawford's own reason for the 2024 change: the paladin was too often in a position where the right choice was simply to smite; they wanted "a range of cool options". https://comicbook.com/gaming/news/dungeons-dragons-paladin-divine-smite-changes/
- [EXCERPT] Paizo, Magus: the turn is rigid (Spellstrike, recharge, repeat). https://paizo.com/threads/rzs436ol
- Our ability-scope.md already covers rote turns.

**Balance heuristics.** I found no page that states "a nova should equal X rounds of normal damage". What exists:
- [EXCERPT] Nine Sides: set enemy hit points as (party damage per round) x (rounds you want the fight to last); a one-round fight is the anticlimax case. https://ninesides.substack.com/p/balancing-combat-for-length-in-d
- [EXCERPT] An RPG Site thread: a burst and a damage-over-time effect of equal total are not equal; the burst is worth more because the fight may end first, so the up-front option should carry less total. https://therpgsite.com/pen-paper-roleplaying-games-rpgs-discussion/resolving-combat-in-fewer-rounds
- [EXCERPT] Pelgrane's "13th Sage: Player Character Damage Math" exists and is the likeliest place for at-will / per-battle / daily ratios. Could not open. https://pelgranepress.com/2017/03/01/13th-sage-player-character-damage-math/
- [my reading] Ratios measured from the rules text I read:
  - Draw Steel Censor, same tier, ignoring the characteristic bonus: signature strike about 3/5/8, top 3-wrath strike 5/8/11, top 5-wrath strike 8/12/15, 9-wrath strike 10/14/20. **The biggest single-target hit is about 2.5 times a free strike's dice**, before up to 3 surges. A modest multiple, on purpose: the fight's damage is spread, the peak is felt but does not delete a boss.
  - PF2e Swashbuckler at level 1: +2 on a normal Strike, +2d6 (7) on a finisher; at level 17, +6 against 6d6 (21). **The finisher's bonus is about 3.5 times the normal bonus**, once per panache.
  - 13th Age: +1d12 on one attack per battle at level 1 (weapon damage there is 1d8 or 1d10 per level, from memory, unverified), so **roughly double one attack**.
  - 5e 2014 for contrast (from memory, unverified): two attacks, both smiting with top slots, on a crit, can exceed 4 to 6 times a normal turn, which is the range that produced the complaints in section 1.
  - So the capped novas that people accept sit around **2 to 3.5 times a normal hit, once per fight or once per build-up**; the one that got nerfed could go well past that and could be repeated in the same turn.

## 6. Accuracy of the big hit

Five mechanisms, with the rules as read.

1. **Decide after the hit** (5e smite, both versions). [READ for 2024.] The resource is never wasted on a miss. Cost: it invites crit-fishing and the "retcon" complaint (section 3), and it removes the tension of the roll.
2. **Half or reduced effect on a miss** (13th Age smite: "deal half damage with the attack if it misses"; PF2e Confident Finisher: half the precision damage on a failure; 4e dailies commonly "Miss: Half damage", from memory, unverified). [READ for the first two.] Note 13th Age's limit: "Rolling a natural 1 has no effect on the target, not even miss damage." A 5% true whiff is kept on purpose. https://github.com/Obsidian-TTRPG-Community/13th-Age-SRD-Markdown/tree/main/Compendium/13A/Combat-Rules/Attacks
3. **The resource is refunded on a miss** (4e Reliable). [READ] "If a reliable power misses every target, the power is not expended." The turn is lost but the nova is not. [EXCERPT] The matching complaint about non-reliable dailies: "you can't recharge a daily that hit [missed]... you're out of luck". https://www.enworld.org/threads/too-many-silos.319004/#post-5834465
4. **See the roll first, then pick the action** (PF2e Devise a Stratagem [READ]; 13th Age flexible attacks [READ]: "make your attack roll, and then use the natural unmodified die result to determine which of your eligible flexible attacks to use"). The big move is chosen to fit the roll.
5. **Accuracy as the payoff of the setup** (4e Avenger's two rolls [EXCERPT]; 13th Age's +4 to hit feat on the smite [READ]; Draw Steel Shadow's discount of 1 insight when he has an edge [READ]).
6. **No miss at all: Draw Steel's tiers.** [READ] "Tier 1: ... 11 or lower... This is the worst outcome a power roll can have. If you're using an ability, a tier 1 outcome means you still do something, but the impact of what you do is minimal." Tier 2 is 12 to 16, tier 3 is 17+, and a natural 19 or 20 is always tier 3. There is also "Downgrade a Power Roll": you may take a lower tier's result when you prefer its effect. https://github.com/SteelCompendium/data-rules-md/blob/main/Chapters/The%20Basics.md

**The comparison that matters for a 3-Tier game** [my reading, measured from the Censor's abilities]:
- On damage, **tier 1 is about half of tier 3 and tier 2 about 70%**: 5/8/11, 10/14/20, 8/12/16, 6/9/13. The floor is high. A 9-wrath strike at tier 1 (10 + M) still out-damages a free signature strike at tier 3 (8 + M). So in Draw Steel the spend itself guarantees the nova is bigger than a normal turn; the roll only decides how much bigger.
- Riders are where tier 1 bites: conditions are gated by potency (P < WEAK at tier 1, < AVERAGE at tier 2, < STRONG at tier 3), so a tier 1 big hit usually lands its damage and loses its control.
- Some top abilities keep the same rider at every tier and scale only the number ("Arrest": grabbed at all three tiers; "Shield of the Righteous": 10/15/20 temporary Stamina). The more a power costs, the flatter its tiers tend to be.
- Abilities with no roll at all exist among the big ones ("Effect:" only), so the resource is the only price.
- [EXCERPT] The stated philosophy, second-hand: "no null results"; "missing as a player in D&D feels bad. Getting missed by a monster in D&D feels awesome." A review notes the principle breaks down in the skill system, not in combat. https://altoholism.blogspot.com/2026/04/null-results-and-gothic-monster-hunting.html and https://splitparty.substack.com/p/draw-steel-part-2
- I searched for complaints that a tier 1 result "still feels like a miss" and found none in the results; that does not mean they don't exist (Reddit was unreachable).

## What this suggests for the Paladin (my judgment, for Lucas to accept or drop)

Other games' answers, not 2d12's rules.

1. **Build in the fight, lose it at the end.** It removes hoarding and the short workday in one move, and it makes the burst a mid-fight event without any rule that forbids an opening burst.
2. **Let the class's normal job feed the pool**: marking a profane target, being hit by it, protecting an ally. Then the turns before the explosion are the "soft tank, soft support" turns, and the explosion is their receipt.
3. **One pool for defense, aid and burst**, so "use all they have" is a real sentence: what he pours into the blow is what he would have protected people with. That is also the "exposed after the dump" cost, with no extra rule.
4. **Cap by one loud lever with a low ceiling** (Draw Steel's "up to 3 surges" is the model), once per turn. Keep it off the action slot that the support uses, unless the trade is intended: that is the one part of the 2024 smite the community rejected.
5. **Size**: the accepted capped novas are about 2 to 3.5 times a normal hit. Above that, bosses need to be built to survive it (Mearls's point).
6. **Tiers**: make the worst Tier of the big hit beat a normal hit's best Tier, or close; let the Tiers scale the number and put the gamble in the rider. If a true whiff is wanted for drama, keep it rare and explicit, as 13th Age keeps the natural 1.
7. **Finisher over opener**: a refund or a bonus when the blow drops its target (13th Age) or when the target is already wounded pushes the explosion to the turn-around moment without forbidding anything.
8. **"Before seeing it coming"**: in every game read here the build is visible to the table (a pool, a die, heat). The surprise is for the enemy in the fiction, not for the players. None of the sources supports a hidden or random trigger as good play.
