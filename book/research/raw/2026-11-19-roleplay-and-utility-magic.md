# Roleplay and magic outside combat: scene-breakers, fun utility, magic that marks its user, magic and the party

2026-11-19

## The question

Lucas, before designing magic outside combat, asked for "info about roleplay and magic out of combat". His standing words: spells should be "unique, cool sounding, you can make arguments for out of combat uses" (decision 49); growth is horizontal; the least ambiguity possible. And decision 106: "I think it's an amazing idea that Paths can cause changes on the user just by them having that knowledge."

**These are other games' rules and other people's opinions, never 2d12's rules. Nothing here is a design.**

## 1. Scene-breakers and how games tame them

### 1.1 The frame: information is the play, not its enemy

- The Alexandrian's answer to "the PCs will just cast speak with dead" [checked]: "the actual action of a mystery is not the withholding of knowledge but rather the discovery of knowledge." The spell "should be no more alarming than an FBI team taking fingerprints". He rejects nerfs: "Nothing is more frustrating to a player than having their smart choices blocked". His fix is on the scenario's side: "design your mysteries to the reality of the spell", because people in the world know the spell exists and "will find ways to conceal their identity" or use it to frame someone.
- A spell breaks a mystery only if "(a) it trivializes all other methods of acquiring information and/or (b) short circuits a specific investigation" [checked, the Zone of Truth post]. "Zone of truth can't short circuit the investigation if the investigation is about figuring who you need to question in the first place."
- Paizo's own rules text agrees and names the stance [read, *Ultimate Intrigue*]: the GM "should apply the principle of 'yes, but. . .' rather than simply saying 'yes' or 'no.'" A divination should "provide clues more than answers".
- GUMSHOE's premise is the same from the other side [lead]: play is about interpreting clues, so core clues are given without a roll.
- The dissent, from a commenter under the Alexandrian post [checked]: this "makes designing mysteries harder than they already are" for a new GM facing "thousands of D&D spells". The burden moves to the GM unless the spell's own text carries the limit.

### 1.2 Family by family

**Divination and truth (mysteries, intrigue).**
- What the printed limits are [read, 5e SRD]: Speak with Dead gives five questions; "The corpse knows only what it knew in life"; "Answers are usually brief, cryptic, or repetitive"; no compulsion to be truthful to an enemy; a mouth is required; once per ten days per corpse. Zone of Truth: "An affected creature is aware of the spell and can thus avoid answering questions"; it "can be evasive in its answers as long as it remains within the boundaries of the truth"; ten minutes. Commune: three yes or no questions; "Divine beings aren't necessarily omniscient"; a growing chance of no answer when repeated in a day.
- What *Ultimate Intrigue* adds [read]: a locating spell needs knowledge ("Characters can't attempt to locate something they don't even know exists"; a unique item must have been seen firsthand); "a thin sheet of lead" blocks it; Detect Thoughts shows "a character's immediate concerns", not the secret; Commune's agent knows its god's portfolio, which "can also lead the PCs to find a cleric of a more appropriate deity", a roleplay step; Detect Scrying turns a scry into information for the target ("a great way to say 'yes, but'"); a murderer "can cast speak with dead herself to forestall future castings". And a warning: an NPC who "conveniently" has the counter only when the PCs act "is sloppy"; a counter must be a habit of the world, or the players stop believing it.
- The Alexandrian's table fixes for Zone of Truth [checked]: several suspects refuse (each for an unrelated secret), so refusal narrows without closing; a culture or a law where casting on someone is "unacceptably rude or unethical"; whether the PCs have the authority to question; "set a timer" on the ten minutes; the "incomplete answer" to the catch-all question.
- The dangerous cases, by two writers [checked]: effects that "contact omniscient beings and receive crystal clear information" (Alexandrian); "It's usually OK if PCs are allowed just one question, but if this is a power they have repeat access to then it becomes very hard to maintain any kind of mystery" (udan-adan). Repeatable and certain is the break; once and partial is a lead.

**Charm and compulsion (social play).** `charm-confusion-summons.md` has the split. New here: 5e's printed aftertaste, "When the spell ends, the creature knows it was charmed by you" [read]; *Ultimate Intrigue*: charm "is not a compulsion", a friend "won't quit her entire life's goal just because a friend asked"; Suggestion must be "presented in a reasonable fashion", which rewards knowing the NPC's motives; Dominate is easy for bystanders to notice (Sense Motive DC 15) [read]. Volume is its own break [checked, udan-adan]: "We hide in the bushes and cast Charm Person on him twelve times!" His replacement: "the ability to scale up a specific emotion requires much more care to use effectively."

**Teleport, flight, shelters (travel, survival, the dungeon as a route).**
- Flight [checked, udan-adan]: at-will flight "is a genie that's really hard to get back in its bottle", "trivialising everything from difficult terrain ('I fly over it') to tripwires". His good version is Levitation: "Slow, vertical-only", needing rope and helpers. The Arcane Library [checked]: "Any adventures of 5th-level or higher must assume the characters can fly", so design some encounters that benefit from flight and some that don't.
- Teleport's printed limits [read, *Ultimate Intrigue*]: the caster "needs to know both the layout of the destination as well as where it is physically located". A blog's table habit [checked, Throwing Irregular Shapes]: teleport-circle addresses are "closely guarded", and wizards keep "large collections of very carefully labelled rocks" because an object from the place removes the error. The limit became a hobby.
- Shelters [checked, Tribality]: the 5e Tiny Hut is "a zero-cost solution to what's often intended to be a meaningful part of the ongoing challenge", and "the possibility of that attack informs where the PCs even try to rest". The cost that vanished was the ritual tag: no slot, only time.

**Food, water, light, healing (scarcity).** Goodberry: ten berries, each "enough nourishment to sustain a creature for one day" [read]. Forum reception [lead]: it "makes survival trivial"; the usual answer is that most tables do not track food anyway. Fixes on record: none in rules text found; house rules shorten it or cost a component [lead].

**Resurrection (stakes).** See `revival.md`.

**Invisibility, disguise, shapechange (infiltration).** udan-adan's line [checked]: invisibility is fine "if it genuinely is only invisibility - meaning that you still make sounds, leave scent trails, make footprints"; "Partial invisibility (e.g. you still cast a shadow) is even better." Disguises "are best when they perfectly mimic appearance but only appearance: when it comes to mimicking voice, gait, mannerisms, knowledge, etc, the PCs are on their own!" Intangibility is good one way ("you can walk through the walls to get in, but then how are you going to get out?") and bad unlimited.

**The lock and the rogue (one more family).** 5e's Knock opens automatically, and its limit is a consequence, not a failure chance: "a loud knock, audible from as far away as 300 feet" [read]. Reception is split between "great when there is no rogue" and "not worth the slot" [lead].

### 1.3 The rarity tag

Pathfinder 2e marks options common, uncommon, rare, unique. Seifter's stated reasons [checked, "Common Ground"]: worldbuilding ("What would a world be like where the wizard was uncommon, or where all healing magic was rare?"), a smaller load on the GM ("If a particular rare spell hasn't shown up in the game, you don't have to worry about how it might interact with... your NPC's plot"), and rewards ("Uncommon options make amazing rewards to find in adventures"). That uncommon spells are mostly the plot-breakers (teleport, scrying, quick divinations) is a forum paraphrase of an organized-play statement [lead], not in the blog. Complaints [lead, Paizo forums]: the tag "covers too many distinct concepts" and assignments look inconsistent.

## 2. What makes utility magic fun

**The one essay that states principles** is Against the Wicked City [checked], from five years of weekly play "with a heavy focus on exploration, problem-solving, and diplomacy". Its opening fact: players "regularly forget which magical weapons their PCs are carrying. What they never, ever forget is exactly who is wearing the Ring of Invisibility to Undead." Its test, in the two lists' headings: "abilities that facilitate creative and intelligent play" against "abilities that short-circuit play without significant limitations". What the good list shares:

1. **It is a component, not the plan.** Invisibility is "more likely to be used as an ingenious component of a cunning plan rather than as a slam-dunk victory condition".
2. **It needs set-up or a helper.** Levitation "requires careful set-up"; darkness to cover an escape asks "how will you see while inside it?"
3. **It has failure points the players must guard.** Of illusions: "the lengths that PCs will go to in order to make sure no-one reveals the trick by just touching their illusionary treasure... are the stuff that truly insane PC plans are made of."
4. **It makes a new situation rather than a result.** Weather, fog, heat: "not inherently beneficial in and of itself, but capable of creating a new situation".
5. **It opens routes.** Water breathing lets "locations... be connected together in new ways"; tunnelling through loose dirt only, "at semi-realistic speeds".
6. **It opens conversations.** "The more things your PCs can interact with, the better. Let them talk to monsters. To animals. To rocks, plants, corpses, water, air... As long as whatever they're talking to is not guaranteed to be useful or co-operative". His example: "OK, how do we bribe the trees into helping us?"
7. **It is narrow and absolute.** "Very specific immunities: Complete immunity to fire... allow situations to be approached in completely different ways."
8. **It is the small version of a scene-breaker.** Surface thoughts and emotions give "an exploitable edge in social situations without having to worry about every mystery collapsing on contact".

**Talking spells as scene generators.**
- *Ultimate Intrigue* [read]: "even the most meticulous murderer rarely thinks of a houseplant as a witness", but animals and plants "look at the world in a different way than people do". The limit is the witness's mind, which is roleplay, not a number.
- The Alexandrian's players keep a "dead-icated bag" of corpses to question again; "the fun part is figuring out all of the gruesome ways these mangled and half-rotten corpses speak" [checked]. One spell was debated "for the better part of half an hour" for its three questions: a small count of questions makes the table talk to each other.
- The cost of no limit [checked, Arcane Library]: "a druid used speak with animals and a few pointed questions to uncover the adventure's surprise villain within the first ten minutes."
- Anecdotes of animals and skulls as recurring NPCs are [lead] (forum threads).

**Cantrip menus.** D&D Beyond's own article [checked]: Prestidigitation "really shines when a spellcaster finds a creative use for one of its many effects and not when used as a replacement for other cantrips". An EN World thread on the 2024 menu cantrips [checked] shows what players do with a closed list: they argue from the fiction of each line (dimming torches as "a mass blindness spell", a false scent for wolves, tremors that blind tremorsense, "allowed... as it made sense to me, even if not specified under the rules"). A menu invites rulings at its edges; that is the fun and the ambiguity together.

**Cost that comes from the same pool** [checked, Throwing Irregular Shapes]: utility spells that spend the combat resource give ordinary encounters "strategic weight". The opposite case is 5e's ritual tag (the Tiny Hut above).

**Toolbox against solution, in one line each (my sorting of the sources above, not a quoted rule):** a solution spell names the obstacle it removes (Knock, Goodberry, Tiny Hut, Zone of Truth); a toolbox spell names a thing it does and leaves the obstacle to the player (an image, a hand, a servant, a voice for a tree). The sources praise the second and tame the first with noise, time, knowledge, awareness or a count.

## 3. Magic as identity: how practicing marks the practitioner

### 3.1 Cosmetic marks

- **Ars Magica, the sigil** [read]: "All the spells that she casts have something in common, which makes it clear to those who know her that she was the caster"; "the scent of orange blossom". Already in 5p.
- **Ars Magica, the Twilight scar** [read]: after surviving Twilight, "Something minor and magical", good or bad by how it went: "the area around the maga always smells faintly and pleasantly of roses, the maga's eyes glitter with many colors, the maga walks a fraction of an inch above any surface"; or unnoticeable: "people around her become slightly more cheerful, insects don't bite her". A mark earned by an event, chosen by the storyguide, kept for life.
- **Mage: the Awakening 2e, the Long-Term Nimbus** [read, fan transcription]: "a series of subtle coincidences that surrounds a mage", "purely story-based", by Path: "Moros bring ghastly hauntings, decay, rust, and mechanical breakdowns"; "Acanthus cause strange luck, lost memories to rise up, or visions of possible fates"; around Thyrsus "spirits are more likely to show up". Its strength follows the mage's power stat. This is the closest printed match to decision 106: the world near the mage changes by what they know, with no roll. Reception: not found.
- **Warhammer's Arcane Marks** [memory; no table could be opened]: a wizard long steeped in one Wind takes on its look and mood (a Bright wizard's reddening hair and temper, an Amethyst wizard's pallor and stillness, an Amber wizard going feral and shunning towns, a Jade wizard waxing and waning with the seasons, a Grey wizard nobody quite remembers). In 2nd edition they came from a table as the wizard advanced. Remembered fondly as flavor; **to verify before quoting.**

### 3.2 Marks others react to

- **Ars Magica, The Gift** [read]: it makes people "suspicious and mistrustful of the Gifted individual, inspiring envy". Play a stranger "as though she has a well-established reputation for dishonesty and unreliability". "–3 penalty to all rolls and totals based on social interaction". Animals: "fear and hostility"; "Gifted characters cannot ride horses without magical aid". "Letters from a Gifted individual do not have this effect", nor does an unGifted go-between; covenfolk "merely become accustomed to 'their' magi". Two dials are sold as a Virtue and a Flaw (Gentle, Blatant). **What it does to the party:** it is the reason the game has companions and grogs, who speak for the magi. Reception [lead, Atlas forum]: one GM saw "over 50% of the covenant" buy the Gentle Gift to escape it; another poster says a table that never dares take a magus to town plays it "harder than intended".
- **Symbaroum** [checked, Geek Native review]: permanent corruption "leaves a stain – visible to a gifted few in the colour of the character's Shadow", and makes one a "target for witch-hunters". Stigmas (a wound that will not heal, claw-like nails) are [lead].
- **Dungeon Crawl Classics** [read, Roll20's copy]: "Low-level wizards are powerful. High-level wizards fear for their souls." Corruption comes on a natural 1 and from some spells; Luck can be burned to avoid it. The minor table is body horror with small penalties: "horrid pustules on his face... -1 penalty to Personality"; ears that "look like a donkey's (character also gains braying laugh)"; eyes "large and unblinking, like a fish"; one result is a gift (infravision). Reception [lead]: loved as the game's signature; a forum thread asks whether it must be permanent.

### 3.3 Taboos and obligations

- **Unknown Armies** [checked for the shape, from Wikipedia and one fan school; printed schools from memory]: "Adepts have spells related to certain obsessions, and must power themselves with related activities." Each school has a taboo that empties its charges. The fan school read [read, Alexandrian]: emotion mages "can lose their existing charges if they do anything to ruin an 'emotional moment'", so "people who are nasty can generate negative charges easily, but they can never do anything even marginally positive." The power source is a way of living, and the taboo is the roleplay. From memory: the drunkard mage who must never be sober, the money mage who may not spend. Widely remembered as the game's best idea [memory].
- **D&D 5e's warlock patron** [memory]: an obligation with no rule; fun when the GM plays the patron, ignored otherwise.
- **Oaths that bind:** already noted in 5p as a Talent idea.

### 3.4 Gradual transformation

- **Ars Magica's Warping** [checked, Redcap]: change from "being exposed to a mystical effect that is not accord with the character's nature"; magi collect it over decades and it ends in Final Twilight. A campaign clock: the record's 5o D already flagged it as new tracking.
- **Symbaroum, DCC** (above), **Shadow of the Demon Lord** [memory; Schwalb's page confirms only that Corruption exists and that the supplement's harsher version is "optional and should only be used if all the players and the GM are in agreement"]: corruption from dark traditions and evil acts, with marks of darkness.
- **The Burning Wheel's emotional attributes** [memory]: Greed, Grief, Hatred, Faith rise in play and retire the character at the top.
- Reception, in general [memory and leads]: transformation is loved in games built on it (DCC, Symbaroum, Warhammer) and bought off or ignored when it is a tax on one class in a game about something else.

### 3.5 Personality leanings

- **RuneQuest: Roleplaying in Glorantha** [read, official wiki]: "Runes are universal archetypes, manifest in the cosmos and in the psyche... an adventurer strongly affiliated with a Rune acts in accordance with that Rune." The same percentage casts the Rune's magic and describes the person. The summary table: Air "violence, pride, unpredictability"; Darkness "secrets, cold, hunger"; Water "blood, mercurial"; Earth "worldly, pragmatism, sensual"; Fire/Sky "purity, chastity, idealism"; each also owns a sense (Air: Smell; Darkness: Listen; Earth: Search; Fire: Scan; Water: Taste). Three uses: casual (roll to see what the adventurer would do), magical tests (a threshold in the Rune), and conflicts where "the player can always choose to disobey". Acting the Rune lets you call on it for a bonus. Its designer on the origin [checked, Well of Daliath]: Pendragon's personality traits "roll[ed] into the Runes". Reception [lead]: one forum poster finds the rules "a little fluffy".
- **Wild magic** (5e) [read for the trigger; reception checked on one EN World page and leads]: a surge only when "the DM can have you roll". Reception splits: fun for some, "anticlimactic" in one player's report, disliked by tables it disrupts, and dead when the GM forgets to call for the roll. A mark that depends on someone else remembering is the one that gets ignored.

### 3.6 Social standing and law

- Ars Magica's Order and its Code; Warhammer's licensed Colleges and witch hunters; Symbaroum's templars; Legend of the Five Rings' priests of rank and forbidden blood magic: all [memory] except Symbaroum's one line above. The Alexandrian's suggestion that mind magic be "a matter of explicit law... tightly regulated" [checked] is the same device used as a scene-breaker fix.
- **Vampire's clans and disciplines** [memory]: the model for "your power says who you are": the power set arrives with a reputation, a flaw and a place in society.
- **True names** (Earthsea) [memory]: knowledge as power over a thing, and a reason to guard one's own name.

## 4. Magic and the rest of the party

- **Rituals with helpers.** Pathfinder 2e [read, mirror]: "A different secondary caster must attempt each secondary check"; a critical success gives +2 to the primary check, a failure –4, a critical failure also lowers the result a step. Reception [checked, Paizo forum]: "secondary casters in rituals do not make things easier, they are a punitive requirement"; "You want as few secondary casters as possible"; the rule "discourages A. Cooperating B. Player involvement". Another group reports the opposite with four helpers [lead]. 13th Age's version only helps (helpers –5 to the DC: `out-of-combat-magic.md`). The lesson on record: a helper who can only make it worse is not invited.
- **A speaker for the mage.** Ars Magica's Gift gives every non-mage a job in town (3.2).
- **Familiars and summoned things as people.** 5e's familiar "acts independently of you, but it always obeys your commands" [read]. Table practice [lead]: the player runs it nearby, the GM voices it far away or "only some of the time"; a GM who uses it to foil the player is resented.
- **The corpse, the tree, the god's agent** (section 2) are NPCs the whole table questions; the count of questions makes it a group decision.
- **Downtime.** Ars Magica's laboratory [checked, Redcap]: an assistant adds to the Lab Total. Wards on a home, sanctums, crafting: [memory] only; nothing new was read.
- **Travel.** Weather sense (Druidcraft), shelter (Tiny Hut), scouting eyes with printed weaknesses (a sensor that "can only squeeze through holes 1 inch in diameter", can be noticed, can be trapped in a container [read, *Ultimate Intrigue*]), addresses and tokens for teleport.
- **Social play.** Omens: a Divination "returns a short phrase, cryptic rhyme, omen", and the GM is told to "be creative and play to your strengths" [read]. Truth with consent and law (1.2). Reputation: the sigil and the Gift.

## What seems to transfer

### (a) The scene-breaking families

| Family | Play it endangers | Fixes on record |
|---|---|---|
| Talking to the dead, to beasts, to plants | Murder mysteries | A count of questions; the witness knows only what it knew and thinks its own way; a body part needed; a wait between castings; killers who plan for it [read, checked] |
| Truth zones, mind reading | Intrigue, interrogation | The target knows; evasion allowed; surface thoughts only; minutes of duration; refusal by several suspects; law and manners [read, checked] |
| Locating, scrying, asking a power | Hunts, hidden lairs | Must know or have seen the thing; range; lead; yes or no from a being with limited knowledge; scrying that can be noticed and answered; "one question" rather than repeatable [read, checked] |
| Charm, suggestion, domination | Social play, NPC agency | Friend, not puppet; the target knows afterwards; a reasonable request only; bystanders notice; scale an emotion instead [read, checked] |
| Flight, teleport | Terrain, travel, the dungeon as a route | Slow or vertical only; must know the place and where it is; guarded addresses, tokens; wards on vaults [read, checked] |
| Shelters | Rest as a risk, where to camp | A real cost (the free ritual is the complaint) [checked] |
| Food, water, light | Scarcity | None found in rules text; tables mostly drop scarcity [lead] |
| Invisibility, disguise, intangibility | Infiltration | Sight only (sound, scent, tracks, shadow remain); appearance only; one-way [checked] |
| Opening | The lockpicker | Automatic but loud [read] |
| Any of them | The GM's prep | A rarity tag: the GM hands it out, as a reward [checked] |

### (b) Stated principles for fun utility magic

1. Information is found, not withheld: magic is one more way to investigate, and it gives "clues more than answers".
2. "Yes, but": the limit is printed in the spell, not improvised against the player.
3. A component of a plan, never the plan.
4. Set-up, helpers or failure points the players must manage.
5. It creates a situation or opens a route; it does not name the obstacle it deletes.
6. More things to talk to, none of them guaranteed useful or willing.
7. Narrow and absolute beats broad and partial.
8. Once and partial is a lead; repeatable and certain ends the mystery.
9. Counters must be habits of the world, visible before they bite.
10. A cost from the pool that matters; a free version is where the complaints are.
11. A helper must be able to help.

### (c) How magic marks its user

| Kind | Game | The mark | Received as |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cosmetic | Ars Magica | Sigil; Twilight scar | Liked; free [read; reception memory] |
| Cosmetic | Mage: the Awakening | Long-Term Nimbus: coincidences by Path | Not found |
| Cosmetic, growing | Warhammer | Arcane Marks by Wind | Fond flavor [memory] |
| Others react | Ars Magica | The Gift: distrust, –3, animals | Defining; often bought off [read; lead] |
| Others react | Symbaroum | Shadow, stigmas, witch-hunters | The game's tension [checked; lead] |
| Taboo | Unknown Armies | Obsession charges, taboo empties them | Its best-loved idea [memory] |
| Obligation | D&D warlock | A patron | Fun if the GM plays it, else ignored [memory] |
| Transformation | DCC; Shadow of the Demon Lord; Ars Magica's Warping | Corruption tables; a lifelong clock | Loved where it is the game; a tax elsewhere [read; memory] |
| Personality | RuneQuest | The Rune's rating is the magic and the temperament; the player may disobey | Core to the game; "a little fluffy" [read; lead] |
| Personality | 5e wild magic | Random surges on the GM's call | Split; dies when forgotten [checked; lead] |
| Standing and law | Ars Magica, Warhammer, Symbaroum, L5R, Vampire | Orders, licences, hunters, clans | Mostly setting [memory] |

Across them: marks that are free, visible and chosen are kept; marks that need a second person to remember are dropped; penalties are bought off when the game sells an exit.

### (d) Raw themes per Path (material only; other games' images, not 2d12's)

- **Fire:** light and warmth as hospitality and safety (campfire, candle, the forge); purity and idealism, sight at a distance (RuneQuest's Fire/Sky: "purity... idealism", Scan); the temper that shows (Warhammer's Bright wizards [memory]).
- **Air:** weather read and nudged; voices carried and sounds thrown (messages, a booming voice); the scout's high view; pride and unpredictability (RuneQuest's Air), smell as its sense.
- **Water:** routes opened (breathing water, rivers and pipes as doors); moving water as a tool (the Control Water that sidelined a naval battle [checked]); "mercurial", blood, taste; cleaning, cooling, preserving.
- **Earth:** shaping ground and slow tunnelling; talking to stone; what metal and lead do to magic (lead blocks detection); pragmatism and the worldly, Search as its sense.
- **Astral:** omens, fate and "strange luck"; asking a power that is not omniscient; guarded addresses and tokens for travel; noticing and answering being watched; true names.
- **Death:** the dead as witnesses with five questions and a damaged voice; "hauntings, decay, rust"; secrets, cold and hunger (RuneQuest's Darkness), Listen as its sense; the unease of animals and strangers.
- **Nature:** beasts and plants as witnesses who think their own way; the land's general sense of "corruption in its midst", never specifics; food and healing as care; spirits and sickness drawn near (the Thyrsus nimbus); seasons in the body.
- **Glamour:** images guarded against a touch; a face that is only a face; an emotion scaled up, not a will replaced; the charm that is known afterwards; dreams and lost memories rising.
