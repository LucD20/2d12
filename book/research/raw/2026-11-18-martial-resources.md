# Martial characters with a spendable resource: the rules and the reception

2026-11-18

## The question

Lucas is considering a spendable resource for martial characters ("Stamina") that parallels the casters' ("Mana"). How have other games given martials a resource to spend, what are the exact rules, and what do players and designers say: what feels good, what feels bad, what was patched? Other games' rules and opinions only.

## 1. D&D 5e Battle Master

**Rules (2014)** [read, dnd5e.wikidot.com, a copy of the PHB text]. A Fighter subclass at level 3. Four superiority dice, d8; a fifth at level 7, a sixth at 15; d10 at level 10, d12 at 18. All dice return on a short or long rest. Maneuvers known: 3, then 5 / 7 / 9 at levels 7 / 10 / 15, one swap each time. One maneuver per attack. Save DC = 8 + proficiency + Strength or Dexterity. Relentless (15): rolling initiative with no dice gives one back. Most maneuvers are declared after the hit and add the die to damage plus a rider (trip, push, frighten, disarm) [memory for the list].

**2024** [checked, dungeonsanddragonsfan.com; the book itself not opened]. The dice, their count and the recovery are unchanged ("All other elements are the same as the 2014 rules"). Four Tasha's maneuvers moved into the core list (Ambush, Bait and Switch, Commanding Presence, Tactical Assessment). Relentless changed: once per turn a free d8 instead of spending a die, so the level-15 Battle Master never truly runs dry. Know Your Enemy was redesigned.

**Reception.**
- Too few dice. [checked, EN World, "The Battle Master Paradigm", ccarpent, May 2025] "only 4 Superiority die, with only one more at levels 7 and 15, make the Battlemaster an underpowered class". Same thread, Chaosmancer (quoted): "Everything after 3rd level is just going down hill" (the best maneuvers are taken at level 3; later picks are leftovers). Tony Vargas (quoted): "There need to be more maneuvers, and they need to be apportioned over levels".
- Short-rest dependence. [checked, D&D Beyond forum, June-July 2025] DrakojaPax: "With no guarantees of a short rest between battles, that's pretty tough." Matt_From_Logistics: "this isn't an issue of how many Superiority Dice you have, it's an issue of how many short rests your table takes."
- The spend-or-save decision is felt as bookkeeping. [checked, EN World, "A tweak for the Battlemaster fighter", Aug 2019] Quartz proposes "per Short Rest to per round"; Esker: that "reduces bookkeeping and the difficulty of having to decide whether they're worth using."
- The rider is the value, not the damage. [checked, EN World, Zardnaar, May 2025] "You don't take maneuvers for extra damage but the rider."
- Against any pool. [checked, same thread, TorgoTheWhite] "I dislike the fact that they cannot fulfill my ideal of the fighter that can just 'go all day'".
- "Every fighter should have maneuvers": [lead, search summaries of EN World and Giant in the Playground] "All fighters should have got Superiority Dice"; a GitP poster calls it "a common sentiment but not one that I agree with". No page with these lines was opened.
- The designer of the 3.5 predecessor [checked, Richard Baker's blog, July 2024] regrets that only "watered-down" Battlemaster maneuvers survive in 5e: "Too bad."

## 2. D&D 4e: at-will, encounter and daily powers for every class

**Rules** [memory; a poster's summary checked]. Every class in the first books has the same frame: at-will powers used freely, encounter powers once per fight (back after a 5-minute short rest), daily powers once per day. A level-1 character has 2 at-wills, 1 encounter, 1 daily. A fighter's daily is a weapon attack, in the same card format as a wizard's spell. Essentials (2010) gave some martial classes (Knight, Slayer) stances and no dailies [memory]. [checked, EN World, Apr 2023] Blue: "In 4e all of the classes (pre-Essentials) had the exact same amount of at-will, encounter, and daily powers"; glass disputes it: "it is not identical even in the first PHB."

**Reception: against.**
- [checked, EN World, May 2012] Kinak: the problem was "the classes all being forced to use the same power structure"; grimslade: "why would you only get to trip a person 1/day because you use the Trippin' Your Face Off Daily power?"
- [checked, EN World, Nov 2018] Jester David: "The needless symetry of all classes having the exact same number of powers, recharge, and format of powers bugged me"; "having to create 60 new powers minimum for each class led to some fantastically mediocre design."
- [checked, EN World, Oct 2021] FitzTheRuke: "nearly every class had a power that did the same thing" (the fluff differed).
- A designer agrees in part. [checked, Mike Mearls relayed by Morrus, EN World, Oct 2018] "I wanted classes to have different power acquisition schedules, and more thematic ties between power types"; "4e had a tendency to build matrices and try to fill them without thinking through whether that was a good idea".

**Reception: for.**
- [checked, EN World, "Martial classes", Oct 2021] Vaalingrade: "Its was so nice being able to play the character type I want with the level of mechanical interest I enjoy"; earlier martials were "shackled and brainboxed to the 'simple' classes". Garthanos: "4e actually kept up the One Man Army role for the fighter." HammerMan: martial heroes "got the best coverage and had the most representation in 4e".
- [checked, Sable GM, Substack, July 2022] "Changes 4e made were highly controversial, but they did, in fact, solve C/MD" (the author still dislikes the result).
- [checked, EN World, May 2024] Oofta: the pushback was about "how it was achieved", not balance itself.
- [lead, search summary] Rob Heinsoo is reported to call "samey" an impression not borne out in play. The interview was not found.

## 3. D&D 3.5 Tome of Battle (Book of Nine Swords, 2006)

**Rules** [checked, two reviews; the book not opened]. Three classes know maneuvers, ready a subset, and expend each on use; all return after the fight. In the fight each class recovers differently:
- **Warblade**: all at once, "a swift action followed by a standard action" (a plain attack or nothing), no maneuvers that round.
- **Swordsage**: the most maneuvers, the worst recovery: "a Full Round Action doing nothing else but recovering a single maneuver."
- **Crusader**: random. Of 5 readied at level 1 he "only starts combat with 2 readied at random"; one more is granted each round; when none are left to grant, all return and he draws again. No action spent.

**Reception.**
- The designer [checked, Richard Baker, 2024]: pitched as "spells for fighters"; he still likes the Crusader's "drawing cards and managing a hand of powers"; "many 3rd Edition players loved it"; it became "a testbed for some 4th Edition ideas". DMDavid (2017): "Warriors gained maneuvers that worked like encounter powers in 4th edition."
- Recovery [checked, EN World, "Tome of Battle for Dummies"]: the Crusader's is "the best recovery mechanic of any of the Martial Adepts" (it costs no action); the Swordsage's is "its awful recovery method". [lead] nearly every Swordsage took a feat to avoid it. [checked, EN World, Cap'n Kobold, June 2019] the random draw was too complicated for a GM's NPCs.
- For [checked, EN World, June 2019] Dragonblade: "The singularly greatest supplement published for the entirety of WotC's run on 3e D&D". Against: The Crimson Binome: with so many magic types already, "adding another one felt unnecessary". Kurotowa (Aug 2006): "So, not too different from casters."
- The nickname "Book of Weeaboo Fightin Magic" [checked, kurieg's review]: the objection was tone (wuxia, supernatural strikes), not the resource.

## 4. Level Up: Advanced 5th Edition

**Rules** [read, a5e.tools]. "You have a maximum number of exertion points equal to double your proficiency bonus" (4 at level 1, 12 at 17). "You regain any spent exertion points at the end of a short or long rest." Mid-day refill: spend Hit Dice, "recovering 1d4 exertion points for each Hit Die expended", 1 minute each. A maneuver costs "one or more exertion points and either a bonus action, reaction, or action"; the cost is printed on each maneuver (1 to 3 seen; [lead] for the full range). DC = 8 + proficiency + Strength or Dexterity. Eleven traditions (Adamant Mountain to Unending Wheel), learned by degree like spell levels. Basic maneuvers cost nothing and anyone has them [lead].

**Reception** [checked, EN World, the publisher's preview thread, March 2021]. Morrus (publisher): "a buffet of over 150 bite-sized options, not unlike spells"; "Combat maneuvers are nonmagical." TwoSix: "This is like getting Book of Nine Swords back in 5e, which makes me so, so happy." The longest argument was the word: dalisprime, "You lose stamina. That's my peeve with the wording here" (you build exertion, you spend stamina). [lead] "3 exertion is a LOT".

## 5. Pathfinder

**PF2 focus points** [read, Archives of Nethys and Roll20's compendium]. "Casting any of your focus Spells costs you 1 Focus Point." Pool = "the number of focus spells you know or 3, whichever is lower." Refocus: "You spend 10 minutes", "This restores 1 Focus Point"; repeatable up to the maximum. Monks (qi spells) and champions use it, so it is PF2's per-fight martial resource, but it is written as spells [memory for the classes]. [lead, Paizo forum] the remaster's change to let Refocus repeat got an "appreciation thread".

**PF2 Stamina variant** [read, Archives of Nethys]. Not a spendable martial pool: hit points are split in two. "A character always loses their Stamina Points first"; Take a Breather costs 1 Resolve Point and 10 minutes to "regain all your Stamina Points"; Resolve = key ability modifier, back overnight. It is a healing rule. The name collides with what Lucas means.

**PF1 Stamina and combat tricks (Unchained, 2015)** [read, aonprd.com]. Pool = "your base attack bonus + your Constitution modifier". "For each uninterrupted minute you rest in this way, you regain 1 stamina point"; not in combat. "Dropping to 0 stamina points causes you to become fatigued". Every combat feat gains a trick paid in stamina; tricks stack on one attack. The book offers four ways in, including "Free for Fighters". Reception [lead, Paizo forum summaries]: strong for characters with many feats; one poster objects that no other pool (ki, arcana) refills this way.

## 6. 13th Age

**Rules, 1e** [read, 13thagesrd.com].
- **Fighter, flexible attacks**: "you roll your attack and then choose which maneuver you want the attack to use." Triggers are the natural roll: Brace for It on any miss, Deadly Assault on a natural even hit, Sword of Destiny on a 20. No pool at all.
- **Rogue, momentum**: a yes/no state. "You gain momentum by hitting an enemy with an attack." "You lose momentum when you are hit by an attack." Most powers only require it; "a few powers specify that you must spend your momentum".
- **Commander, command points**: built in the fight. "You start each battle with 1 command point"; Fight from the Front: on a melee hit "gain 1d3 command points"; Weigh the Odds: a standard action, "Gain 1d4". Commands cost points; "Excess command points go away at the end of the battle."

**Reception and the patch.** [checked, Rob Donoghue, Aug 2013] flexible attacks will be "a little bit awkward until the fighter player gets familiar enough with his own triggers"; "an odd cadence". [lead, Tapatalk] players "want more control over when their powers can be used". **13th Age 2e patched both** [read, Pelgrane's "13A Changes from 1e"]: "As a rule, the fighter no longer uses flexible attacks. Its maneuvers build on its basic melee attack"; the rogue's Momentum "is gone, replaced by Bravado, a resource that accumulates when you hit with an attack and does not go away when you're hit".

## 7. Pools built or refreshed in the fight

- **Draw Steel**: already in this project (`nova-burst.md`, `raw/2026-11-14-D...`): every class, martial or not, builds a heroic resource each turn and loses it at the fight's end.
- **Lancer, ICON** [read earlier, same raw file]: Lancer's heat is a cost that fills toward a penalty, cleared by an action. ICON's Resolve rises 1 a round, is shared by the party, and pays once-per-fight Limit Breaks.
- **Nimble** [checked, the designer Evan's blog, July 2024]: each martial has its own in-fight build. The Berserker rolls a Fury Die each raging turn, "NO limit", "if your Rage ends, your Fury Dice are lost"; the Oathsworn gains a Judgment Die when attacked; the Hunter gains charges on initiative. [lead] Fury Dice were later changed "to be a spendable class resource".
- **Beacon** [checked, its itch.io page; the free Player Guide not opened]: once-per-quest Limit Breaks per job; MP exists (a Skald chains effects "as long as they have the MP"). Whether martial jobs spend the same MP: not confirmed.
- **Trespasser** [lead]: paywalled; its page names Endurance and Effort without rules.

## 8. The design question

No essay on exactly this was found. What exists:
- Mearls (above): the fault of 4e was the identical schedule, not that martials had powers.
- Sable GM [checked]: recoverable maneuvers (Path of War, the Tome of Battle's Pathfinder heir) "essentially completely closes off the gap in effectiveness between casters and martials in combat", but not out of combat, and draw the "anime fighters" objection.
- Baker [checked]: martial adepts were no more broken than high-level wizards.
- The repeated player objection is fiction first ("forgot how to trip"), sameness second.

## Patterns across the games

**How the pool refreshes.**
- Per day: only 4e's martial dailies. The most attacked ("1/day").
- Per short rest: Battle Master, A5E. Liked in play, but its worth depends on how the table rests, and players hoard.
- Per fight: 4e encounter powers, PF2 focus (10 minutes), PF1 stamina (a point a minute), Tome of Battle between fights.
- Recovered inside the fight by an action or a draw: Tome of Battle. An action spent recovering is disliked (Swordsage); recovery that costs nothing is liked (Crusader).
- Built in the fight, lost after: 13th Age Commander, Nimble, Draw Steel, ICON.
- No pool, a trigger: 13th Age 1e Fighter and Rogue. Both replaced in 2e.

**What the well-liked ones share.** A menu of riders chosen at the moment of the hit (Battle Master, Tome of Battle, A5E). A refresh the player can count on in every fight. A structure that differs from the caster's even where the idea is the same (Tome of Battle's three recoveries are still praised twenty years on).

**What the disliked ones share.** A limit the fiction cannot explain (a daily trip). The identical schedule and count for every class. Too few uses to matter, with the best options taken first. Control taken away (flexible attacks; momentum lost when hit). A turn spent only on recovering.

## What seems to transfer

My own reading, not rules.
- The complaint about 4e was the identical frame, more than martials having a resource. Stamina and Mana can be parallel in idea and still differ in how they refill and what they buy.
- Refresh per fight or build in the fight. Per day drew the fiction complaint; per short rest made the class depend on the table's habits. A fatigue word like Stamina already explains a per-fight refill.
- Keep free options beside the pool (A5E's basic maneuvers, 4e's at-wills). "Go all day" is what some players want from a fighter.
- Spend at the moment of the hit, for a rider. That is the part of the Battle Master everyone praises.
- Do not make recovering cost the turn; if recovery is in the fight, attach it to something the character does anyway (the Warblade's plain attack, the Commander's hit).
- Give enough uses to spend every round or two; four a rest read as too few and produced hoarding.
- Spread the options over the levels so the last picks are not leftovers: this fits growth by options, not numbers.
- Name it carefully: "exertion" started a naming argument, and PF2 already uses "Stamina" for a hit-point rule.
