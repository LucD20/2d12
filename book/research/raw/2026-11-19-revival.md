# Bringing back the dead: how other games do it (raw report)

**Date:** 2026-11-19

**Lucas's question, in his words:** "I think bringing back the dead is necessary for the health of the game, but with extreme limits and likely just once. I like no revive spells but this is a big missed mechanic in games that do have it that I think is my job to fix." (`book/magic-design.md`, decision 93; `rules-backlog.md`, "[Q] BRINGING BACK THE DEAD".)

**What was read first:** `book/research/README.md`, `book/rules-src/04-health.md`, decisions 36, 93 and 94, the Momentum rule (Chapter 2). No research file here covered revival before this one.

**How far to trust it.** These are other games' rules and other people's opinions, never 2d12's.

- **[read]** rules text on a page that opened. **[checked]** an opinion on a page that opened. **[lead]** a search summary, or a page that refused to load. **[memory]** Claude's own knowledge, no page opened.
- Every page was read through a summarizing tool, not seen raw. The quotes are as the tool returned them: check a quote on its page before it is repeated to anyone.
- About half of the games on the list are [memory] or [lead]. The list of what did not open is at the end.

## 1. D&D and its family: the price moved from the character to the purse

- **AD&D 1e / 2e.** Each return needed a "resurrection survival" roll from Constitution, cost 1 point of Constitution, and a character could never be raised more times than their starting Constitution score. [lead: search summary of forum posts quoting the books; the 2e page at discmaster.textfiles.com returned 503] The Alexandrian adds that 3rd Edition dropped such limits largely because players were already ignoring them. [checked] https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/49125/roleplaying-games/ptolus-running-the-campaign-death-at-tier-2
- **Gygax's own position**, as quoted by DM David: "There MUST be some final death or immortality will take over", and also "Death due to combat is no great matter in most cases". [checked] https://dmdavid.com/tag/how-character-death-lands-dd-in-a-tug-of-war-between-game-and-story/
- **Old-School Essentials.** Raise Dead with a time limit by caster level and two weeks of weakness. [memory; the SRD returned 500 twice]
- **D&D 3.5.** Raise Dead: a 5,000 gp diamond, the loss of a level (2 Constitution at level 1). [memory; d20srd.org and dandwiki refused] DM David calls level loss the best balance of fear and lasting consequence. [checked, same page]
- **Pathfinder 1e.** Raise Dead: a 5,000 gp diamond, dead at most 1 day a caster level, the soul must be "free and willing", and "The subject of the spell gains two permanent negative levels when it is raised." [read] https://www.d20pfsrd.com/magic/all-spells/r/raise-dead/
- **D&D 4e.** A level 8 ritual, 500 gp (5,000 at paragon, 50,000 at epic), dead at most 30 days, a -1 penalty until three milestones. [lead: search summary of a fan wiki; the wiki returned 402]
- **D&D 5e.** Revivify: a 3rd-level spell, 1 action, "diamonds worth 300 gp, which the spell consumes", on "a creature that has died within the last minute", back at 1 hit point, no other cost. Raise Dead: 5th level, 1 hour, a 500 gp diamond, dead at most 10 days, then "a -4 penalty to all attack rolls, saving throws, and ability checks" that drops by 1 each long rest. [read] http://dnd5e.wikidot.com/spell:revivify and http://dnd5e.wikidot.com/spell:raise-dead (dndbeyond's spell page returned 404). DM David: "Fifth edition suffers from D&D's most namby-pamby version of raise dead." [checked]
- **Pathfinder 2e.** Resurrect is not a spell but an uncommon ritual: 1 day, gemstones worth 75 gp × the target's level, two secondary casters, three skill checks, a target up to level 10 dead under a year. It fails if "the target's time has come" or the target is unwilling. Success: back at 1 Hit Point, Clumsy 1, Drained 1 and Enfeebled 1 for a week that nothing shortens. Critical success: "a permanent mark from the afterlife". Critical failure: something goes badly wrong (a spirit in the body, an undead). [read on a community copy of Player Core; Archives of Nethys did not give the page] https://publish.obsidian.md/merovia/PF2+Character+Building/Spells/Resurrect
- **Matt Mercer's rules (Critical Role, Tal'Dorei).** A ritual: up to three companions each make a contribution check; "The base DC for the final resurrection check is 10", -3 for each contribution that works, +1 for each that fails, and it rises "by 1 for each previous successful resurrection the character has undergone". "On a failed check, the soul does not return and the character is lost." A 1-action spell (Revivify) allows no contributions; its failure does not lose the soul but adds 1 to later DCs. [read on Nerdist's reprint] https://nerdist.com/article/use-critical-roles-resurrection-rules-in-your-own-campaign/ **Reception:** praised for the scene it makes (ScreenRant: "much more dramatic") [checked] https://screenrant.com/dnd-critical-role-dm-character-death-resurrection-rules/ ; fans note that on the show nearly every ritual succeeded, and that the rule is partly made for an audience [lead]. Critical Role's fourth campaign is reported to start with resurrection magic not working at all. [lead]

**The pattern:** each edition took the cost off the character (a count, a point of Constitution, a level) and put it on the purse and on a few days of penalty. The complaint about a "revolving door" grew as that happened. [judgment, from the sources above]

## 2. Games that count, limit or refuse

- **13th Age** counts on **both** sides. The cleric's Resurrection is cast "only once per level, and a limited number of times in your life". The first time is one standard action. The second "costs you roughly half your hit points and daily powers/spells". The third "has to be as a ritual". The fourth "nearly kills you". And: "The fifth time you resurrect someone, that's the end of your story and you die." The character returned comes back weaker each time, and if the target has been raised more often than the caster has cast it, there is a 50% chance the target's count is used. [read] https://www.13thagesrd.com/classes/cleric/ Its optional rule: "PCs can only be fully slain by named villains." A party that flees takes "a campaign loss" in place of a death. [read] https://www.13thagesrd.com/combat-rules/ Pelgrane's Gareth Ryder-Hanrahan: campaign losses "work like the side effects of the Resurrection spell", worse each time. [checked] https://pelgranepress.com/2022/04/19/13th-age-ideas-that-arent-worth-a-full-page-xx-article/
- **Dungeon World: Last Breath.** The dying character rolls with no modifier: "On a 10+, you've cheated Death", "On a 7–9, Death himself will offer you a bargain. Take it and stabilize or refuse and pass beyond the Black Gates", on a 6 or less "your fate is sealed." [read] https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/moves/ Its cleric's Resurrection is "always possible", but the GM names conditions: "It's going to take days/weeks/months", "It will require a lot of money", help from someone, a sacrifice. [read] https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/classes/cleric/
- **Daggerheart: death moves.** At the last Hit Point the player chooses. Blaze of Glory: one action that critically succeeds, then death. Avoid Death: unconscious, then roll the Hope Die, and at or under your level you take a scar, a Hope slot crossed out for good: "If you ever cross out your last Hope slot, it's time to end your character's journey." Risk It All: roll, and live or die on which die is higher. Resurrection exists but is "often a long, difficult, and costly process" set by the GM (and a level 10 spell). [read on a fan copy of the SRD] https://daggerheart.su/rule/death
- **Draw Steel.** A dead hero "can't be brought back to life without the use of a special powerful item, such as a Scroll of Resurrection." [read] https://steelcompendium.io/v2/Browse/rule/health/stamina/ No class has a revive. Titles such as Presumed Dead and Saved for a Worse Fate let the Director return a hero as a story event. [checked] https://altoholism.blogspot.com/2025/08/death-and-draw-steel.html
- **Warhammer Fantasy Roleplay: Fate points.** No resurrection; a small stock of lives paid for in advance: "You may spend a permanent Fate point to avoid death", by being left for dead or by the blow missing. [read on a blog that says its rules are "mostly copied from 4ed"] https://warhammerstormofshadows.blogspot.com/2021/02/rules-of-game-pt-2-fate-resolve.html
- **Fabula Ultima.** A PC at 0 Hit Points surrenders (a consequence, not death) unless the player chooses to Sacrifice; a sacrificed character is gone for good and achieves something extraordinary. [lead: a TV Tropes summary; the conditions for a Sacrifice are memory]
- **Phoenix: Dawn Command** (Keith Baker). Death is the way to advance: a character returns stronger, but "you only get to come back eight times." [checked] https://keith-baker.com/rpg-ramblings-death-and-games
- **Blades in the Dark.** A dead scoundrel can go on as a ghost, with its own playbook. [memory; bladesinthedark.com returned 404]
- **Burning Wheel.** No return; a Persona point can be spent to survive a Mortal Wound. [lead: forum summary]
- **Pendragon.** No return on the injury page: a knight at 0 Hit Points "dies if not restored to positive Hit Points by midnight"; a miracle (the Grail) is the Gamemaster's. [read] https://pendragonwiki.chaosium.com/rules/injury-and-health.html The player goes on as the heir. [memory] **The One Ring** also has heirs. [lead]
- **Ars Magica.** Raising the dead is outside the Limits of Magic. [memory; the Limits themselves: `research/ars-magica.md`]
- **Savage Worlds.** A Resurrection power at the highest rank, costly, with a heavy penalty on the roll and an hour's casting. [memory; the search found no text]
- **Dungeon Crawl Classics.** "Recovering the body": a Luck check to find the fallen character alive, with a permanent loss. [memory]
- **Mörk Borg, Forbidden Lands, Symbaroum.** No resurrection in the core rules; a table of injuries stands between Down and dead. [memory] **Shadow of the Demon Lord** and **Dragonbane:** not verified either way (searches found nothing).

## 3. The failure modes and the arguments

1. **The revolving door.** The Alexandrian: "Raise dead really is a game changer." "Death doesn't have the same meaning." He does not conclude it must go: "life-or-death is often the least interesting thing at stake", and removing the spell alone is not enough, since the dying rules and the scenarios were built around it. [checked]
2. **A tax in gold.** DM David: in 5e most characters "can afford the cost without borrowing". [checked] Darker Dungeons' fix keeps the diamond but makes it unbuyable: rare diamonds "can't be bought on the open market". [read] https://giffyglyph.com/darkerdungeons/grimoire/4.0.0/en/death_and_resurrection.html
3. **The player sits out.** Gnome Stew: "Several sessions? Really? I'd have a mutiny on my hands if I made someone sit out that long!" [checked] https://gnomestew.com/waiting-for-the-new-character-how-long-is-too-long/ A long quest to revive someone makes this worse unless the player has something to play.
4. **A penalty heavy enough to matter makes a new character the better deal.** With level loss, players weighed a weakened old character against a fresh one; in the Gnome Stew comments some chose the return and its penalties, others kept a spare character ready. [checked] The Alexandrian's note that tables ignored AD&D's limits is the same failure seen from the other side. [checked]
5. **The world stops making sense.** EN World's hafrogman: "If you trivialize death, that has serious and far reaching consequences on the world, setting and society." Quickleaf wants the book to cover what a return does to succession and inheritance. [checked] https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=5867634 D&D Beyond's own advice for a murder mystery is to keep the party under level 5 or to remove the victim's head. [checked] https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1654-how-to-run-murder-mysteries-in-d-d Keith Baker's answer in Eberron: "raise dead is rarely used on Khorvaire", and it can go wrong. [checked]
6. **A die decides when a player stops playing a character.** EN World's CM: "I could never let a single die roll tell somebody when they should stop playing a particular character." [checked] This is the case for the return existing at all, and for the games that give the choice to the player (Daggerheart, Fabula Ultima, Last Breath's bargain).
7. **What makes a return feel earned,** by these writers: the party takes part (Mercer's contributions); it can fail; it leaves a mark that is a story rather than a number (Baker's character who "returned without his shadow"; Pathfinder 2e's mark); it is scarce in the world; the next one is visibly harder (13th Age, Mercer). Prismatic Wasteland goes furthest: "true resurrection is impossible", and what comes back is changed (no memories, a new body, a child). [checked] https://www.prismaticwasteland.com/blog/we-live-again
8. **Video games.** [memory, nothing opened] Baldur's Gate 3's 200 gold at Withers is the death tax in its plainest form; Darkest Dungeon's Death's Door puts a roll and a lasting penalty at the threshold in place of a revive; Fire Emblem and XCOM show that permanent loss with a reload button gets reloaded.

## 4. What seems to transfer

Observations, not rules. Each shape: where it comes from, its known weakness, then the 2d12 part it could hang on.

1. **A count on the character.** AD&D (never more than starting Constitution), Phoenix (eight), Mercer (+1 DC each time). Weakness: a generous count is ignored, and "once" needs a rule for the second death.
   *Hangs on:* Wounds. The tables already add 1d6 for each Wound of the same kind, so a Wound that a Rest cannot remove is a built-in "harder next time".
2. **A count on the one who revives, with a rising price.** 13th Age (five in a lifetime, the fifth kills the caster). Weakness: a second healer resets it, which is why 13th Age also counts the target.
   *Hangs on:* Rally Points given up for good by the one who performs it; the Paladin is already the one who unlocks other people's Rally Points (decision 36).
3. **Lives paid in advance.** WFRP's Fate points. Weakness: it is not a return; the death never happens, and the stock reads as extra hit points.
   *Hangs on:* the "Soft" death row (25 to 26) of the Dying and Shattered Tables, still a placeholder.
4. **A bargain at the threshold.** Dungeon World's Last Breath. Weakness: the GM must invent a good bargain on the spot, the opposite of least ambiguity unless the bargains are listed.
   *Hangs on:* the Dying and Shattered Tables' last rows; Dying against Shattered could give two different bargains.
5. **A short window.** 5e's Revivify (one minute). Weakness: it becomes an in-fight heal with a gold price, the revolving door itself.
   *Hangs on:* the end of the encounter as the clock. Decision 36 (no healing from spells) already shuts the cheap version.
6. **A ritual the party performs, which can fail for good.** Mercer; Pathfinder 2e. Weakness: a table that loves the character fudges the roll; one that doesn't sees a second death by die.
   *Hangs on:* a Rest (a day in a safe place), which already carries the Wound checks: the return happens in town, not in the dungeon.
7. **Conditions set case by case.** Dungeon World's Resurrection, Daggerheart, Draw Steel's scroll. Weakness: everything is the GM's call, and the dead character's player waits through the quest.
   *Hangs on:* nothing existing; it would need the list written, and something for that player to play.
8. **A permanent mark that shrinks a resource.** Daggerheart's scar (a Hope slot), AD&D's Constitution point. Weakness: too heavy and a new character is the better deal; it is also vertical, a number going down.
   *Hangs on:* a Wound that never heals, or one Rally Point fewer. Pathfinder 2e's mark and Baker's lost shadow are the horizontal kind (a trait others can read).
9. **A penalty that fades.** 5e (-4, a long rest each), 4e (three milestones), Pathfinder 2e (a week). Weakness: a tax in time, paid by waiting in town.
   *Hangs on:* Rests, or Wounds that the Rest checks remove at the usual rate.
10. **A cost on the whole party.** 13th Age's campaign loss. Weakness: it punishes the players who did not die, and needs a GM with a campaign to wound.
    *Hangs on:* Momentum, which already drops when a PC goes Down and is lost on a retreat.
11. **Death chosen, for a price paid to the party.** Daggerheart's Blaze of Glory, Fabula Ultima's Sacrifice. Weakness: it is not a return; but these games need less of one because the player chose.
    *Hangs on:* "keep fighting" while Dying or Shattered, already a choice to risk the table.
12. **Play on as something else.** Blades' ghost, Draw Steel's titles. Weakness: a second set of rules for one character.
    *Hangs on:* nothing existing.

**Seen across the list:** the returns that writers praise combine a count (1 or 2), the party's part (6) and a mark (8); the ones they mock are a window (5) with a fading penalty (9) and a price in gold. Draw Steel and Dungeon World keep the return out of the spell list altogether, which sits close to "I like no revive spells".
