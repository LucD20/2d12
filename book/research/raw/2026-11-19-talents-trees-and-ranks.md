# Talent trees, specializations, careers and ranked talents: how other games shape them (raw report)

**Date:** 2026-11-19

**Lucas's words:** "The talents is going to be the actual meat of the game, so far we have been doing the skeleton. Talents is going to be the main thing, how things work, how all of it comes together. How a diplomatic paladin differs from a quiet, ranged paladin, with similar strengths and capabilities. I don't want to fall into the trap of skill feats from 2e, or skill tax from 1e and 3.5, or competing talents like edges from Swade (why nobody really gets social edges instead of combat edges)."

**Scope of this report:** the SHAPE talents come in (tree, pyramid, list, ranks, paths, careers), in tabletop games and in the video games whose designers wrote about it. Other games' rules and other people's opinions, never 2d12's. Nothing here designs 2d12's Talents.

**What was read first:** `book/rules-src/05-characters.md`, `book/rules-src/set-aside/16-talents.md`, the entry "TALENT STRUCTURE: PROPOSAL" in `book/rules-backlog.md`, `book/magic-design.md` (decision 121, section 5l), `book/research/README.md`, `book/research/crucible.md` and its raw report (not repeated here: only Crucible's tree is new).

## 1. Fantasy Flight / Edge Star Wars (Edge of the Empire, Age of Rebellion, Force and Destiny)

**Shape.** Career, then specialization; a specialization is a fixed tree.

- A character has one career for life and buys specializations. "Characters cannot buy a new career but may buy additional specializations - whether they are under their career or not" [read, Wikipedia's summary of the rulebooks].
- "'Specialization' is the fancy, official word for a talent tree" [checked, FFG forum]. Each tree is 20 boxes, 4 columns by 5 rows [memory]; the rows cost "5 exp for the first level, 10 exp for the second, then 15 exp, then 20 exp, and finally 25 exp for the last level" [read, the rule as quoted on the forum].
- Talents "must be unlocked in the order they appear on the specialization tree" [read, Wikipedia]: you may buy a box only if a drawn line joins it to one you own [memory]. Some trees are four straight columns, some snake sideways.
- **Passive and active.** "Passive talents are abilities that are always on... Active talents are abilities that require a difficulty roll to turn on prior to use, and in some cases can only be used a limited number of times in a given session" [read, Wikipedia].
- **Ranked talents.** "Some talents have levels and can be purchased more than once. The talent's levels stack, even if they are bought for different amounts of experience points" [read, Wikipedia]. Grit (+1 strain threshold), Toughened (+2 wound threshold) and Dedication (+1 to a characteristic, bottom row, the only way to raise one after creation) are the known ones [memory].
- **More specializations.** A new one costs 10 XP times the number you will then have, plus 10 if it is outside your career [lead: forum summaries agree; the forum also says "he pays less. 10 xp less, specifically", checked].
- **Arithmetic** (mine, from the costs above): a whole tree is 300 XP; the cheapest straight line to the bottom row is 75 XP; play gives roughly 10 to 20 XP a session [checked: "with 10 XP per session you're looking at months of play before you get your first Dedication talent"].
- **Force powers** are separate: a base power bought once, then its own small upgrade tree (Strength, Range, Magnitude, Control, Mastery), gated by Force Rating, which itself sits as a talent in Force trees [memory]. **Signature abilities** (later career books) attach under a career's tree, need bottom-row talents to connect, and have their own upgrade rows [memory].
- **Non-combat talents are in the same trees**, mixed by theme: the Scoundrel tree is described by a player as "combat talents, social talents, and other utility talents" [checked]. The social/combat competition is softened by theme (a Politico tree is mostly social), not removed.
- **Retraining:** none in the rules [memory].

**Reception.**

- **Dedication as the destination.** "I think it's either or, either there are a few talents to dip into or its a race to dedication" [checked]. A reviewer: "Optimizers took note of which trees provided the shortest path to 'Dedication'... but the Talents along the way were good enough that the optimization decisions weren't necessarily obvious"; and the math: "sink every single XP you could spare at character creation into attributes... Then, race to Dedication" [checked, Cannibal Halfling].
- **Throwaway trees.** "There are a few trees that seem specifically designed for players to take them and buy down to the Dedication talent and then drop them" [checked].
- **Filler.** "Many trees have a lot of 'junk' talents in between the good ones" [checked]. A spec-by-spec fan revision names them: "The lone rank of Codebreaker and Confidence both feel like filler to me"; "The lone rank of Solid Repairs feels like filler" [checked]. Its fixes mostly swap a one-rank situational talent for Grit or a second useful rank: the number talents are the tree's padding and also what players ask for more of.
- **Situational talents called bad:** the thread's own answer is "If your GM never puts you in a situation where a skill is useful of course it will be useless" [checked].
- **Order tax.** Because a second tree's price depends on how many you own: "I want to grow my character organically and not have to pay a concept tax for doing so" [checked].
- **How different two Hired Guns are.** The first split is the specialization (Bodyguard, Marauder, Mercenary Soldier are different trees) [memory]; inside one tree, two characters differ mainly by which column they went down first and converge as XP fills it. The second tree is where they diverge again: players describe buying a tree, making "a beeline to the talents I really want", then filling the rest later [checked].
- **Praise.** Players who like trees like the direction and the readable sheet: the tree is the character sheet's second page, boxes ticked [memory]; "I greatly enjoy the Specialization trees" [lead]; a tree gives "direction" where a list does not [checked, paraphrase of the "Salvaging the Specialization" thread: "It will still be thematic but not as rigid, more open, but not a classless hot mess"].

## 2. Genesys: the pyramid

**Shape.** A free list in five tiers costing 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 XP. The one rule, quoted on the forum from the book: "After buying the new talent, your character must have more talents in the next lowest tier" [read]. So 5/4/3/2/1 for a first Tier 5. "The first Tier 5 talent costs 175 total XP to unlock... Subsequent T5 talents require 75 each" [checked]. A ranked talent climbs: "Repeatable talents like Grit are counted as the next tier each time they're taken" [checked]. Prerequisites between talents remain only as "Improved" chains [checked].

**Why, as far as could be found.** No designer statement opened. A reviewer: "Genesys has no Talent Trees, the designers realized that creating enough interesting ones for a generic game simply wasn't going to happen" [checked, Cannibal Halfling]. When the Expanded Player's Guide later gave rules to build trees, the same site: "Specialization trees are significantly more work than the talent pyramid, and doing them well requires its own set of playtesting as well as writing" [checked].

**Reception.**

- For: "Now you don't have to take talents you don't want to just because they are in your way in the talent trees" [checked]. Dedication now costs "on the order of double or triple", which the reviewer calls "absolutely a good thing" because it ends the race [checked].
- Against: "It does mean however that the top talents will be very common, which I don't like"; "I do worry that some talents are simply more attractive to almost everyone, which may lead to less differentiation between characters" [checked]. And the pyramid's base is its own tax: five Tier 1 picks must exist before the Tier 5 one, whatever they are (mine, from the rule).
- Players then rebuilt trees for their settings, and FFG published the method: the tree was missed for **theme and direction**, not for its costs [checked, the forum threads and the EPG review].

## 3. Other Star Wars games, for contrast

- **Saga Edition:** five classes; "Each class progresses along 'character trees'... where characters are built with talents and feats... This allows for greater customization and more variety amongst characters of the same class" [read, Wikipedia]. A talent at each odd class level from that class's several short trees (three to eight talents, a prerequisite line or two), a feat on the other levels [memory]. Trees are small themed lists more than trees. Multiclassing opens other classes' trees [memory].
- **d6 (West End):** no talents; a template, skill dice, specializations of a skill [memory]. Two smugglers differ by dice and by play.
- **SW5e:** 5e's frame: class, an archetype chosen once at level 3, feats competing with ability increases [memory].

## 4. Warhammer

- **WFRP 4e.** A career has four levels; each level lists skills, a few talents and the characteristics you may advance; you buy from the level you are in, then pay to move up or to another career [memory]. "Characters are now much more free to advance their Characteristics and Skills independently of their careers, and the cost in experience points scale with higher numbers" [read, Wikipedia]. A talent costs 100 XP plus 100 for each time already taken [lead]; many can be taken up to a characteristic's bonus [lead: "the Int Bonus dictates how many times it can be taken"]; most add +1 Success Level per rank on one named kind of Test [lead]. **Complaints** [checked, EN World and Winds of Chaos forum posters]: "Talents need to be remembered, all the time... and they too exacerbate the effect of the skilled steamrolling regular opposition"; "talents make 'hit more often' even more valuable, since nearly all of them add Success Levels". The earlier backlog line that its XP is "a shopping list" stands as [memory]. Non-combat talents are in the same lists, but the career decides which you see: a Lawyer's list is social. The career is the theme filter the tree is in FFG.
- **WFRP 2e:** talents are one-off, no ranks, bought only when the current career lists them; you change career to reach others [memory]. Fewer, flatter, remembered more easily; less freedom.
- **Dark Heresy 1e / Rogue Trader:** career rank tables: each rank a priced list [memory]. **Dark Heresy 2e / Only War:** no lists; every talent has two aptitudes and a tier, and "Talents are divided into 3 tiers... the price depends on the number of matching aptitudes and the tier" [lead]. The complaints [checked/lead, FFG forum]: "Ambidextrous at 200 with WS and BS, 300 with just one, but 600 xp without"; "it can create huge power gaps between PCs at the same XP level"; beta critics said it "encourage[s] cookie cutter characters" [lead]. Prerequisite chains (a weak talent required for a strong one) are the "talent tax" [memory].
- **Wrath & Glory:** an archetype at creation, then a flat list bought with XP, each talent with a cost and prerequisites (tier, keyword, attribute) [memory]. **Soulbound:** an archetype grants a core talent and a list to choose from at creation; afterwards any talent whose requirement you meet, a flat XP price; miracles and spells are bought the same way [memory].

## 5. Three degrees in one talent: AGE, Symbaroum, Lancer

- **AGE (Dragon Age, Fantasy AGE, Modern AGE, The Expanse).** A talent has Novice, Journeyman and Master degrees, each a separate written benefit; requirements are an ability or a focus; class talents come at set levels. A **specialization** is a talent with three degrees opened at a set level (Fantasy AGE: level 4, then every other level). **Focuses** (the skill layer: +2 on one kind of test) are gained on their own track at other levels, so a focus never competes with a talent [all memory]. One blogger: talents "do not have a discernable formula between requirements and ranks... and each benefit value" [checked, Herding Dice]; a review liked that "it's up to the individual player how their character will progress" [lead].
- **Symbaroum.** No classes or levels: about 35 abilities, each Novice / Adept / Master, bought with XP at 10 / 20 / 30 [lead]; each degree a different effect, often a new action or reaction, not a bigger number [memory]. Start: two Novice and one Adept, or five Novice [lead]: the depth-or-breadth choice is on the first page.
- **Lancer.** 29 talents in the core data file, each with exactly three named ranks, each rank its own rule: Ace is "Acrobatics", "Afterburners", "Supersonic" [read, `lancer-data/lib/talents.json`]. A pilot starts with three talents at rank I and gains "+1 Talent Rank" per License Level: "Advance an existing talent to the next rank (I→II or II→III), or take a new talent at rank I" [read, fan rules reference]. So 15 ranks at LL12: five talents finished, or up to fifteen at rank I. Licenses run beside them (three ranks of gear per frame), core bonuses come every third LL [read]. **Retraining is narrower than "freely":** each time LL rises you may make one reallocation, for example "Move all rank points from one talent to any other talent" [read, the same reference]. Reception: "Lancer doesn't even really have trap options, either" [checked]; "some of the most complex character advancement options I have ever seen in an RPG - but the process of advancing is actually quite streamlined" [checked]. Talents are combat only: the non-combat pilot is a separate, light layer (triggers), so nothing competes [memory].

## 6. Crucible (Foundry VTT), the tree itself [read, source]

- **One tree for everyone**, a hexagon around six abilities (Dexterity, Toughness, Strength, Wisdom, Presence, Intellect): "divided into six sections, with each section representing a different Ability". 109 nodes in rings: 12 at tier 0, 18 at tier 1, 24 at tier 2, 24 at tier 3 of which 12 are signature nodes, 30 at tier 4. The compendium holds 370 talents; a node holds several (about 5 to 15 per ability per ring); 144 grant an action.
- **What gates a pick:** (1) a node must touch one you own, or be on the innermost ring; (2) the node's tier sets a level and a score in its ability (innermost: score 2; tier 1: 3; tier 2: level 2 and score 4; and up); a node on the border of two abilities needs one point less in each; (3) the talent may add a requirement. Every talent costs 1 point; 3 points at creation and 3 a level.
- **Node types label the content:** attack, melee, ranged, magic, defense, heal, spell, move, utility, skill, signature. Skill and utility nodes are in the same tree, bought from the same 3 points.
- **Signatures:** "Signature Talents represent the culmination of the abilities required to select them, and are mirrored across two ability scores. Taking a Signature Talent forms a bridge to its paired ability score". Capped by level: "your first Signature Talent at level 3, and one additional Signature Talent at every third level thereafter, to a maximum of six". 66 signature talents on 12 nodes (Spellblade, Conjurer...).
- **A caster and a non-caster** are the same tree walked from different sides: Rune talents sit on the innermost nodes of Wisdom, Presence and Intellect, Strike talents on Strength and Dexterity; one point buys a non-caster a Rune.
- **Retraining:** "You may reset your purchases on the Talent Tree at any time".
- **A travel cost exists:** the code lets a point buy an empty node to pass through ("Spend talent point to purchase empty node"), marked temporary.
- **What the shape does** [mine]: position is prerequisite, so no talent needs a written "requires X"; the picture shows a build's ability mix at a glance; the price is that reach, not interest, decides what is available, and it needs the software to be read at all. No reception was found.

## 7. Paths, class talents and the rest

- **Shadow of the Demon Lord / Weird Wizard:** no free picks. A novice path at level 1 (4), an expert path at 3 (16), a master path at 7 (64) [read, Schwalb; counts checked]. "With no requirements to meet and no restrictions on what paths you can choose" [read]. A path is a fixed bundle delivered over levels. Reviewer: "In most other games these choices would come with prerequisties, but not in SotDL" [checked]. Variety comes from 4 × 16 × 64 combinations, not from picks inside a path. Nothing is filler by position because nothing is positioned; nothing is retrained. Weird Wizard keeps the three steps [memory].
- **13th Age:** each class lists about seven to nine talents; you take three at level 1 (a few more later for some classes); feats in three tiers (adventurer, champion, epic) attach to a talent or power and deepen it [memory]. Two fighters differ by three big choices made at once.
- **Dungeon World compendium classes:** a small move list unlocked by something that happened in play ("when you have..."), not by level [memory]. The gate is fiction.
- **Earthdawn:** a Discipline gives listed talents at each Circle; each talent has its own rank bought with Legend Points; raising a set number of talents to a rank opens the next Circle [memory]. Ranks are pure number (the step you roll). Deep, vertical.
- **Exalted 3e:** Charms in a prerequisite tree for each of 25 Abilities, several hundred for Solars alone [memory]. One long read-through calls a pair of trees "good Charm sets, with only an average amount of bloat for Charms that handle circumstances that don't come up much" and praises a branch where charms "only serve as prerequisites for each other so you don't feel that you're paying an XP tax" [checked, inklesspen]. A fan diagnosis: "a few too-big Charms that swallow too much design space" [lead]; dice-trick charms ("every other Charm was a dice rerolling mechanic") [lead]. The praise that Larceny, Sail, Socialize or Craft trees are as large and as real as Melee is common [memory; the read-through treats them with equal weight, checked]. **Exalted Essence:** "a more streamlined, easier to pick up and play version" [checked, Blizzard Watch]; fewer Charms, universal ones shared by all Exalt types, with "Modes" (variants inside one Charm at no extra cost) [lead].
- **Legend of the Five Rings 5e:** the school is a curriculum. Each school rank lists skills and techniques; "The character does not have to buy any specific one of those advancements, but has to spend a certain about of XP on the things on that list... Characters can spend XP on things that are not on the list, but such expenses do not count towards increasing in school rank" [checked, the beta review]; in the final rules off-list spending counts half [checked, forum]. A gate by amount spent on a theme, with no order inside it.
- **Burning Wheel / Torchbearer:** lifepaths give skills and traits; traits are voted by the table; wises are narrow knowledge skills [memory]. No tree. **The Riddle of Steel:** proficiencies with manoeuvres opened by proficiency level [memory]. **Trail of Cthulhu:** none: ability pools only [memory].
- **Daggerheart:** a class reads two of nine domains; "Choose two level one cards from your class's domains"; a subclass gives "its Foundation card" [read, SRD site], later a Specialization and a Mastery card, and a new domain card each level up to your level, with a small active loadout and the rest in a vault you can swap [memory]. Cards are the sheet.
- **Pathfinder 2e archetypes:** a Dedication feat, then feats from that archetype's list, and a rule that two more must be taken before another Dedication [memory]. A list behind one door. **D&D 5e subclass:** one choice, then no picks [memory].
- **Draw Steel:** class, subclass at level 1, a kit (gear and fighting style, swappable), then at set levels "Choose one heroic ability from the following options, each of which costs 3 wrath to use", then 5, 7, 9; a signature ability "can be used at will"; **Perks** arrive in their own slots at other levels (crafting, exploration, interpersonal, intrigue, lore, supernatural) [read, Steel Compendium]. Combat and non-combat never share a slot.
- **ICON** (jobs with talents picked from a job's list; narrative "bonds" on a fully separate track), **Beacon** (Lancer's shape in fantasy: classes with three ranks of abilities), **Gubat Banwa** (disciplines; techniques picked per level), **Nimble** (class features by level, a subclass, few picks), **DC20** (a class talent or general talent at set levels, a multiclass talent as one of the choices, "paths" for martial or spellcaster points): all [memory], not verified.

## 8. Video games: what their designers wrote

**World of Warcraft, 2004 to 2012: trees.** Three trees a class, five-rank talents, a point a level; "the majority of all talents were passive"; "Many talents were considered to be 'filler' talents, granting little benefit but being placed in such a position that taking them was required in order to gain access to higher tiers" [read, Warcraft Wiki].

**Mists of Pandaria (2012): trees removed for one choice of three every fifteen levels.** Greg Street's post [read, in full on Engadget]:

- "Choosing between a talent that grants 10% damage and one that grants 5% damage, all else being equal, isn't interesting... Choosing between a talent that grants you a root or a snare can be interesting. Which does more damage? Hard to say. Which is better? It depends on the situation."
- "In the case of the old trees, choosing the talent you want from among the talents that don't interest you isn't an interesting decision - it's a multiple choice test, and an easy one at that."
- "Look, we tried the talent tree model for seven years. We think it's fundamentally flawed and unfixable... It's not a matter of coming up with enough fun mechanics... The problem is the extreme number of combinations. When you have such a gigantic matrix, the chances of having unbeatable synergies, or combinations of talents that just don't work together is really high."
- "the alternative is to continue on with bloated trees that have a ton of inconsequential talents that you have to slog through to get to the fun stuff."
- On why a row holds three talents of the same kind: "what if the Ret player had to choose from three healing talents and couldn't sacrifice healing for damage? Now it gets interesting."
- "It is hard to determine the DPS value of Improved Sprint or Lichborne. Most of the Mists talents are things like the latter."
- Mandatory abilities left the choice: "Instead of having to click Raging Blow, we'll just give it to you, because frankly if you skip it, you're making a mistake".

**Dragonflight (2022): trees back.** Blizzard's preview [read]:

- "Visually, trees are still an intuitive way to represent the different paths one could follow while making many choices about a character. Trees also communicate dependencies and magnitude intuitively, without a lot of added rules."
- **Two trees, so two kinds of choice never compete:** "The class tree... has a higher proportion of utility choices... The specialization tree is predominantly focused on bonuses that enhance your power in your main role. Keeping these separate is one way of ensuring that you have some choices in both areas".
- "our goal in designing the trees is not to trap players in complicated choices in order to make a functional build... it's to set up the trees with intuitive paths that lead you to reasonable builds."
- Asked whether some points are obvious: "Often, yes... it would probably be a mistake if all 61 points were always heavily in play".
- Must-haves: "the more likely something is to be all but required for some players, the more we must place it in the tree in an appropriate location - one that still leaves the rest of your build choices very open."
- Rules: an arrow means you need one of the talents above; only two rows are gated by points spent ("unlike in pre-Mists of Pandaria trees, this is not the case for every row"); octagonal "choice" nodes hold two talents, one taken; the draft trees still carry "[2]/[4]/[6]%" multi-rank nodes.
- Free change: "you can change your talents anywhere, anytime, at no cost" and saved loadouts [read, the later post].

**Diablo.** II: a tree per tab, 20 ranks a skill, synergies added later so that early skills stayed worth points [memory]. III removed points and trees; Jay Wilson: "we focused a lot more on restricting the number of skills you have and having that be the interesting choice, as opposed to skill points, which are really commitments before you even know what you're committing to" [read, IGN as relayed by Blizzplanet]. IV put a tree back after players called the first design shallow; David Kim: "the Talent system needs more depth. Similarly, Skill system progression felt too simple, which created issues where a player would have no meaningful reason to spend their skill points"; the new tree splits actives from passives, "The branches contain skills and skill upgrades, while the roots contain powerful passive effects" [read].

**Guild Wars 2 (2015).** Trait lines with points that also gave stats became specializations: pick three of five lines, each with three automatic minor traits and three tiers of three; "They separate stats from traits, opening up builds by trait choice rather than stat choice"; "By locking traits to tiers and reducing the overall number of traits, we've made each choice much more compelling" [read, ArenaNet].

**Path of Exile.** One shared web of about 1,300 to 1,500 nodes, class as starting point; small "travel" nodes (+10 to an attribute) are the price of distance, "notables" are the named ones, "keystones" change a rule with a drawback; respec deliberately costly [lead and memory: no designer page opened]. It is the purest "filler is the cost of the path" design, and it works there because planning the route is the game.

**Skyrim.** A constellation of perks per skill, gated by that skill's level and by the perk before; most early perks are five ranks of +20% [memory]. Lead designer Bruce Nesmith on the removal that went with it: "We got rid of attributes in Skyrim and you know who complained? Almost nobody" [read, PC Gamer].

**Pillars of Eternity.** Josh Sawyer: "Talents are things that are not core to classes but can be used to flesh them out in different ways"; they "can either extend or modify standard class abilities" or "grant access to outliers" [read through a summarizing tool, RPG Codex interview]. His stated aim elsewhere was to narrow the gap between viable and optimal builds [lead, GDC 2016 talk].

**Borderlands** (three trees a character, five-rank number nodes, a game-changing skill every few rows and a capstone), **Divinity: Original Sin 2** (no tree: a flat talent list every few levels, schools gated by a point count) and **Baldur's Gate 3** (5e's feats, competing with ability increases): [memory], no designer text found. **Mark Rosewater**'s writing on choices (players take the strongest option even when it is less fun; a choice with a right answer is a test, not a choice) is [memory] and matches Street's first quote.

## 9. The six questions

**(1) Trees, lists, pyramids, paths.**

| Shape | Praised for | Blamed for |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed tree (FFG, old WoW, Borderlands) | Theme and direction; a readable sheet; "dependencies and magnitude intuitively, without a lot of added rules" [read, Blizzard] | Filler between the good boxes; one destination everybody races to; cookie-cutter routes; much work to write [checked] |
| One shared graph (Crucible, Path of Exile) | Every hybrid exists without a rule for it; the picture shows the build | Unreadable without software; travel costs; reception unknown for Crucible |
| Pyramid (Genesys) | No box "in your way"; cheap to write for any setting [checked] | Same popular talents for everyone; a tax of base picks; no theme [checked] |
| Flat list with prerequisites (Saga, W&G, Soulbound, D:OS2, old 2d12) | Total freedom; easy to extend | Chains become taxes; combat crowds out the rest; the list grows unreadable |
| Ranks inside a talent (Lancer, AGE, Symbaroum) | Depth or breadth is a real choice; each step is news | Fewer talents written per page of rules; III must be worth as much as a new I |
| Paths at set levels (Demon Lord, 5e subclass, Daggerheart's subclass cards) | No traps, no planning, fast | Little choice between the set levels |
| Careers / curricula (WFRP, L5R) | The world decides what you see; non-combat lists exist by profession | Bookkeeping; "a shopping list" |
| A row of like-for-like choices (WoW 2012, GW2, Draw Steel's "choose one 5-cost ability") | Each choice is between comparable things; combat never competes with utility | "Fewer choices" (Street concedes it) |

**When a drawn connection adds something a prerequisite line does not:** when one node can be reached from more than one parent (Dragonflight: "at least one of those previous talents"; Crucible: any neighbour), when position itself carries meaning (how deep, which ability, which ring), and when the picture is the sheet. A single chain (A, then Improved A) says nothing a "Requires A" line does not.

**(2) Filler and tax: why they appear, how they were removed.** Causes on the record: the tree must have a fixed number of boxes (FFG's 20) [mine, from the fan revisions]; rows gated by points spent need cheap things to spend on [read, Warcraft Wiki]; a point every level needs more picks than there are good ideas ("we don't think it's humanly possible to have 40-50 fun, interesting and balanced talents in a tree") [read]; a single best node at the end turns the rest into a road (Dedication) [checked]; a number that must be kept up. Removals: give the mandatory thing free (WoW 2012; Dragonflight's auto-granted abilities); take stats out of the pick (GW2); price the destination out of a race (Genesys); gate only a few rows (Dragonflight); let position be reached from several sides; cut the count (MoP: 6 choices; GW2: 9 traits).

**(3) Ranks inside a talent.** Three written steps are praised where found (Lancer, Symbaroum, AGE) and criticised only for uneven worth (AGE) [checked/memory]. The repeatable +1 is what fan revisions call filler and what WoW's designer dismissed: "Do you really notice that you now kill a creature in 2.9 GCDs instead of 3 GCDs?" [read]. Yet FFG players still want Grit and Toughened, because the numbers matter there. In WFRP 4e the ranked +1 SL is the named cause of the memory load [checked].

**(4) How many, and how many remembered.** Counts (mine, from the rules above): FFG, 10 to 25 boxes after a year, most passive; Genesys, 15 for the first Tier 5; Lancer, 3 talents at start, 15 ranks at the end; Demon Lord, 3 paths; 13th Age, 3 talents; WoW, 41 to 76 points before 2012, 6 after, 61 now; Crucible, 3 a level. No source measured recall. The indirect evidence: WFRP's "need to be remembered, all the time" [checked]; Blizzard's "it would probably be a mistake if all 61 points were always heavily in play" [read]; Daggerheart caps the active cards and vaults the rest [memory]. Always-on talents need no recall; conditional modifiers ("+1 SL on Tests to...") are the ones forgotten.

**(5) What makes two characters of one class differ.** FFG: which tree, then which second tree. Genesys: nothing structural (the worry on record). WFRP: career history. Lancer: which three talents, and how deep. Demon Lord: the expert and master path. 13th Age: three of nine talents. Draw Steel: subclass, kit, one ability per cost, and perks on a separate track. Crucible: where on the wheel you walked. WoW now: the class tree's utility picks, kept apart from the role tree on purpose.

**(6) A tree when numbers are flat.** Most trees found are built on numbers, and the filler is exactly their number nodes. The examples of horizontal content: Lancer's ranks (each a new reaction, move or exception); Symbaroum's degrees; Crucible's talents that grant spell parts, change which ability a Rune uses, or bridge to another sextant; Path of Exile's keystones (a rule changed, with a cost); WoW 2012's rows ("a root or a snare... It depends on the situation"); Draw Steel's perks. In those, what the structure is used for is not "bigger" but **sequence** (learn the base thing before its exception), **theme** (things that belong together sit together) and **exclusion** (one of these, not both). Where a design had only horizontal content and no numbers to pad with, it ended up small: three ranks, rows of three, three paths.

## 10. What seems to transfer

**(a) The shapes.**

| Shape | Games | What it solved | What it cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed tree per specialization | FFG Star Wars, Saga (small), Borderlands, WoW to 2012 | Theme, direction, a sheet you can read | Filler, a race to one node, order taxes, heavy to write |
| One shared graph | Crucible, Path of Exile | Hybrids for free; position is the prerequisite | Software needed; travel nodes |
| Tier pyramid | Genesys | Works for any setting; no blocked box | Sameness; base-tier tax; lost theme (trees were added back) |
| Flat list, prerequisites | Old 2d12 chapter, W&G, Soulbound, DH2 (priced by aptitude) | Freedom | Chains as tax; price gaps; unreadable when long |
| Three ranks in a talent | Lancer, AGE, Symbaroum, Beacon | Depth against breadth; each step a new rule | Each talent is three designs |
| Paths at set levels | Demon Lord, Weird Wizard, 5e, Daggerheart subclass cards | No traps, no hoarding | Few decisions |
| Career / curriculum | WFRP, L5R 5e | The fiction filters the list; non-combat lists exist | Bookkeeping |
| Like-for-like rows, separate tracks | WoW 2012, GW2, Draw Steel, AGE's focuses, Dragonflight's two trees | Combat and utility never compete; every option comparable | Fewer picks |

**(b) The documented causes of filler and tax, as tests.**

1. Does the structure need a fixed number of boxes? (FFG's 20.) Then some will be padding.
2. Is there one node everybody wants at the end? (Dedication.) Then the rest is a road.
3. Must points be spent in a row to open the next? (Old WoW.) Then cheap nodes exist to be spent on.
4. Are there more picks than good ideas? (Street: 40 to 50 a tree is not "humanly possible".)
5. Is a pick a number that an expected roll assumes? Then it is a tax.
6. Is a prerequisite weaker than the thing it opens, and never used again? (DH2 chains; the opposite is Exalted's Ride branch, praised.)
7. Does the price of the same character change with the order bought? (FFG's second specialization: "a concept tax".)
8. Can two options in one slot be compared by a number? Then one is right (Street's 10% and 5%).
9. Is a talent a conditional modifier to remember? (WFRP 4e.)

**(c) What the video game designers concluded, in their words.**

- Street, 2011: "interesting choices are fun. The word 'interesting' is key"; trees are "fundamentally flawed and unfixable" because of "the extreme number of combinations"; mandatory things are given, not chosen.
- Blizzard, 2022: trees "communicate dependencies and magnitude intuitively, without a lot of added rules"; keep utility and power in separate trees; "not to trap players"; not every point needs to be a live decision; change is free.
- Wilson, 2011: skill points "are really commitments before you even know what you're committing to".
- Kim, 2020: with too little structure "a player would have no meaningful reason to spend their skill points".
- ArenaNet, 2015: "separate stats from traits"; fewer traits, "each choice much more compelling".
- Read together: both of Blizzard's teams went from trees to almost nothing and back to trees, and what survived each time was (i) free respec, (ii) power kept apart from utility, (iii) mandatory abilities taken out of the choice.

**(d) What in 2d12's files already resembles one of these shapes.**

- `book/rules-src/set-aside/16-talents.md`: a flat list with a category, a level and Prerequisites, and chains (Brawler, then Kickboxer): the Saga / Wrath & Glory shape, with the chain that section 9 (1) says a drawn tree adds nothing to.
- `book/rules-src/05-characters.md`: slots of different kinds at set levels (a Skill Talent at 1 and 3, a Talent at 2, a Stage Talent at 4, 8, 12): separate tracks, as AGE's focuses, Draw Steel's perks and Dragonflight's two trees; the Stage Talent's rhythm is Crucible's signature every third level and Lancer's core bonus every third LL. The Hallmark "choice of two" and the two Escalation abilities per step are like-for-like rows (WoW 2012; a Dragonflight choice node). The Repertoire's Picks by Frequency are Draw Steel's "choose one" by cost. "Swapping on a Rest" is Dragonflight's and Crucible's free reset, wider than Lancer's one reallocation a level.
- `book/rules-backlog.md`, "TALENT STRUCTURE: PROPOSAL": ranks I to III (Lancer; note that Lancer's are three written rules, never a repeat), Minor and Major (a two-tier price), two tracks that never compete.
- `book/magic-design.md`, section 5l: every seed is "the base rule carries the limit; a Talent lifts it": the keystone / rule-exception kind of node, with no number in it. "A summoner's chain" and "a counterspell tree" are sequences (the base thing, then its exceptions), the one job section 9 (6) found a structure doing when nothing grows vertically. Section 5d's Mastery (levels in a Path) is the only ranked track already decided.
- `book/research/crucible.md`: the composing grammar bought as Talents; this report adds the tree those Talents sit on (section 6).
