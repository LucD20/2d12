# Enemies in tactical TTRPGs: what tables say they like and dislike

**Date:** 2026-11-25. **Asked by:** Lucas, before rebuilding the Bestiary's numbers from zero. **Scope:** opinions (forums, blogs, GM-advice essays, designer commentary, reviews) about enemies in general, minions, solo bosses, enemies above and below the party's level, elites and the weaker-than-a-PC rank. Other games' opinions only; nothing here is a 2d12 rule.

## How far to trust it

Every statement carries one of four marks.

- **[checked]** an opinion on a page that was opened today; the author or poster and the site are named. **Caveat:** pages were opened through a fetch tool that returns an extract made by a small model, so each quote is the tool's extract of the page, not a character-by-character reading. Long threads were read one page at a time (the page number is given); the other pages of the thread were not read.
- **[read]** rules text on a page that was opened (same caveat).
- **[lead]** seen only in a search tool's summary, or on a site that refused. Open the page before quoting it to anyone. The project's own record: about a third of such summaries were wrong or unfindable.
- **[memory]** from Claude's own knowledge of a game's rules, no page opened. Used only to explain what an opinion is about.

"Recurs" means several independent sources said it; "one voice" means one person did. EN World dominates the sample because it opens; Reddit, RPG Stack Exchange, the Paizo forums and Giant in the Playground did not (list at the end). That skews the sample toward long-time D&D 4e / 5e / Pathfinder 2e GMs.

## 1. Enemies in general

### Takeaway
What tables praise is an enemy that **does something of its own that the players must answer** (a signature ability, a behaviour, a change in the fight); what they resent is an enemy that only **absorbs hit points, takes their turn away, or cancels their ability with no cost and no warning**. GMs want a block that runs without a lookup and worry more about the number of different blocks in one fight than about the size of any one.

### Cited findings

**What is liked**

- **Exception-based monsters with their own tricks, recurs (4+ sources).** [checked] Mannahnin, EN World, 2025: "Most 4E monsters are fun and interesting and present tactical challenges even in the absence of interesting terrain", against "Most 5E monsters are inherently boring unless you add interesting terrain and objectives other than whittling down the HP" (https://www.enworld.org/threads/dungeoncraft-interview-with-mike-mearls.711720/page-16). [checked] Dualazi, EN World, 2016, on 5e monsters: they "have few-to-no options aside from generic attacks", and spellcasting is slapped on in place of an ability (https://www.enworld.org/threads/thing-i-thought-4e-did-better-monsters.512546/page-3). [checked] The Angry GM, April 2026, praises 4e-style exception-based design that builds monsters "like cards", each with distinct tactics (https://theangrygm.com/the-monsters-know-better-than-you/). [checked] James van Tonningen's review of Draw Steel: Monsters (GamingTrend, February 2026) rates highest that monsters are defined by what they do (https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/draw-steel-monsters-review/).
- **A behaviour that follows from the block, so players can learn it.** [checked] Keith Ammann (excerpt, 2019): a creature's ability scores give it a fighting style; creatures favour the features that tilt the odds; DMs go wrong when the fight becomes a "boring slugfest" with enemies charging straight in (https://reactormag.com/excerpts-the-monsters-know-what-theyre-doing-keith-ammann/). [checked] The Angry GM gives examples of traits that dictate play: berserkers want to be bloodied; ghouls paralyse so others attack with advantage (same Angry GM URL). [checked] Ben Torell on Lancer (Gordian Blade, January 2025): reusing consistent enemy builds lets players master a faction; constant new enemies left his players "overwhelmed" (https://gordianblade.substack.com/p/supercharge-your-lancer-campaign). One voice, but it is the only source found that names **learning the enemy** as the pleasure.
- **A threat beyond damage that changes where players stand.** [checked] Chris Sims (Critical Hits, 2010): gas-spore minions with a death burst added a threat most minions lack; "players learned to keep their distance" (https://critical-hits.com/blog/2010/07/22/minions-are-spice).
- **A visible change during the fight, recurs.** [checked] The Angry GM, 2015: transformations "break up the fight and force the players to change up their tactics"; he prefers "a small number of very obvious changes" to many small stat tweaks, placed just when the fight would become "an exchange of blows and a fight of attrition" (https://theangrygm.com/elemental-boogaloo/). [checked] Jack99, EN World, 2008: monsters that enrage at set hit point thresholds (https://www.enworld.org/threads/solo-monsters-and-the-risk-of-boredom.235212/, page 2). [checked] Nimble's designer Evan, 2024: a new dangerous ability at half health and another near death, so the fight escalates as the heroes get close to winning (https://nimblerpg.com/blogs/news/legendary-monsters-in-5e-how-to-fix-them; the designer's own promotion, not a table report).
- **Telegraphing and no surprise counters.** [checked] Mike Shea (Sly Flourish, December 2024) on boss protections: explain the mechanic before the fight, warn when such a boss appears, and set no "gotchas" where players spend abilities without knowing they can be countered (https://slyflourish.com/dreaded_blessings.html). [lead] An EN World thread "Telegraphing Attacks" has a poster narrating a wind-up the turn before a recharge attack so players can scatter (https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=7631332; the page did not open). [lead] The Angry GM is reported to have a "fully charged" rule where the GM announces the big attack a turn ahead (https://theangrygm.com/barking-at-your-players-advanced-combat-narration; not opened).
- **Enemies exist to make the PCs look good.** [checked] Mike Shea, 2017: boss fights exist to make the characters look awesome; "the goal of our game is fun" (https://slyflourish.com/improving_boss_fights.html). [checked] OldeGreybeard (Substack, 2024): build encounters around the party's strengths; a mob is "a perfect target" for a party that can hit several foes (https://oldegreybeard.substack.com/p/minions-gimmick-or-game-changer).
- **Morale, flight and surrender, recurs (3 sources), with a real cost.** [checked] Justin Alexander (The Alexandrian, 2021): fights to the death remove prisoners, pursuit and returning villains; he recommends a morale system, which also gives players a tactical reason to rout rather than kill. But he names why GMs avoid it: a fleeing NPC breaks the room's "firewall", prisoners are a chore, and encounters balanced for fights to the death feel like "cake-walks" when enemies run (https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/46079/roleplaying-games/the-death-wish-of-the-common-npc). [checked] Ammann: seriously wounded creatures flee, except fanatics and those who expect to be hunted down (Reactor URL above). [checked] GMMichael, EN World, 2020, wants a "Flees on" entry on each monster (https://www.enworld.org/threads/ending-the-slog.674185/).

**What is disliked**

- **The bag of hit points and the slog, recurs widely.** [checked] orbitalflower, 2021, on 4e: high hit points against damage, the "padded sumo"; four first-level kobold skirmishers totalled 135 hit points (https://orbitalflower.github.io/rpg/failure-dnd-4th-edition.html). [checked] Grazzt, EN World, 2008: higher-level fights "turned into grind-fests" (Risk of Boredom URL above). [checked] Reynard, EN World, 2020: "a boring slog of swings and misses and hit point attrition" (Ending the Slog URL).
- **The mop-up: decided but still running. Recurs; the fix is disputed.** [checked] Same thread: Dausuul: mop-up "can usually be handwaved", and any monster with self-preservation "will break and flee"; commandercrud: "Never just 'call it' that's the most lame way to end a combat ever"; GMMichael: hand-waving is fine, but not in a boss fight; Reynard hesitates because the resources spent still matter. [checked] Alexander Macris (Arbiter of Worlds, 2022) gives the structural reason: in attrition combat "the outcome of the battle becomes increasingly predictable as it proceeds" (https://arbiterofworlds.substack.com/p/analyzing-tabletop-rpg-combat-system).
- **Losing a turn, recurs.** [checked] pukunui, EN World, 2022: "not being able to do anything on your turn in combat", "especially not fun" over consecutive rounds (https://www.enworld.org/threads/its-not-fun-when.693809/). [checked] Distracted DM, EN World, 2024, wants stun "scary but not 'don't get to play the game'"; the softer versions floated in the thread are move-or-act (the 5e playtest's Dazed) and lower save DCs (https://www.enworld.org/threads/stun-the-fun-killer.703292/). [checked] Campbell, EN World, 2025: "The lose your turn stuff is never really fun and not very tactical either"; **disagreement**, Thomas Shey in the same thread: shutting opponents out of actions "can be very tactical" (https://www.enworld.org/threads/what-gets-me-playing-draw-steel-and-not-pathfinder-2e.714673/page-19). The project's `conditions-and-senses.md` already holds this as a lead; it is now checked.
- **An enemy that negates the player's ability for free, recurs (section 3 has the detail).** [checked] Quoted by Reynard, EN World, 2022: "It's not fun when you use your best spell and the BBEG just shrugs it off with no effect"; Greenstone.Walker: an enemy's counterspell can feel like "nooo fun for you this turn!" (It's not fun when URL). **Counter-voice:** mamba, same page: the same outcome comes from an ordinary made save.
- **Too many conditions on the PCs.** [lead] A Paizo poster on Pathfinder 2e: frightened, sickened, slowed and the like lead to "a lot of wasted actions and bad turns" (search summary; Paizo refused). [checked] Zaukrie, EN World, 2024: "The real problem here is conditions .... Some of them make the game less fun" (https://www.enworld.org/threads/legendary-resistance-shouldnt-be-optional.705677/page-9).
- **Reactions and triggered abilities: the GM's memory, not the players' feelings.** [checked] Justin Alexander, 2008: 4e's immediate actions fire on other characters' turns, so the DM holds many abilities in mind at once (https://thealexandrian.net/?p=1946). [checked] billd91, EN World, 2023: "I wouldn't add reactions to a minion's repertoire", because of table fuss (https://www.enworld.org/threads/minions-with-damage-thresholds.701247/page-6). No opened source complained, from the player's chair, about reactions that punish every action.

**What GMs want from a stat block**

- **Self-contained, no lookups, recurs strongly.** [checked] EN World, 2014: Sammael: "when I run a creature I want its stat block to be self-contained"; Obryn: "At-table usability and reference minimization are seriously that important to me", and cut the number of spells a monster. **One counter-voice:** Jeff Carlsen likes the plain spell list (https://www.enworld.org/threads/spells-in-stat-blocks-are-terrible.356994/page-4).
- **Fits a page or a card; a role label; a line of tactics.** [checked] EN World, 2023: grimmgoose (quoted by Quickleaf): "Honestly I just want the 4E statblocks back" with a role and short tactics notes; Zaukrie: "for complex monsters, 4e was the easiest block to read and run"; dave2008 keeps spells in the block to "3 or 4 max"; Sacrosanct and el-remmen want one monster a page; Tonguez wants an index card (https://www.enworld.org/threads/in-search-of-the-ideal-monster-presentation.700490/page-2).
- **Complexity is counted per encounter.** [checked] Alexander, 2008: Keep on the Shadowfell's encounter A3 has five kobold types with about 12 usable abilities between them, against about 14 for one 3e horned devil; mixing types is what makes a low-level 4e fight as heavy to run as a 15th-level 3e one. [checked] Noah Grand (Substack, 2024) advises keeping a boss fight to about three stat blocks (https://noahgrand.substack.com/p/youll-never-walk-alone). [checked] Nimble's designer calls 5e legendary blocks "packed with information I don't need". [checked] Van Tonningen on Draw Steel: the complexity is the appeal, and he has run "twenty or more monsters" with coloured bases, but it makes the monsters hard to modify.
- **A block says what, the GM supplies why.** [checked] The Angry GM, 2026: the block records what happens at the table; "You are executing a game. You are not roleplaying monsters."

### Inferences (mine)
- Nobody in the sample gave a number of abilities that is "too many" for one enemy. The two figures on record are per encounter: about 12 abilities across 5 types read as heavy (Alexander), and "about three stat blocks" (Grand).
- The liked features all create a decision for the players; the disliked ones all remove one. Stun on a PC and a free negation on a boss are the same complaint from two sides.

### Gaps
- No opened source on weaknesses to find, or on immunities as such; nothing from Keith Ammann beyond the book excerpt; Matt Colville's videos were not opened (no transcript found).
- Telegraphing is supported by one checked source (Shea, about protections) and two leads.

## 2. Minions and fodder

### Takeaway
The concept is loved more than any single rule for it. Praise recurs for the same four things (the heroes feel strong, area attacks matter, the GM tracks nothing, turns are fast). The complaints split in two families that pull in opposite directions: **"too fake / too free"** (1 hit point, auto-damage) and **"my damage was wasted"** (overkill, no carry-over). Every variant buys one at the cost of the other or of bookkeeping.

### Cited findings

**Why liked (recurs, 6+ voices)**
- [checked] The poll in "Minions: What Bugs You?" (EN World, 2009, 163 voters, several answers allowed): **44.0% had no problem** with 4e minions as written; thecasualoblivion: "about the best cannon fodder I've seen from an RPG" (https://www.enworld.org/threads/minions-what-bugs-you.259359/).
- [checked] WayOfTheFourElements, EN World, 2020: "Minions were the single greatest 4e innovation"; as a DM, tracking hit points for 50 or 200 opponents "is a chore". Li Shenron: against one-hit mooks the tactic becomes "to maximize their number of targets" (https://www.enworld.org/threads/the-impact-of-overkill-damage.673747/).
- [checked] Harlekin, EN World, 2009: his players "really like if they manage to kill 3-4 minions in a single attack" (https://www.enworld.org/threads/one-hit-kills-unsatisfying.249421/page-5). Halivar: four PCs against 250 zombie minions was "Serious fun" (What Bugs You, page 3).
- [checked] Chris Sims: minions are a spice that gives a burst of "my character is awesome"; if any still stand in round three they have done their job. [checked] The Angry GM, 2015: hacking through waves is "absolutely awesome"; he liked 4e minions more than its elites and solos (https://theangrygm.com/more-grist-for-the-mill-minion-groups-in-dd-5e; the last 6% of the page was not read).
- [checked] Tigris, EN World, 2026: 4e minions "worked well as a tactical element" (https://www.enworld.org/threads/rpg-evolution-the-mook-the-bad-and-the-ugly.718973/).

**Why disliked**
- **Automatic damage kills them for free: the top complaint.** [checked] Same poll: **37.1%** chose "Automatic damage automatically kills a minion", the largest complaint. doctorhook: "Minions are supposed to be easy kills, but not free kills"; Rhuarc: auto-damage "makes them just too worthless"; keterys names the powers that break them (Minion House Rules, below). The usual house rule was a save against it. **Disagreement:** avin doesn't mind (page 3).
- **"A missed attack never damages a minion" bothered few:** 10.1% in the same poll [checked]. [memory] The 4e rule: 1 hit point, never damaged by a miss. [checked] Shardstone, EN World, 2023, puts the line elsewhere: a made save for no damage is fine; what breaks belief is "the attack roll hitting and not doing anything repeatedly" (Damage Thresholds URL).
- **Fake, gamey, world-breaking: a steady minority.** [checked] In the poll: high-level monsters with 1 hit point 27.0%; normal monsters that become minions later 21.4%. Herremann the Wise (quoted by doctorhook): "They feel false and contrived to me in play", and they give the PCs "a perceived power that they have no right to". Raven Crowking: it makes "the imaginary world less real, for me". ExploderWizard: a monster that gets "punked in a single hit" is satisfying when the PC's strength did it, not a rule (one hit kills URL); and from the search summary of the same thread [lead]: killing in one hit "because that's what they're designed to do is not satisfying". ccs, 2020: minions are wasted time; a big hero should drop the full Monster Manual creature. **Defence:** Doug McCrae: "One hit point is code for 'dies in one hit from a PC'"; Hjorimir treats the single hit point "as a narrative construct"; Bert1000, 2019: "simply a gamist mechanic to emulate something that is true in the world" (https://www.enworld.org/threads/in-defense-of-4e-a-new-campaign-perspective.656542/page-10).
- **Wasted damage (overkill): recurs, mostly as a reason for the newer variants.** [checked] Michael Tresca (EN World column, May 2026): wasted overkill frustrates players; mass minions work at low levels and turn tedious at high ones. [lead] An EN World GM: his players hated minions "because they feel cheated of their damage rolls" (search summary; not on the page opened). [lead] Another: any minion system without overkill provides "only half" of what minions can. **Counter:** FrogReaver, 2020: overkill is overrated as a cost; Stalker0, 2009: fine for minions to "die by the bucket load".
- **They die before they do anything; too little damage for their price.** [checked] Dausuul, Jack99 and Rhuarc (What Bugs You, pages 2 to 3) all raise minion damage; thecasualoblivion: 12 minions, one area spell, 3 left; Sims agrees "to a point"; The Angry GM: "nothing more than speedbumps". Low risk for the reward: 25.2% in the poll. [checked] Cadfan: "minions work best as garnish rather than a main course."
- **Never hitting at all:** no opened page made this complaint about minions.

**The variants**
- **4e, 1 hit point:** above. [lead] A designer excerpt quoted on Paizo says the team first tried small hit point pools and settled on 1 so minions reliably drop in one hit (Paizo refused).
- **Two-hit and bloodied-then-dead minions: tried often, opinions split.** [checked] For: Nebulous, 2008: "Bloodied the first hit, dead the second hit"; Obryn sometimes gives "two hits before death (the first one bloodying them)"; Tourq (comment on Sims) uses them often. **Against:** Syrsuro, 2008, tried a bloodied marker and dropped it because "players just like wiping out those minions" (https://www.enworld.org/threads/4e-needs-monsters-between-minion-and-normal.246197/); The Angry GM: two-hit minions fixed some problems but sat awkwardly with the hit point system. [lead] A Giant in the Playground GM: always-two-hit minions hurt a table with two strikers, since a big hit and a small hit both needed two swings; another found them easy to track and a cure for the 1 hit point complaints (https://forums.giantitp.com/archive/index.php/t-477959.html; refused).
- **A variable number of hits by the quality of the hit: the most common house rule found, and well received.** [checked] "Minion House Rules" (EN World, 2009): under a damage threshold the minion is bloodied, at or over it the minion dies, a second hit always kills (ImperialParadox, Nytmare); Lakoda: "I would count all critical hits as beating the threshold"; fodigg: the aim is to stop kills by "really low damage rolls", not to make minions hard. The objections are practical: keterys wants no threshold to look up; DanmarLOK: a threshold "tends to devalue the controllers ability to control masses of minions", because spread damage is low (https://www.enworld.org/threads/minion-house-rules.249776/). FireLance, 2008: "A critical hit would kill such a monster immediately." TheHand (quoted), 2023: below the threshold the minion "became 'bloodied'", to soften the sting of a hit that does too little. VenezuelanWiz used threshold zombies so strikers still shine while the monsters keep a "mook feeling".
- **13th Age, a shared pool with overflow.** [checked] Sean Clark (Pelgrane Press, 2016): a well-placed attack that clears a crowd is satisfying, and the pool avoids the dragging of many one-hit minions on bad dice; costs: the partial-damage math is hard to follow with miniatures, and one sword blow killing several needs narration (his table's rule: describe it or it counts as one kill) (https://pelgranepress.com/2016/07/11/13th-age-mooks-destined-to-die/). [checked] EN World, 2014: Jer: "A very useful mechanic for theater of the mind type play"; (Psi)SeveredHead: it makes area and multi-target attacks "really strong"; Uskglass: "it's intended to be that way" (https://www.enworld.org/threads/13th-age-stealing-the-mook-rules-from-13a.358398/). [checked] The Angry GM **rejected** a shared pool for a grid game because D&D is precise about individual targets; his groups split one monster's hit points in four, with one shared turn and one shared reaction.
- **Flee, Mortals! (MCDM, for 5e).** [memory] A minion dies to damage; damage over its hit points can remove more minions in reach; a group attacks with one roll. [checked] billd91, EN World, 2023, who "did NOT like" 4e's minion rules on immersion grounds, calls MCDM's the first he would use; Shardstone: "They seem the most elegant and functional to me." [lead] Another poster: MCDM "solved the minion problem".
- **Draw Steel squads.** [read] Minions are bought four at a time and eight fill one hero's slot; a squad shares one Stamina pool; when one source (not an area) removes two or more minions' worth, the minions nearest the victim also fall; and "The knowledge of how minions work isn't a secret and shouldn't be kept from the players" (https://steelcompendium.io/v2/Read/bestiary/monster-basics/; first 100,000 characters). [checked] Van Tonningen: one roll and one pool keep turns fast. [checked] Dan Sz (Altoholism, 2025), on the Summoner's squads: two squads are "far more manageable", but moving the pieces will still take time (https://altoholism.blogspot.com/2025/11/breaking-down-minions-and-squads-for.html). No Director-side complaint was found; the search was thin.
- **Daggerheart.** [memory] Extra damage in steps removes extra minions; a group attack. [checked] EN World, 2025: zakael19: "Minions when you want to put tokens down and roll lots of dice", hordes for describing a crowd without managing it; Vael: hordes have "less upkeep" (https://www.enworld.org/threads/daggerheart-general-thread.713596/page-122). [lead] Elsewhere in that thread minions are called "worthless" and "a pain to run".
- **Savage Worlds Extras.** [memory] Shaken, then out on one wound. [checked] Retreater's first fight (EN World, 2024) dragged because the Extras had Toughness 9: a 9 on 2d6 to Shake, 13 to Wound; Reynard: "Mooks should go down fast" (https://www.enworld.org/threads/my-savage-experience.708390/). The fodder stopped being fodder as soon as most hits did nothing.
- **Feng Shui mooks.** [checked] Ruin Explorer, 2008, notes Feng Shui made mook status explicit; Tigris credits it as the origin. No opinion on its rule was found.
- **Lancer Grunts.** [checked] Torell uses Grunt and Elite templates "to represent cannon fodder and field officers". No table opinion on the Grunt was found.
- **7th Sea brute squads.** [checked] Jeffzilla, 2026: pooled units "eliminate the need for complex record-keeping".

**Should players know which are minions? Opinion leans to yes, by description.**
- [checked] EN World, 2008: Mengu: "per Mearls, the players should know what is and isn't a minion" (Mearls's own words not found); SDOgre hid it at first and his players "really struggled"; Jarjaxle and chaotix42 describe minions differently but won't name them; Ruin Explorer asks whether hiding it is "a little bit like cheating" (https://www.enworld.org/threads/should-players-know-minions-are-minions-from-a-rules-tactics-pov.235541/). [checked] Herremann the Wise objects that expecting minions "shifts the tactical focus". [checked] Clark suggests the opposite trick: let players take mooks for normal monsters at first. [read] Draw Steel prints that it is not a secret. [lead] The worry about spending a daily power on a minion appears only in a search summary.

**How many, and how GMs run them**
- [memory] 4e prices four minions as one standard monster. [checked] Somebloke, 2009: four is "rubbish", "8 at least"; Obryn halves their XP to double their number; Stalker0: the ratio should rise with level. [read] Draw Steel: eight to a hero. [checked] Sims: minions go in the gap between this level's budget and the next, never as the main challenge; arrive in waves or mid-fight. [checked] Running many: The Angry GM's groups of four take one turn; ruemere rolls once a group; AlViking uses 5e's mob rules; Jacob Lewis capped his groups at about four after replies called automatic group damage too much at low level (https://www.enworld.org/threads/make-minions-great-again.670980/).

### Inferences (mine)
- The house rule tables converged on most is "a weak hit bloodies, a strong hit or a critical kills, the second hit always kills". Its stated benefits: the low roll no longer kills, and the striker's big hit is still rewarded. Its stated cost: a marker per minion, and low spread damage (the controller's) kills less.
- Overflow is praised where position is loose (13th Age, theatre of the mind) and rejected or bounded where it is exact: The Angry GM refuses it on a grid; Draw Steel limits it to the nearest minions and excludes areas. No opened source complained about the *absence* of overflow as such; the complaint is "my big hit was wasted", and a hit-quality rule is the other answer found to it.
- The largest single complaint (auto-damage) is about damage that needs no roll, not about misses.

### Gaps
- Colville's minion video, Reddit, Stack Exchange: not opened. Table reports on Draw Steel and Daggerheart minions are thin; Feng Shui, Lancer and Savage Worlds have almost none here.

## 3. Solo bosses

### Takeaway
The diagnosis is nearly unanimous: one creature against a party loses on turns, is shut down by one condition, and then either dies in a round or grinds. The fixes rank clearly in what players say they accept: **more turns, and protection that costs the boss something the players can see**, are approved; **a free negation (legendary resistance) is the most disliked rule in the whole sample**; "boss plus others" is the most common advice.

### Cited findings

**The problems (each recurs)**
- **Action economy.** [checked] The Angry GM, 2015: "The action economy just worked against the monsters"; 5e solos "don't have enough actions" (https://theangrygm.com/return-of-the-son-of-the-dd-boss-fight-now-in-5e/). [checked] Mike Shea, 2021: bosses "typically don't have the tools or action economy to deal with a full party of adventurers", often true even of legendary ones (https://slyflourish.com/action_oriented_monsters.html).
- **Shutdown.** [checked] Shea, 2011, on 4e: "Dazing a solo is roughly five times more effective" than dazing a normal creature (https://slyflourish.com/solo_shakers.html). [checked] keterys, EN World, 2009: stunning a solo also cancels its immediate actions and wastes its recharge (https://www.enworld.org/threads/solo-monster-encounter-problems.265016/page-2). [checked] Noah Grand, 2024: stun-or-hold against a solo is "fundamentally a lose-lose proposition": it works and the fight is a non-event, or it is negated and the player loses the turn. [checked] Shea, 2024: a boss in a forcecage "can't do anything"; it is not filling its role.
- **Dies at once, or grinds.** [checked] Celebrim, EN World, 2018: a single boss "often goes down like a chump against the combined novas of a D&D party" (https://www.enworld.org/threads/boss-monsters-i-just-say-no.665710/). [checked] Ycore Rixle, 2008, on 4e solos: "They're boring. There aren't any climactic moments" (https://www.enworld.org/threads/discussing-4e-subsystems-elites-solos-minions-and-monster-xp.243712/page-2); keterys: some solos "seem designed to do crappy damage for waaaay too many rounds"; orbitalflower: Wizards later cut solo hit points by about 20%. Already in the project, checked there (`nova-burst.md`): Mike Mearls, 2025: a semi-optimised party "can vaporize boss monsters in a round or even less".
- **Static.** [checked] The Angry GM: "Fights were too static, too focused on dogpile strategies"; Plane Sailing, 2008: solos need ways out of the fighter's lockdown.

**The fixes and what is said of each**
- **Legendary resistance (5e): widely disliked.** [checked] Evan of Nimble: "an UNFUN, band-aid mechanic". Staffan, EN World, 2021: "just terrible" (https://www.enworld.org/threads/pf2-second-attempt-post-mortem.682276/page-21). Reynard, 2024: "wasting abilities -- especially big abilities -- feels bad" (https://www.enworld.org/threads/a-better-model-for-legendary-resistance.705137/, page 6). tetrasodium: it lands on the players who don't build for damage, "most impacted but least likely to shine". [lead] Players burn it with cantrips; a long wait for a turn that the DM then negates. **Defences:** James Gasik: it is Hexproof, protection from cheap removal; Blue: it holds the action economy even; Stormonu wants the DM's choice kept. [lead] GMs report it lands better when each use is announced as a small win for the players.
- **Alternatives posters call fair.** [checked] Sulicius: a use should cost the monster something, "burn legendary actions"; Zaukrie: "Using a legendary action to shrug off a condition is a great idea"; James Gasik: "what we really need is not more protection, but more turns", and "I wish more spells worked more like Sleep" (control that works under a hit point threshold); Reynard: if the protection is used, the player's spell is not spent; Stormonu: a visible "Bloody" or "Vulnerable" state that says when such spells work; mellored: turns at initiative +20, +10 and 0, so a stun costs a turn, not a round. From the search summary of the same threads [lead]: a negated effect leaves a weaker version behind; fixed triggers in place of a DM judgement call.
- **Pay to shake a condition.** [checked] Shea's 2011 "status shakers": the solo removes an effect but takes damage, or passes it to an ally, or becomes vulnerable; his principle: swap one penalty for another, never simply remove, and "not to be used lightly". [read] Draw Steel has an End Effect rule: take damage that "can't be reduced in any way" to end a save-ends effect (Steel Compendium; that it sits on solos and leaders is [memory]). [checked] Nimble's designer wants players to trigger the boss's resistance because using it "costs it dearly". [checked] Shea, 2024, "dreadful blessings": two or three a boss, a fixed number, announced.
- **Saves at the start of the turn (4e house rule): disputed.** [checked] renau1g: it "totally nerfs the status effects of the controller"; keterys: control is already far stronger on a solo, the change is "just a bit more sane".
- **Pathfinder 2e's Incapacitation: accepted in principle, resented in effect.** [checked] Justice and Rule, EN World, 2025: "for the most part, is a good rule"; Staffan: such spells are "often" useless in PF2; stonehead prefers a version tied to Bloodied, "a much better implementation of incapacitation than RAW" (https://www.enworld.org/threads/homebrew-rule-incapacitation-bloodied-and-powering-through.715231/). Staffan, 2022: "put hp caps on strong debuffs", so control finishes a weakened foe (https://www.enworld.org/threads/regarding-competence.685164/page-6). [lead] Paizo forums: "the WORST feature of PF2E"; against it, the trait is what stops the "I win" button.
- **More turns.** [checked] dave2008: acting more often "was the 4e MM3+ solo design strategy"; Zaukrie gives solos a 45% chance to act after each PC's turn. [checked] Nimble: the legendary monster acts after each hero, so one block fits any table size. [checked] Van Tonningen: Draw Steel's solos, two turns a round plus villain actions, are the book's best feature. [checked] The Angry GM's paragon monster: one full turn per remaining hit point pool, a turn lost with each pool; damage cannot carry between pools and conditions end when a pool falls. [lead] A Giant in the Playground GM: it "worked exactly as advertised"; another: "too gamey for my tastes". **One contrary voice** [lead]: "giving solos more attacks isn't really engaging".
- **A scripted arc (Colville's villain actions).** [checked, through Shea's summary] One extra action per round in a set order, simplified by Shea to Position, Escape, Explode; he adds "Keep your hand on the HP dial". [read] Draw Steel: three villain actions, each once an encounter, one a round. Colville's own video was not opened.
- **Boss plus others: the most common advice (6+ voices), with two warnings.** [checked] Shea, 2017: "Bosses don't fight alone." Noah Grand: a slightly weaker boss with a strong second and minions who make attack rolls. Lewis Pulsipher, 2018: "numerous monsters of several different kinds in a climax rather than one super boss". [checked] PF2e: CapnZapp, 2019: "Already L+2 is borderline", add low-level adds; dave2008: "I don't think PF2e does solos very well" (https://www.enworld.org/threads/creating-interesting-solo-monster-encounters.667807). **Warnings:** Ralif Redhammer: his eight players "still focus fire on the 'boss'" past the minions; Brian Liberge (comment on Sims): players clear the minions first and the boss sits less engaged.
- **Environment and stakes.** [checked] cmad1977: "a dynamic environment makes solo type monsters memorable"; HeinorNY: half the fun is the fight, half "mission accomplished".

**How long**
- [checked] Shea, 2023: no ideal count; many expect about three rounds for a fight; a multi-phase boss "might run six or nine", and a three-phase boss should not end in three rounds; "a battle should end precisely when it means to" (https://slyflourish.com/rounds_of_combat.html). [checked] David Hartlage (DM David, 2025): "Monsters typically die in three rounds" (https://dmdavid.com/tag/checks-versus-attack-rolls-every-aspiring-rpg-designer-and-some-veterens-should-read-this/, read through his March 2025 archive page). [checked] vagabundo, 2008: a solo as a set piece of "a few hours". [lead] Forum figures run from 5 to 10 rounds; a 13th Age thread: an hour is fine, a finale may take a session.

### Inferences (mine)
- The accepted anti-shutdown rules share three properties: the players' ability still does something, the boss visibly pays, and the players know before they commit. The rejected one has none.
- Two fixes found for "stun deletes the boss" need no immunity at all: more turns (a lost turn is a fraction of the round) and control that works fully only under a hit point threshold.

### Gaps
- ICON's legends, Lancer's Ultras, Knights of Last Call, The Rules Lawyers, Mark Seifter: nothing opened. Draw Steel's solos rest on one review.

## 4. Enemies above and below the party's level

### Takeaway
Where numbers scale, a higher-level enemy is felt first as **the PCs' abilities stopping working**, not as the enemy being strong; a lower-level one as either trivial or the pay-off of growth, depending on whether it still does anything. Bounded accuracy kept its promise for hordes and broke solos. For flat games, direct testimony on how "stronger" reads is thin.

### Cited findings

- **Higher level = whiff, crits taken, tools switched off (Pathfinder 2e; recurs).** [checked] payn, 2022: "you have a snowballs chance in hell of scoring a critical" against enemies that often do; "The enemy gets to enjoy a full tool kit while you toss origami figures to papercut it to death." Staffan: "resourceless skill actions are generally doomed to fail against bosses." Nilbog, 2019: the party "struggles to hit them" and "the monsters are critting easier". Retreater, 2021: "two criticals a turn on a single character" was not rare (https://www.enworld.org/threads/pathfinder-2-and-the-two-dichotomies.679170/). kenada: "The stat discrepancy felt bad for my players." payn: "boss fatigue".
- **The defence.** [checked] The-Magic-Sword: "Chipping down a terribly powerful foe is pretty much what defines a boss monster"; his optimised group "stomped" monsters far above them. kenada, 2020: higher level means harder to hit and to incapacitate, and criticals are "a dynamic scaling factor", so PF2 avoids 4e's hit point bloat (https://www.enworld.org/threads/pathfinder-2e-actual-play-experience.667587/page-27). Thomas Shey: tables win by trying combinations they don't normally try.
- **The preferred repair is the same as for bosses:** a lower-level boss plus others ([lead] Paizo: "PL+1 or PL+2 with a few minions or hazards"; [checked] CapnZapp).
- **Lower level: trivial, or pleasure.** [checked] CapnZapp (quoted): PF2 "automookifies" low-level monsters; dave2008 dislikes "A narrow range of automatic solos and mooks". Retreater: low-level enemies in numbers "just aren't a threat at all". **But** JmanTheDM: "the wave of mooks crashes against the wall of my players with little to no effect", and his players liked it; ronaldsf: more lower-level monsters avoids the sense that enemies always outclass the PCs; payn wishes his GM sent goblins at level 5 so the party could use its whole kit; The-Magic-Sword: many weak Shadows still wore the party down.
- **The treadmill and its defence.** [checked] EN World, 2010: ExploderWizard's jibe about making "boars level appropriate"; Hussar: "D&D has ALWAYS scaled opponents according to the needs of the 'story'" (https://www.enworld.org/threads/why-must-numbers-go-up.274553/page-26). The "old threats become fodder, which shows growth" defence was not found stated on an opened page; [lead] a poster says that feeling, "gnats to be swatted aside", does not happen in 5e past CR 3 or 4.
- **Bounded accuracy in practice.** [checked] For: cmad1977, 2015: "my 15th level fighter can't just wade through an army of orcs"; Sacrosanct's level 6 to 7 party nearly lost its rogue to kobolds; The Crimson Binome: one poor area damage roll and the surviving orcs took half the party (https://www.enworld.org/threads/bounded-accuracy-scenario-in-actual-play-low-cr-mobs-vs-higher-level-pcs.473148/). Against: Eric V, 2024: at high level it "ends up being white-room theory", gnolls were a nuisance; Arvin Natsuko: bigger spells turn weak foes "into nothing"; Tony Vargas, 2016: big hordes are "Potentially deadly, but also potentially dull"; pemerton: with expected damage a round the only measure, the 4e solo experience is lost.
- **When hits barely move, what reads as tougher?** [checked] Ovinomancer, 2015: high AC with low hit points was easy, lower AC with more hit points was the real challenge; "consider the ease of dispatch on a successful hit." [checked] Rob Heinsoo, 2020: raising a monster's level raises the numbers on both sides and does not make a tough fight of the same level, so 13th Age has a separate strength dial (https://robheinsoo.blogspot.com/2020/04/monster-design-tools-using-sizestrength.html). [checked] Mannahnin: hit points alone make a monster boring; Zardnaar: the first 4e Monster Manual had "Bloated HP very low damage". [lead] Mearls's reported fix for 5e monsters: fewer hit points, more damage. [checked] Savage Worlds: Toughness, not the to-hit number, decided whether Retreater's enemies felt beatable.
- **Missing.** [checked] Macris: on a miss a player "contributes nothing to the outcome of the battle", worst when turns are minutes apart; reliable moderate damage lets players feel they contributed. Dan Sz: "Missing in D&D is not really fun", but being missed by a monster "feels awesome". zakael19: Draw Steel "has a bias towards always letting you do something on a turn." **Against removing the miss:** The Angry GM, 2026: "the real problem here isn't actually the miss", it is slow turns; damage on a miss is "A little consolation prize"; a miss is the right gradual pressure. Hartlage: with many attack rolls, "Characters sometimes miss, but they hit often and still contribute." [lead] A critic: Draw Steel's roll "just fits you into 1 of 3 tiered results".

### Inferences (mine)
- The complaint about higher-level enemies is nearly always worded as the PCs' failure (I miss, my spell fails, my tricks are doomed), almost never as the enemy's strength. More hit points alone is called boring; more damage, more turns and abilities that must be answered are what posters ask for.
- Both sides of the miss debate agree a wasted turn hurts in proportion to the wait for the next one.

### Gaps
- OSR tables, Lancer tiers, Draw Steel's level gaps: no direct testimony found. The "fodder shows growth" defence is unverified.

## 5. Elites and lieutenants

### Takeaway
Little is said about elites, and that is the finding: when noticed, it is for being longer, not different.

### Cited findings
- [checked] Ycore Rixle, 2008: "No problems here, except that they sometimes drag on too long." WhatGravitas: some elites carry a bloodied trigger, which fits a creature that counts as two.
- [checked] Rob Heinsoo: 13th Age's double-strength monsters exist so a named NPC, "a spellcaster or wrecker", is "twice or three times as much trouble to handle"; the 150% elite suits dangerous leaders "who still appear in groups".
- [checked] Noah Grand: the boss's strong second-in-command covers what the boss can't do in one turn. Tony Vargas, 2009: a larger group led by an over-level elite makes the real challenge. Zakael19 on Daggerheart: leaders that activate several allies "really help an encounter feel better". Torell: elite templates as "field officers" make named characters stand out.
- [read] Draw Steel: an elite fills two hero slots; a leader the same and "can stand toe-to-toe with two or more heroes".
- [lead] 4e posters propose that an elite have two to four choices of at-will attack, or powers tied to its one encounter.

### Inferences (mine)
- The elites that are praised have a job in the group (commander, the boss's second, the wrecker); the ones merely tolerated are doubled hit points.

### Gaps
- No thread found on "what makes an elite feel elite"; Lancer's Elite and Veteran: no table reports.

## 6. The weaker-than-a-PC rank that still has hit points

### Takeaway
Tables asked for this rank before games printed it, and usually built it as a two-hit monster or a lower-level standard; printed versions draw almost no comment.

### Cited findings
- [checked] zoroaster100, EN World, 2008: minions have 1 hit point, normals take about four hits; he asks for a tier with half the hit points and half the XP. Replies: FireLance: workable, the cost is tracking, and a critical should kill outright; Yumepenguin: lower-level monsters that "die in 2 - 3 hits as opposed to 4 or 5"; Syrsuro (against): the real issue is encounter level, and players like one-hit kills.
- [checked] Heinsoo: 13th Age's weaklings (13 True Ways) are tougher than mooks, not killed in batches, and deliberately less of a combat threat so that their abilities are the problem.
- [read] Draw Steel: a horde creature is half a hero. [checked] Van Tonningen finds the horde and platoon labels "somewhat redundant" to the monsters' rules.
- [checked] The Angry GM's 5e groups (a quarter of a monster's hit points each) are this rank in effect, chosen so that each member is a real target.
- [lead] PF2e's level -2 creatures: "rarely used in serious encounters" (Paizo).

### Inferences (mine)
- The demand is for something that dies in two or three hits, which is also what the two-hit minion house rules were reaching for. Where minions already take a variable number of hits, tables may not tell the two ranks apart by durability alone; no source addresses this.

### Gaps
- No praise or criticism of a printed half-strength rank was found.

## Questions this raises for 2d12 (questions, not answers)

1. A Minion's ticks fall by the hit's tier, which is the "variable hits by quality" rule that tables converged on. The objection on record is that low, spread damage kills less. Does a Tier 1 result of an area effect remove a tick, and is that the outcome wanted?
2. The largest minion complaint was damage that needs no roll. Does anything in 2d12 deal such damage, and what does it do to ticks?
3. There is no overflow. The source of the overkill complaint is a big hit wasted on a small target. Does Tier 3 removing 3 ticks from a 2-tick Minion read as waste at the table, or does the tier already carry the reward?
4. Do players know which enemies are Minions and how many ticks they have? Most GMs describe it; one game prints that it is no secret.
5. A Grunt has hit points; a Minion takes about two hits. What tells them apart at the table besides bookkeeping?
6. "Classes must never fully shut a boss down": the accepted versions share three properties (the ability still does something, the boss visibly pays, the players know beforehand). Which does the Boss rule have?
7. With 4 AP and three tiers, does a condition cost a Boss part of a turn or all of it? More turns were named as an alternative to immunity.
8. With flat numbers there is little "whiff" to signal a stronger enemy. Posters call more Health alone boring. What says "above us"?
9. Bloodied and Strained are two visible thresholds. A change at half health is among the most-praised features. Does a monster change at either?
10. Morale: does a block say when the enemy runs, and does a rout count as the fight won?
11. Complexity is counted per encounter. How many different blocks and distinct abilities does one 2d12 fight put before the GM, with squads of three in one slot?
12. A miss exists at Tier 1. Both camps agree a wasted turn hurts in proportion to the wait. How long is the wait between a PC's turns when the GM runs squads and an Elite?
