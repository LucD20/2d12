# Recovery clocks, rest, and self-harm costs for casting

2026-11-18

## The question

In 2d12 a caster can Overreach: take a little Nonlethal damage to make a spell bigger. A 10-minute Breather removes all Nonlethal damage, so the cost is almost free between fights. If Nonlethal instead lasted until a Sleep, casters would need rest and martials would not. What do other games show about how fast a cost comes back, and about characters in one party recovering on different clocks?

## 1. The five-minute workday

**What it is.** Alphastream (2012, reposted 2015) [checked]: "after a very short period of time (such as one battle), the players decide to rest again". He says the players are not wrong: "The wizard is right. In most classic adventures, the party should rest and come back", and it is a problem because "the balance of the game assumes you don't rest too often". David Hartlage (DM David, 2017) [checked] gives the cause as "Many, perhaps most, narrative adventures lack time pressure" and the effect: "When players expect fewer fights in a day, spellcasters show more power."

**The designer's statement.** Jonathan Tweet, "Third Edition and Per-Day Spells" (EN World, 2020) [checked]: "Classes with lots of per-day power are too strong when there are one or two fights per day" and "Whatever per-day powers are balanced at one rate of fights per day are necessarily unbalanced at faster or slower rates." 3e left the rate to the table: "DMs rule on how many encounters the party has in a day". Per-encounter powers were rejected then because they "seemed too artificial".

**What designers did.**
- *D&D 4e.* Tweet [checked]: "Fourth edition established balance among the classes by giving all of them per-day and per-encounter powers." The rules [memory]: a short rest is 5 minutes and renews encounter powers; healing surges are the daily limit; an extended rest restores dailies and surges; every second encounter (a milestone) gives an action point, a small reward for pressing on.
- *13th Age* [read, 13thagesrd.com]: "After four battles, you'll usually earn a full heal-up", and "If the party is able to rest and decides to heal-up ahead of time, they suffer a campaign loss." The clock counts fights, not hours. Tweet [checked]: "We turned 3E's four-fights guideline into a hard rule." One EN World poster names this the recovery rule they dislike most [lead].
- *D&D 5e (2014).* Rules [read, Roll20 compendium]: a short rest is "at least 1 hour long"; a long rest "at least 8 hours long", restores all hit points, and no more than one "in a 24-hour period". The Dungeon Master's Guide's budget is six to eight medium or hard encounters a day with about two short rests [memory]. Reception [checked]: a DM David commenter says 5e "still bases its class power balance on a six encounters day"; in an EN World poll (July 2022) on "I've seen the 5-minute workday regularly in 5e", 31.6% said true, 5.1% true until a house rule, 63.2% false. EzekielRaiden there: "full casters get all the resources they'll ever get for the day all at once".
- *Pathfinder 2e.* No daily attrition is assumed for hit points (section 3). EN World posters [checked]: payn, "it was designed to get out of resource attrition on the adventuring day"; Retreater, "Attrition, for better or worse, doesn't have a place in PF2"; kenada on full hit points, "It's an assumption made to reason about the guidelines for building encounters." Spell slots stay daily, so the caster is still the clock: !DWolf, "Usually what stops them is the wizard running out of resources".
- *Draw Steel* (already in this project's notes): each fight won gives a Victory, heroes start the next fight with heroic resource equal to their Victories, and only a Respite (a day) restores Recoveries and ends the streak [memory for the Respite]. Pressing on is rewarded where the other games only punish resting.

## 2. Short-rest and long-rest classes in D&D 5e

**Rules.** 2014 Fighter [read]: Second Wind and Action Surge each say "you must finish a short or long rest before you can use it again". Warlock slots and Monk ki also return on a short rest; Wizard and Cleric slots on a long rest [memory].

**The table problem.** The balance needs about two short rests a day, each an hour long, and the characters who want that hour are not the ones who want to sleep. [lead, EN World summaries]: players "skip short rests very often in favour of long rests"; full casters were "significantly better than the likes of the fighter or monk when games had few short rests"; one poster: the only times the group split up were "when they wanted different rest schedules"; another: "2-3 short rest per long rest do seem to even out the resource differences". [checked] Burnside (EN World, 2022) names the dynamic at the table: "We need a LONG REST/But you CAN'T HAVE ONE".

**2024 revision.** It did not put everyone on one clock; it gave short-rest classes a way to recover without the hour, and long-rest classes a little on a short rest.
- Monk, Uncanny Metabolism [read, D&D Beyond]: "Once per Long Rest, regain all Focus Points when you roll Initiative."
- Warlock, Magical Cunning: half the slots back with a 1-minute ritual [lead], once a long rest [memory].
- Fighter, Second Wind: several uses, one back on a short rest, all on a long rest [memory; D&D Beyond lists it only as "Revised"].
- Barbarian: "also get rages back after a short rest, but only 1" [lead]. A poster reports the lead designer saying every class should have a short-rest incentive [lead, secondhand].

## 3. Nonlethal, stamina and "breather" damage

**D&D 3.5 / Pathfinder 1e nonlethal** [read, PF1 Core Rulebook on aonprd; 3.5 is the same wording, memory]: it "is healed quickly with rest"; "You heal nonlethal damage at the rate of 1 hit point per hour per character level"; and "When a spell or ability cures hit point damage, it also removes an equal amount of nonlethal damage." So a level 5 character loses 40 nonlethal in a night, or to any cure spell: the clock is hours, and healing magic shortens it.

**Starfinder** [read, aonsrd]: "You can spend 1 Resolve Point to regain lost Stamina Points, up to your normal maximum", which "requires 10 minutes of uninterrupted rest". Resolve returns once a day with "a full 8 hours of uninterrupted rest". The fast pool is refilled by spending the slow one: the breather is quick but not free.

**Pathfinder 2e Stamina variant** [read, aonprd]: Take a Breather is 10 minutes and 1 Resolve Point: "you regain all your Stamina Points". Both pools refill with "a full night's rest". The same page offers "No-Limit Stamina" (no Resolve cost) and warns to drop the Steel Your Resolve feat with it, "as it's too powerful if there's no cost to use it!": Paizo's own note that a free 10-minute refill changes what other things are worth.

**Pathfinder 2e Treat Wounds** [read]: "You spend 10 minutes treating one injured living creature"; "The target regains 2d8 Hit Points"; "The target is then temporarily immune to Treat Wounds actions for 1 hour". Reception [checked, EN World]: CapnZapp, "Medicine is what makes healing cheap and plentiful" and "the game totally expects you to start each encounter at full health"; Philip Benz, "The thing I don't like about PF2 RAW healing is that it eats into table time"; MerricB, "the party spent a lot of time after each combat healing up"; Retreater, "many GMs just hand wave healing as long as the characters have paid the 'feat tax' and have time." The cost became bookkeeping, and tables skip the bookkeeping.

**Savage Worlds** [read, a fan wiki's copy of the rules; edition not named]: three states on three clocks. Shaken clears in the round: "At the start of their turn, Shaken characters must attempt to recover", or a Benny "at any time". Fatigue: "Unless otherwise specified by the source, Fatigue and Exhaustion improve one level per hour." Each source of Fatigue may set its own time (radiation: one level per 24 hours [lead]). Wounds take days [memory].

**Second wind and breathers.** 5e Second Wind [read]: a bonus action, "1d10 + your fighter level", back on a short rest. 4e Second Wind [memory]: once an encounter, spends a healing surge (a daily resource). Draw Steel's Catch Breath [memory, in project notes]: spends a Recovery. In all three the in-fight heal is quick, and in two of them it draws on the day's pool.

## 4. Optional self-harm to cast harder

**Pathfinder 1e kineticist, Burn** [read, aonprd]: "For each point of burn she accepts, a kineticist takes 1 point of nonlethal damage per character level." "This damage can't be healed by any means other than getting a full night's rest." The cap is "3 + her Constitution modifier". Two more rules decide behaviour: Gather Power (a move action, or a full round) lowers a blast's burn cost by 1 to 3, and Elemental Overflow gives attack and damage bonuses equal to current burn. Reception [lead, Paizo and GitP summaries; pages refused]: burn was called "a disguised HP chassis modification" and "the worst class mechanic in the game"; a kinetic healer's cost read as "reducing somebody's maximum health for that day"; defenders said the aim was "to reduce Burn at all costs so you never take Burn in the first place" and that "a well-managed kineticist will not be spending very much burn at all". Players argued over what "a full night's rest" means. The pattern in those reports: a little burn taken early in the day as a buff, then burn avoided, with turns spent gathering power instead. Pathfinder 2e's kineticist [read] has no burn, no self-damage and no daily resource; its limit is inside the round ("only one overflow impulse per round").

**Dungeon Crawl Classics, spellburn** [read, Roll20 compendium]: "the wizard temporarily expends points of his Strength, Agility, or Stamina score"; "For every ability point he expends, the wizard adds +1 to his spell check"; "Ability scores lost via spellburn heal back at the rate of 1 point per day that the caster does not spellburn again." Burning 20 at once makes the next check a natural 20. Recovery is slow and stops if you burn again, so it is a save-it-for-the-big-moment tool; a Goodman Games forum thread notes the healing wording is "frustratingly imprecise" [lead].

**Blades in the Dark, pushing yourself** [read, bladesinthedark.com]: "take 2 stress" for +1d, +1 effect, or acting while incapacitated. Stress does not come back by resting: indulging a vice "can only be done when the crew has downtime", clearing "stress equal to the highest die result". "When a PC marks their last stress box, they suffer a level of trauma", permanent. Every character uses the same track, so nobody rests more than anyone else, and pushing is used constantly but watched.

**Shadowrun 5e, drain** [read, a fan summary only]: resisted with "Willpower + your tradition's mental attribute"; "Drain is usually Stun damage"; "you may choose any Force up to twice your Magic". Casting above Magic makes the drain Physical [lead: 4e corebook wording, "dangerous overcasting", "causing Physical Drain rather than Stun"]. Drain cannot be healed by magic or medkits [lead]. Stun returns by the hour and Physical by the day [memory], so overcasting moves the cost from a fast clock to a slow one.

**Dark Sun, defiling** [checked, athas.org, Raddu, 2012]: the price is paid by others. 2e: "the defiler leveled faster than the preserver"; in 4e "all arcane casters could defile" and "only allies take damage from a defiling player". The author's view: it "should be a vile deed and possibly only allowable in the direst situations". No recovery clock; the limit is social.

**Warhammer Fantasy Roleplay 4e, overcasting** [lead]: extra success levels on the casting roll buy extra damage or targets; the cost is the miscast risk, not a pool, so nothing recovers.

## 5. The principle

No essay states it outright; Tweet comes closest. Put together from the sources above:
- A cost is priced by its clock, not its size. Paizo's own warning on No-Limit Stamina, and the PF2 tables that hand-wave Treat Wounds, show a 10-minute cost becoming free outside a fight.
- A cost on the night's clock is a daily resource whatever it is called. The kineticist's burn was read as lower maximum hit points for the day [lead].
- Daily power is balanced at one rate of fights only (Tweet). When classes hold different amounts of it, the party rests on the clock of whoever holds the most.
- The published ways out: put every class on the same clocks (4e; Blades' stress), count fights instead of hours (13th Age), drop daily attrition (PF2 hit points), or pay for pressing on (Draw Steel, 4e milestones).

## Patterns

- **Per round** (Shaken, PF2 overflow, gathering power): the cost is tempo. Used every round; never causes a rest.
- **Per fight** (4e encounter powers, 13th Age rally): spent every fight, so it defines the class's normal turn. No rest pressure; fights can feel the same.
- **10 minutes** (Starfinder Stamina, Treat Wounds, 4e short rest): free unless a second, slower resource pays for it (Resolve, surges) or the fiction puts a clock on the party. Tables hand-wave it.
- **Hours** (3.5 nonlethal, Savage Worlds Fatigue, 5e short rest): long enough to be skipped at the table; 5e's hour is the documented case.
- **A night** (5e slots, burn, Resolve): a daily budget. Players either nova and rest, or hoard and never use it.
- **A day or more** (DCC spellburn, Blades stress, Shadowrun Physical drain): emergency use only, or managed across sessions.
- **Two clocks in one party:** the slowest clock's owner asks to stop; the others either lose nothing (and agree) or lose their tempo (and argue). 5e's 2024 answer was to give each side a little of the other's clock.

## What seems to transfer

My own reading, not rules.
- Moving Nonlethal to the Sleep clock would make Overreach a daily resource in everything but name; the kineticist is the closest precedent, and its reported result was avoidance plus argument about what counts as a rest.
- A 10-minute cost can keep its bite if the Breather itself spends something slow, as Starfinder does. 2d12 already has that slow resource in Rally Points, and martials hold it too.
- A cost that lives inside the fight (tempo, a Bane, lost Guard, damage that only matters until the fight ends) never touches the rest question. PF2's remastered kineticist took this road.
- With fights of about 3.5 rounds, damage that lasts "this fight" already bites if it is large enough to change who drops; its size inside the fight matters more than its duration after.
- If one character's costs outlast the others', the party rests on that character's clock. Blades and 4e avoid it by giving everyone the same track.
- Spam is governed by two things together: the clock and whether there is a cheaper way to get the same effect. Burn was avoided because Gather Power existed; spellburn is hoarded because it stacks to a guaranteed 20.
- A cost paid by allies (defiling) is limited by the table, not the rules: the opposite of least ambiguity.
- Rewarding pressing on (Victories, milestones) answers "who needs rest" from the other side and applies to every class equally.
