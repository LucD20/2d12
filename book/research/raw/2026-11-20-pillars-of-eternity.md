# Raw: Pillars of Eternity (1 and Deadfire), mechanics to borrow for Talents and class options

**Date:** 2026-11-20. Pillars' rules and other people's opinions, never 2d12's. The "For 2d12" lines are [mine]: suggestions for Lucas, not decisions.

## The question

What can 2d12's Talents and class options borrow from Pillars of Eternity 1 (2015) and Pillars of Eternity II: Deadfire (2018)? Their mechanics, what players love and dislike in them, and what survives at a real table (dice, paper, a GM). Eight topics: Paladin orders and priest gods; resources that build in a fight; the attack resolution and armor; Engagement; Afflictions and Inspirations; weapon modals and Soulbound weapons; ranger and talent design; tabletop adaptations.

## 1. Paladin Orders and the priests' gods

### How it works

**The Deadfire Paladin** [read, Fextralife Paladin; Shacknews subclasses]. Resource: **Zeal**, a per-encounter pool (3 at the start, +1 a Power Level, cap 11 [lead, the fandom Zeal page via snippet]). Core: **Flames of Devotion** (a weapon attack with +10 Accuracy and added fire; usable at range), **Sworn Enemy** (+20% damage against one marked foe), **Lay on Hands**, **Zealous Aura** (5 m; three mutually exclusive modes: **Focus** +5 Accuracy, **Charge** +25% Stride and +12 Defense when disengaging, **Endurance** +1 Armor Rating; Intellect widens it) and the passive **Faith and Conviction** (defenses that scale with how well your reputation matches your order's favoured dispositions). The aura upgrades fork into **Exalted Focus / Exalted Charge / Exalted Endurance** [checked, Fextralife Zealous Aura; their effects: Exalted Focus adds a Hit-to-Crit chance, Exalted Charge converts some incoming Hits to Grazes, Exalted Endurance adds regeneration, all [lead]].

**Each order changes one core ability, and has no penalty** (unlike every other class's subclass) [read, Shacknews; Fextralife]:

| Order | Changes | How | Favoured / disfavoured dispositions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bleak Walkers | Flames of Devotion | "Black Flames": +corrode damage and **Sickened** (−5 Con, −25% healing received) | Aggressive, Cruel / Benevolent, Diplomatic |
| Kind Wayfarers | Flames of Devotion | "White Flames": a hit **heals nearby allies** | Passionate, Benevolent / Cruel, Shady |
| Darcozzi Paladini | Lay on Hands | also gives a minor **Flame Shield** (armor vs freeze, burns attackers) | Clever, Passionate / Cruel, Stoic |
| Shieldbearers of St. Elcga | Lay on Hands | the target **cannot die for 4 s**; +1 Engagement with a shield | Diplomatic, Honest / Aggressive, Cruel |
| Goldpact Knights | Sworn Enemy | "Gilded Enmity": +4 Armor Rating **until enough hits break it** | Stoic, Rational / Aggressive, Passionate |
| Steel Garrote (DLC) | heals from damage to afflicted targets; a Garrote ability | | Rational, Cruel / Benevolent, Diplomatic |

Each order also has two order-only talents (17 optional paladin talents in all, per a forum snippet [lead]); the one read: Bleak Walkers' **The Black Path**, "When the paladin defeats an opponent, nearby enemies are Frightened" [lead, two snippets].

**The PoE1 Paladin** [read, GameBanshee abilities and talents; Ludo.guide orders]. Flames of Devotion 2/encounter (+20 Accuracy, +50% damage as Burn); Lay on Hands 2/encounter; three **modal auras** at level 3 (Zealous Focus: +6 Accuracy and 15% of Grazes become Hits; Zealous Endurance: +3 DR and 15% of Hits become Grazes; Zealous Charge: +2 move and +15 Defense when disengaging); **Exhortations** (Liberating: suspends an ally's hostile effects; Reinforcing: +25 Deflection; Reviving: raises a downed ally with temporary Endurance); **Sworn Enemy** 3/rest; **Inspiring Triumph** (when the paladin downs a foe, nearby allies +7 all defenses); **Coordinated Attacks** (the closest ally attacking your target gets +10 Accuracy). Each order had **two talents that each modify a core ability**: Bleak Walkers: Remember Rakhan Field (Flames +25% as Corrode) and The Black Path; Shieldbearers: Shielding Flames (Flames gives nearby allies +10 Deflection) and Shielding Touch (Lay on Hands gives +8 Deflection); Goldpact: Enduring Flames (Flames leaves a Burn over time) and Bond of Duty (Liberating Exhortation also protects against Charm/Confuse/Dominate); Kind Wayfarers: The Sword and the Shepherd (Flames heals nearby allies) and Strange Mercy (a kill heals allies nearby); Darcozzi: Inspiring Liberation (Liberating Exhortation also gives Accuracy) and Fires of Darcozzi Palace (effect not found) [read for Ludo.guide's pairings and GameBanshee's texts; the Kind Wayfarer/Goldpact texts are [lead]].

**The priests' gods.** PoE1: each god's talent gives **+10 Accuracy with that god's favoured weapons** plus **one special version of a spell in the god's theme** [read, GameBanshee priest talents]: Eothas (flail, morning star; a heal that shortens Frightened/Terrified), Wael (quarterstaff, rod; a special Dazzling Lights), Magran (sword, arquebus; a special Burst of Summer Flame), Berath (mace, great sword; a special Corrosive Siphon), Skaen (club, stiletto; a lesser Sneak Attack). Deadfire: each god **grants nine spells automatically, one per Power Level**, ending in the god's **Symbol** and a capstone [read, Shacknews]: Magran: Fan of Flames, **Spiritual Weapon**, Ray of Fire, Shining Beacon, **Flame Shield**, Pillar of Holy Fire, Torrent of Flame, Symbol of Magran, **Magran's Might**; Skaen ends in Minor Avatar, Symbol, Revenge of Skaen; Berath in Symbol, Hand of Berath; Eothas in Symbol, Light of Eothas; Wael in Symbol, Blessing of Wael. Deadfire update 40: only paladin and priest **must** take a subclass, and some order/god combinations are barred "for mechanical reasons" [read].

### What players love

- **The order rewrites a button you already press.** The favourites named are the ones whose change shows on every use: Bleak Walkers "the ideal choice for the DPS Paladin", "the best Alpha strike Paladins, since no other order adds an extra damage type to their Flames"; Kind Wayfarers the strongest overall for healing, White Flames and Strange Mercy [lead, snippets of several Obsidian threads and a guide]. On Steam, players pick an order first "for its roleplay" and build around it [checked, Steam 2828702373002632119].
- **The disposition tie** "adds a nice extra incentive to RP properly and stick to the approach of things your order... is supposed to have" [lead].

### What they dislike

- **Some orders are one weak tweak.** Goldpact Knights "don't seem great by comparison", liked "for roleplay and lore rather than raw power" [lead]. Kill-triggered order talents (Black Path, Strange Mercy) are criticized because they need the killing blow [lead; Ludo.guide's own note: "kill-triggered talents may be weak" [checked]].
- **The disposition tie is a dialogue tax for some:** "much more restrictive to roleplay because dialogue becomes taking a multiple choice test" [lead]; a Nexus request to remove the penalty [lead]; several threads asking how the math works ("does Obsidian even understand their own math", a thread title [lead]).
- Not verified: whether players felt the Deadfire orders were too small a difference (the thread "paladin orders and their influence on gameplay" refused).

### Tabletop fit

High. "Order X changes ability Y in this one way" is a printed line on a card; no bookkeeping. The disposition tie is the one part that needs a GM's judgment of behaviour (ambiguity) and was disliked by part of the audience.

### For 2d12 [mine]

The three steps Lucas's players asked for exist almost exactly in Pillars: **weapon** (Flames of Devotion's added damage type + rider; PoE1's god-favoured weapons), **aura** (Zealous Aura, three exclusive modes), **inward** (Deadfire capstones like Magran's Might, Minor Avatar; Lay on Hands variants). The borrowable rule: **a god (or order) does not add new buttons; it rewrites the damage subtype and the rider of buttons the class already has** (Smite's subtype and Tier 3 rider, the stance's mode, Lay On Hands' rider). Avoid the disposition-scaled defenses (a number driven by GM judgment); if a tenet matters, make it a written trigger, not a meter.

## 2. Resources that build during a fight

### How it works

- **Deadfire's shape** [checked, Gamepressure; Sawyer's own forum wording is [lead], a snippet]: almost everything is **per encounter** or a pool that refills between fights: Discipline (fighter), Zeal (paladin), Guile (rogue), Rage (barbarian), Bond (ranger), Mortification (monk), spell casts per encounter for casters. Abilities "that were once per rest can be now used once per battle" [checked, Gamepressure]. The one per-rest resource is **Empower** (limited uses per rest; boosts one ability's Power Level or refills the class pool; the count differs by source: "3 per rest, 1 per encounter" on the wiki, "twice per rest, +10 Power Level" in an older post [lead]).
- **Cipher Focus** [checked, RPG Codex on Kickstarter update; read, Fextralife]: "ciphers begin combat with a modest amount of Focus" and gain more **by dealing weapon damage** (Soul Whip); powers spend it, so ciphers must "dive into physical combat to generate more". Powers need a target with a soul. Talents tweak start, cap and gain (Keen Mind +10 starting Focus; Soul Mind: Focus passively while not damaged). Subclasses: **Ascendant** (bigger cap; reaching max Focus gives a temporary "Ascended" state with costs at 0, but weaker while not Ascended), **Soul Blade** (dumps the whole pool into raw damage), **Beguiler** (refunds Focus vs targets open to Sneak Attack) [read, Shacknews].
- **Chanter phrases** [read, Fextralife Chanter]: the chanter builds a **chant** at level-up: an ordered loop of passive Phrases (each 6 s, each a small aura buff/debuff, with **Linger** so consecutive phrases overlap). Each completed phrase adds a point; **Invocations** spend a set number of phrases. The chanter attacks or casts normally while chanting. Subclasses rewrite costs (Skald: offensive invocations −1, others +1, weapon crits 50% chance to add a phrase; Troubadour: longer Linger, all invocations +1; Beckoner: summons doubled, cheaper [Shacknews] or dearer [Fextralife]; Bellower: invocations eat all phrases) [read].
- **Monk Wounds** [checked, RPG Codex on update 52]: "you want to take damage, so you get Wounds, so you can perform extraordinary attacks." Taking damage past a threshold gives Wounds; abilities spend them; Wounds vanish at the end of combat. Talents change the threshold (Lesser Wounds) or give a chance of a Wound on a miss (Imagined Pain) [read, Fextralife via snippet]. Helwalker starts with Wounds and gains Might per Wound but takes more damage per Wound; Shattered Pillar gains Wounds **from damage it deals** instead [read, Shacknews].
- **Rogue** [lead, Obsidian update 71 and the Fextralife Sneak Attack page, both seen as snippets]: **Sneak Attack** adds damage against a target that is Flanked **or has any Affliction** (or in the fight's first 2 s); **Deathblows** adds more when the target has **two or more** qualifying conditions. Subclass Streetfighter: stronger when Flanked or Bloodied, weaker otherwise.
- **Barbarian** [lead]: **Carnage** (every melee hit also strikes nearby foes), **Brute Force** (melee attacks Fortitude when it is lower than Deflection), Rage spent on Frenzy etc. **Fighter**: **Unbending** (a share of each blow taken becomes healing over time) [lead].
- **Deadfire multiclassing** [read, update 40]: chosen at creation; one ability from each class at each Power Level, but Power Levels arrive later ("Fireball at 5th level, but a battlemage... 7th").

### What players love

- Sawyer's own verdict: "I think focusing less on powers as inherently per-rest versus per-encounter will probably be a good thing"; "most players will not take per-rest abilities" [checked, RPG Codex GDC Europe 2016 interview]. He also wanted to keep "the long term strategic wear-down" so it still feels "D&D-ish" [checked, same].
- The cipher's loop (fight to fuel your powers) is the design most often adapted to paper (a 5e and a PF2 conversion found; section 8) [checked]. Player love for it is **not verified** (no page with players saying it).
- The monk's "pain as fuel" is called one of the best front-line classes by guides [lead].
- Chanter: reviewers liked the concept ("layering chants into a song") [lead, RPGWatch snippet].

### What they dislike

- **Chanter: "quite boring to play"**, "fewer options than any other class, so they are pretty boring to build", "you just sit there and auto-attack" [lead, several Obsidian thread snippets; "Chanters need a redesign" and "how are chanters being made more fun to play" are thread titles]. The passive-loop resource plays itself.
- **The death of Vancian casting** was mourned by some ("the loss of Vancian casting", a thread title; "attrition should be through damage taken, not ability use", a fan opinion) [lead].
- Multiclass vs single class: a near even poll (65 / 61) and a long argument that single class is either "10x stronger" or pointless [lead].
- Ranger Bonded Grief (see 7) is the resource-adjacent complaint most repeated.

### Tabletop fit

- Per-encounter pools: excellent (a box to tick, refilled after the fight).
- Cipher Focus: good if gaining it is discrete (the 5e conversion: one Focus die per weapon hit [read]); the real-time "damage dealt = Focus" is computer bookkeeping.
- Chanter: the loop of phrases with linger and overlap is computer bookkeeping; the core (a counter that rises each round, spent on big effects) is trivial on paper, and 2d12 already has it (Escalation, Surge).
- Wounds: one counter, gained on a clear trigger; fine.
- Sneak Attack/Deathblows keyed to conditions: excellent (look at the target's conditions).

### For 2d12 [mine]

2d12 already has the Chanter's core (Surge rises with Escalation). Worth borrowing: **martial pools fed by a defined event** (Focus on a hit at Tier 2+, Wounds when you lose Vigor, a Phrase when an ally acts on your aura) as class Features, and **Talents that change the feed, not the size** (start with 1, gain on a miss too, gain when hit instead of when hitting): the horizontal shape of Keen Mind / Imagined Pain / Shattered Pillar. And the rogue's **"bonus against any condition, more against two"** as a teamwork reward that reads the existing condition list (no flat roll bonus: extra damage or a rider). Avoid a resource that runs itself (the chanter's lesson).

## 3. The attack: miss / graze / hit / crit, and armor

### How it works

- **One roll, four bands** [read, Fextralife Combat and Attack Resolution; Obsidian update 39]: d100 + (Accuracy − Defense). Deadfire: 30 or less Miss, 31–50 Graze, 51–100 Hit, over 100 Crit (Fextralife; the fandom table seen in a snippet says Miss 1–24 / Graze 25–49, and Shacknews says 25–50 [lead]). Graze: −50% damage **and −50% duration**; Crit: +25% damage, +50% Penetration, +25% duration [read, Fextralife]. Four defenses: Deflection (weapons), Fortitude (body), Reflex (areas), Will (mind).
- **History.** The 2013 design (update 39): equal Accuracy and defense gave Miss 01–05, Graze 06–50, Hit 51–95, Crit 96–100, and "the majority of attacks wind up being Hits or Grazes" [read]. Sawyer later: "crit = 150% max damage/duration, hit = normal, graze = 50% minimum, miss = no effect" [lead]. PoE1 shipped with Graze 16–50 [lead, manual errata via snippet]. Deadfire: grazes **only for attacks that can graze** (a graze roll on a non-grazing attack is a Miss) [read, Fextralife]; Gamepressure says grazes "appear only with given abilities" [checked]; a forum snippet says the first beta had no graze on regular attacks and a thread "graze back in" followed [lead]. **Which attacks graze in the final Deadfire is not settled by what I could open.**
- **Conversions as a design space** [read, GameBanshee; Fextralife Inspirations]: many effects move a result one band: Zealous Focus (15% of Grazes → Hits), Zealous Endurance (15% of incoming Hits → Grazes), Aware (50% Graze → Hit), Intuitive (+25% Hit → Crit), Paralyzed targets (25% of hits → crits), Berserker (Hits → Crits in melee).
- **Armor (Deadfire)** [read, Magic Game World; Fextralife]: each damage type has an Armor Rating; the weapon has Penetration. Penetration ≥ Armor: full damage; ≥ 2× Armor: +30%; below Armor: **−25% per point short, to −75%** (Magic Game World and the fandom snippet). The beta was a flat −70% when short ("No Penetration (−70% Damage)" on Fextralife; "even one point below you will do 30% damage" on a Nexus page) [lead]. PoE1 used Damage Reduction (a flat subtraction) instead [read, update 39]. Sawyer on the earlier "DT" model: it "worked well on paper and scaled well, it was unintuitive when put into the game" [read, update 39].

### What players love

- Sawyer: "Grazes, hits, and crits were the norm, so damage output felt much better"; "dealing some damage was better than no damage at all" (the article's account of his view) [checked, Shacknews history]. A graze "sits between a full hit and a miss" [lead].
- Armor by damage type made weapon choice matter (a Steam poster: weapon users "need extra weapon slots to switch damage types against resistant enemies") [checked, Steam 1696046342864588592].

### What they dislike

- Grazes "made combat feel too difficult to track" amid effects and UI [checked, Shacknews]; Sawyer himself: "the graze/hit/miss/crit system is a little bit more complex than the basic hit mechanic" and the UI "does not give great feedback" [checked, RPG Codex interview]. Beta threads "Graze gotta go?", "Is the regular graze really needed" [lead]; "graze-heavy streaks feel bad... a miss streak... reads as stalled fights" [lead].
- Penetration: "armor penetration is a pain in the ass, stacking armor is OP" (a thread title) and "how does the penetration mechanic feel like" (ten pages) [lead]. The flat −70% cliff is what was patched [lead].

### Tabletop fit

- Bands on a roll: excellent (2d12 already does it with Tiers). Percent conversions ("15% of grazes become hits") are not: a table needs a trigger, not a percentage.
- Penetration vs Armor per damage type: one subtraction per hit and a stepped result is playable; a separate Armor Rating per type per creature is a lot of numbers on a stat block. Obsidian itself: the CRPG uses "a virtual 100-sided die with formulas that would be hard to calculate at a physical table" [read, "What is Eternity?"].

### For 2d12 [mine]

2d12's Tier 1 / 2 / 3 / Critical is already Pillars' miss/graze/hit/crit, with misses rare by design. Two things to borrow: (1) **the "some effect below the DC" principle as a card property** (Deadfire: only some attacks graze) = a Tier 1 line on chosen cards, and **a Tier 1 condition lasts less** (graze halves duration); (2) **"move one Tier" effects as Talent material** (Zealous Focus, Aware, Paralyzed's hits-to-crits): the trigger made discrete (on Doubles, once a round, against a Pressed target), never a percentage and never a flat number. Armor: 2d12's Resist/Weak by subtype already covers it; Pillars' lesson is **no cliff** (the flat −70% was hated and patched to steps).

## 4. Engagement

### How it works

[read, Obsidian update 44; Fextralife Combat] A melee combatant with a melee weapon, standing still, **engages** a foe who comes near, up to an **engagement limit ("the standard is 1")**. Moving any significant distance away provokes a **Disengagement Attack** (bonus Accuracy, much more damage, briefly stops the target). Engagement ends if the engager switches target, moves, is paralyzed/knocked down or does a non-melee action; Might afflictions, Paralyze, Petrified and Prone end it. Abilities: Fighter **Defender** (engage 2 more, longer reach), Rogue **Escape** (leave without the attack), Barbarian **Wild Rush**, Wizard **Grimoire Slam** (knock back out of it). Deadfire: Unbroken fighters +1 Engagement, Shieldbearers +1 with a shield, Spear modal "Engaging Thrust" and Pollaxe "Engaging Threat" add engagement, the **Swift** inspiration "cannot be Engaged", Ghost Heart pets immune [read, Shacknews; Fextralife Inspirations]. Stated purpose: "melee characters' inability to control an area and ranged characters' ability to 'kite' melee characters" [read, update 44].

### What players love

Mostly accepted at PoE1's launch (Beamdog thread [checked]): "It should be pretty easy to stab someone who turns around and tries to run away" (meagloth); it makes kiting a wizard "much harder... Neat" (CrevsDaak); "it makes moving your guys a lot more tactical" [lead]; it comes from 3rd-edition tabletop (jjstraka34) [checked].

### What they dislike

- "A love it or hate it affair"; a poll 26 yes / 31 no on keeping it [lead, Obsidian snippets]. RPG Codex's review: an instant attack with no recovery or animation breaks the game's own logic [lead].
- **Unclear triggers**: "unexplained disengagement attacks", sound hiding the indicator (Qbert); too sensitive to small moves (typo_tilly) [checked, Beamdog]. The AI exploiting it (shades teleporting onto mages) [checked].
- **Turn-based Deadfire**: "disengagement attacks happen often when closing the distance to the enemies... So it does the opposite of what it's supposed to do"; on-hit effects from them "mess up your entire turn" [lead]. A community patch reworked disengagement [lead].
- Sawyer: engagement "can be useful, but it needs to be looked at again to make it clearer"; "I don't think it needs to be exactly what it was in Pillars" [checked, RPG Codex interview].

### Tabletop fit

Better at a table than on screen: the complaints are about invisible triggers and real-time pathing, which a grid and a written rule remove (it is the attack of opportunity with a **limit on how many foes one fighter can hold**).

### For 2d12 [mine]

2d12 has "Engaged" (ranged penalty). Borrowable: **an engagement limit as a number a front-liner can widen** (Defender +2, a spear +1, a shield +1): that is horizontal (more foes held, not a bigger roll) and a clean Talent and Technique hook; and the counter-Talents (Escape, Swift: cannot be Engaged). Lesson: write exactly what starts and ends Engagement, and **don't let approaching provoke** (the turn-based complaint).

## 5. Afflictions and Inspirations (Deadfire's keyword system)

### How it works

[read, Fextralife Afflictions and Inspirations; Magic Game World] Every status that touches an attribute is grouped under it, **three tiers each**, each tier −5 to that attribute plus a growing effect:

| Attribute | Tier 1 | Tier 2 | Tier 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Might | Staggered (cannot Engage) | Dazed (+ −4 Penetration) | Stunned (cannot act, defenses down) |
| Constitution | Sickened (−25% healing) | Weakened (−50% healing) | Enfeebled (cannot be healed, hostile effects last longer) |
| Dexterity | Hobbled (Stride down) | Immobilized (cannot move) | Paralyzed (cannot act, 25% of hits become crits) |
| Perception | Distracted (Flanked) | Disoriented (+50% Recovery) | Blinded (−10 Accuracy, range halved) |
| Intellect | Confused (hits friend or foe) | Charmed (fights for the enemy, basic attacks) | Dominated (fights with everything) |
| Resolve | Shaken (−Power Level) | Frightened (no hostile abilities) | Terrified (only flees) |

Inspirations mirror them (Might: Strong / Tenacious / Energized; Con: Fit / Hardy / Robust; Dex: Quick / Nimble / Swift; Per: Insightful / Aware / Intuitive; Int: Smart / Acute / Brilliant; Res: Steadfast / Resolute / Courageous), each +5 plus a growing extra.

Rules: **an Inspiration and an Affliction of the same attribute cancel each other** (usable to strip an enemy's buff or an ally's debuff) [read, Magic Game World; fandom snippet]; two of the same type: "the stronger of the two will prevail", they don't stack; reapplying the same refreshes duration [read]. **Resistance to an attribute downgrades incoming afflictions one tier and blocks tier 1; Weakness raises them one tier** [read, Fextralife; a developer-confirmed reply in a forum thread, per a snippet [lead]]. Petrify is a special fourth Dex tier that resistance does not stop [lead]. Generally "only the best bonuses and the worst penalties are applied" [read].

Sawyer's reasons, as far as found: status effects were reorganized ("cleaning up how status effects are organized"); stacking "could be simpler and cleaner"; and hard counters should not be total: "It's not like they're immune to all the status effects, just a subset" [checked, RPG Codex interview]. GameBanshee's GDC report: Sawyer criticized Baldur's Gate II's "hard counter" approach [checked].

### What players love

- It teaches itself: any Intellect inspiration counters every Intellect affliction, so one learned rule covers many effects [read; the praise is mine, no player page found saying it].
- The cancellation as a tactic (cleanse by buffing) is presented as an option by guides [checked, Magic Game World].
- Mostly positive in TB mode reviews; no direct page [not verified].

### What they dislike

- **Confusing names and too many keywords**: "Affliction/Inspirations unnecessarily obfuscated" (thread title); "too many inspirations and afflictions", the "X Afflictions" naming is confusing; "lots of confusing talent descriptions"; many abilities share one icon [lead, Obsidian snippets]. A later post: less convoluted after a while, but not free of confusing parts [lead].
- **Resistances too easy to get** ("are affliction resistances too strong / too easy to obtain", a thread title) [lead].
- **Suppression**: buffs of the same kind silently not stacking ("just found out about suppression in Deadfire") [lead].
- "Whats the point of Brilliant inspiration" (some top tiers judged useless) [lead].

### Tabletop fit

Very good: a ladder per family is a small table; "resistance = one tier down" and "opposite cancels" are one-line rules. The −5 attribute riders are the computer part (every tier changes derived numbers) and can be dropped.

### For 2d12 [mine]

The strongest idea for the **"bosses never fully shut down"** rule: **a boss is not immune; its Resist to a condition family moves the condition one rung down** (Stunned-like → Dazed-like; tier 1 blocked), and Weak moves it one up. That uses 2d12's own Resist/Weak words. It needs 2d12's conditions grouped into **families with rungs** (for example Slowed → Immobilized; Hindered → Dazed → Staggered; Frightened as a rung) and each rung's rule written once (least ambiguity). Also borrowable: **"an opposite boon cancels the condition"** as Talent or Paladin material (Shield of Faith or Lay On Hands removes one family). Avoid Pillars' mistakes: a name that says the family, few families, and a written stacking rule.

## 6. Weapon modals, proficiencies and Soulbound weapons

### How it works

- **Proficiency** [read, Shacknews; Fextralife]: a character is proficient with a few weapon types (two at creation); using another weapon has **no penalty**, you only lack its modal. Devoted fighters pick one weapon (better crits and Penetration with it, Accuracy penalty with others, no further proficiencies).
- **Each weapon type has one modal (a toggle with a trade-off)** [read, Shacknews table], for example: Great Sword **Savage Attack** (more damage, less Accuracy), Sword **Half-Sword** (+Penetration, −Deflection), Estoc **Vulnerable Thrust** (Penetration, −Deflection), Rapier **Needle Strike** (+Accuracy, slower), Dagger **Parrying Blade** (+melee Deflection, less damage), Morning Star **Body Blows** (lowers the target's Fortitude, less damage), Club **Bewildering Blows** (lowers Will), Flail **Unbalancing Strikes** (lowers Reflex), Hatchet **Interfering Strikes**, Battle Axe **Bleeding Cut** (bleed, slower), Mace **Rending Smash** (damages armor, slower), Spear **Engaging Thrust** (engage one more foe, less Stride), Pollaxe **Engaging Threat**, Pike **Exposing Strikes** (exposes weak points to allies, less damage), Quarterstaff **Defensive Strike**; Hunting Bow **Rapid Shot** (faster, less accurate; −15 Accuracy per one guide), War Bow **Overdraw** (Penetration, slower draws), Crossbow **Interrupting Shot**, Arbalest **Overbearing Shot** (knocks Prone), Arquebus **Aimed Shot**, Blunderbuss **Powder Burns**; Small shield **Binding Block** (a parried melee attack gives Accuracy on your next attack), Medium **Block** (can fully resist one weapon attack), Large **The Wall** (big ranged/Reflex reduction, cannot move).
- PoE1 had generic modals as talents instead (Savage Attack +20% damage −5 Accuracy) and the **Weapon Focus** talents: +6 Accuracy with a group named by social class (Soldier, Knight, Noble, Peasant, Ruffian, Adventurer) [read, search summary of the fandom and Prima pages].
- **Soulbound weapons** (PoE1 White March onward, Deadfire) [lead]: a unique weapon bound to one wielder that unlocks powers and **more of its lore text** level by level through objectives (kill N of a creature type, reach an attribute score, or quest steps). Steadfast's final unlock once required 30 Might (changed in patch 3.02) [lead].

### What players love

- "Some of the modals are fantastic while others are meh"; Morningstar's Fortitude drop praised for its synergy with Fortitude-targeting abilities; the boss-fight toggles (Estoc "for boss fights but not much else") [lead].
- A beta poster's principle: weapon-style talents "shouldn't be a flat passive bonus but rather modals that you can enact for trade offs" [lead]. Close to 2d12's own rule.
- Soulbound: the concept (a weapon that grows with your story) is liked; quest-based unlocks "were fine" [lead].

### What they dislike

- Penetration modals "useless past early game"; Savage Attack's Accuracy cost can make it worse than a normal attack; "the designers seemed to run out of interesting ideas for modals"; "I don't need even more per-encounter stuff to micromanage" [lead].
- Sawyer on PoE1's modals: "often confusing" and "often buggy" [checked, RPG Codex interview].
- Weapon Focus: "I do avoid taking Weapon Focus skills" since no group fits a character; wish to pick "3 weapons of your choice" (mainer) [checked, Steam]; "never made a martial character... that didn't have weapon focus" [lead]: the tax and the odd grouping at once.
- Soulbound: kill-count objectives make "the entire team stand around while the character with soulbound items... hits a creature repeatedly" [lead]; the all-unlocks achievement is rare (about 15%) [lead].

### Tabletop fit

Modals: good (a stance on the card: on or off, a cost and a gain, no math). Soulbound by kill counts: bookkeeping and grind; by story beats: excellent at a table (the GM hands out the unlock).

### For 2d12 [mine]

2d12's Mastery Techniques can carry the modal shape: **a weapon's signature stance with a cost** (Half-Sword: ignore a Resist, lose Guard; Overdraw: more damage, one fewer shot), which is horizontal and never a bare number. The modals players liked are those that **change the target** (lower a defense, knock Prone, engage another foe), not the ones that add Penetration. Soulbound: a named weapon whose Techniques unlock at **story beats the GM marks**, never kill counts. Avoid Weapon Focus (a flat number tax on odd groups).

## 7. Rangers, archers, and the talents players rate best and worst

### How it works

- **Ranger (Deadfire)** [read, Fextralife Ranger names; effects from snippets [lead]]: resource **Bond**; an animal companion; **Marked Prey** (you and the pet deal more to one mark, once per encounter), **Wounding Shot** (bleed), **Takedown** (pet knocks Prone), **Hobbling Shot**, **Concussive Shot** → upgrade **Concussive Tranquilizer** (interrupts even on a graze, and shortens the target's buffs), **Evasive Roll**, **Driving Flight** (shots continue to a second target), **Twinned Shot**, **Stunning Shots** (a passive: hits stun or interrupt while the pet is engaged with the target), **Binding Roots**, **Play Dead** (revives the pet), passives Marksman and Gunner. Subclasses [read, Shacknews]: **Sharpshooter** (more Hit-to-Crit beyond 4 m, more Penetration within 4 m; slower, lower Deflection), **Stalker** (both gain defenses within 4 m of each other; Bonded Grief beyond 7 m), **Ghost Heart** (spirit pet summoned for Bond; immune to Bonded Grief and to Engagement).
- **Bonded Grief** [lead]: when pet or ranger is knocked out, the other suffers heavy penalties (PoE1: −2 Might, −2 Resolve, −10 Accuracy; the pet dies if the ranger dies).
- **PoE1 talents** [read, GameBanshee; RPG Codex on Sawyer]: a flat list of passives (class talents like Intense Flames +50% Burn, Deep Faith, Shielding Touch; general Weapon Focus, Savage Attack, Gunner...). Sawyer: talents "can either extend or modify standard class abilities" or "grant access to outliers" (from the earlier research file). **Deadfire**: talents folded into the class ability tree; at each Power Level you pick actives or passives, and many actives have **forked upgrades** (Zealous Aura → three Exalted versions; Concussive Shot → Concussive Tranquilizer) [read, Fextralife].
- **Subclasses with a penalty** (every class but paladin and priest) [read, Shacknews]: Berserker (hits to crits, but Confused and can't see your Health), Devoted, Unbroken (+1 Engagement, lower Stride and Reflex), Assassin (stealth damage, but take more damage), Streetfighter, Skald, Troubadour.
- **Sawyer's rules**: "We wanted to have no bad builds"; no attribute requirement that "affect[s] viability"; no dump stats [checked, RPG Codex GDC 2016 report]; multiclassing kept so "the character should be viable" [read, update 40].

### What players love (rated well)

Rogue Sneak Attack/Deathblows on conditions; Paladin Flames of Devotion and the auras ("paladins only really need passive aura and a few key abilities to be useful", jasta85 [checked, Steam]); Driving Flight "can be reason enough to pick a Ranger" [lead]; Marked Prey "a nice +10 to accuracy, the Ranger's forte" [lead]; the summon invocations ("some of the summons are just too good", D'amarr [checked, Steam]); Stunning Shots with a pet holding the target [lead]. The subclass trade-offs give strong identity (Berserker, Helwalker) [mine, from their texts].

### What they dislike (traps, boring)

- **Bonded Grief and fragile pets**: "are ranger pets pretty useless", "how often does your pet die", "micro your pet" [lead, many thread titles]: a penalty that punishes the class for its own feature.
- **Kill-triggered talents** (Black Path, Strange Mercy, Inspiring Triumph): weak, since the killing blow is not yours to choose [lead; Ludo.guide [checked]].
- **Flat passives**: Deadfire's fighter tree "a barren field", "fighters are certainly a boring class" (contested as forum bias) [lead]; "beta 4: new passives are scams" (title) [lead]; Weapon Focus (above).
- Chanter's set-and-forget loop (section 2); PoE1 "Ancient Memory... too slow and too weak" [checked, Steam, reported second-hand].
- Sawyer on PoE1 levels: some players felt leveling lacked impact, a "perceived lack of value" [checked, GDC 2016 report].

### Tabletop fit

All of the above is card text. Bonded Grief and real-time companion micro are the parts that do not travel well (two bodies to run, a penalty when one drops).

### For 2d12 [mine]

For the Archer: the **Sharpshooter split** (a reward at long range, a different reward up close) gives one character two modes without a bigger number; **Driving Flight** (a hit continues to the next foe in line) is a clean Trick Shot with Line of Effect; **Stunning Shots' "while an ally holds the target"** rewards teamwork with Engaged. For Talents in general: **forked upgrades of a card you own** (choose one of two) are Pillars' best horizontal growth; **subclass trade-offs** (a gain and a cost on the same feature) avoid permission and tax; avoid kill triggers and flat passives.

## 8. Tabletop adaptations

### What exists

- **Obsidian's own pen-and-paper RPG** (Sawyer; announced 9 February 2017 as a Deadfire backer reward; a Starter Guide, then a Player's Guide alpha on 8 May 2019; a later rework teased by Sawyer on Bluesky) [lead, wiki and GamesRadar snippets]. It **does not use the CRPG's rules**: "will not be using the Pillars of Eternity computer game system or that of any other RPG" [lead]. What was seen: versus tests "collapsed into obstacle tests"; a re-roll of all dice in a check after seeing them; Endurance lost by "Soaking" and "Exert" (Exert pays for abilities like Charge); a "short rest" with "mechanical meaning but no mechanical definition" left to "Group Decision" icons [checked, RPG Codex thread page 4, Sawyer's answers quoted by a poster, Feb 2020]. RPG Codex reaction: mostly mockery ("Who is this game for, Josh? WHO?") [checked].
- Obsidian's statement about the CRPG: a "custom rule system heavily inspired by classic and contemporary tabletop" games, but "a virtual 100-sided die with formulas that would be hard to calculate at a physical table" [read, "What is Eternity?"].
- **Fan conversions**: a 5e Cipher on GM Binder: one **Focus die** added to a pool "after hitting with a weapon" (bonus action at 1st level, any hit at 2nd), dice d4 → d10 by level, spells cost 2 dice per spell level, an "Enduring Focus" floor the pool refills to after a rest, and the pool drops to that floor after ten minutes without a weapon attack; the author warns the rules "technically allow for exploits" (fighting things to build Focus) [read]. A 5e Cipher on EN World and a PF2 Cipher on Paizo (the poster notes PF2's own "focus points" clash in name) [lead]. An Obsidian-forum homebrew with weekly drafts (action points, exploration, unique weapons) [lead]. Nerdist made D&D sheets for Deadfire companions [lead].
- **Deadfire's turn-based mode** (patch 4.1) is the nearest thing to a table translation: Recovery became **Initiative**; one Standard or Cast action plus movement per turn; modal toggles and some abilities became free actions; Unbending lasts 2 rounds [lead / read for Unbending on Fextralife]. Reception: "more engaging... more fluid and less hacky" (TechRaptor) [lead]; complaints about disengagement on approach (section 4) and "the turn order is completely broken" (a thread title) [lead].

### What translated well

The class resources (pools, Focus on a hit), modal stances, the order/god rewrites, the affliction ladders and the band-based roll. What did not: percentages (conversions, damage multipliers), real-time ticking (phrases, linger, Wounds over time), per-type armor numbers on every creature, and the disposition meter.

### For 2d12 [mine]

2d12 is already where the turn-based mode had to go (AP turns, Tiers, Escalation). The fan cipher's **"one die added to a pool on a hit, spent on powers, pool falls back between fights"** is a ready pattern for a martial-caster hybrid.
