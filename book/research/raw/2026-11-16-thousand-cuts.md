# "Death by a thousand cuts": many small hits without many slow rolls (raw report, 2026-11-16)

**The question:** how published games deliver "a damage dealer that hits a lot of times, not one big hit" without many slow rolls and without one repeated button, and what players said. For the Archer (MASTER-PLAN item 5). Not repeated here: what `archer.md` and `raw/2026-11-16-archer.md` already have (Twin Strike and the flurry ranger as one-button play, the shots that move, the price of range, reload, Lancer's Gunslinger die).

**Tags.**
- [read] = rules text I read in full myself. Where the text came from a copy that is not the publisher's (a fan wiki, a community rules site, a fan's summary), the tag says so.
- [checked] = an opinion, or a rule as a poster quotes or describes it, read on its own page.
- [lead] = seen only in a search summary; the page was not opened or refused. Never to be used as a quote.
- Reddit, Giant in the Playground, RPG.net: refuse readers, left alone. The Modiphius forum asks for an account (so everything about Conan's Volley is [lead]). Two Pelgrane pages and one Tapatalk page returned 403.

One pass, by one agent, not re-checked. Poster names on EN World were taken by a script from the post header; a name could be off by one post.

## 1. One roll, several hits

### 1.1 Degrees of success = number of hits

**Warhammer 40k RPGs (Dark Heresy and its family).** The book was not read. [checked] The rule as a poster explains it, FFG forum archive, "Auto-fire / Enemy balance", Charmander2, Mar 3, 2011: "each hit from autofire counts as a separate hit- toughness and armor are subtracted from each roll separately. You're only getting bonus hits for each full degree of success. So if you have a modified BS 60 on full auto and you roll a 41-50 that's one additional hit. 31-40 is two additional hits. For semi-auto it's for every two full degrees of success." Full auto also gave +20 to hit and semi-auto +10 (several posters, same thread). https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/43263-auto-fire-enemy-balance
- Rolls at the table: 1 attack roll (d100), then damage **for each hit**, each reduced by armor and toughness separately.
- Reception [checked], same thread: Charmander2: "As for autofire in general, it is a massive game changer in all the systems. It acts like a damage multiplier." The GM who opened it (SynnerG): "anything coming at them was shredded [...] The group as a whole did sit down after the credits rolled and the players agreed it seemd a bit OP". jareddm: "One of the simplest and most common fixes I've seen in terms of auto-fire is to simply switch the rules for semi-auto and full-auto. That way you're getting a +20 on semi and +10 on full." Also "Full Auto vs. Semi Auto" (Massaker, Jun 17, 2009): "the RAW renders weapons capable of only semi-auto fire nearly useless". https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/12996-full-auto-vs-semi-auto
- What it shows: nobody in these threads calls it slow. The complaints are all balance: one roll that both hits more easily **and** multiplies the hits.

**GURPS 4e rapid fire.** The Basic Set (p. B373) was not read. [checked] The rule as a blogger explains it, RogerBW's Blog, "Rapid Fire and Shotguns in GURPS 4th edition", 15 January 2014: "You make a single attack roll just as you normally would. Add the rapid fire bonus, if any. If you miss, you've missed with all your shots. If you hit, you've hit with at least one shot; work out the margin of success. Each full multiple of Rcl that you succeed by gives you an extra hit." To spray several targets you split the shots and "allocate one wasted shot for each empty yard you traverse". https://blog.firedrake.org/archive/2014/01/Rapid_Fire_and_Shotguns_in_GURPS_4th_edition.html
- Rolls: 1 attack roll; the weapon's Rcl number is the price of each extra hit, so a weapon can be "many shots, few hits" or "few shots, all hit". Damage per hit. No reception read.

**Genesys / Star Wars (FFG), Auto-fire.** The book was not read. [checked] The text as a poster quotes it from the Genesys core book, FFG forum archive, "How is Autofire?", KelYco, Aug 24, 2018: "If the attack hits, the attacker can trigger Auto-fire by spending 2adv. Auto-fire can be triggered multiple times. Each time the attacker triggers Auto-fire, it deals an additional hit to the target. Each of these counts as an additional hit from that weapon, and each hit deals base damage plus the number of Success on the check." c__beck, Dec 1, 2017: "+1 purple die to the attack and can spend 2 advantages to hit again. Can spread additional hits against other targets in range." https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/264317-how-is-autofire
- Rolls: 1 pool. Damage is fixed (base + successes), so **no second roll at all**: every extra hit is the same number again, each reduced by soak separately (c__beck: "It's not one hit of 14 damage, it's two hits of 7 damage. Note that soak reduces damage from each hit.").
- Reception [checked], same thread: Glororhan, Jan 26, 2018: "Even requiring two advantages, auto-fire is pretty bananas in terms of raw damage output vs a single target. I think a simple fix is to only allow it to be used to hit multiple targets, so no stacking multiple hits on one target." FinarinPanjoro, Oct 17, 2018, on a house rule that raised the cost against tough enemies: "This prevented a lot of one round wipe outs of both nemesis and PCs by high-level characters using autofire." Daeglan, Jan 26, 2018: the cost should rise with each extra hit. Richardbuxton defends it as genre: "Every good Western or Civil War Movie has a scene with a Gatling gun slaughtering an entire town, that's Autofire."
- The Linked quality: not read.

**Cyberpunk RED autofire** [lead]: on a hit, 2d6 multiplied by the amount the roll beat the DV, up to the weapon's cap (3 or 4). One roll, one damage roll, a multiplier in place of counted hits. A search summary reports players calling the 2d6 "very swingy". Not opened (the fan page returned 403; the discussion is on Reddit).

**Shadowrun, Warhammer Fantasy:** not searched.

### 1.2 Several dice in one throw, one per shot

**Savage Worlds (Adventure Edition).** [read, on a fan wiki's copy; one sentence is cut off on the page] "Rate of Fire is how many shots (Shooting dice) a ranged weapon can fire in one action. [...] a machine gun with a Rate of Fire of 3 can fire three shots per action. When you use a weapon with a Rate of Fire higher than 1, declare how many of the shots are aimed at each target, [...] The Wild Die can only replace one of these rolls. Firing more than one shot in one action from a weapon subtracts 2 from the attacker's Shooting rolls due to Recoil." https://tabletoprpg.quest/doku.php/savage/combat
- Rolls: one throw of N skill dice plus one Wild Die, read die by die; then damage for each hit. Frenzy (the melee version) was not on the page. No reception read.

**Gloomhaven.** [read, a hosted copy of the rulebook] "Any time an Attack ability is performed, a separate attack modifier card is drawn for each individual target of the attack." And for areas: "each target constitutes a separate attack (drawing its own attack modifier card), but all attacks together make up a single attack action." https://mahmati.kapsi.fi/bg/gloomhaven/gloomhaven_rulebook.html
- So a multi-target card is one decision and one card flip per target; the attack's value is printed (no damage roll). Reception of multi-target or of stacking Poison and Wound: not found ([lead] only: Poison adds +1 to each attack against the target, which is a per-hit trigger).

**Strike!** [read, a community rules summary, not the book] One d6 on a fixed table: "6 Critical Hit: Effect and 2 times [damage] | 4-5 Solid Hit: [damage] and Effect | 3 Glancing Hit: [damage] or Effect (player's choice) | 2 Miss Gain a Miss Token | 1 Miss Gain a Miss Token and a Strike". Damage is the number printed on the power. An area power "makes a separate attack roll against each target and is considered a Miss only if every roll is a Miss." Some powers have no roll: "the power just always does whatever it says it does." https://lyssa-rpg-docs.neocities.org/strike/rules-summary
- Rolls: 1d6 for each target, no damage roll, no modifiers. Reception: not found beyond one itch.io comment [lead] calling the combat "fun, and easy to learn".

### 1.3 One roll applied to every target

**Draw Steel.** [read] The general rule: "When an ability has multiple targets (whether a strike with more than one target or an area affect), you make one power roll and apply the total to all targets. If you have edges or banes [...] against some but not all of your targets, you might apply a different tier outcome to individual targets."
- *Two Shot* (Rapid-Fire kit's signature): "*When you fire two arrows back-to-back, both hit their mark.* Ranged 12, Two creatures or objects. Power Roll + Might or Agility: ≤11: 4 damage / 12-16: 6 damage / 17+: 8 damage". The kit's fantasy line: "for archers who want to deal maximum damage by shooting as many arrows as possible into nearby enemies".
- *Double Strike* (Dual Wielder): the same numbers in melee, with: "If you use this ability on your turn, you can use it against one target, then use your maneuver and your move action for that turn before using the ability against a second target. You still use the same power roll for both targets."
- The Fury: "Then make one power roll that targets each enemy you move adjacent to during this shift."
- Rolls: 1 (2d10), no damage roll, never a miss. Note what it is: **"two arrows" means two targets**; against one enemy the Rapid-Fire archer has no "many hits" at all, only +2 damage on other abilities.
- Reception: none found for Two Shot or the kit. For the roll itself [checked], haihala's blog, "Screw tabletop simulators", Dec 7, 2025: "The way damage is dealt is genuinely one of the best design decisions to come out of the game. It fixes so many problems and speeds up the game tremendously." And: "Reducing the amount of die rolls and back and forth speeds up everything." https://haihala.github.io/blog/draw-steel-impressions

**D&D 4e.** [read]
- *Split the Tree* (ranger daily 1): "Targets: Two creatures within 3 squares of each other. Attack: Dexterity vs. AC. Make two attack rolls, take the higher result, and apply it to both targets. Hit: 2[W] + Dexterity modifier damage." Two dice, **one result for both targets**.
- *Spray of Arrows* (daily 9): "Close blast 3 [...] Target: Each enemy in the blast you can see. Attack: Dexterity vs. AC. Hit: 2[W] + Dexterity modifier damage. Miss: Half damage." *Hail of Arrows* (encounter 27): "Target: Each enemy in range". One attack roll for each target.
- Against them, the two-roll powers: *Twin Strike* ("One or two creatures [...] two attacks. Hit: 1[W] damage per attack"), *Two-Fanged Strike* ("If both attacks hit, you deal extra damage equal to your Wisdom modifier"), *Thundertusk Boar Strike* ("With each hit, you push the target 1 square. If both attacks hit the same target, you push the target a number of squares equal to 1 + your Wisdom modifier"). The last two are the "both hit" rider: the second roll changes the effect, not only the total.
- Reception of Twin Strike: `archer.md`. None read for the others.

**Pathfinder 2e.** [read]
- *Penetrating Shot* (ranger 10, two actions): "Make a single ranged Strike with the required weapon against the chosen target and your hunted prey. [...] Roll damage only once, and apply it to each creature you hit. A Penetrating Shot counts as two attacks for your multiple attack penalty."
- *Impossible Volley* (in the earlier report): "Roll the damage only once for all targets."
- *Double Shot* (fighter 4, flourish, two actions): "Make two Strikes, each against a separate target and with a –2 penalty." *Triple Shot*: the same target allowed; a third action for "three ranged Strikes instead of two. If you do, the penalty is –4." *Hunted Shot* (ranger 1, flourish, one action): "Make two Strikes against your hunted prey [...] If both hit the same creature, combine their damage for the purpose of resistances and weaknesses." *Flurry of Blows*: the same sentence for two unarmed Strikes.
- Rolls: PF2e's multi-shots are two or three full attack rolls and two or three damage rolls; the one-roll forms exist only for lines and bursts.

**ICON 1.5.** [read] An area attack rolls once against the main target and the rest take the flat number: "Attack: On hit: 2[D] + fray. On miss: fray. Area effect: fray". And small repeated instances written as text, no roll: "Exceed: Deal 1 damage again to your target. At round 4 or later, increase this damage to 6"; "they take 1 divine damage three times at the end of that turn"; "You can sacrifice 2 after this ability resolves to deal 2 damage again to all adjacent foes."
- Rolls: 1. **"Deal X damage again" is ICON's way of writing a second hit**: the same instance repeated, so every per-instance effect (its Counter: "deal 2 damage back, each time damage is applied"; armor) applies again. No reception found.

### 1.4 The second hit decided by the first die

**13th Age (1e).** [read] *Double Ranged Attack*: "When you attack with a ranged weapon that does not need to be reloaded, your default option is to make a double ranged attack. Your weapon damage die drops one notch, usually from d8s to d6s. If your first attack is a natural even roll (hit or miss), you can make a second attack as a free action. [...] declare it before rolling your attack; the single attack will deal the normal damage dice". Feats: "Your second attack gains a +2 attack bonus if it is against a different target"; "Once per battle, you can use Double Ranged Attack after an odd attack roll"; epic: "You can also use your move action in between your two attacks".
- *Flexible attacks*: "allow you choose your target first, make your attack roll, and then use the natural unmodified die result to determine which of your eligible flexible attacks to use."
- Rolls: 1, and 2 half the time. The price of the chance is paid up front (a smaller die).
- 2e [checked, a build guide on Pelgrane's site, Jay Godden, July 31, 2026]: the ranger's default is now the *Twin Arrows* talent: "You attack twice, though your damage die drops to a d6 and you have a chance to deal only half damage. This still deals much more expected damage than just shooting once, and you can split the two arrows to different targets across the map". And the single big shot is kept as the alternative: "Lethal Hunter basic attacks do have a higher expected damage than an unaugmented double ranged attack and so are worth using on dangerous single enemies too." So 2e seems to have dropped the even-roll gate for two real attacks; the talent's own text was not read. https://pelgranepress.com/2026/07/31/13th-age-2e-pregenerated-character-builds-endless-volley-ranger/
- Reception of the even-roll gate: not found. [lead] 2e took flexible attacks off the base fighter.

**Nimble.** [read, on the Nimblenomicon, a community rules site] There is no attack roll: "Roll the die listed on the spell, weapon, or ability, and deal that much damage. However, if you roll a 1, you miss, and the attack has no effect. For attacks with multiple dice, the leftmost die is called the Primary Die. It determines whether the attack is a hit or a miss." "Rolling the max on a Primary Die is a critical hit (crit). When you crit, roll the Primary Die again, and add the result to the total. Repeat this each time you roll the maximum". Three actions a turn, and: "A hero may attack more than once on their turn, but these additional attacks are rushed, imposing cumulative disadvantage for each additional attack after the first—increasing the chance to miss and lowering the average damage for subsequent attacks." https://nimblenomicon.pages.dev/core-rules
- Rolls: 1 throw for each attack, and nothing else. Three attacks are three throws. The repeat penalty is on the same die that is the damage, so a rushed attack is both likelier to miss and smaller. A small die misses more often (a d4 misses 1 in 4, a d12 1 in 12): many small weapons whiff more by construction.
- The designer on why [checked], Evan (Nimble Co.), July 1, 2024: "Extra Attack is a bad band-aid fix to a core problem in 5e: damage scaling and frequently missing attacks. [...] it [...] compounds a worse problem D&D 5e has: Slog. Extra Attack makes turns take longer and longer as you level up and that means more time spent waiting, and less time playing. MORE vanilla attacks doesn't make the game more fun or more interesting". His replacements scale one attack instead: a Fury Die "added to every attack you make"; the monk's "Add bludgeoning damage equal to your Swift level to all of your melee attacks" with "Your unarmed strikes are not subject to disadvantage imposed by Rushed Attacks." https://nimblerpg.com/blogs/news/the-core-problem-of-d-d-martial-classes-and-how-nimble-fixes-it
- Players' reception: not found (only the publisher's own copy: "No more waiting 15 minutes for your turn, just to miss your attack").

## 2. A pool or track that builds with each small hit

**Exalted 3e: withering builds, decisive cashes out.** [read, a fan's step-by-step summary of attack resolution, not the book] "*withering:* The target loses Xi, where X is the successes from the damage roll. You gain (X + 1)i." "*decisive:* The damage roll is (your current Initiative). [...] The target takes X health levels". "If this is a decisive attack, your Initiative resets to 3." A failed decisive: "you lose 2i, or 3i if your Initiative is 11+." https://gist.github.com/drumanagh/01ebace965e8b66066d358874a8b3042
- Rolls: each withering attack is an attack roll and a damage roll, and it does **no harm**: it only moves Initiative. Only the decisive hit wounds.
- Reception [checked]: Cross Planes, review, May 9, 2016: "Withering attacks are the strike/parry/thrust/parry/riposte that we see so often in melee battles on film. They are the ebb and flow of the fight [...] Decisive attacks are the blows that maim or kill your opponent, the death strike." https://www.crossplanes.com/2016/05/review-exalted-3rd-edition-from-onyx.html . Gamersplane, MoMo, Oct 6, 2016: "this tug-o-war of Initiative, and then the fight comes to this great, climactic moment as someone with enough built up Initiative just rolls a pile of 20+ dice, killing their opponent instantly, often enough." And its flaw: "there isn't much keeping your enemies from building up Initiative on your squishier allies and then slamming you with a big Decisive attack." https://gamersplane.com/forums/thread/2655 . EN World, Staffan, Mar 18, 2026: "Withering attacks create some form of cumulative positional advantage, which you then spend when making a decisive attack in the hopes of actually hurting your opponent. That's just a Doylist separation however – in the fiction, combatants are generally trying to kill one another". https://www.enworld.org/posts/9877862
- **For "not one big hit": this is the opposite fantasy.** The small hits are setup and the payoff is the one big hit; what players praise is exactly the climax. Whether the cash-out can be avoided: see the held meters below.

**Pathfinder 2e swashbuckler: panache, then a finisher.** [read] "You gain panache by performing actions that have the bravado trait." "Finishers are spectacular finishing moves. You can use a finisher only if you have panache, and you lose your panache immediately after performing a finisher. Once you use a finisher, you can't use actions that have the attack trait for the rest of your turn." *Precise Strike*: "you deal 2 additional precision damage. If the Strike is part of a finisher, the additional damage is 2d6 precision damage instead."
- So the same class has both modes: a small flat rider on every hit while the state is **held**, or a big die for **spending** it.
- Reception [checked], Paizo forum, "What do you think of the Remastered Swashbuckler?", Deriven Firelion, Apr 6-7, 2025: "they are still a big hit class like the magus"; "Their damage is tied to a single finisher strike that if it misses, their damage is terrible and they cannot attack again hoping for a better roll"; "If you have a under 40 percent chance to hit on your first attack, then a single big hit class would hit every other round maybe." Ascalaphus, Apr 7, 2025: "sometimes the only real way to deal with high AC enemies is just to keep trying to hit them". https://paizo.com/threads/rzs66gpg . [lead] other posters in the same family of threads say playing without spamming finishers "feels more swashbuckler-y".
- What it shows: the complaint about the cash-out is the complaint about one big hit: all the eggs in one roll.

**13th Age rogue: a meter with two values, kept by default.** [read] "You gain momentum by hitting an enemy with an attack. You lose momentum when you are hit by an attack. The default is that you can use momentum powers without losing momentum, but a few powers specify that you must spend your momentum to use them." Nothing to count: you have it or you don't. No reception read.

**Draw Steel: per-round income and surges.** [read] The Shadow: "At the start of each of your turns during combat, you gain 1d3 insight. Additionally, the first time each combat round that you deal damage incorporating 1 or more surges, you gain 1 insight." The Tactician: "the first time each combat round that you or any ally damages a creature marked by you [...] you gain 1 focus." Surges: "Whenever you deal rolled damage, you can spend up to 3 surges to deal extra damage to one creature or object targeted by the ability. Each surge you spend deals extra damage equal to your highest characteristic score."
- Two things to note: the triggers are **"the first time each round"**, never "each hit" (so the income cannot be farmed with many small hits and is counted once a round); and surges are a small pool spent a few at a time on any damage, not one cash-out.
- Reception [checked], EN World, Tigris, Mar 18, 2026: "ressources which build up in an encounter like in Draw Steel are just per encounter ressources. Its a more complicated (and more prone to repetition) way of doing encounter powers. Its just inverse power points." Staffan, same day, on the alternative: "One advantage of cooldowns is that they enforce some variety in ability use. With a fungible resource (e.g. 'mana'), it can often be easy to find out what ability gives you the most bang for the buck and use that all the time". The designer's side is in `nova-burst.md`.

**ICON: a token that flips the next use.** [read] "Combo - Actions with Combo have two versions, a base version and combo version. When you use the base ability, gain a combo token. Any time you use a combo ability and have a token, you use the combo version instead, discarding the token. You can only have one combo token at once, and discard all tokens at the end of combat." Example: *Low Blow* ("Foe is slashed. If they are already slashed, they gain hatred of you") then its combo *The Hook* ("Gains range 2 and effect: Shove character 1 towards you"). No reception found.

**Tokens earned and spent per hit: the old verdict.** [checked] EN World, "Would this be too much bookkeeping?", Apr 6, 2010. Sammael: "this sounds similar to the Iron Heroes token system, which I found too fiddly." Stormonu: "I would have used the Iron Hero system, but the tracking tokens turned me off. [...] wizards and psions [...] their points/resources are set before combat begins and it isn't added to; you simply count down your resource as it is used, which generally makes it easier to follow/track." Dausuul: "quite workable as a resource management tool, but it should be the only form of resource management used in combat, aside from the all-important action economy. And there should be strong incentives to not build up more than a few tokens in the pool, no more than 1-3 most of the time." ExploderWizard: "constant recharges and expenditures on a per hit basis is a bit much." https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=5144647
- One thread, five posters, 2010; the three rules of thumb in it (count down not up; one pool only; 1 to 3 tokens) are the only explicit tracking-cost advice found.

**Slay the Spire's Shivs** (the video-game case). [checked, a guide on Untapped.gg; no author or date in my extract] "Shivs are 0-Cost colorless Attack cards that deal 4 damage and Exhaust when played. They are generated by other cards in your deck rather than drafted directly [...] other cards like Accuracy also amplify their damage. So the name of the game is to get more and more Shivs with cards like Infinite Blades while buffing their damage output." https://sts2.untapped.gg/guides/silents-shiv-mechanic
- The structure: the small hit is worth little alone, and the build is about cards that pay **for each one played**. A designer's statement on it was searched for and not found. The computer counts; a table would not.

**Not found:** WoW combo points in tabletop form, Fabula Ultima, Path of Exile / Diablo on-hit design, Monster Hunter's bow and dual blades, Gloomhaven Wound and Poison reception. (Lancer's Gunslinger die, a d6 counted down on each hit: in the earlier report.)

## 3. Ammunition or "arrows" as a die or a pool

**The Black Hack 2e: the usage die, and its exception for ammunition.** [checked: the text as a poster quotes it] EN World, "Do you track ammunition?", Voadam, Mar 18, 2025: "When that item is used its Usage Die should be rolled. If the result is 1-2 then the Usage Die is downgraded to the next smallest die in the following chain: Ud20 > Ud12 > Ud10 > Ud8 > Ud6 > Ud4 When you roll a 1-2 on a d4 [...] the item is expended". And: "USAGE DIE & AMMUNITION When tracking the Usage Die for ammunition such as arrows and bullets, roll the Ud after the combat is resolved." His comment: "A base d8 usage die set of arrows will last a minimum of 3 full encounters [...] And no chance of running out in the middle of a fight." https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=9615060
- The same poster, before he found the exception: "I find that usage die mechanics would often be more cumbersome than actually tracking individual arrows if you are adding a roll after each bow shot and then possibly needing to adjust your supply rating. Again with my high level fighter shooting five arrows a round five extra die rolls and possible supply adjustments would be more of a speed bump than knock off five arrows at the end of your attack."
- Same thread: James Gasik: "I'm reminded of all the times in World of Warcraft where raids stalled out because some idiot Hunter forgot to stock up on ammo. [...] it was eventually changed that you no longer needed to buy arrows, likely to avoid this very issue." Crimson Longinus asks how often tracked ammunition ever runs out; Hussar: "if you have extended adventures with no 'town' in the middle, it's surprising how fast archer characters run out of arrows."

**Black Sword Hack refuses the usage die for arrows.** [read, the game's own site] "The Usage die should be seen as a 'push your luck' mechanic. It can grant you some advantages, but it can also blow up in your face at the worst possible moment. Some games use this mechanic to manage concrete resources like rations, or arrows. Not so in Black Sword Hack. Counting 20 arrows is not rocket science". And the averages it gives: "Ud20: 30 - Ud12: 20 - Ud10: 14 - Ud8: 9 Ud6: 5 - Ud4: 2." https://blackswordhack.github.io/1rules.html

**Giffyglyph's Darker Dungeons (5e supplement): a roll on every shot.** [read] "Instead of tracking each individual shot, take a d12—this is your *Ammunition die*. Roll it whenever you take a shot: if you roll a 1 or 2, the die gets one size smaller." https://giffyglyph.com/darkerdungeons/grimoire/4.0.0/en/ammunition_dice.html (This is the form Voadam calls a speed bump for anyone who shoots several times a round.)

**Conan 2d20: Loads spent for a bigger shot.** Everything here is [lead]: the Modiphius forum needs an account and the book was not found. Search summaries of the forum say: a weapon with the Volley quality doesn't count shots; the archer carries "Loads" (reloads), and spending one Load on an attack adds a bonus d20 to the test and one damage die on a hit. A thread is titled "Volley Quality, is it me or is it lame?" (the complaint, as summarized: a whole quiver for a small bonus); others debate whether the Load's benefit lasts the scene. https://forums.modiphius.com/t/volley-quality-is-it-me-or-is-it-lame/7052 (unread)
- This is the closest published thing found to "a pool of dice that stands for many shots": ammunition as a few abstract units, each spent to add dice to one roll. Its reception could not be read.

**Not read:** Forbidden Lands' resource die for arrows (a search summary says the die shrinks on a 1 or 2 and runs d6 to d12; not verified), Blades in the Dark's load, Mothership, Ironsworn's supply, the 2d20 SRD's Salvo, Achtung! Cthulhu, Star Trek Adventures.

## 4. Small hits that matter for what each one does

**The enemy of many small hits is anything subtracted from each hit.** [checked]
- Pathfinder 1e, Paizo forum, "Does Damage from Multiple Attacks Stack for Purposes of Overcoming DR?", Orthos, Jun 8, 2012: "DR pretty much ruins any attempt at 'death of a thousand cuts' style characters, sadly." https://paizo.com/threads/rzs2o8y9
- Pathfinder 2e's answer is the sentence in Hunted Shot, Twin Takedown, Flurry of Blows and Double Slice ("combine their damage for the purpose of resistances and weaknesses"). Paizo forum, "Design wise, why do Double Slice and Flurry of Blows have combined damage?", WatersLethe, Jan 5, 2026 (5 favorites): "The reason they're combined is so that they're not utterly boned by resistances. You're already not guaranteed to hit both attacks, so trying to overcome resistance is already punishing as heck. Finally landing both strikes only for the full damage to be eaten to no effect would have people flipping the table." Teridax, Jan 6, 2026: it "also limits burst damage by preventing double-tapping resistances or double-dipping into sneak attack damage or the like, so it makes those actions more consistent both ways." Deriven Firelion, Jan 6, 2026: "Dual Wield looks cool in the mind's eye. That's the main reason to take it." https://paizo.com/threads/rzs7jkg5
- The mirror: every flat **bonus** added to each hit favors the multi-hitter (4e's Twin Strike; in this thread, Titanium Dragon on precision damage: if Double Slice applied it twice it "would be so good literally every rogue would be stupid not to take it"). The same games that protect multi-hits from resistance also stop them from doubling the big rider ("You add any precision damage only once").
- In the 40k and Genesys threads above, the same fact is the balance lever: "soak reduces damage from each hit".

**Consistency: the many-hits fantasy as never whiffing.**
- [checked] Jonathan Tweet (D&D 3e lead designer, 13th Age co-designer), EN World, "The Overkill Damage Fallacy", Jun 11, 2019: "reliability and consistency matter a lot to whichever side has the advantage. The underdog in a fight benefits from increased randomness, while the favored group benefits from a lack of surprises. In RPGs, the PCs are almost always the favored side. A disadvantage of a single large attack compared to two small attacks is that the single attacks are more likely to produce an unlikely result. When balancing 3E, we tried to take this factor into account, but it's a slippery one." https://www.enworld.org/posts/7619028/ (Ovinomancer, same page: a "single big hit is swingier (ie flatter) than 2 little hits".)
- [read] Damage on a miss. 13th Age basic attacks: "Miss: Damage equal to your level"; Two Weapon Mastery's feat: "increase the damage you deal with missed attacks by adding your level to it"; and the floor kept on purpose: "Rolling a natural 1 has no effect on the target, not even miss damage." ICON: "Light attack (1 action): Make an attack roll. On hit: [D] + fray. Miss: fray"; "Fray damage is a fixed value based on job. [...] (you don't need to roll anything!)"; "Auto-hit - This attack doesn't make an attack roll but always scores a hit". Lancer, *Reliable*: "It always does {VAL} damage, even if it misses its target or rolls less damage. Reliable damage inherits other tags (such as AP) and base damage type but not tags that require a hit, such as Knockback." PF2e *Certain Strike* (fighter 10, press): "Failure: Your attack deals any damage it would have dealt on a hit, excluding all damage dice." *Exacting Strike* (press): "Failure: This attack doesn't count toward your multiple attack penalty."
- Lancer's sentence is a useful rule of thumb: the floor carries the damage, **not** the riders that need a hit.
- Reception of Reliable, fray and miss damage as such: not found (the 13th Age and 4e reception of "half on a miss" is in `nova-burst.md`).

**Hit counters and riders that escalate within a turn.** [read] 4e's "both hit" riders (Two-Fanged Strike, Thundertusk Boar Strike, above). ICON's "If they are already slashed" (the second application does something else) and "gain +1 vigilance for every status your foe is suffering from, to a maximum of three". Lancer's *Burn*: "They immediately take that much burn damage, ignoring Armor, then mark {VAL} Burn on their sheet, adding it to any existing Burn [...] on a failure, they take damage equal to their total marked burn": a stack that every small hit adds to and that ignores armor. No published "third hit on the same target this turn staggers it" was found in the games read.

**Hits as presentation.** [checked] Fantasy Strike (a fighting game) forum, "Nothing but the hits!", David Sirlin (the game's designer), July 16, 2018, answering why a move that hits four times deals the same 1 damage: "Moves only have 1 hit unless there is a reason for them to have more. The two reasons for them to have more are either to make the look/feel cooler, or to affect the hitstun/blockstun of the move. [...] Valerie's ground super. If it was juts one hit that would feel pretty flat. Grave's ground super. The 3-hit animation is just more satisfying than a 2-hit one would be." mysticjuicer, same day: "also so that different moves interact with armor in different ways." https://forums.fantasystrike.com/t/nothing-but-the-hits/959
- In tabletop terms this is Draw Steel's Two Shot flavor line and Conan's Loads: one number, described as several hits. Sirlin's two reasons are feel, and a second property that the hit count changes (there: how armor and stun react).

**Small fixed numbers.** Strike! (section 1.2) and ICON's fray are the two games read where a hit is a small printed number. **Not found:** Panic at the Dojo, Fight!, Fabula Ultima.

## 5. Speed at the table

**Why Draw Steel has no miss.** [checked] James Introcaso (lead designer), interviewed by Jon de Nor, Goblin Points episode 2025.16, published 20 August 2025: "not missing, right, which is core to Draw Steel definitely, and core to the experience and an important part of the experience, is not unique to Draw Steel, right? There's a lot of games that that work that way. People will bring up Into the Odd [...] That's a game where you don't miss, right? And you have HP and it stands for hit protection [...] there's plenty of video games that work that way too, where missing rarely happens or never happens." He ranks it below the resource loop: "that gameplay loop is more important than your core mechanic". https://goblin-points.pages.dev/2516
- A sentence by Matt Colville on why was searched for twice and not found in text (it is said in videos). The reviewers' reading ("missing as a player in D&D feels bad") is in `raw/2026-11-14-DF-verified.md`, 1.12.
- [checked] haihala, Dec 7, 2025, on what the single roll removes: "Instead of: Player: I would like to hit the monster / DM: Aight, roll to hit / Player: 18 to hit / DM: 18 hits, roll damage / Player rolls like three dice and takes 10s to do math / Player: 15 points of thronging damage. You get: Player rolls 2d10, adds one number, quickly picks the bucket based on result / Player: I hit the monster for 15 thronging damage". "Being fair, you can optimize the first one kind of by rolling damage while rolling to hit [...] but you still get the math break and more communication overhead."
- [checked] DM David, "Checks Versus Attack Rolls: Every Aspiring RPG Designer (and Some Veterans) Should Read This" (date not captured): "D&D swingy d20 attack rolls play well because players make far more attack rolls than other tests. The high number of attack rolls tends to even out the random swings of each roll." And: "Repeating the same attack can be fun. Even a missed attack only brings momentary disappointment; the game speeds on to another turn." https://dmdavid.com/tag/checks-versus-attack-rolls-every-aspiring-rpg-designer-and-some-veterens-should-read-this/

**Extra attacks as slog.** Evan (Nimble), section 1.4. [checked] EN World, "Attacks per round.", GX.Sigma, Nov 10, 2012 (the 5e playtest): "In a theoretical sense: Yes! Everyone should have tons of attacks at higher level. In an actual gameplay sense: F*** no. Do you know how many die rolls that would be per round?" VinylTap, same thread: "more attacks helps balance out the swinging feeling of a d20." https://www.enworld.org/threads/attacks-per-round.331546/

**Making several rolls fast.**
- [checked] Justin Alexander, The Alexandrian, "Random GM Tip – Fistfuls of Dice", January 18, 2021: "Start by rolling your attack die and your damage die at the same time. [...] In a typical combat with fifteen combatants, your group will be making ninety attack rolls (or more!). If you're saving just four seconds per roll, that adds up to 5 minutes per combat." "These mechanics rarely have a decision point between the two rolls, so there's no reason not to make both rolls at the same time." On colors: "when I make iterative attacks in 3rd Edition I use red dice for the first attack, black dice for the second attack, and blue for the third", and the limit: "trying to remember that the ogre was blue, the goblin was red [...] it tends to bog down." Or "Read left to right". https://thealexandrian.net/?p=45834
- [checked] EN World, "Surprisingly Lethal Side Effect of Multiattack", Oofta, Jan 24, 2017: "I do frequently roll all my attacks at once to save time. I have a system so that I know which attack hits first: I always use them in the same order. So my blue die are first, my green die second and so on." https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=7006179 . [lead] the objections found in summaries: rolling together breaks when an early hit changes a later one (the target drops, a rider is chosen after seeing the roll).
- [checked] Paizo forum, "Speeding Up Combat" (Pathfinder 1e), Franz Lunzer, Aug 9, 2009: "Better use color-coded dice. [...] Best use sets of color-coded dice, to roll attack and damage at once." Quandary proposes "don't roll damage dice, just use the average"; Misery, Aug 10, 2009: "We've done this and it's actually not a bad system to use. [...] past level 11 or 12 we've tended to move towards this and it can help a lot by having a sheet listing what the average damage for what attack/spell is." https://paizo.com/threads/rzs2jtnd
- In the rules read, the same tricks built in: fixed damage (Genesys, Strike!, ICON's fray, Gloomhaven), the damage die as the attack roll (Nimble), all the shots' dice in one throw (Savage Worlds), one roll for all targets (Draw Steel).

**The falling value of the next attack.** [read] PF2e: -5 then -10 (agile -4 / -8; the flurry ranger -3 / -6, or -2 / -4 agile). [checked] EN World, The Crimson Binome, Mar 9, 2018, on the design: "By applying a -10 penalty to that third attack, and having that be the one you give up when you need to move, it means you still get to keep most of your total effectiveness in a round where you have to move." https://www.enworld.org/threads/from-7-action-types-to-pathfinder-2s-new-3-action-economy.665200/page-8 . Paizo forum, Ludovicus, Nov 12, 2018 (the playtest): "PF2's much-maligned low success rate for level-appropriate challenges is a direct consequence of the action economy as it applies to multiple attacks." https://paizo.com/threads/rzs42d3h . The exact phrase "the third attack is a trap" was not found on an opened page. [lead] an EN World poster: the characters facing "a hopeless -10" are those with better things to do with the action.
- Read with `archer.md`: the penalty makes the third attack the thing you trade away; the flurry ranger, whose penalty is cut, is the one players call monotonous.

## 6. Variety for a multi-hitter

**Pathfinder 2e's traits.** [read] *Flourish*: "You can use only one action with the flourish trait per round." (Hunted Shot, Twin Takedown, Double Shot, Flurry of Blows are all flourish: one multi-attack button a round, and they compete with each other.) *Press*: "An action with the press trait can be used only if you are currently affected by a multiple attack penalty. [...] Some actions with the press trait also grant an effect on a failure." *Finisher*: no more attack actions that turn. *Stance*: one a round.
- Press is the "different second attack" device: the follow-up is a different card with its own consolation. [checked] Paizo forum, "Fighter Feat: Exacting Strike", breithauptclan, Aug 3, 2019: "Its purpose it to give you better odds of hitting twice in the round. Swing once with full attack bonus. Swing again with the -5 penalty using Exacting Strike. - If you miss, swing again with a -5 penalty [...] - If you hit, you have done the two hits that you wanted. You still have one action to spend as you like." https://paizo.com/threads/rzs42o8v
- The limit of the method is in `archer.md`: with flourish and press in place, the ranger was still "Gravity Weapon > Hunt Prey > Hunted Shot [...] Repeat".

**13th Age monk: a sequence in the rules.** [read] "When you learn a monk form, you learn all three elements of that form: an **opening attack**, **flow attack**, and **finishing attack**." "After you use a flow attack, your next monk attack must be a finishing attack from any form you know, or it can be another opening. (You can't use flow attack twice in a row.) After a finishing attack you must start over with an opening". "you can use an opening, flow, or finishing attack from ANY of the forms you know." "After using an opening attack you gain a +1 bonus to AC. After using a flow attack, you gain a +2 bonus to AC. After using a finishing attack, you gain a +3 bonus". The sequence runs **across rounds** (one attack a turn), so the variety is from turn to turn. Reception: not found.

**ICON's Combo** (section 2): the same button alternates between two versions. **13th Age's flexible attacks and the even/odd die** (section 1.4): the die picks among the options after the roll.

**Draw Steel:** a signature ability (free, every turn) beside heroic abilities paid from the income; the criticism read is Tigris's "more prone to repetition" (section 2). **Cooldowns:** Staffan (section 2): they "enforce some variety in ability use", "But I'm not sure that milk is worth the moo in TTRPGs", because "tracking it is a pain".

**Gloomhaven's hand:** each card can be played once before a rest, so no attack repeats; rules not quoted here, reception not searched.

**Not found:** a published tabletop rule of the form "each different action this turn gets a bonus" (searched as combo and variety bonus; nothing readable), Hades-like variety bonuses, Panic at the Dojo's stances and action dice.

## Patterns

Ten ways published games deliver "many small hits". "Rolls" = what one use costs at the table.

1. **The margin of one roll is the number of hits.** 40k RPGs (one extra hit per degree of success, or per two), GURPS (per multiple of Rcl), Genesys (per 2 advantages), Cyberpunk RED (a multiplier) [lead].
   - Rolls: 1 attack roll; then damage per hit (40k, GURPS) or none (Genesys: fixed).
   - Tracks: nothing but ammunition.
   - Reception: never called slow; called too strong in every thread read: "It acts like a damage multiplier", "pretty bananas [...] vs a single target", "one round wipe outs". The fixes players reach for: extra hits only on **other** targets, or a rising price per hit. Evidence: medium (three threads, 2009 to 2018, all on guns; the rule texts are second-hand).

2. **One roll, applied to every target.** Draw Steel (the general rule; Two Shot, Double Strike), 4e's Split the Tree (two dice, the higher, both targets), PF2e's Penetrating Shot and Impossible Volley (damage rolled once), ICON's areas (the others take the flat fray).
   - Rolls: 1. Tracks: nothing.
   - It delivers "many hits" only against **many enemies**; against one target it is one hit.
   - Reception: the single roll is praised for speed ("speeds up the game tremendously"); nothing read on these abilities themselves. Evidence: rules strong, reception weak.

3. **The die itself grants the second hit.** 13th Age 1e (a natural even roll, with a smaller damage die paid up front), Nimble (the max on the die explodes), 4e's "if both attacks hit" riders, ICON's "Exceed: Deal 1 damage again".
   - Rolls: 1, sometimes 2. Tracks: nothing.
   - Reception: none found; 13th Age 2e appears to have replaced the even-roll gate with two real attacks, one of which may deal half (a guide's description).

4. **The floor: a hit that cannot whiff.** Draw Steel's tiers (4 / 6 / 8), 13th Age's miss damage, ICON's "Miss: fray" and auto-hit, Lancer's Reliable, PF2e's Certain Strike, Nimble's missing only on a 1.
   - Rolls: unchanged. Tracks: nothing.
   - The argument for it comes from designers: Tweet ("the favored group benefits from a lack of surprises [...] single attacks are more likely to produce an unlikely result"), Introcaso ("not missing [...] core to the experience"). Lancer's limit is the cleanest: the floor carries damage, not riders. Evidence: strong for the design intent, thin for players' words.

5. **Hits as description of one number.** Fantasy Strike (Sirlin: "Moves only have 1 hit unless there is a reason for them to have more [...] to make the look/feel cooler"), Draw Steel's Two Shot flavor, Conan's Loads [lead].
   - Rolls: 1. Tracks: nothing.
   - Reception: Sirlin says a multi-hit must feel better or change how something else reacts; Conan's Volley thread title asks if it is "lame" [lead, unread]. Evidence: weak (one designer post from a video game).

6. **A flat rider on every hit.** PF2e's Precise Strike (+2 a Strike), Nimble's monk and Fury Die, Slay the Spire's Accuracy on Shivs, Draw Steel's kit bonus per target, Gloomhaven's Poison [lead].
   - Rolls: unchanged. Tracks: nothing (a number on the sheet).
   - It is what makes small hits add up, and it is also what made Twin Strike the best button. The counterweight is anything subtracted per hit: "DR pretty much ruins any attempt at 'death of a thousand cuts' style characters". PF2e handles both ends in one sentence (combine the damage for resistance; precision only once), and players approve ("not utterly boned by resistances"). Evidence: strong for the resistance problem (two threads, 2012 and 2026).

7. **A meter the small hits fill, cashed out in one blow.** Exalted 3e (withering to decisive), PF2e's swashbuckler (panache to finisher), Lancer's Gunslinger die.
   - Rolls: the build hits are full attacks. Tracks: one number.
   - Reception: praised as cinematic, and for the climax ("someone with enough built up Initiative just rolls a pile of 20+ dice"); criticised as one big hit with a build-up ("Their damage is tied to a single finisher strike that if it misses, their damage is terrible"). It is the nova pattern, not the thousand cuts. Evidence: medium.

8. **A meter that is held, or spent in small amounts.** 13th Age's momentum (on or off; kept by default), PF2e's panache as a state with small passive bonuses, Draw Steel's surges (up to 3 on any damage) and its once-a-round income triggers, ICON's single Combo token.
   - Rolls: none added. Tracks: a flag, or a pool of about 3.
   - Reception: the per-hit token economy is the thing called fiddly ("constant recharges and expenditures on a per hit basis is a bit much"; "no more than 1-3 most of the time"; counting down is easier than counting up and down). Draw Steel's build-and-spend is called "more prone to repetition" by one poster. Evidence: medium-weak (one old thread, one recent one).

9. **Several real attack rolls, made cheap.** Savage Worlds (one throw of N dice), Nimble (the damage die is the attack), Strike! and Gloomhaven (no damage roll), table practice (attack and damage together, colors by order, average damage).
   - Rolls: N dice, one throw. Tracks: nothing.
   - Reception: the practice is recommended wherever it is discussed ("If you're saving just four seconds per roll, that adds up to 5 minutes per combat"); its limit is any decision between the rolls. Evidence: medium.

10. **A falling return on repeating, and a different card for the follow-up.** PF2e (the multiple attack penalty; press actions with a failure effect; one flourish a round), Nimble (cumulative disadvantage on rushed attacks), Savage Worlds (recoil), 13th Age's monk (opening, flow, finishing), ICON's Combo (base and combo versions alternate), cooldowns.
    - Rolls: unchanged. Tracks: "what I did last" (monk, Combo) or nothing.
    - Reception: the penalty is accepted as the action you trade for movement; cooldowns "enforce some variety" but "tracking it is a pain"; and the PF2e ranger shows the method failing when one class is exempted from the penalty. Evidence: medium.

**An eleventh, about the resource:** ammunition as a die or a few abstract units (Black Hack: rolled after the fight; Darker Dungeons: on every shot; Conan: Loads spent for an extra d20 and damage die [lead]). A roll per shot is called "more cumbersome than actually tracking individual arrows" for anyone who shoots several times a round; Black Sword Hack refuses the die for arrows; no game was found where the player spends dice from an "arrows" pool to add hits, apart from Conan, whose reception could not be read.
