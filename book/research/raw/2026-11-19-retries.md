# Retrying a failed Check: what games print

**Date:** 2026-11-19.

**The designer's words:** "This is a huge problem in 2e. You can just Force Open, Pick a Lock etc non stop until you succeed. We need to put something like: You can't try again if you fail at a task such as picking a lock, forcing open a door, repairing etc until the situation changes, you try something different or the GM decides. This of course doesn't apply to different ways of doing something: If you fail at picking a lock, you can still try to force it open. If you fail on repairing a bridge, you can still attempt to find other ways to cross it etc. I think Draw Steel does it. Feel free to research about it."

**What was read, and how far to trust it.** About 60 searches and page loads, one researcher. Read in full or in the relevant section: Draw Steel's Tests chapter (the Steel Compendium's transcription of *Draw Steel: Heroes*); Pathfinder 2e's Pick a Lock, Force Open, Recall Knowledge, Repair, Craft and GM Core's Recall Knowledge on Archives of Nethys; Pathfinder 2e legacy skill actions on Roll20; Pathfinder 1e's skill format, Knowledge, Diplomacy and Disable Device on d20pfsrd; the Year Zero Engine SRD (Old Gus' transcription of Free League's SRD v1.0); Blades in the Dark's SRD (Action Roll, Consequences); Call of Cthulhu 7e's free rules on Chaosium's wiki; Fate Core's SRD (actions); Daggerheart's SRD 1.0 (core mechanics); Worlds Without Number's free PDF (skill checks). Opinion pages opened: the Alexandrian, DMDavid, Alphastream, Sly Flourish, the Angry GM, Necropraxis, the Burning Wheel forum, EN World, a Draw Steel review (Cybernetic Coven). **Refused:** every paizo.com page (403), d20srd.org (403), dandwiki (403), the Angry GM's skill article (504). **Not found as text:** the 2014 DMG's page (only forum quotes), 13th Age, Savage Worlds, Torchbearer and Mouse Guard, Ironsworn, Genesys, Warhammer 4e, Shadow of the Demon Lord, Mothership, GURPS, Cypher, 7th Sea: these are [memory] or [lead] only. No MCDM design commentary on tests was found.

Other games' rules, never 2d12's. Nothing here is a proposal.

---

## 1. Draw Steel: the designer is right, it has one

**The rule** [read], *Draw Steel: Heroes*, Chapter "Tests", in the section "How to Make a Test", under its own heading **"Can I Try Again?"**, placed between "Tests During Combat" and "Heroes Make Tests":

> "In many cases when you fail a test, you can't attempt the test again unless the circumstances of the test change. For instance, if you attempt an Agility test to pick a lock and fail, you can't attempt to pick the lock again unless you get better lockpicks, oil the lock, have someone demonstrate how to pick a similar lock, and so on. The Director decides when the circumstances have changed enough to allow a new attempt at a test."

Source: https://steelcompendium.io/v2/Read/heroes/tests/

**What it defines and what it leaves.** "The circumstances of the test change" is defined only by its three examples, all of them changes to *the same method* (a better tool, a prepared lock, a lesson); the judge is the Director. It does not say whether another hero may try, nor how long the failed try took ("How Long Does It Take?": "The amount of time required for a task involving a test is determined by the Director") [read]. "In many cases" makes it a default, not an absolute.

**What surrounds it** [read], same page:
- **When to test:** "only when the player's hero attempts a task where the consequences of failure are interesting or dramatic, and where failure won't grind the story to a halt." Its own example of a free retry: a hero who fails a waist-high wall in a quiet street "can stand up to either try again or walk around the wall. As such, no test is required."
- **Failure must not end the story:** "the test shouldn't be their only option to get the information. If the test fails, perhaps the heroes need to go to a flying library"; "A failed test should always result in a story becoming more interesting, not in the action coming to an end."
- **Plain failure can be converted:** "When a hero rolls a failure without a consequence, the Director can offer to let them succeed with a consequence instead", e.g. break down the door "and lose 1d6 Stamina".
- **A paid reroll:** "Whenever you make a test whose outcome you don't like, you can spend a hero token to reroll the test. You must use the new roll."
- **"It Just Works!":** a clever, outside-the-box approach may need no test, but "such clever ideas often work for free the first time, but the Director could decide they require tests if they are used again." A limit on repeating a *success*.
- **Montage tests,** "Can't Use the Same Skill Twice": "An individual character can't use the same skill more than once in a montage test. Though multiple heroes can use the same skill..." and "New Challenges for Each Test": tests against an obstacle "already overcome" don't count. **The one place Draw Steel rules per person, not per party.**

**Reception.** Thin. A review (Lhuzie, Cybernetic Coven, 2026-06-05) [checked] complains that skills have "12 possible outcomes and almost half of them get in the way", and asks "Why can you keep failing and twiddling your thumbs" (https://cyco.substack.com/p/draw-steel-part-2): a complaint about failure states, not about the retry rule. Alphastream (2026-04-21) [checked] praises the "won't grind the story to a halt" language and notes that "generally, games don't allow rerolls" (https://alphastream.org/index.php/2026/04/21/how-many-times-can-a-pc-reroll-a-check/). No forum thread about "Can I Try Again?" was found (Reddit refuses).

## 2. The other printed rules

### D&D 5e
- **2014 DMG p. 237, "Multiple Ability Checks"** [checked, quoted by a D&D Beyond poster; the rest from memory]: "Sometimes a character fails an ability check and wants to try again." In some cases the only real cost is time; "To speed things up, assume that a character spending ten times the normal amount of time needed to complete a task automatically succeeds at that task." [memory] It goes on: no number of repeats turns an impossible task possible; in other cases a failure rules out the same check, with the example of a lie to a guard that "won't work" again, though the party may try "a different guard post", where the DM may raise the difficulty. https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/dungeon-masters-only/8800-paid-dming-yes-no-maybe/previous
- **2024 DMG p. 28, "Trying Again"** [checked, quoted by an EN World poster]: "Sometimes a character fails an ability check and the player wants to try again." "For some tasks, however, the only consequence of failure is the time it takes to attempt the task again." "If failure has no consequences and a character can try and try again, you can skip the ability check". https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=9697399
- **Jeremy Crawford, 2017** [read]: "The DM says if an ability check is warranted, sets the DC & decides whether a check can be retried", pointing to DMG p. 237. https://www.sageadvice.eu/if-a-player-attempts-an-ability-check-and-fails-can-they-try-again/
- **Older D&D** [checked, quoted by DMDavid]: AD&D 1e on bending bars: "The attempt may be made but once, and if the score required is not made, the character will never succeed at the task." Alphastream [checked]: some editions let a character retry after gaining a level (AD&D 2e's Open Locks [memory]).

### Pathfinder 2e
Retry text exists action by action, never as a general rule [read, AoN and Roll20]:
- **Pick a Lock** (Player Core p. 247): no Failure entry; "Critical Failure You break your toolkit and leave behind obvious damage." **No line on trying again.**
- **Force Open** (Player Core p. 234): no Failure entry; Critical Failure "jams the door, window, container, or gate shut, imposing a –2 circumstance penalty on future attempts to Force it Open." **A worse next try, not a limit.**
- **Repair** (Crafting): Critical Failure "You deal 2d6 damage to the item." No retry line. **Craft:** "If you want to try again, you must start over."
- **Recall Knowledge** (Player Core p. 231): no retry line on the action. **GM Core p. 54** puts it in the GM's chapter: "Once a character has attempted an incredibly hard check or failed a check, further attempts are fruitless—the character has recalled everything they know about the subject"; after a success, "adjust the difficulty to be higher for each attempt". https://2e.aonprd.com/Rules.aspx?ID=2638
- **Legacy (Core Rulebook):** Identify Magic "can't try again for 1 day"; Learn a Spell "can try again after you gain a level"; Decipher Writing "a –2 circumstance penalty to further checks". https://roll20.net/compendium/pf2/General%20Skill%20Actions%20(Legacy)
- **The complaint** [lead, Paizo threads refuse]: a thread titled "Is there a general rule on retrying skill checks?" whose answer in the snippet is "no, there isn't a general rule"; "Does anybody else think it's weird you can't retry knowledge checks?". https://paizo.com/threads/rzs43d3z

**So the designer's reading of 2e holds** [read]: the two actions he names carry no retry line, and their critical failures are the only brake.

### D&D 3.5 and Pathfinder 1e
d20srd.org refused; Pathfinder 1e (the same skill format) was read on d20pfsrd [read]:
- The format: a "Try Again" (now "Retry?") paragraph; "If this paragraph is omitted, the skill can be retried without any inherent penalty other than the additional time required."
- **Take 20:** "Taking 20 means you are trying until you get it right, and it assumes that you fail many times before succeeding"; "Taking 20 takes 20 times as long".
- **Knowledge:** "No. The check represents what you know, and thinking about a topic a second time doesn't let you know" anything new.
- **Diplomacy:** "You cannot use Diplomacy to influence a given creature's attitude more than once in a 24 hour period." "If a request is refused, the result does not change with additional checks, although other requests might be made."
- **Disable Device:** "You can retry checks made to disable traps if you miss the check by 4 or less", but failing by 5 or more springs the trap.

https://www.d20pfsrd.com/skills/ ; https://www.d20pfsrd.com/skills/diplomacy/

### Year Zero Engine (Mutant: Year Zero, Forbidden Lands, Alien, Vaesen...): the closest text to the designer's
Free League's YZE SRD v1.0, "Pushing Your Roll", "Only Once" [read]:

> "You can only push a skill roll once. If you don't succeed on your second try, you are stuck dealing with the consequences. As a rule, you only have one chance to succeed with any action. Once you have rolled the dice—and pushed the roll—you may not roll again to achieve the same goal. You need to try something different or wait until the circumstances have changed in a substantial way. Or let another player character try. This rule does not apply to combat, where you can attack the same enemy multiple times."

And "The Art of Failure": "Failure must not stop the story completely. Even when you fail, there must be a way forward—perhaps at the cost of additional time, risk, or resources, but still a way." Pushing "always comes with a risk or a cost", which varies by game. https://callmepartario.github.io/og-yzesrd (an independent transcription under Free League's licence).

### Blades in the Dark
No general ban; the ban is **one consequence the GM may choose** [read], "Consequences & Harm":
- **Lost Opportunity:** "This consequence represents shifting circumstance. You had an opportunity to achieve your goal with this action, but it slips away. To try again, you need a new approach—usually a new form of action or a change in circumstances." Example: a failed Skirmish to trap a noble means "you'll have to try another way—maybe by Swaying her".
- **Worse Position:** "You haven't failed, but you haven't succeeded yet, either. You can try again, re-rolling at the new, worse position." A retry priced by escalation (controlled, risky, desperate).
- Action Roll: on a 1-3, "Usually, the action just fails completely"; "withdraw and try a different approach" is the position text's own phrase.

https://bladesinthedark.com/consequences-harm ; https://bladesinthedark.com/action-roll

### Burning Wheel: "Let It Ride"
*Burning Wheel Gold* p. 32 [checked, quoted on two pages]: "A player shall test once against an obstacle and shall not roll again until conditions legitimately and drastically change." And: "A GM can not call for multiple rolls of the same ability to accomplish a player's stated intent." It binds both sides: no player retry, no GM re-roll of a success. Reception [checked]: posters call "changed enough" "a judgement call"; whether *another* character may try is split on the official forum: one says it "applies to a single player's roll, not to multiple players' tests", another that "the collective players can't attempt the same thing twice". https://necropraxis.com/2016/09/19/let-it-ride-or-push-your-luck/ ; https://forums.burningwheel.com/t/letting-it-ride-example/15700 . No words of Luke Crane on it were found.

### Mouse Guard and Torchbearer
[lead, forum quotes of the book]: on a failed test the GM chooses **a twist** (a new obstacle; the goal is blocked) **or a condition** (success, with Tired, Angry, Sick, Hungry or Injured); not both (Mouse Guard p. 90). Posters read it as "no back and forth testing to overcome the same obstacle"; Torchbearer forum: "There's no let it ride in Torchbearer, but it felt wrong to test again for the same thing". A failure always changes the situation, so there is nothing to retry.

### Call of Cthulhu 7e: push once
[read] "If you can justify it through your investigator's actions, you can 'push' a failed skill roll. Pushing a roll allows you to roll the dice a second time; however, the stakes are raised." "If you fail a second time, the Keeper gets to inflict a dire consequence"; the Keeper may foreshadow it first. https://cocwiki.chaosium.com/rules/game-system.html . [memory, from the Keeper Rulebook] only one push; not in combat or for Sanity; the justification must be a change in what the investigator does (more time, more force, a new angle).

### PbtA and Dungeon World
[read] "A character can't take the fictional action that triggers a move without that move occurring"; on a 6-, "that's up to the GM but you also always mark XP" (Dungeon World, Roll20 SRD). **No printed retry ban was found;** a miss lets the GM make a move, so the fiction has changed when the player acts again. Fan readings call "repeat only after the situation changes" a let-it-ride import [lead].

### Fate Core
[read] "When you fail an overcome action, you have two options. You can simply fail ... or you can succeed at a serious cost." A tie is success "at a minor cost". **No sentence about trying again** on the SRD's action pages. https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/four-actions

### GUMSHOE
[checked] "If a scene contains a core clue and a player character uses an Investigative Ability relating to that clue," the character finds it (https://pelgranepress.com/2017/09/29/gumshoe-rules-summary/). No roll, so no retry. General abilities can fail; no retry rule was found.

### Daggerheart
[read, SRD 1.0 core mechanics] Failure with Hope: "You fail with a minor consequence and gain a Hope, but the spotlight swings to the GM." Failure with Fear: "You fail with a major consequence and the GM gains a Fear". **No retry rule.** The spotlight swing is the brake: every failure hands the GM a move. https://rules.omnisgm.com/en/daggerheart/srd-1.0/core-mechanics/

### Worlds Without Number
[read, free PDF, "Skill Checks"] "On a failure, the PC either can't accomplish the feat at all or they achieve it at the cost of some further complication". Rolls only for tasks outside the background. **No retry rule.** The Expert's class ability, "Once per scene, the Expert may reroll any non-combat skill check", treats a second try as a class feature.

### Games not verified
- **13th Age:** "failing forward" is in the core book [lead: "p. 42" in a forum post]; Rob Heinsoo on turning "fail-forward moments on skill checks into fail-sideways" [checked]. No retry text found.
- **Savage Worlds:** Bennies reroll a Trait roll [lead]; no printed ban found. The "can't be attempted again unless circumstances change" line the brief asks about was **not found**.
- **Cypher:** a retry "Must apply 1 level of effort" [lead, Justin Alexander's Numenera cheat sheet; no rules text].
- **GURPS** (B348, "Repeated Attempts"): a cumulative −1 a retry, some tasks one try only [lead, a fan paraphrase].
- **Warhammer 4e:** Extended Tests; retries "with a –10% penalty for each additional test" [lead, possibly another edition].
- **Ironsworn:** a miss triggers Pay the Price [lead]; no retry text found.
- **Mothership, Shadow of the Demon Lord, Genesys, 7th Sea:** nothing found.

## 3. The design arguments

**Why "roll until you succeed" happens.** The Angry GM (2019) [checked]: a check needs a chance of failure and a cost of failure, "such that the party can't keep doing the same thing over and over until they get it right"; and time "usually doesn't carry a cost unless there's a time limit" or a wandering-monster system. https://theangrygm.com/gambling-on-action/ . Necropraxis [checked]: let it ride and push your luck sit on "a spectrum of resolution finality".

**Failure mode 1: the party stuck behind one door.** Every game with a ban also prints a guard against it [read]: Draw Steel ("won't grind the story to a halt"; another route to the same information), YZE ("there must be a way forward"), 5e 2014 (another guard post). Alphastream [checked]: don't make one failed check the only path; bashing the door is still open, at a time cost.

**Failure mode 2: arguing over "different".** No game defines it beyond examples, and all of them name the GM as judge (Draw Steel, Crawford, Burning Wheel's "legitimately and drastically"). The Alexandrian (2016) [checked] gives the most usable test: look first for "a change in approach"; an interruption is not a change; a "significant change in circumstance" is rare (a "seismic event"); and the GM may instead modify the standing result. https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/38313/roleplaying-games/the-art-of-rulings-part-8-let-it-ride . DMDavid (2021) [checked]: "a change in approach allows a reroll" (a lever after failing to move the stone). https://dmdavid.com/tag/how-to-avoid-boring-rerolls-of-dds-ability-checks/

**Per person or per party?** Printed answers differ:
- **Another character may try:** YZE ("Or let another player character try"); 5e 2014's lie example (a new guard, who may be harder).
- **Per character:** Draw Steel's montage ("An individual character can't use the same skill more than once ... Though multiple heroes can use the same skill").
- **Per target:** Pathfinder 1e's Diplomacy (a creature, 24 hours).
- **Unsettled:** Burning Wheel's own forum.
- Sly Flourish (2018) [checked]: curb "full table rolls" by "determining if only one character can reasonably perform the check or by accepting only the first roll". https://slyflourish.com/ability_check_toolbox.html

**The time spent.** Skip-the-roll (5e 2024), ×10 (5e 2014), ×20 (Take 20), or a ladder of waits (EN World posters [checked]: an action, a minute, an hour, a day). Draw Steel leaves the duration to the Director. All of them work only if time is scarce.

**Does tiered "fail forward" make the ban unnecessary?** The games with the strongest fail-forward print a ban anyway [read]. Draw Steel keeps a plain "you simply don't do what you set out to do" result; YZE keeps a plain failure until the push. [judgment] The ban lives exactly on the no-consequence failure, the one result a player can re-roll for free. Blades and Mouse Guard need no ban because every failure must change the situation (a lost opportunity, a twist); 5e 2024 and Draw Steel's "no test is required" remove the roll when nothing is at stake.

**"Say yes or roll."** Vincent Baker's *Dogs in the Vineyard*: the GM's conflict rule is "Roll dice or say yes" [checked, Wikipedia]. Its corollary: once the dice are out, the result stands. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogs_in_the_Vineyard

## What seems to transfer

### (a) The shapes of a retry rule

| Shape | Games | Known cost |
|---|---|---|
| **A per-task ban until the situation changes** | Draw Steel, Burning Wheel, YZE (after the push), PF2e Recall Knowledge, PF1 Knowledge and Diplomacy requests, 5e 2014 (some cases) | "Changed enough" is a GM call each time; needs a no-dead-end guard beside it |
| **A ban per person, others may try** | YZE; Draw Steel montages; 5e 2014's guard post | The party tries each member in turn: one roll becomes one roll per character |
| **A ban per party or "first roll only"** | Sly Flourish's option; one Burning Wheel reading | The best-suited character must go first; others sit out |
| **A timed lockout** | PF2e Identify Magic (1 day), Demoralize, Track; PF1 Diplomacy (24 h); PF2e Learn a Spell, AD&D (a level) | Worthless when time is free |
| **A cost a retry** | Draw Steel's hero token, WWN's Expert reroll, Cypher's Effort [lead], CoC's Luck | A currency to track; when it is plentiful, the spam is back |
| **A worse next try** | PF2e Force Open (−2 on a critical failure), Decipher Writing; Blades' Worse Position; GURPS [lead] | A small penalty doesn't stop a free retry |
| **Push once, at a raised stake** | Call of Cthulhu 7e, YZE | Needs a stake the GM can name each time; then a hard stop |
| **One roll decides; let it ride** | Burning Wheel, the Alexandrian | GM judgment on scope and on what ends the ride |
| **Skip the roll when retries are free** | 5e 2024, Draw Steel ("no test is required"), Take 20 / ×10 | Time must be scarce elsewhere, or it is a free success |
| **Every failure changes the situation** | Blades (Lost Opportunity), Mouse Guard (twist or condition), Daggerheart (the GM's move), PbtA | The GM invents a consequence every time |

### (b) The definitions printed, verbatim

- **Draw Steel:** "unless the circumstances of the test change ... unless you get better lockpicks, oil the lock, have someone demonstrate how to pick a similar lock, and so on. The Director decides when the circumstances have changed enough".
- **YZE:** "You need to try something different or wait until the circumstances have changed in a substantial way. Or let another player character try."
- **Blades:** "To try again, you need a new approach—usually a new form of action or a change in circumstances."
- **Burning Wheel:** "until conditions legitimately and drastically change".
- **PF1 Diplomacy:** "the result does not change with additional checks, although other requests might be made."
- **PF2e GM Core:** "further attempts are fruitless—the character has recalled everything they know about the subject."
- **The Alexandrian** (commentary): "a change in approach"; a "seismic event"; an interruption doesn't count.
- **5e 2014** [memory]: the same lie won't work again; a different way, or a different guard post.

### (c) The designer's wording beside the nearest rules

His sentence: "You can't try again if you fail at a task such as picking a lock, forcing open a door, repairing etc until the situation changes, you try something different or the GM decides. This of course doesn't apply to different ways of doing something".

- **Nearest overall: Year Zero.** "You may not roll again to achieve the same goal. You need to try something different or wait until the circumstances have changed in a substantial way." It has his two exits (something different; a changed situation) in nearly his words, and adds a third he leaves open: "Or let another player character try."
- **Draw Steel** has the ban and "The Director decides", but there **the GM judges whether the circumstances changed**; it is not a third, free-standing exit as in his "or the GM decides". Its examples of a change are better tools, an oiled lock, a lesson: **the same method improved**. His examples ("you can still try to force it open"; "other ways to cross it") are **a different method**. Draw Steel doesn't say a different method is allowed; Blades ("a new form of action"), YZE ("try something different") and the Alexandrian ("a change in approach") do.
- **Burning Wheel's** "legitimately and drastically" sets a higher bar than his "the situation changes", and it also forbids the GM from asking for the same roll twice.
- **Left open by his sentence, and settled differently by the games:** whether another character may try (YZE: yes; Draw Steel montages: each character once; Burning Wheel: disputed); what the failed attempt cost in time (Draw Steel: the Director); and whether a plain failure can instead become success at a cost (Draw Steel's conversion, Fate, Mouse Guard), which removes the case the ban exists for.
