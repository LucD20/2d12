# Magic outside a fight: rituals, free-form effects, and two-word magic in other games

2026-11-19

## The question

Lucas, decision 87 of `book/magic-design.md`: "people will pick spells that buff for 10 minutes and then can just cast them as a more permanent buff. Should we make these combat only and the 'out of combat' stuff we create per path or school?" The working idea: the Path says what the magic is made of, the School what it does, so the pair tells a player what they can attempt; higher levels open wider uses; a small pool (Surge held outside a fight) pays. Decision 49: spells should be "unique, cool sounding, you can make arguments for out of combat uses". So: how do other games handle magic outside combat, above all free-form, "verb + noun" and ritual magic, and what do players and designers say works or fails?

**These are other games' rules and other people's opinions, never 2d12's rules. Nothing here is a design.**

## 1. Rituals as a separate list

**D&D 4e.** Rules [memory]: utility magic was pulled out of the power lists into rituals. Anyone with the Ritual Caster feat (free for a few classes) casts from a ritual book; each ritual has a level, a casting time (10 minutes is typical, some an hour or more), a component cost in gold each casting, a market price to learn, and usually a skill check (Arcana, Religion, Nature, Heal) whose result sets how good the effect is. No daily limit: gold and time are the limit.
Reception [checked, EN World, two threads, page 3 of each]:
- Gold. Dungeoneer: "tying ritual casting to gold was definitely a mistake." Ryujin, on gold given for rituals: "The players just banked it towards purchasing magic items and consumables". AbdulAlhazred puts it on the treasure system: "The problem isn't rituals, or alchemy, etc. It is parcels."
- Time. mkill: "It's just too damn long in most cases, without any way to reduce it within the rules." Tony Vargas: "A casting time of 10 minutes or an hour really is an uneccessary limitation."
- Indifference even when free. generalchaos34, whose players were given rituals: "they just dont seem to want to use them".
- Defenders use them heavily at paragon level (Evilhalfling: "it comes up all the freaken time") or for the world (S'mon: "they're a fantastic world-building tool").
- Fixes posters proposed: a renewable resource instead of gold, components found in play, casting inside a short rest. A search summary adds two more reasons [lead]: players think in "attacks and skill checks" and forget a third kind of action, and at high level the cost becomes trivial.

**D&D 5e.** Ritual rule [memory]: a spell with the ritual tag can be cast in 10 extra minutes without a slot; the wizard casts any ritual in the book. No gold. The cost is time only, and the list is a subset of ordinary spells.
Utility cantrips are "minor magic by theme" as a closed menu. Prestidigitation [read, dnd5e.wikidot.com]: one of six listed effects a casting (a harmless sensory effect; light or snuff a candle, torch or small campfire; clean or soil 1 cubic foot; chill, warm or flavor 1 cubic foot for 1 hour; a mark for 1 hour; a trinket that fits in a hand until the end of your next turn), and "up to three of its non-instantaneous effects active at a time". Druidcraft [read]: four listed effects (tomorrow's weather, a bloom, a sensory effect in a 5-foot cube, light or snuff a small fire). Thaumaturgy [memory]: the same shape for a priest (a booming voice, flickering flames, tremors, a door flying open). The pattern: a theme, a list of four to six effects, a size cap in cubic feet, a duration cap, a count cap. Commentary [checked, Periapt Games, "On edge cases and magic", 2024]: a detailed list gives players what they need to decide, and its cost is that it "implies that anything not listed isn't possible".

**Pathfinder 2e** [read, Archives of Nethys, Player Core p. 389]: "A ritual is an esoteric and complex spell that anyone can cast." No slots; the primary caster need not be a spellcaster but must know the ritual, and its rank is at most half their level; secondary casters need nothing; at least 1 hour, often days of 8 hours; a component cost; secondary skill checks first (a critical success +2, a failure -4 to the primary check), then a primary check at a very hard DC; four degrees of outcome; rituals are "never common". The limits are stacked: rarity (the GM hands them out), helpers, time, gold, and a roll.

**13th Age utility spell** [read, SRD]: a wizard gives up a slot for "the utility spell" and gains "a range of useful non-combat spells of the level you memorized it or below": one combat slot buys a whole menu, decided at the day's start.

**Shadow of the Demon Lord** [memory]: no separate ritual subsystem in the core book; utility spells sit in each tradition beside attacks and draw on the same castings per spell rank. Not verified.

**Draw Steel.** Abilities outside combat [read, steelcompendium.io, Troubadour "Drama"]: "Though you can't gain drama outside of combat, you can use your heroic abilities and effects that cost drama without spending it", but then "you can't use that same ability or effect outside of combat again until you earn 1 or more Victories or finish a respite"; an ability that takes unlimited drama is used "as if you had spent an amount of drama equal to your Victories." (The Talent's clarity has the same rule [lead].) So: each ability once per win, its size set by the party's streak. Projects [memory; the chapter would not load]: at a respite a hero makes a project roll and adds it to a running total toward a goal; a project may need an item and a source of knowledge.

**Daggerheart** [read, a fan rules site quoting the core rule]: the closed position. "You can't just make up magic effects that aren't on your character sheet or cards." Flavor is free and "won't give you access to new effects"; "at the GM's discretion, they might allow you to creatively apply an existing spell in an unusual way."

## 2. Free-form rituals built from a spell or a domain

**13th Age rituals** [read, SRD, in full]: "Rituals are spells cast outside of combat for various free-form magical effects." Four steps: choose the spell "that will be used and expended"; say what you want and gather ingredients; spend "1d4 minutes/quarter-hours/hours (as determined by the GM)"; make a skill check with a magical background. "The higher the level of the spell consumed by the ritual, the greater the effect." Results are "outgrowths of the spell's normal effects", "don't have to be taken as a precedent for future rituals", and "Failure should fail forward." The Cantrip Mastery talent [read]: non-combat "cantrip-style versions of any wizard spell you have memorized", with the line "none of these uses should be combat relevant or deal damage."
The designers' own patch [checked, Gareth Ryder-Hanrahan, "Expanded Rituals", Pelgrane Press, 2023]: the core rules "are simple and largely in the purview of the Gamemaster", so he adds a price list. The DC starts 10 above normal, then: **resonance** (a very fitting spell -5, an unfitting one +5, the other players as a "jury" on borderline cases), time (1d4 minutes +5, hours -5), helpers (-5; a village -10), components (extra -5, none +10), targets (the party +5, a large area +10), repetition (+5, then +10: a ritual done before gets harder, unless it is "inlaid" at one place). On a failure the caster picks one drawback per 5 points missed.

**Fabula Ultima** [read, the Ritual rules page in full]. A class gives a **discipline**, each with a one-line domain: Elementalism "Walk on water, shape rock, snuff out fires, cause a rainstorm or summon powerful cyclones"; Entropism "Cause the decay of physical matter, twist the flow of time, teleport creatures or items"; Spiritism "Sense the presence and feelings of creatures, put someone to sleep or embolden their heart"; also Arcanism, Chimerism, Ritualism.
A written never-list. Ritual magic "should never be able to" do any of: "Causing direct damage" (collateral damage is allowed); "Inflicting or removing status effects"; making characters "lose or gain HP, MP, IP, Fabula Points or Ultima Points"; "Replicating the mechanical benefits of an existing spell or Skill (however, narrative effects such as teleportation or flight are okay"; "Generating creatures or equipment, or granting them permanent abilities."
The procedure: describe the effect and the area; "The Game Master has final say on whether a given effect can be accomplished, as well as which Ritual discipline it falls within"; the GM reads the cost from two tables; a Magic Check against the potency's Difficulty Level; on a failure the effects are "twisted in a catastrophic way".

| Potency | MP | DL | Examples (verbatim) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minor | 20 | 7 | Create a flash of light, block a passage, shatter a glass. |
| Medium | 30 | 10 | Create an illusion, treat an illness, locate someone or something, sense emotions, provide short-term energy. |
| Major | 40 | 13 | Sense thoughts, influence emotions, dispel a curse, temporarily alter the weather, provide long-term energy. |
| Extreme | 50 | 16 | Weaken a divine entity, prevent a catastrophe, cause a week-long change in a creature or location. |

| Area | MP | Examples (verbatim) |
|---|---|---|
| Individual | ×1 | A human-sized creature, a door, a tree, a weapon. |
| Small | ×2 | A few human-sized creatures, a large creature, a small clearing, a room, a railway car, a hut. |
| Large | ×3 | A crowd, a small forest, an airship or galleon, a castle hall, a house, a giant creature. |
| Huge | ×4 | A fortress, a lake, a mountaintop, a village, a city block. |

Cost = potency × area (20 to 200 MP). Four priced sample rituals follow, each with its failure ("Sensing whether a person is hostile is a Spiritism Ritual of minor potency affecting an individual area (20 MP, DL 7)"). Others can help (a Group Check); one rare ingredient halves the cost, once, and "finding it should be its own adventure". In a conflict a ritual first needs a Clock filled (4, 6, 6, 8 sections by potency). The Tinkerer's Projects reuse the same two tables with money for MP and a third, Uses (consumable ×1, permanent ×5). Note what is absent: duration and range have no table (they hide in the potency examples: "temporarily", "week-long"), and the caster's level changes nothing but the size of the MP pool.
Reception [checked, the page of a fan supplement, "Rosalind's Guide to Traditional Rituals"]: "sometimes players forget about Ritual spellcasting", worst for players from "very rigid spellcasting structures"; its remedy is "ready-made, pre-written example Rituals".

**Dungeon World, Ritual** [read]: "When you draw on a place of power to create a magical effect, tell the GM what you're trying to achieve. Ritual effects are always possible, but the GM will give you one to four of the following conditions": "It's going to take days/weeks/months"; "First you must ____"; "You'll need help from ____"; "It will require a lot of money"; "The best you can do is a lesser version, unreliable and limited"; "You and your allies will risk danger from ____"; "You'll have to disenchant ____ to do it". No scope table, no level: the cost list is the whole rule, and the place of power is the gate.
Reception [checked, the archived Dungeon World community, 2013 and 2014]: Adam Koebel (co-designer): Ritual is "tell them the consequences and ask", and for a cost "Pick the first thing you think of and ask the player why it matters." Clarity Flowers: "Don't make my mistake and make a ritual cost that slows down the adventure." Erik Buchanan: "any ritual that requires time is hard to pull off" in a dungeon. The gate is soft in practice (Jim V: "I always err on the side of leniency on what counts as a place of power."). [lead]: posters call the conditions "very arbitrary, almost entirely GM fiat" and note there is "no guidance as to what a 10th level Wizard can do, say, vs a 1st level Wizard".

**Blades in the Dark** [read, the SRD]: a ritual is found (a source, as the payoff of a score), learned (a long-term project), and defined once by three questions: "What does the ritual do and how is it weird?" (the player answers), "What must I do to perform the ritual, and what is its price?" (the GM answers), and a third on the belief or fear it leaves. After that it is fixed. "A ritual takes at least one downtime activity to perform and inflicts stress on the caster according to its magnitude." The **magnitude table** is one seven-step ladder (0 to 6) read on six axes at once: area ("A closet" to "A city block"), scale ("1 or 2 people" to "A colossal gang (160)"), duration ("A few moments", "A few minutes", "An hour", "A few hours", "A day", "Several days", "A week"), range ("Within reach" to "Across the city"), quality, force (a "candle flame" to "molten lava"). The rules call it "a tool to help the GM make judgment calls", and a longer casting time lowers the stress. The negotiation happens once per ritual, not once per casting.

**Whitehack miracles** [checked, The Last Redoubt blog; the rulebook was not opened]: a Wise character writes **wordings**, short open phrases; a broad one ("Shadows") is more useful and costs more, a narrow one less. The player says the effect, the referee prices it in hit points from a table of four magnitudes, and the player lowers it by accepting more time, an ingredient or a weaker effect. Whether the wording and the character's vocation fit moves the price. The blog's own caution: high costs make players "try harder to find arguments". Its defense: the table is "self-correcting".

**Maze Rats** [read, a fan web copy of the CC BY text]: each morning a spell slot is filled by rolling a two-word name from tables (an effect, element or form, physical or ethereal). "The GM then tells the player the spell's general effects, based on its name." "If the suggested effect matches the spells name and the situation very closely, the GM should usually allow it." "The GM is the final arbiter". One use, then gone: ambiguity is tolerated because the spell never returns.

**Knave** [read, the 1.0 PDF]: 100 one-sentence spells with no levels; "L" is the caster's level, "an item is an object able to be lifted with one hand, and an object is anything up to human size", and "all spells with ongoing effects last up to L×10 minutes, and have a range of up to 40 feet." Each spell book "can only be used once per day", holds one spell and fills an item slot. Three global definitions do the bounding for the whole list; the sentence does the rest ("Control Weather: You may alter the type of weather at will, but you do not otherwise control it").

**Barbarians of Lemuria** [memory]: no spell list; four tiers of effect (cantrips, then First, Second and Third Magnitude, from "a trained person with tools could do it" to world-shaking), each with a rising Arcane Power cost and a number of **casting requirements** to accept from a list (a rare item, hours of ritual, a special place or time, a sacrifice). Not verified.

**Savage Worlds** [memory; see `magic-schools.md`]: fixed powers, and the trapping (fire, ice) is the fiction that the GM may let matter at the edges. Arcane Protection is a power that makes hostile powers harder against its target: nothing on it for this question.

**Genesys** [memory for the rules; checked for reception]: a few spell actions (Attack, Augment, Barrier, Conjure, Curse, Dispel, Heal, Utility), each a skill check at a base difficulty, each added effect raising it; 2 strain a casting; Utility is the catch-all for small narrative effects. Reception [checked, the archived FFG forum, "Genesys magic too open/easy?"]: the answers to a caster who does too much are all GM tools: upgrade the difficulty, add setback dice, enforce strain (kaosoe: "That's not an insignificant cost"), spend threat on the folly table.

## 3. Verb + noun and sphere systems

**Ars Magica, spontaneous magic** (only what the note lacks) [read through the reader]: "Before casting the spell, the maga must decide on the effect she is trying to create"; a good roll may extend range, duration or target but not change the effect. **Ceremonial casting** stretches a spontaneous spell: "fifteen minutes for every magnitude of the spell", two scholarly Abilities added, and "the level of the spell is limited by the time the maga spent in casting it". Time is the scope dial.

**Mage: the Ascension** [memory]: nine Spheres rated 1 to 5; each dot is a broader verb (roughly: sense, touch, control, command, master); an effect needs every Sphere it touches at the dot its verb needs; subtle "coincidental" magic is safe and "vulgar" magic draws Paradox. No effect list binds the player. Reception [checked, Mage: the Podcast, "Session Zero Tools"]: the questions a table must settle before play are the system's open seams: "How strict should Sphere requirements be?", "How much does Paradigm limit what characters can do?", "Should magical effects be negotiated quickly or carefully built?"; "Some tables love detailed Sphere debates. Others want fast rulings and narrative flow", and "The problem happens when half the table expects one and half expects the other." [lead]: a fan page says a Storyteller "can not account for all the things that even a beginning Sphere Mage can get up to". The complaint that one player's mage owns every scene was not found on a page.

**Mage: the Awakening 2e** is the same family with the ambiguity priced out. [memory]: ten Arcana, and thirteen named Practices unlocked by dots (1: Compelling, Knowing, Unveiling; 2: Ruling, Shielding, Veiling; 3: Fraying, Perfecting, Weaving; 4: Patterning, Unraveling; 5: Making, Unmaking): a verb list by rank. Dots above what a spell needs give free **Reach**. [read, a fan copy of the spell factors]: every factor has two ladders, and one Reach moves a factor from the standard ladder to the advanced one. Duration: 1, 2, 3, 5, 10 turns at 0, -2, -4, -6, -8 dice; advanced: a scene or hour, a day, a week, a month, a year, then indefinite (which needs a Reach and 1 Mana). Scale: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 subjects (arm's reach to "A ballroom or a small house"); advanced: 5 to 160 subjects, up to "A campus, or a small neighbourhood". Range: touch, or sensory with a Reach. So rank widens the verbs and gives free steps; everything else is paid in dice.

**GURPS Ritual Path Magic** [memory for the mechanics]: nine Path skills (Body, Chance, Crossroads, Energy, Magic, Matter, Mind, Spirit, Undead) and seven effect verbs (Sense, Strengthen, Restore, Control, Destroy, Create, Transform), each Lesser or Greater; a ritual's energy cost is the sum of its effects and modifiers (duration, range, area, damage); the caster gathers energy by repeated rolls, so a big ritual is slow, and the answer for combat is a charm prepared in advance. Syntactic magic (GURPS Thaumatology) is plain verb + noun skills. Reception [checked, two reviews]: Ref's Place: it "can bog the game down as players improvise new spells"; Pseudoboo: the spell-building rules span five pages and need a summary, and the grimoire of 80 worked spells is what players actually pattern from. The publisher's blurb admits "a lot of judgment calls to make for the GM" [lead].

**Invisible Sun** [read, a fan copy]: several magics side by side, by size. Long-form magic is the ritual tier: each working has a level that sets its cost and potency, one roll per hour of working, listed materials consumed, and "Failure means that the working fails and all required materials and bene spent are lost." A group shares the cost under one lead. The Weaver's two-word aggregates were not read.

**Spheres of Power** (Pathfinder) [read, the wiki]: a sphere gives a base ability and talents widen it. Two walls: rituals are a separate written list by level (1 minute at level 0 to 2 days at level 9, with materials), and **advanced talents**, the world-changing ones, "are available only with GM permission". The dangerous utility is fenced off by a label, not by a price.

**RuneQuest sorcery** [lead]: each spell is manipulated on strength, range and duration, capped by the sorcerer's free INT; a fan designer reports sorcerers "tended not to modify their spells", putting everything in power. If true, free ladders get climbed on one axis only.

## 4. Scope ladders, side by side

| Game | What is bounded | How | What rank changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fabula Ultima [read] | Potency (4 steps), area (4 steps) | Named steps with examples; cost = product | Nothing but the MP pool |
| Blades [read] | Area, scale, duration, range, quality, force | One 0-to-6 ladder, six columns of examples; the level is the stress | Nothing (Tier, a little) |
| Mage: the Awakening [read] | Potency, duration, scale, range, casting time | Two ladders a factor, a dice penalty a step | Verbs unlocked; free Reach |
| Ars Magica [note] | Range, duration, target | Five named steps each, +5 levels a step | The casting total |
| Pathfinder 2e [read] | Each ritual's own text | Written; four outcomes | Rank at most half level |
| 13th Age [read] | "outgrowths of the spell's normal effects" | The GM; time in three sizes | A higher spell, a greater effect |
| Knave [read] | Duration, range, object size | Three global definitions and L | L in the sentence |
| 5e cantrips [read] | Cubic feet, 1 hour, three at once | A closed menu | Nothing |
| Dungeon World [read] | Nothing | Seven costs | Nothing |

The steps are nearly always **named things** (a room, a house, a village; an hour, a day, a week), not measures. Duration ladders converge on moments, minutes, an hour, a day, a week, a year. Permanence is always special: a ritual-only step (Ars Magica), a Reach plus Mana (Awakening), a ×5 multiplier (Fabula Ultima's Projects), or forbidden ("granting them permanent abilities").

## 5. Costs and limits seen

- **Time:** 10 extra minutes (5e); 1d4 minutes, quarter-hours or hours (13th Age); 15 minutes a magnitude (Ars Magica); an hour to days (Pathfinder 2e); a downtime activity (Blades); days to months (Dungeon World). Complaint: time out of proportion idles the table (4e, Dungeon World).
- **A renewable pool:** MP (Fabula Ultima), stress (Blades), hit points (Whitehack), strain (Genesys), a spent spell (13th Age), once per win sized by the streak (Draw Steel).
- **Money or components:** 4e and Pathfinder 2e. 4e's lesson: gold competes with permanent gear and loses.
- **A special ingredient as an adventure:** Fabula Ultima (halves the cost, once), Blades (the source), Dungeon World ("First you must ____").
- **A check with consequences:** fail forward (13th Age), "twisted in a catastrophic way" (Fabula Ultima), four degrees (Pathfinder 2e), drawbacks picked by the margin (Expanded Rituals).
- **Helpers:** Pathfinder 2e's secondary casters, Fabula Ultima's Group Check, Invisible Sun's shared cost. They put the whole party in the caster's scene.
- **A place:** Dungeon World's place of power; an "inlaid" site (Expanded Rituals).
- **Repetition priced:** Expanded Rituals (+5, +10); Draw Steel (once until the next Victory).
- **A permission label:** uncommon and rare rituals (Pathfinder 2e), advanced talents (Spheres of Power).

## 6. Reception

**Praise.** Free-form magic carries play: one GM reports free-form spells "would propel the narrative on their own" [lead]; Dungeon World's ritual costs "drove the next two sessions" [checked]; rituals are liked as world-building even where players ignore them (4e).

**Blame, and what was done about each.**
1. **Ambiguity and negotiation.** Periapt Games [checked]: fuzzy systems leave players unable to judge before spending; negotiated effects are "Mother May I"; a hybrid where the GM scales effects by power level is the worst of both; his advice is to pick one approach, not mix them in one game. Answers seen: priced example tables (Fabula Ultima), a price list for every dial (Expanded Rituals, Awakening), negotiate once and write it down (Blades), other players as a jury (Expanded Rituals).
2. **Slow play.** Ritual Path Magic "can bog the game down" [checked]; a session-zero question for Mage [checked]. Answers: worked examples to copy (the grimoire, Fabula Ultima's samples, Rosalind's Guide), doing the arithmetic away from the table (charms, Blades' fixed rituals), fewer dials (two in Fabula Ultima).
3. **Disuse**, the opposite failure. 4e (gold, time, a third kind of action nobody remembers) and Fabula Ultima ("players forget about Ritual spellcasting"). A separate free-form subsystem is invisible to players used to lists; the fix both communities reached for is a pre-written list of examples.
4. **The caster solves everything.** No essay found; the complaint appears only in a listicle's praise of Knock ("just Knock and boom, it's open") [lead]. The answers in rules text are never-lists: Fabula Ultima's five points; 13th Age's "none of these uses should be combat relevant or deal damage"; Draw Steel's once per win.
5. **No difference by level.** Dungeon World [lead]. Answers: rank unlocks verbs (Awakening's Practices, Ascension's dots), caps the list (Pathfinder 2e), or gives free steps on the ladders (Reach).

## What seems to transfer

### (a) The distinct shapes out-of-combat magic takes

1. **A closed list of rituals** (4e, Pathfinder 2e, Spheres of Power's rituals). Each a written card with time, cost and outcomes. Unambiguous. Weakness: under-used when the price competes with gear or the time idles the table; arguments have no room.
2. **A themed menu of small effects** (5e's Prestidigitation, Druidcraft, Thaumaturgy; 13th Age's utility spell). One entry, four to six listed effects, hard size caps. Weakness: the list reads as the limit ("anything not listed isn't possible").
3. **A combat spell spent for a related effect** (13th Age rituals, Cantrip Mastery; Daggerheart's "creatively apply an existing spell", GM permitting). The spell is the ticket and the theme. Weakness: all GM in its plain form; many damage spells have little to say; it took a ten-line price list to firm it up.
4. **A domain sentence plus a price table** (Fabula Ultima; Blades' magnitude; Whitehack's wordings). Describe anything inside the domain, the table prices it. Weakness: the GM still classifies potency; players forget it exists; level changes nothing.
5. **Costs only, anything possible** (Dungeon World). One paragraph. Weakness: scope is "anything", the conditions are fiat, the gate softens at once.
6. **Verb + noun ranks** (Mage, Ars Magica's spontaneous magic, Ritual Path Magic). The pair says what is possible, rank how far. Weakness: negotiation and slow play are the known price; every such game ends up shipping a grimoire of worked examples.
7. **Negotiated once, then fixed** (Blades' three questions; a researched ritual in Spheres of Power). A free-form effect becomes a written one before first use. Weakness: slow to acquire; needs downtime.
8. **The combat ability itself, rationed outside** (Draw Steel; 5e's ritual tag; Knave's one book a day). Weakness: it is the pre-buff road unless duration is cut, which is the road decision 87 already closed.

### (b) How games keep free-form magic unambiguous

- **A never-list written before the always-list.** Fabula Ultima's five never-points remove direct damage, conditions, resource changes, copies of existing spells and Skills, and permanent creations: almost everything a combat card does. 13th Age says the same in one sentence. Ars Magica's Limits (already in the note) are the fictional version.
- **Scope tables with named examples at each step**, not measures (a hut, a castle hall, a village; an hour, a day, a week). Blades puts six axes on one ladder so one number answers them all.
- **Fixed prices:** a product of two tables (Fabula Ultima), a penalty a step (Awakening), a DC modifier a dial (Expanded Rituals). The player can compute the cost before asking.
- **Priced worked examples, each with its failure** (Fabula Ultima's four; Ritual Path Magic's eighty). Both communities asked for more of these than the books gave.
- **Global definitions** that bound a whole list in three lines (Knave: item, object, L×10 minutes, 40 feet).
- **Who decides the borderline case, written down:** the GM has "final say on whether a given effect can be accomplished, as well as which Ritual discipline it falls within" (Fabula Ultima); the other players as a jury (Expanded Rituals); no precedent (13th Age) against settled once (Blades).
- **Fit as a number:** resonance (-5, 0, +5) in Expanded Rituals; a narrow wording costs less than a broad one in Whitehack. An argument for fit changes the price, not whether it is possible.
- **Never mix the two modes on one effect** (Periapt's advice): the games that last keep a precise list for the fight and a priced table for outside it, with a wall between.

### (c) What a two-word pair has been made to do elsewhere

- **Gate what may be attempted** (Mage, Ars Magica, Ritual Path Magic): the noun is the subject, the verb the operation, the rank in each the reach. The pair is the permission; all sizing happens elsewhere.
- **Carry a one-line domain** (Fabula Ultima's disciplines: one sentence of "you could use it to…" each). Six sentences define the whole free-form space; with a pair, the sentence sits in the cell or is composed from one sentence a row and one a column (Ars Magica's five verbs are defined that way, each with a hard edge: see the note).
- **Unlock verbs by rank** (Awakening's thirteen Practices over five dots; Ascension's dots): a higher level in the verb axis adds new kinds of doing, not a bigger number, and spare rank gives free steps on the scope ladders (Reach).
- **Name a single-use spell** (Maze Rats): the two words are the entire rules text and the GM reads them; it works because the spell is gone after one use.
- **Set the price of a stretch** (Whitehack's wordings and vocation; resonance): how well the words fit the request moves the cost.
- **Choose the roll and the helper** (Fabula Ultima: the discipline sets the two Attributes of the Magic Check; Pathfinder 2e: the primary skill sets the tradition).
- **What no game found here does:** use the pair alone as the scope limit. Wherever verb + noun is the gate, a separate ladder, price or never-list carries how big, how long and how many.
