# Raw: casting without slots; Mana for casters and Stamina for martials

**Date:** 2026-11-18. Other games' rules and opinions, never 2d12's.

## The question

Lucas does not want spell slots. He is considering "a 'Stamina' mechanic that expands to Martials, differently named. Casters use Mana, Martials use 'Stamina'. This is an extra layer yes." How do tabletop games limit casting without slots, and how do games with one pool for casters and another for martials (or one pool for everyone) work, and how are they received?

## 1. DC20

**Rules.**
- The publisher's page (Beta 0.10.5) [read]: "Martials get Stamina Points and Attack Maneuvers"; "Spellcasters get Mana Points and Spell Enhancements". Everyone also has 4 Action Points a round [checked, Gartside].
- A fan reference for Beta 0.10 (Elorea wiki) [read, unofficial]: Stamina Points pay for Maneuvers; "Martial classes typically start with 2 SP at Level 1"; SP "Recover on Short Rest (1 hour)"; "Some abilities restore SP on other triggers". Mana pays for spells; "MP recovers on Long Rest (8 hours)"; a **Mana Spend Limit** per casting equal to Combat Mastery (1 MP a spell at levels 1 to 2), "the limit, not the cost".
- **Sources disagree on Stamina's refresh:** the fan page says a short rest; a GameSkinny guide written for beta 0.7 says "Stamina is recovered when combat ends" [lead: summary only, page refused]. The game is a beta and this changed between versions.
- [memory, not opened] In 0.10 each martial class has a "Stamina Regen" line: once a round it regains Stamina on a class trigger (a heavy hit, an ally helped, and so on). Stamina can be spent in place of an Action Point for a Maneuver [lead]. So the martial pool is tiny (2 to about 6) and cycles inside the fight; the caster pool is larger and drains over the day.
- The 0.10 changelog [lead]: "Techniques have been removed from the game"; the Stamina and Mana progressions were both redone.

**Reception.** Thin. One GM's report (Kirk Gartside, 2025) [checked]: martials have "a diverse array of maneuvers" comparable to casters' spells, and "managing the extensive information" was the hard part; his tracking sheets "proved time-consuming". No opened page criticizes or praises the split itself.

## 2. Fabula Ultima

**Rules.**
- Everyone has Mind Points. From the unofficial Foundry system's code [read]: max MP = Willpower × 5 + level + class benefits (+5 a class that grants it); max HP is the same formula on Might; max Inventory Points = 6 + benefits (+2 a class). One formula, two pools, every class.
- MP are "the resource used by heroes and NPCs alike to power most abilities" (Cannibal Halfling review, 2023) [checked]. A sample spell costs "10 MP per target", up to three [lead].
- Inventory Points are a second, abstract pool: spend them to produce a consumable on the spot. Elixir: 3 IP, recover 50 MP [lead, two summaries agree]. IP are refilled in town for money [lead].
- Resting restores HP and MP in full; in the wild it takes a Magic Tent (4 IP) [memory + lead].

**Reception.** Roll20's blog [lead]: MP are "easier to understand and manage" than slots. One player (clouder, bearblog) [checked]: "a party can handle two conflict encounters before needing to rest", and he finds that pacing hard to design around. Fights are "designed to go no longer than about 3 or 4 rounds" (alex5101) [checked]. No opened review complains that martials and casters share MP. [mine] The potion pool (IP) is what stops MP being a pure daily budget: a second resource refuels the first.

## 3. D&D 5e Spell Points, and psionic power points

**Rules (5e DMG variant, read on dnd5e.wikidot)** [read]. A spell costs 2 / 3 / 5 / 6 / 7 / 9 / 10 / 11 / 13 points for levels 1 to 9. The pool: 4 at level 1, 27 at 5, 64 at 10, 133 at 20; all back on a long rest. **The anti-nova rule:** "You can use spell points to create one slot of each level of 6th or higher. You can't create another slot of the same level until you finish a long rest."

**3.5 psionics** [memory; d20srd refused]: a power costs 1, 3, 5 ... 17 points by level; you can add points to augment it; **you can never spend more points on one power than your manifester level**; all points return after a night's rest.

**Reception.**
- EN World [lead]: the pool equals the point value of all a caster's slots, so it is "a definite buff to casters" (low slots become more top spells).
- Black Citadel's guide (Rich Kibble, 2022) [checked]: a sorcerer can "burn through their entire spell point pool in one battle"; and "More math is involved in keeping track of spell points than with the standard rules."
- Idiomdrottning [checked]: the variant is the worst of both: "you still have to use slots, it's just that you spend points to get slots."
- Gygax, The Dragon #16, quoted by the nuolde blog (2017) [checked]: "Spell points add nothing to D&D except more complication, more record keeping, more wasted time." The same post: flexibility "in a poorly-designed Spell Point system can exacerbate Linear Fighters, Quadratic Wizards."
- [mine] Both D&D point systems needed a second rule to stop the nova: a per-level daily cap (5e) or a per-spell spend cap (3.5). DC20's Mana Spend Limit is the 3.5 answer.

## 4. Savage Worlds

**Rules** (a fan rules site, SWADE) [read, unofficial]. A power's points are spent on activation; "A character recovers 5 Power Points per hour spent resting". **Shorting:** cast without the points at -1 a missing point, and a failure is a Critical Failure. **Backlash** (a critical failure) "causes a level of Fatigue and all currently active powers instantly terminate." The starting pool is usually 10 [memory]. A Benny buys back 5 points [lead].

**"No Power Points"** [lead: fan documents in summaries; no rules page opened]: no pool; the caster subtracts half the power's point cost (rounded down) from the casting roll; a maintained power is -1 to later casting rolls.

**Reception.** A fan setting that makes No Power Points its default [checked]: it "reduces bookkeeping" and "emphasizes magic outside of combat", and combat powers carry a "greater risk of failure". A therpgsite poster [lead] calls Power Points a "bolted-on" system in an otherwise light game. [mine] The two options show the trade plainly: a pool is certain but tracked; a penalty is untracked but makes the big spell the one most likely to fail.

## 5. A cost in fatigue or harm instead of a pool

- **GURPS** [read, an unofficial study page; low-FP effects from memory]. A spell costs Fatigue Points, the same pool a fighter spends on extra effort; HP can pay instead at -1 to the roll a point. Skill 15 cuts the cost by 1, 20 by 2: a master casts small spells free. About 1 FP back per 10 minutes. At the table: a few big spells, then a tired wizard; the discount turns mastery into at-will magic.
- **Shadowrun** [read: a fan page, edition unnamed; 4e text as a lead]. No pool. Every spell has a Drain Value; the caster rolls Willpower + a tradition attribute and takes the remainder as Stun damage, Physical if it exceeds Magic; drain "cannot be healed by magic or medkits". The caster chooses the Force, so chooses the risk. At the table: an extra roll every spell; the mage's limit is his own damage track.
- **Dungeon Crawl Classics** [read, Roll20 compendium]. A spell check every cast; a low result loses the spell for the day. **Spellburn:** "For every ability point he expends, the wizard adds +1 to his spell check"; it heals "1 point per day"; 20 points at once is an automatic natural 20. Mercurial magic: each wizard rolls a personal quirk for each spell. At the table: swingy, memorable, and the nova costs the wizard's body for days.
- **Warhammer Fantasy Roleplay 4e** [lead; Cubicle 7's page refused]. No pool: a casting test against the spell's Casting Number; a big spell needs Channelling first (an extended test over rounds); doubles cause miscasts. A forum poster [lead] asks whether wizards are playable, since channelling takes the turns the fight lasts.
- **Shadow of the Demon Lord** [read, the designer's blog, 2015]. Slots attached to each spell: "At Power 1, you have 1 casting for all rank 1 spells you know"; "Rank 0 spells, minor spells, have unlimited castings"; "everyone regains all expended castings after they complete a rest." Nothing to add up, and a caster cannot pour everything into one spell.
- **Symbaroum** [memory for the official rule; a 2016 blog and an EN World adaptation as checks]. Each casting gives temporary corruption (a blog's summary: "a point of corruption for each spell cast"; an adaptation: 1d4), which clears after the scene; past a threshold tied to Resolute it becomes permanent, and enough permanent corruption ends the character. The limit is dread rather than a budget.
- **Mage: the Ascension** [lead only]. Obvious ("vulgar") magic earns Paradox, more with witnesses; Paradox discharges as damage or flaws. The limit is how visible the magic is, not how much.

## 6. At-will magic limited by action cost and frequency

- **D&D 4e** [checked, EN World thread, 2008]. At-wills are "the meat and bones of what you will be doing most of the time" (Mengu); encounter powers are "more reliable" (keterys); a character out of dailies should be at "about 80% power" (SSquirrel, citing the designers). The cost: Wizards "lost of lot of Wizardly flavor" (Prestidigitalis). The project's `nova-burst.md` has Mearls on the same point (the daily to at-will gap is far bigger in 5e than in 4e).
- **13th Age** [memory]: at-will, per-battle, recharge (a roll after the fight) and daily, with a full refresh every four fights rather than on sleep. One EN World poster dislikes exactly that "encounter-based recharge" (Cadence, 2026) [checked].
- **Draw Steel:** covered in `nova-burst.md` and `caster-class-shape.md`.
- **Lancer** [memory + project notes]: weapons are at-will; systems are Limited (a few uses a mission); pushing costs Heat, a meter that fills. **ICON** [memory]: abilities are at-will; a party Resolve meter builds toward limit breaks.
- **Is "no resource" liked?** [checked, EN World, "Let's Talk About Character Resources", 2026]. Split. TwoSix: without consumable resources a game is "a prompt engine with randomizers". Scribe: "can do without this kind of overhead these days". RenleyRenfield: "the more knobs and buttons and levers and counters you have the less we are roleplaying", and he praises a BRP variant where spells are free and mana only pushes them. JohnSnow wants one "stamina" pool for everything, base abilities usable when "wiped out", and no "traps in how the resource gets used".

## 7. Built in the fight, or spent from full

- **Built (start low, grow).** Draw Steel's lead designer James Introcaso (Rascal, 2025) [checked]: Victories work "like a limit break bar in a Street Fighter game"; "when you rest, you lose your victories"; "And that feels more heroic, right?". A tester-side poster (JonDeNor, EN World, 2025) [checked]: varied gains were hard to plan around, so the game settled on "a steady drip of resources on your turn". The price, already in `nova-burst.md`: a slow round 1 in fights of three or four rounds.
- **Spent (start full, run dry).** Dominions' fatigue (`dominions-magic.md`) and every pool above. Players hoard: a 5e GM worries about players "getting bored and disengaging" while saving resources, and "there will always be some players who hoard resources" (JThursby, Oofta, EN World, 2022) [checked]. Valiant Horizon Tactics' designer [checked]: characters have 8 Stamina but arrive at a fight with "about 4-6", and are told not to "blow all of it at the start".
- [mine] DC20 does both: Stamina is small and regained in the fight; Mana is large and daily. The two names carry two rhythms, not two flavors.

## 8. The video-game roots

- Mana as a game word came from tabletop first. Jon Peterson (2016) [checked] credits Costikyan, Perrin, Bonewits and Garfield, inspired by Niven's stories; spell points date to 1978 (Adventures in Fantasy) [checked, nuolde].
- Health, mana and stamina are "staples of the PC game industry" (Delta Vector, 2015) [checked]. The computer does the arithmetic and refills the bar by the second, which is why the trio reads as obvious and costs nothing there. The same author, for the table: "I'd like to do away with tracking stuff on paper altogether", and he proposes placing a counter only when a resource is **out**.
- Valiant Horizon Tactics' designer [checked]: "nothing slows a game down quite like players tracking a bunch of passive abilities"; he uses one Stamina pool for every class and lets the abilities differ.

## 9. The design question

- **Slots are a pool with a built-in nova cap.** Giant in the Playground posters [lead]: slots are "a pyramidal pool", and the excess power of a broken spell "is capped by the number of spell slots of that level". Remove the pyramid and a cap must be written (section 3).
- **Refresh.** Daily pools give the five-minute day (M.T. Black) [checked]: resting after one or two fights "virtually eliminates the resource management aspect". Per-encounter refresh removes it, and some dislike its artificiality (payn: "I've never liked the idea of encounter powers") [checked]. Composer99 [checked]: "all non-at-will resources should be on the same rest schedule". Brent Newhall [lead]: pool size and refresh rate are one dial.
- **Two names, one mechanic.** EN World on Draw Steel's nine named resources (2025) [checked]: "These aren't different resources (you spend them in the same way too)" (Neonchameleon); "the Gygax Thesaurus problem of too many synonyms" (Tonguez); against: "the real difference is / should be what it can be spent on" (mamba), and the names "had 0 impact on how long it took players to learn" (gnarlygninja).

## Patterns

- **Size.** Small and cycling (DC20 Stamina 2 to 6; Valiant Horizon 8) or large and draining (Fabula Ultima about 40 to 60 at the start [mine, from the formula]; 5e 4 to 133). Small pools are counted in tokens; large ones need subtraction every turn.
- **Refresh.** In the fight (Draw Steel, DC20 Stamina), by the hour (Savage Worlds, GURPS), by the rest (Fabula Ultima, 5e, DC20 Mana), or never needed (drain, corruption, miscasts: the cost is paid at once).
- **What stops a nova.** A per-cast spend cap (DC20, 3.5 psionics), a per-level daily cap (5e), uses tied to each spell (Demon Lord), action cost and casting time (WFRP), harm that scales with the push (Shadowrun, DCC), or starting empty (Draw Steel). No pool read works without one.
- **Tracking.** Lowest: frequency or per-spell castings, and penalty systems (No Power Points). Middle: a small token pool. Highest: a large pool with varied costs, or a roll after every spell (drain).
- **Two pools or one.** They are really one mechanic when size, refresh and spending rule match (Draw Steel's names; Fabula Ultima uses one name honestly). They are two when the rhythm differs (DC20).

## What seems to transfer (my reading only)

- A pool without slots needs a nova rule written with it; the per-cast cap is the simplest one found.
- "Mana and Stamina" earns two names only if they refresh differently. With the same refresh, players will call it one pool with synonyms.
- A small pool counted in tokens costs far less at the table than a large one with subtraction.
- A pool regained inside the fight cannot be hoarded and gives no five-minute day, but it needs a start value or round 1 is slow.
- A cost paid in harm or fatigue adds no new track if the game already has one (`dominions-magic.md`, Strain, is this family: GURPS, Shadowrun).
- Whatever the pool, the sources agree a character at zero should still have a real turn (4e's 80%, JohnSnow's "wiped out").
- Savage Worlds shows a pool and a penalty can be two faces of one rule (pay, or short it at a risk).
- 2d12 already has AP and Frequencies; the stated objection to a further pool is tracking, not the idea.
