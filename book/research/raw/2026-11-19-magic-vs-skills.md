# Magic that outshines the non-magical outside a fight: the diagnosis and what games did

2026-11-19

## The question

Lucas, about to design magic outside combat: "This is a huge can of worms, with the biggest problem being solving everything with magic, or martials feeling lame etc." The first report of today (`2026-11-19-out-of-combat-magic.md`, section 6) found the shapes of out-of-combat magic and did not find this complaint on any page it opened. This report fills that gap: the diagnosis, the mechanisms, what games did and how it went.

**These are other games' rules and other people's opinions, never 2d12's rules. Nothing here is a design.**

## 1. The diagnosis

**The gap is about breadth outside the fight, not damage.** The 3.5 tier list, as copied [checked, EN World post 5663167 and 2d4chan's "Tier System"; the original not opened]: Tier 1 is "Capable of doing absolutely everything, often better than classes that specialize in that thing. Often capable of solving encounters with a single mechanical ability and little thought from the player." Tier 4 is "Capable of doing one thing quite well, but often useless when encounters require other areas of expertise". Tier 5 adds that "DMs may have to work to avoid the player feeling that their character is worthless". The scale never measures damage; it measures how many kinds of problem a class can answer. The wiki's gloss [checked]: the list measures "general ability to get shit done, rather than pure, balls-out combat power".

**"Quadratic" now means new verbs, not bigger numbers.** bert1001 [checked, EN World, 2022]: martials "mostly get more of the same but bigger", casters "open up wildly new areas of power"; the wizard can "bend reality in many ways they couldn't at 1st or 3rd level to outright bypass problems". Celebrim, same thread, names the remaining problem "problem-solving ability and utility".

**The outcome, not the process, is what a niche protects.** Alexander Macris (designer of ACKS) [checked, "On Niche Protection, Part II", 2022]: a niche fails when the designer looks at "the task process and not on the task outcome". If Rinaldo alone can climb but everyone else can fly, "Rinaldo's niche has been destroyed". "You have a chance to pick a lock? I just use knock. You have a chance to hide in shadows? I just cast invisibility." He calls solving it "the bugbear of five generations of game designers".

**Substitutability.** Sable [checked, "Attractors and caster/martial disparity", 2022] defines the superiority exactly: a caster can substitute for a martial across the usual challenges and the reverse is not true. Seven challenge areas are named (combat, social, logistics and survival, information, security, puzzles, the unique); magic reaches all seven. The companion post [checked] walks Pathfinder 1e area by area: Charm Person against Diplomacy ("a good 20% of social encounters in published adventures can be immediately solved by it"), Invisibility against Stealth, flight against Climb and Swim, and the security point that a defender must close every way in while a caster needs one (teleport, telekinesis on the handle, a summoned helper, charming the owner).

**It is not mainly a combat complaint, and not about rivalry.** Jiggy [checked, Paizo forum, "Dispelling Myths: The Caster-Martial Disparity"]: "for any given non-combat task, the magical solution is typically faster and more likely to succeed than the nonmagical solution (if a nonmagical solution even exists at all), and at a relatively trivial cost". On why it matters with friendly players: a player ends up choosing between "stay true to my concept but mostly be a spectator until something needs its HP depleted" and engaging the game at the cost of the concept.

**The image.** "Angel Summoner and the BMX Bandit. Together, they fight crime!" [checked in use, EN World, "PC exceptionalism"; the origin, a Mitchell and Webb sketch, is memory]. The reply there: "at some point there is a very real exchange rate between mechanical agency and narrative agency."

**The spell list, by skill.** 5e's Knock [read, SRD]: a target "held shut by a mundane lock or that is stuck or barred becomes unlocked, unstuck, or unbarred", no roll; its one cost is "a loud knock, audible from as far away as 300 feet". Pass without Trace [read]: "+10 bonus to Dexterity (Stealth) checks" for everyone chosen within 30 feet. Comprehend Languages [read] is a ritual: no slot. A GM's guide [checked, The Arcane Library] names the lists to watch: divination and enchantment for mysteries and NPCs (Zone of Truth, Speak with Animals, Charm Person), and travel (Dimension Door, scrying plus Teleport).

## 2. Why it happens, by mechanism

1. **The spell is certain where the skill rolls.** Giant Octopodes [checked, EN World]: "Knock requires no check and bypasses the barrier regardless of type or DC", while the lockpick can fail and jam; magic "will completely oblivate the need for a check, or provide such strong bonuses as to render the check effectively meaningless".
2. **One need a day.** Celebrim [checked]: at-will looks better than once a day in theory, but "in a typical adventuring day you probably only encounter one need for stealth, or one need to climb". A slot covers it, so "the skill has no use"; in AD&D 2e "it was almost always an upgrade to replace the thief with another caster".
3. **No opportunity cost.** Shiroiken [checked]: the attrition 5e expects "is not the most common form of play"; casters spend slots outside fights and "still have plenty of spell slots left". Macris [checked]: a once-a-day power only balances a constant one "when the game provides mechanical or narrative reasons why the time horizons cannot be manipulated".
4. **Breadth chosen daily.** Michael Sayre of Paizo [checked, quoted on Paizo's forum from his 2023 posts]: "if a character has the potential to do anything and a goal of your game is balance, it must be assumed that the character will do all those things". "It's ultimately not possible to have the same class provide both endless possibilities and a balanced experience".
5. **Magic is general by default.** Sable [checked]: "If the designers do not carefully think about what their magic can do, it will be capable of anything whatsoever." Kevin Crawford [lead, a search summary of his 2019 Reddit AMA]: limits must be specified for magic "in a way you don't for a swordsman or a master thief", because nobody has an intuition for what sorcery cannot do.
6. **Magic is exclusive.** Sable [checked]: a character is magical or not; the non-magical cannot sense, analyze or answer magic, so a magical problem has one kind of solver.
7. **Skills were written timidly, spells boldly.** Celebrim [checked]: designers "aired on the side of caution" with skills and feats, "binary pass/fail mechanics for getting through DM created gates", and imported spells whole. The Know Direction column [checked] puts it as martials designed with "no magic means real physics".
8. **Casters also have the skills.** Sable's count for Pathfinder 1e [checked]: skill access is about equal, and the wizard is above average. "All martial classes in D&D need to be skill monkeys" (Celebrim).
9. **It drifts back.** Sable [checked] calls the disparity an attractor: each new book adds spells, so a fixed game slides toward it again.

## 3. What games did, and how it went

**D&D 4e.** [memory] Utility powers for every class; non-combat magic moved into rituals (a feat, gold, ten minutes); skill challenges where every skill can count. Sable's verdict [checked]: these changes "did, in fact, solve C/MD", at a price he would not pay. The price as others saw it: OldeGreybeard [checked] says the split made it look as if combat spells were all a wizard had; the rituals went unused (already in `out-of-combat-magic.md`; a poster's report that Wizards "think people are not using rituals much and are thinking about new ways to price them" [checked as a report, EN World 2011]). **Martial Practices** (Martial Power 2, 2010), rituals for martials: Critical Hits' review [checked]: "I like the idea in theory", but some "overlap clearly into arcane territory". gribble [checked, EN World 2009] gives the sharper objection: a feat, 50 gp and a healing surge for a +5 to a disguise, and once the practice exists the GM must rule either that it is the only way or that it is worthless; the first choice has "reduced the options available to PCs outside of combat". This is the permission failure `roleplay-skills.md` already lists.

**D&D 5e.** Ritual tags make some utility free of slots [read: Comprehend Languages]. 2024 added Tactical Mind for the fighter: "Spend Second Wind to add 1d10 to a failed ability check; retain the use of Second Wind if you still fail" [checked, RPGBot's summary]; the rogue's Reliable Talent moved from level 11 to 7 [lead]. Knock and Pass without Trace kept their effects [memory]. Critics say the skill gap narrowed and the verb gap stayed [lead].

**Pathfinder 2e.** The clearest "magic helps the skill" rules.
- Knock [read]: "grants a +4 status bonus to any creature that tries to open the target door, lock, or container with an Athletics or a Thievery check." The caster may try the check, and the rogue with the spell on the lock is better than either alone.
- Incapacitation [read]: such a spell is one degree weaker against a creature of more than twice its rank.
- Rarity [read, GM Core]: uncommon options "might have unusual capabilities with large ramifications for the campaign setting or the types of narrative moments common in a heroic fantasy game"; raise dead is the example; Interplanar Teleport is tagged Uncommon [read]. The GM grants them.
- Skills go superhuman at the top. Legendary Sneak [read]: "You can Hide and Sneak even without cover or being concealed." Legendary Negotiation [read]: three actions to stop a fight and open talks.
- How it went: Ascalaphus [checked, Paizo 2023]: a utility spell rarely solves a hazard now; "the DC is gonna be roughly the same and your bonus is gonna be roughly the same". Many Paizo threads say casters, wizards above all, feel weak [lead: titles such as "4 years of PF 2: Wizards are weak"]. Sable [checked] expects the gap to return as books add spells. Skill feats read as permission (`roleplay-skills.md`).

**Worlds Without Number** [read, "New Spell Criteria" and the section on importing spells]. The principle in print: "Spells should not substitute for other classes. If a spell gives a mage the combat prowess of a Warrior or substitutes for an arbitrary selection of skills, it's too much." And by table: "If you have a stealthy, sneaky Expert in the party, don't let the wizard add spells that let them replicate or better that talent." **It rejects the Pathfinder fix:** "Spells should not offer simple numeric bonuses... Magic is meant to create impossible effects and alter the ground rules of a situation. If it simply amplifies the existing abilities of the group, it changes nothing in actual play"; a spell that makes you "10% better is a very boring spell". Also: no petty magic, buffs no longer than a scene (a day only for those whose "purpose is to make an adventure possible"), and mages "are intended to find themselves in some situations where their magic is simply not relevant". On the skill side: tasks "common to their background never require a skill check"; no roll where failure "would make the PC seem notably incompetent at their role in life".

**13th Age** [read, SRD]: every character gets 8 background points in broad player-written backgrounds, added to any check they fit; a ritual needs a background check (first report).

**Draw Steel** [read]. Perks "give them heroic moments outside of combat" for every class, in six groups (crafting, exploration, interpersonal, intrigue, lore, supernatural). "Perks are special because they allow a hero to attempt a specific task without a test"; except for supernatural perks, the Director "can always allow a hero to attempt a mundane task mentioned in a perk by making a test". So certainty is given to the non-magical too. In a montage test a clever item or ability earns automatic successes toward a shared total, and a hero "can't do anything else as part of the montage test until each other hero involved" has acted: magic is a contribution to the count, and turns are forced. A word search of the Negotiation chapter found no rule for spells or psionics changing a negotiation (not read line by line). Lucas's own view that many perks are over the top is in `roleplay-skills.md`.

**Blades in the Dark** [read, Rituals]: the Ritual ability is needed; each ritual is found, then learned as a long project; it costs a downtime activity and stress by magnitude; the GM may tick a clock for what it stirs; the forces "cannot be considered a reliable or safe source of power". Magic pays in the currencies everyone uses (stress, downtime, coin). [memory] Attune is an action any scoundrel has.

**Fabula Ultima** (first report): rituals may never replicate "the mechanical benefits of an existing spell or Skill". **Daggerheart** [read, SRD errata line]: a PC "can spend a Hope to add a relevant Experience's modifier"; Experiences are player-written and every class has them.

**From memory only, unchecked.** Shadow of the Demon Lord: professions as descriptors for everyone, few castings a spell. Shadowrun: every spell risks drain; technology rivals magic; anyone can perceive some magic (Sable says so [checked]). Earthdawn: every adept is magical, so there is no mundane class. Exalted and wuxia games: the martial's non-combat feats are mythic by right. Ars Magica: the troupe, where each player has a magus and a companion and takes turns (Macris praises it as the way it "could maintain operational differences" [checked]). The One Ring, Forbidden Lands, Dragonbane, Mythras: magic is rare, costly or risky, so skills carry play. Burning Wheel: sorcery is a skill test with tax. Savage Worlds: powers cost points; Edges are the non-caster's permanent tricks. Dungeon World: the Ritual move always works at a price the GM names. Old-school D&D: scarcity (few slots, a spell must be found); Macris notes thieves were still judged unnecessary.

## 4. Does it matter outside a fight, and the opposite failure

- **"It is the GM's job."** Dragonblade [checked, EN World 2012] describes the rotating spotlight (each class shines in its scene) and its flaw: it "is very dependent on how well the DM, the adventure itself, and the players can smoothly shift the spotlight"; in a session of talk the fighter "might as well go play Nintendo". Sable [checked] files "Rule Zero" under faulty solutions: the GM can fix it, but the "vast majority of the GMs have neither the experience, nor the time, nor the inclination". He also lists why a table may never see the gap: few sessions, modest casters, a GM who tilts without noticing, players who do not mind.
- **"The caster never has the right spell."** Maxperson [checked]: critics argue from a "white room"; slots spent on utility are lost to combat. Sayre [checked] answers for the designer: the game must assume the potential is used.
- **Equal impact, not equal power.** Umbran [checked, quoted in a reply]: the point is "to have all characters equally able to impact the course of fictional events, which is not the same thing" as equal reality-altering power.
- **Magic hedged until nobody uses it.** 4e's rituals (first report). Pathfinder 2e's caster complaints above. Crawford's own warning that a spell which only adds a bonus "changes nothing in actual play". Sable on hostile limits (the stolen spellbook, the lynch mob): such an approach "directly targets player fun".
- **Martials turned into casters.** Celebrim [checked]: fixes that give fighters "time limited powers to impose reliable narration" solve it "by erasing martial classes". Sable on Path of War: "anime fighters" for some tables.

## 5. Non-magical characters feeling "lame"

- **What hurts** [checked, Sable]: "people like to feel useful"; useless for one task is tolerable, "useless for an entire session, that feels really bad". Jiggy: the spectator. Tier 5: "one trick ponies".
- **Being good without paying.** Shiroiken [checked] wants martials "more helpful in non-combat situations without spending resources (which is generally what martials are supposed to do)". Rules that deliver it: Worlds Without Number's no-roll rule for one's own trade [read]; 5e's Reliable Talent [memory: no roll under 10]; Draw Steel's perks without a test [read].
- **Things only they do.** Celebrim's list of skills the fighter never got [checked]: porter, running, endurance, tactics, command, demolition. Macris: "qualitative differences are the most important and valuable weapon" for a niche. Legendary Sneak [read].
- **Strength that is theirs.** Sable [checked]: power from items "can feel quite lame"; it "does not feel 'inherent'".
- **Simple and dependable reads as strong.** Sayre [checked]: the class with "raw power to do a basic function well with a minimal amount of technical skill" will "feel more powerful" than the versatile one.

## What seems to transfer

### (a) The mechanisms, as a checklist to test a magic rule against

1. Does the effect reach an **outcome a skill also reaches** (past the door, unseen, up the wall, the truth out of him)? (Macris: outcome, not process.)
2. Is it **certain** where the skill rolls? (Knock.)
3. Is its bonus so large that the roll is decided? (+10.)
4. Does it cover the **one need of the day**, so the at-will skill never gets its turn? (Celebrim.)
5. Is anything given up to cast it: does the resource come back before it is missed? (Shiroiken, Macris.)
6. Can the caster **change the toolkit** between problems? (Sayre.)
7. Does it **remove the scene** (skip the journey, the lock, the negotiation) or change how the scene is played?
8. Can it fail, and does failing cost what a failed skill costs?
9. Can a non-caster perceive it, answer it, or help with it? (Sable: exclusivity.)
10. Does the caster have the same skills as well? (Sable's count.)
11. Does growth add a **new verb** to the caster and only a number to everyone else? (bert1001.)
12. Will every later spell be checked against this list? (Sable: the attractor.)

### (b) The fixes

1. **Magic adds to the skill roll.** Pathfinder 2e's Knock. Cost: reported as dull; Crawford rejects it outright ("changes nothing in actual play"); casters feel weak.
2. **Magic changes what is possible, never does what a skill does.** Worlds Without Number; Fabula Ultima's never-list. Cost: someone must judge each spell against the skill list; Crawford leaves it to the GM, table by table.
3. **A wall by area** (magic cannot touch one sphere, for example the social one). Sable's first option. Cost: it has to be a decision made early and held in every later spell.
4. **Narrow magic** (a theory of magic; few spells known). Sayre's kineticist; Sable's Avatar example. Cost: some players want the generalist under that name.
5. **Rarity on the scene-skippers** (teleport, raising the dead). Pathfinder 2e. Cost: all GM; called inconsistent [lead].
6. **Certainty for the non-magical too.** Draw Steel's perks; no-roll rules; Reliable Talent. Cost: Lucas already finds perks over the top; written as feats they become permission (4e's Martial Practices, Pathfinder's skill feats).
7. **Skills go mythic at the top.** Legendary skill feats; Exalted [memory]. Cost: "anime fighters" for some tables.
8. **Broad skills or backgrounds for everyone.** 13th Age, Daggerheart, Celebrim's "skill monkeys". Cost: free-form backgrounds are hard to balance; the caster gets them too.
9. **Magic outside combat open to all, paid in a shared currency.** 4e's rituals, Blades. Cost: disuse when the price competes with gear or the time idles the table.
10. **Magic is loud, slow or draws attention.** 5e's Knock at 300 feet; Blades' clocks; Shadowrun's drain [memory]. Cost: slides into punishing the caster.
11. **Structured scenes where magic is one contribution.** Draw Steel's montage; 4e's skill challenge. Cost: "the roll replacing play" (`roleplay-skills.md`).
12. **Everyone plays the wizard sometimes.** Ars Magica's troupe. Cost: a different kind of campaign.

### (c) What already resembles something in 2d12 (observations, from the project files)

- **`book/roleplay-skills.md`, proposals 1 and 2** ("a skill or Talent never grants permission"; "No certainty") answer the Martial Practices and skill-feat failure. Proposal 2 also means the non-magical side has no certainty; checklist item 2 then turns on whether magic outside a fight has it.
- **Proposal 5, Routine** (a Tier 2 without rolling when trained and unpressed) is the family of Worlds Without Number's no-roll rule and Reliable Talent: being good without spending.
- **Proposals 8 and 9** (Backgrounds as 1 Boon; Challenges with any approach) are 13th Age's backgrounds and Draw Steel's montage. Magic's place inside a Challenge is not written.
- **`book/magic-rules-draft.md`, section 3:** "A casting is always noticed" is fix 10 already decided, and `book/ideas/expedition-loop.md`'s Alert ("a loud event +1") is a place where being noticed has a number.
- **Section 9:** a spell with a duration is combat only. That removes the long buff (Pass without Trace, Fly, Invisibility as cast in a fight) from outside play before any out-of-combat rule exists. `book/items-economy.md`, Part 2, item 14 ("It can change the *circumstances* of a fight... not its *numbers*") is Crawford's sentence nearly word for word.
- **Section 9, the Surge pool:** it refills when the party gains Momentum, and `book/ideas/expedition-loop.md` item 3 gives Momentum for any encounter overcome, "fight or not". The price of out-of-combat magic is then earned by everyone's play, skills included, and does not return with a night's sleep: this bears on checklist item 5.
- **Section 2:** "A level is never a number on a roll or a DC" closes Pathfinder's large-bonus route (item 3) by an existing decision. "A Magic Talent or a class opens one Path and one School" means magic is not a birthright (Sable's exclusivity), and few spells known is fix 4.
- **Section 2 again:** "Spells can be swapped on a Rest" is the daily toolkit of checklist item 6, in a smaller size.
- **Section 10, the Limits of Magic,** is a never-list, the tool Fabula Ultima and Crawford use; none of its three limits is about skills yet.
- **`items-economy.md`, Part 2, item 13** names Truespeech, Message, Light and Water-breathing as lasting effects: Truespeech is the Comprehend Languages case of section 1, Water-breathing the "makes an adventure possible" kind Crawford allows for a day.
