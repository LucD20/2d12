## Part 1. D&D 4e: what the rules say [READ unless tagged]

### 1.1 The Paladin's frame (PHB p.89)
- Role text: "Defender. You are extremely durable, with high hit points and the ability to wear the heaviest armor. You can issue bold challenges to foes and compel them to fight you rather than your allies."
- Key abilities: **Strength, Charisma, Wisdom** (three), and Constitution sets surges. HP 15 + Con score at level 1, +6 a level. **Healing surges 10 + Con modifier** a day. +1 Fortitude, Reflex and Will. Plate and heavy shields from level 1.
- Class features: Channel Divinity, Divine Challenge, Lay on Hands. Divine Power lets Lay on Hands be swapped for Ardent Vow or Virtue's Touch.
- Four builds in the final text: Avenging (Str, two-hander, damage), Protecting (Cha, shield, healing and bolstering), Ardent (Str/Wis, "more like a striker than other paladins"), Virtuous (Cha/Wis, saves and conditions).

### 1.2 The mark
- **Marked** (PHB p.277): -2 to attack rolls for any attack that doesn't include the marker; one mark at a time, a new one replaces the old; it ends if the marker falls unconscious. The glossary adds the design note: "Most marking effects have very short durations, or else they require the marking creature to remain a threat to the marked target."
- **Divine Challenge**, final wording (updated 2010-11-17, "revised for clarity and ease of use"): At-Will, **Minor Action, Close burst 5**, one creature. You mark it; the mark lasts until you use the power again. "The mark also ends at the end of your turn if you didn't engage the target, meaning you neither attacked it during your turn nor are adjacent to it at the end of your turn." While marked, "the target takes radiant damage the first time each round when it targets any of your allies with an attack power that doesn't include you as a target": **3 + Cha modifier** (6 + Cha at 11th, 9 + Cha at 21st). Once per turn. The damage needs **no attack roll and no action from the paladin**.
- **Divine Challenge**, the earlier wording (seen in a GitHub code-search result that quotes the older text): the same damage, plus: "On your turn, you must engage the target you challenged or challenge a different target... If none of these events occur by the end of your turn, the marked condition ends and **you can't use divine challenge on your next turn**." The 2010 update dropped the lost-turn penalty. Its Special line: "it doesn't rely on the intelligence or language ability of the target. It's a magical compulsion".
- **Divine Sanction** (Divine Power p.82): a mark placed by powers and feats, for the duration that power states. Same damage as Divine Challenge (3 / 6 / 9 + Cha, first time each round it attacks without including you). "Divine sanction is meant to complement divine challenge. You can use divine challenge to mark one creature and use divine sanction to mark others. Divine sanction has fewer restrictions than divine challenge so that you can easily use the two in concert." **No engagement clause.**
- Sanction sources read: **Ardent Strike** (at-will, Str or Cha vs AC, 1[W] + mod, sanction until the end of your next turn; usable on a charge); **Call of Challenge** (utility 2, encounter, minor action, close burst 3, every enemy in it is sanctioned until the end of your next turn); **Ardent Vow** (below).
- **Mighty Challenge** (feat, Divine Power p.135): Divine Challenge's damage gains + Str modifier. (It names Divine Challenge only.)

### 1.3 The other defenders' punishments, for comparison
- **Fighter, Combat Challenge**: immediate interrupt; when an enemy marked by you is **adjacent** and shifts or attacks someone else, you make a **melee basic attack** against it. (The fighter marks whatever it attacks: [MEMORY].)
- **Warden**: Warden's Fury (immediate interrupt, Str vs Fortitude, 1[W] + Str, and the target grants combat advantage to everyone until the end of your next turn) or Warden's Grasp (immediate reaction, burst 5: slide 1, slowed, cannot shift). (Marks every adjacent enemy as a free action each turn: [MEMORY].)
- **Swordmage**: Aegis of Shielding (minor, burst 2; if the marked target hits someone else within 10 squares, immediate interrupt: reduce the damage by 5 + Con) or Aegis of Assault (teleport next to it and make a melee basic attack).
- **Knight / Cavalier, Defender Aura** (Essentials): an aura 1; any enemy in it takes -2 to attacks that don't include you. No mark.
- Reading the four side by side: the Paladin's punishment is the only one that is **automatic, at range 5, and costs no action**; it is also the **smallest hit** and the only one tied to a "stay engaged" duty. The Swordmage's Shielding aegis is the closest thing in 4e to "soft tank": it does not take the hit, it shaves it.

### 1.4 Lay on Hands and the leader side
- **Lay on Hands**: at-will power, **minor action**, melee touch, usable **Wisdom modifier times a day (minimum 1), once a round**. "You spend a healing surge but regain no hit points. Instead, the target regains hit points as if it had spent a healing surge. You must have at least one healing surge remaining."
  - So the heal costs the paladin's own daily staying power, not the ally's; it works on an ally who has no surges left; and its size is the **ally's** surge value (a quarter of the ally's HP: [MEMORY]).
- **Ardent Vow** (replaces Lay on Hands; Wis modifier times a day, minor, touch): the next attack against the target deals + (5 + Wis modifier), and "whenever you attack the target before the end of the encounter, it is subject to your divine sanction until the end of your next turn". This is the trade the book offers: **give up the healing to become a single-target hunter**.
- **Virtue's Touch** (replaces Lay on Hands; Wis modifier times a day): remove one of blinded, dazed, deafened, slowed, stunned, weakened.
- **Channel Divinity** (one use per encounter, whichever you pick): **Divine Mettle** (minor, burst 10: one creature makes a saving throw with + your Cha modifier) or **Divine Strength** (minor: + Str modifier damage on your next attack this turn). A support option and a small burst option share one slot.
- At-wills (level 1): **Enfeebling Strike** (Cha vs AC, 1[W] + Cha; "If you marked the target, it takes a -2 penalty to attack rolls until the end of your next turn"); **Bolstering Strike** (Cha vs AC, 1[W] + Cha, you gain temporary hit points = Wis modifier); **Holy Strike** (Str vs AC, 1[W] + Str radiant, + Wis modifier damage if you marked the target); **Valiant Strike** (Str vs AC, + 1 to the attack roll per adjacent enemy); **Virtuous Strike** (Divine Power: Cha vs AC, radiant, +2 to your saving throws; counts as a melee basic attack); **Ardent Strike** (above).
  - Note the pattern: two PHB at-wills pay **more when the target is the one you marked** (Enfeebling, Holy Strike). The mark is also the Paladin's own damage and debuff key.
  - Note the split: half attack with Strength, half with Charisma, and riders want Wisdom.

### 1.5 Essentials: the Cavalier (Heroes of the Forgotten Kingdoms)
- **Defender Aura** (aura 1, -2 to attacks that don't include you) + **Righteous Radiance**: **opportunity action**, melee 1; trigger: an enemy in the aura **shifts** or attacks an ally and not you; effect: **3 + Cha modifier radiant** (6 / 9 + Cha at 11th / 21st). No attack roll.
- **Holy Smite**: encounter, **free action**, trigger "You target an enemy with an at-will weapon attack power"; the target takes **2 + Cha radiant** (4 / 7 / 10 + Cha at levels 7 / 17 / 27), and "If the triggering attack hits, the target is also dazed until the end of your next turn". Declared when you target, so before the roll; the damage lands **even on a miss**. Extra uses come at levels 3 and 13 (the class's level list names "Level 3 Extra Holy Smite", "Level 13 Extra Holy Smite").
- **Righteous Shield**: encounter, immediate interrupt, burst 3; trigger: an ally within 3 is damaged by an attack. "You take the damage of the triggering attack instead of the target. This damage ignores your immunities and resistances. In addition, you gain a +2 power bonus to attack rolls until the end of your next turn."
- **Spirit of Sacrifice**: +1 healing surge, and you may use your second wind as a minor action on an ally within 5: you gain nothing, the ally regains a surge's worth. **Restore Vitality** (utility 2, daily): the target regains your surge value and makes a saving throw.
- Avatar of Righteous Nobility (daily 15): the aura and the Radiance's range become 3 for the encounter.

### 1.6 Essentials: the Blackguard (Heroes of Shadow), the striker paladin
- **Dark Menace**: + Cha modifier damage on weapon attacks against an enemy granting you combat advantage.
- **Dread Smite**: the same free-action trigger as Holy Smite; 2 + Cha cold and necrotic (4 / 7 / 10 + Cha), and on a hit ongoing 5 (save ends; 10 at 17th, 15 at 27th).
- **Shroud of Shadow** (encounter, minor): partial concealment and 5 temporary hit points (10 / 15).
- **Spirit of Vice**: Domination ("Once per round while you have temporary hit points, you can take a free action to deal damage to yourself right before making an attack roll... equals your Charisma modifier. You then gain a bonus to the damage roll... equals the damage you took") or Fury (+2 damage with combat advantage, +4 while you are bloodied or next to a bloodied creature).
- **Vengeance Strike** (at-will): 1[W] + Str, + twice the number of adjacent enemies to damage (max +8). It keeps plate and stands in the crowd; its damage comes from **being surrounded** and from **paying its own buffer**.

### 1.7 The Avenger (PHB2), the divine striker
- **Oath of Enmity**: encounter (special), minor action, close burst 10, one enemy you can see. "When you make a melee attack against the target and the target is the **only enemy adjacent to you**, you make two attack rolls and use either result." It lasts the encounter or until the target drops, "at which point you regain the use of this power".
- **Armor of Faith**: +3 AC in cloth or no armor, with no shield.
- **Censure** (pick one): Pursuit (if the oath target moves away willingly, + 2 + Dex modifier damage against it until the end of your next turn; 4 / 6 + Dex later); Retribution (when any **other** enemy hits you, + Int modifier damage against the oath target until the end of your next turn, **cumulative**); Unity (+1 damage per ally adjacent to the target).
- Reading: the Avenger is "I pick one enemy, and everything that tries to stop the duel makes it worse for him": the enemy runs (Pursuit pays), the enemy's friends hit you (Retribution pays). It is a mark turned inside out: the reward is on the Avenger's own attack, not a penalty on the enemy.

### 1.8 Divine against profane
- **Turn Undead** (Cleric, Channel Divinity, encounter): close burst 2 (3 / 5), **each undead** in it: Wis vs Will, 1d10 + Wis radiant (half on a miss), push 3 + Cha modifier squares, immobilized until the end of your next turn. A whole power that only works on one creature type.
- The Avenger's features name "abjure undead" as its Channel Divinity. (Its text was not read.)
- In 4e the divine-vs-profane lever is mostly a **keyword pair**, not a class rule: the divine classes deal radiant damage, and the monster books give most undead "vulnerable radiant" (a flat extra, e.g. vulnerable 5) [SNIPPET: dnd4.fandom.com Undead and Radiant pages, a GiantITP excerpt thread, an EN World thread]. Radiant still hurts everything else, so the divine character is never locked out. All of the Paladin's mark damage is radiant, so its punishment is larger against undead for free.

## Part 2. D&D 4e: what players say [SNIPPET: seen only as search excerpts]

### 2.1 Why the launch Paladin was called a weak defender
- GiantITP, "4e defenders, Who's Who among tanks": posters called the Divine Challenge "barely a deterrent"; the Fighter was most often ranked first, the Paladin drew the most criticism; a poster credited Divine Power as a major upgrade because the "Divine Challenge/Sanction response is action-free". https://forums.giantitp.com/archive/index.php/t-108242.html
- GiantITP, "Playing a Paladin": "Divine Challenge, no matter how good you make it, is really only affecting one target"; and "the punishment power of their marking ability is fairly weak in comparison" to the class's other defensive tools; a Strength paladin "will have a harder time being the only defender, especially for a party of more than 4". https://forums.giantitp.com/archive/index.php/t-320767.html
- Invincible Ink, "4e: The Paladin's Engagement": automatic no-action damage needed a drawback, and the one chosen was that "you need to stay **engaged** with the target"; this gives the enemy an out, "getting too far away from you"; and the Paladin, the one PHB class starting in full plate (which slows you), finds engagement "a lot harder when you're moving in 5s and your opponent is moving in 7s". https://press.invincible.ink/4e-the-paladins-engagement/
- Invincible Ink, "4e: The Paladin's Plight": Divine Power's Divine Sanction "addressed the problem of enemies dragging the Paladin around the field". https://press.invincible.ink/4e-the-paladins-plight/
- EN World, "paladin divine challenge" and neighbors: core-only, Divine Challenge "isn't particularly spectacular"; charging was the usual way to keep up with a challenged target. https://www.enworld.org/threads/paladin-divine-challenge.273288
- The ability split: a critique says the Paladin "has one bad problem, which it shares with the Cleric and the Warlock: split-stat dependency", about half the attack powers using Strength and half Charisma (source: one of Tribality part five / the Evil GM blog; the search did not make clear which). The Evil GM blog calls it the most multi-attribute-dependent class in 4e and notes "Lay on Hands is limited by wisdom". https://theevilgm.wordpress.com/2012/02/28/armed-and-armored-exploring-the-4e-paladin/ ; https://www.tribality.com/2015/04/23/the-paladin-class-part-five/
- RPG.net, "[4E] Paladins": the advice is to pick Strength or Charisma and max it; a Charisma-only paladin is "not only viable but often preferred to its strength brother" (this wording came through a search summary, possibly from GiantITP rather than this thread). https://forum.rpg.net/index.php?threads/4e-paladins.600209/

### 2.2 What fixed it
- The Paladin's Handbook ("Pillars of Faith (and Facestabs)", by Litigation): after errata the default mark is "perfectly clear and actually even buffed a little bit from its original form"; Mighty Challenge "makes a Straladin's Divine Challenge viable, and a Baladin's pretty damn dangerous"; and "The best part, however, is the ability to Divine Challenge two enemies" (a later feat or path). https://www.enworld.org/threads/pillars-of-faith-and-facestabs-the-paladins-handbook-by-litigation.469141/
- EN World: a poster says they can "consistently tie down at least two opponents every turn" with Divine Challenge plus Ardent Strike's Sanction. https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=5042950 (thread "Divine Challenge=Divine Sanction?")
- EN World, "Cleaner post-DP Paladin?": others call the two overlapping marks a kludge and want them merged. https://www.enworld.org/threads/cleaner-post-dp-paladin.263958/
- Invincible Ink ("The Paladin's Engagement" or "Plight"): after the supplements the Paladin is "one of the best and most interesting types of Defenders you can make".
- An effectiveness guide to the defenders: "The paladin is not particularly sticky, but at the same time it is impossible for an enemy marked by a paladin to avoid punishment for ignoring the mark." https://itchyfish.com/effectiveness-guide-to-4th-edition-dd-defenders/
- My summary of the fix [judgment]: three things. A second, lighter mark with no upkeep, attached to attacks and to a burst (many targets). One attack stat allowed per build (Ardent Strike takes Str or Cha). And a way to add the unused stat to the punishment (Mighty Challenge).

### 2.3 Ranked against the Fighter, Warden, Swordmage
- RPG.net, "[4th ed] How do the defenders stack up?": by options, Fighter and Paladin first, then Warden, Swordmage, Battlemind, with Cavalier and Knight last; but "neither the Paladin nor the Warden comes close to a Fighter". The Warden "might be the stickiest defender we have, if not the most damaging". https://forum.rpg.net/index.php?threads/4th-ed-how-do-the-defenders-stack-up.704924/
- RPG.net, "[4e] Defenders, what's good what's not?": Fighters "are not as supportive of allies as Paladins, but they do more damage and handle swarms better"; the Fighter is the best all-round, the Paladin and Swordmage "good, interesting options". https://forum.rpg.net/threads/4e-defenders-whats-good-whats-not.691051/
- The fighter is "the stickiest of defenders" because it marks by attacking and punishes with a real attack; a Shielding Swordmage "has no ability to punish enemies that ignore a mark, but can defend allies that are attacked when a mark is ignored" (the itchyfish guide, above).
- The pattern across threads [judgment]: the Paladin is rated the **most supportive and the least sticky**; its punishment is certain but small; its weak point is a crowd.

### 2.4 Lay on Hands and the leader secondary
- EN World, "Paladin and Lay on Hands": a commenter calls it "retroactive defending" (it heals the damage the defender failed to stop). https://www.enworld.org/threads/paladin-and-lay-on-hands.230282/
- EN World, "Healing Hands + Devoted Paladin = uber healing?": with the feats, "+10-12 hp on a LoH pretty much doubles the surge value of most non-defenders"; others answer that the feats are not an efficient buy for a defender. https://www.enworld.org/threads/healing-hands-devoted-paladin-uber-healing.260897/
- Tribality part five: Lay on Hands is the one feature in every edition's paladin; in 4e the paladin spends the surge and the target gets the hit points; uses hang on Wisdom and surges on Constitution, so the class wants four scores. https://www.tribality.com/2015/04/23/the-paladin-class-part-five/
- The Evil GM blog: the class is "often built as a hyper-defender or defender+leader".
- No first-hand table report on how the surge cost *feels* came up. What the rules make certain [judgment from the READ text]: the paladin has the largest surge pool in the PHB frame (10 + Con), so the heal is a transfer from the character with the most to the character with the least; and it is a minor action, so it never costs the paladin's attack.

### 2.5 The Cavalier and the Blackguard
- Square Fireballs: the Cavalier is the Knight crossed with the old Paladin; Righteous Radiance is the Knight's Battle Guardian merged with Divine Challenge; it "can't spike damage nearly as high as even a melee basic attack"; the Radiance needs an opportunity action, so a dazed Cavalier loses it; a level 1 Cavalier makes one build choice. https://squarefireballs.blogspot.com/2010/11/essentials-paladin-defenders-aura-shift.html
- Blog of Holding, "A Cavalier Analysis of the Cavalier": positive; "the auto damage does go off if the enemy shifts, so it gives the Paladin some nice stickiness"; Holy Smite is clearly better than the Knight's Power Strike; likes Righteous Shield. https://www.blogofholding.com/?p=1967
- The Cavalier's Guide ("Holy Riders", Litigation): "However tepid Righteous Radiance's damage is, the Cavalier redeems himself as a defender with this excellent encounter feature" (Righteous Shield, a "martyr-style" power). https://www.enworld.org/threads/holy-riders-the-cavaliers-guide-by-litigation.469135/
- GiantITP, "Cavalier's Righteous Radiance: How would you fix it?": a thread premised on the Radiance doing too little. https://forums.giantitp.com/archive/index.php/t-228522.html
- RPG.net "[4E] Paladins": the Cavalier is "a weaker defender than the original Paladin but some people consider it easier to play".
- Blackguard: a Heroes of Shadow review calls it "the most durable striker I've ever seen" (https://daegames.blogspot.com/2011/04/heroes-of-shadow-review.html); the RPG.net review likes Vengeance Strike scaling with adjacent enemies (https://www.rpg.net/reviews/archive/15/15325.phtml). There is a Blackguard guide, "Tainted Faith" (https://www.enworld.org/threads/tainted-faith-the-blackguards-guide.471429/), not seen.

### 2.6 The Avenger
- GiantITP "[4e] Avenger Essentials" and "Oath of enmity.": the oath is the class's best feature and also carries the whole class; it "loses in damage output what it makes up in accuracy"; the designers are said to have overvalued the double roll. https://giantitp.com/forums/archive/index.php/t-134087.html ; https://forums.giantitp.com/archive/index.php/t-203324.html
- EN World "Avenger Oath of Enmity... how cool": praise for the flavor as some of 4e's best. https://www.enworld.org/threads/avenger-oath-of-enmity-how-cool.310147/
- Minor Actions blog: the oath takes tactical movement and bookkeeping to keep up, hard for a new player. http://minoractions.blogspot.com/2009/03/so-you-want-to-make-avenger-huh.html
- Reading [judgment]: players love "never miss my chosen enemy" as a feeling, and say reliability reads as weaker than big numbers even when the math is fine.

### 2.7 What makes a punishing defender fun
- RPG.net, "[D&D 4e] Defenders and their Mark": the choice is the DM's, "always a deliberate and calculated one", and the mark exists "to force the DM to make some difficult tactical choices for a change"; the mark-breaker "can either chase after you (probably provoking OAs on the way), or try to hit one of your allies and doing piddly damage"; "everyone who has played a 4e defender for long has seen their marks obeyed and disobeyed"; it "does very much depend on the DM and their choice of tactics". https://forum.rpg.net/index.php?threads/d-d-4e-defenders-and-their-mark.465144/
- The Swordmage handbook and "Ultimate Defenders Ho!": defending is "giving your enemies no good choices, and making them choose between them"; and "+2 to defenses is as much a punishment for attacking the defender as the mark's -2 penalty is to attack the defender's allies" (high defenses are half of the trap). https://www.enworld.org/threads/masters-of-blade-magic-a-swordmage-handbook-by-herid_fel.469143/ ; http://www.dyasdesigns.com/dragon_phoenix/Ultimate%20Defenders%20Ho!%20_%20The%20Wizards%20Community.htm
- "The Art of Defending" (mkill; and "Mark 2"): stickiness defined as "how much incentive there is for your marked opponent to stay at where you want him to be and/or hit you instead of your friends". https://www.enworld.org/threads/the-art-of-defending-by-mkill.469247/ ; https://www.enworld.org/threads/the-art-of-defending-mark-2-d-d-4e.704739/
- Invincible Ink, "4e D&D: Marks Are Great": marks give reliable outcomes that don't hinge on persuading the DM; the fun is "choices about where you stand and what targets you care about". https://press.invincible.ink/4e-dd-marks-are-great/
- Takeaways [judgment]:
  1. A punishing defender is satisfied **both ways**: obeyed, the plan worked; disobeyed, the damage triggers. A punishment that needs a roll and can whiff removes the second pleasure; the Paladin's automatic one never does.
  2. A small certain punishment gets ignored by a DM once the squishy is worth more than the sting. The Paladin was called weak exactly there, and the fixes raised the sting or the number of targets, not the certainty.
  3. Upkeep that the enemy can break by walking away feels bad on the slowest class in the game.

## Part 3. 13th Age 1e: what the rules say [READ: 13 Vaults SRD copy, source "13th Age, p.110"]

### 3.1 The frame
- +2 to Strength or Charisma. HP (8 + Con modifier) x 3 at level 1. **8 recoveries**, recovery die d10. AC 16 in heavy armor (17 with a shield) + the middle of Con / Dex / Wis + level; PD 10; MD 12.
- Basic melee attack: Strength + level vs AC; hit WEAPON + Strength; **miss: damage equal to your level**.
- 3 talents at level 1, a 4th at level 5, a 5th at level 8. **No powers and no spells**: one class feature and the talents are the whole class.

### 3.2 Smite Evil (the only class feature)
- "You can use this talent **once per battle, plus an additional number of times per day equal to your Charisma modifier**."
- "As a free action **before you make a paladin melee attack roll**, you can declare that you're using a Smite Evil attack. Add **+1d12** to the damage roll AND deal **half damage with the attack if it misses**."
- Feats: Adventurer: "+4 attack bonus" on Smite Evil attacks. Champion: 2d12. Epic: 4d12.
- Notes [judgment]:
  - Nothing in the text says the target must be evil. The name is flavor.
  - Decided before the roll, but half damage on a miss means it is **never wasted**: the choice is "which enemy and which round", not a gamble.
  - The two pools are the nova: one use each battle for free, and Charisma-many extra uses a day that can all be spent in the fight that matters (one per attack roll).
  - The size shrinks with level. Weapon damage in 13th Age is one weapon die per level [MEMORY], so at level 1 a longsword hit is 1d8 + 4 (8.5) and Smite adds 6.5 (+76%); at level 5, 5d8 + 8 (30.5) and Smite adds 13 with the Champion feat (+43%), or 6.5 without it (+21%); at level 8, 8d8 + 12 (48) and 26 with the Epic feat (+54%). It only keeps up if you keep paying feats. This is the arithmetic behind "the least offense-oriented character in the party" (2.x below) and the 2e change (Part 5).

### 3.3 The talents
- **Bastion**: +1 AC. "Once per battle when a nearby ally is hit by an attack, you can choose to lose hit points equal to half of that damage, and have your ally take only half of the damage instead. The damage you lose can come from temporary hit points, but isn't affected by damage resistance and other tricks to avoid the damage." Feats: +1 recovery; once a day use it twice in a battle; Epic: "your ally takes no damage. You still lose hit points equal to half the damage."
  - It is a **split**, not a redirect: at base both take half; no action named, "nearby" (no adjacency), and it is chosen after the hit is known.
- **Paladin's Challenge**: "When you **hit** an enemy with a melee attack, you can choose to challenge that enemy as a free action. Until the end of the battle... you **each** take a -4 attack penalty against all other creatures and a -4 penalty to disengage checks from each other." The penalty switches off for the rest of the attacker's turn once it attacks its rival (so a multi-attack monster can hit you first and then your allies freely). One challenge at a time. It ends when either drops, when **you hit a different enemy**, or when the creature flees far away and you let it go. Feats: Adventurer: the enemy's penalty (not yours) is "-4 or the escalation die, whichever is higher"; Champion: two challenges at once; Epic: challenged enemies are vulnerable to your attacks.
  - A mark with **no damage at all**: pure penalty, and **symmetric**, the paladin is bound too.
- **Lay on Hands**: "Twice per day as a quick action, you can heal yourself or an ally next to you with a touch. You spend the recovery while the recipient heals as if they had spent the recovery." Feats: + twice your Cha modifier to the healing; Champion: it "uses a free recovery instead of one of your own"; Epic: four times a day.
  - The 4e design carried over whole (your resource, their heal, a quick action), with a feat that removes the cost.
- **Way of Evil Bastards**: "When one of your Smite Evil attacks **drops a non-mook enemy to 0 hp, that use of Smite Evil is not expended**." Champion: also when it drops three or more mooks. Cannot be taken with the Path below.
  - The refund-on-kill: the smite becomes a finisher you aim at the wounded enemy, and a chain if the dice allow.
- **Path of Universal Righteous Endeavor**: nearby allies +1 to all saves. Champion: all your attacks deal holy damage.
- **Fearless**: immune to fear; "+1 melee attack bonus against enemies that are **not engaged by any of your allies**. The bonus increases to +2 against enemies with fear abilities." (A reward for going in alone, the same idea as the Avenger's isolation.)
- **Implacable**: "You can roll saves at the **start** of your turn instead of at the end." Champion: once a turn, lose hit points equal to your level to reroll a save.
- **Divine Domain**: one cleric domain with its once-a-day invocation; may be taken twice. **Cleric Training**: one cleric spell; Champion: cast the cleric's Heal twice a day.
- For comparison, the Fighter's defender tools [READ]: **Threatening** (enemies take a penalty to disengage from you; with feats a failed disengage costs them damage and makes them vulnerable) and **Skilled Intercept** (once a round, a save of 11+ to step in front of an enemy moving to attack a nearby ally; in heavy armor you take half damage if it hits).

### 3.4 Smite Evil and the escalation die [judgment from the READ text]
- Smite Evil is **not gated** on the escalation die (unlike the fighter and others). The only link in the class is Paladin's Challenge's Adventurer feat (the penalty becomes the die when it passes 4, so at 5 or 6).
- Because the die adds to every PC attack roll, the two "make this one count" tools pull in opposite directions: the **+4 feat** matters most in rounds 1 and 2, when the die is 0 or 1 (it is a private escalation die), while **half damage on a miss** makes waiting less necessary. So a paladin can smite on round 1 without regret, where most 13th Age classes hold their best for later.
- With Way of Evil Bastards the best moment moves **late**: you wait for a wounded non-mook to get the use back.
- Fan material shows where tinkerers felt the gap: an "Eternal Smiting" epic talent ("If, at the start of your turn, the escalation die is even and you have no use of Smite Evil left, regain one use") and a quick-action basic attack when the die is even [SNIPPET: 13thage.org Vault, third-party talents; https://13thage.org/index.php/abilities/422-paladin-epic-talents ; https://www.13thagesrd.com/classes/paladin/paladin-talents-3pp/].

## Part 6. What this says to a "soft tank, soft support, soft nova" paladin [judgment; other games' lessons, not rules]

1. **"Cannot take hits for you" has two tested shapes.** Halve it for both (13th Age's Bastion: once a battle, you lose half, the ally takes half) or shave it (the Swordmage's Shielding aegis: minus 5 + Con). The full redirect (the Cavalier's Righteous Shield) is the "hard tank" version, and it is the one players praise by name as the martyr moment. All three are **once per fight or once per round**: none is a standing rule.
2. **The punishment must be certain, and big enough to matter.** 4e's 3 + Cha with no roll was called "barely a deterrent" at launch, and "impossible to avoid" later: the same rule, judged by its size and by how many enemies it reached. If the paladin "isn't a consistent damage dealer", the punishment can be something other than damage: 13th Age's challenge is a -4 with no damage, and it is rated a strong defender tool.
3. **Do not hang the mark on upkeep the enemy controls.** The engagement clause on the slowest class is the single most-cited flaw; the fix was a mark with a fixed short duration placed by your attacks.
4. **A mark that binds the paladin too** (13th Age: you also take -4 against everyone else, and hitting another enemy ends it) reads as a duel and is a ready-made "soft" limiter: he protects against one enemy, not the room. The crowd is the paladin's weak point in both games; an occasional burst mark (Call of Challenge, encounter, burst 3) is how 4e patched it without making it constant.
5. **Healing that spends the paladin's own staying power** (4e's surge, 13th Age's recovery) is the same idea in both games and survived into 13th Age 2e as a base feature: a minor / quick action, touch range, a few times a day. It makes "you won't go down" cost him his own durability, which is the soft tank and the soft support in one rule. The warning from both games: when it competes with the damage option for the same slot (Ardent Vow replaces Lay on Hands; Divine Mettle and Divine Strength share one Channel Divinity; talents in 13th Age 1e), players get only one half of the class.
6. **The nova that works is "declare before the roll, never fully wasted, few uses, stackable on the day's big fight".** Smite Evil (half on a miss), Holy Smite (the damage lands on a miss, the daze only on a hit). Its known failure is size: it must stay large against a normal hit as levels rise (13th Age 2e's change). The refund-on-kill (Way of Evil Bastards) is the cheapest "explode before they see it coming" hook found: it turns one use into a chain when aimed well.
7. **Pay more against the marked enemy.** 4e's Holy Strike and Enfeebling Strike, and Ardent Vow, make the mark the key to the paladin's own offense and debuff. This ties the three "softs" to one target and gives the turn a real decision (whom do I bind?) in place of "attack, smite when available", which is the exact boredom complaint about 13th Age 1e.
8. **Divine against profane is cheapest as a keyword.** 4e did not write "against undead" on paladin powers: it made the paladin's damage radiant and the undead vulnerable to it, and kept one loud creature-type power (Turn Undead) on the cleric. The class works on any enemy and shines on the profane.
9. **Split attack stats are remembered as the class's worst design fault**, more than any single power. One attack stat; other stats feed riders.
