### B 2.2 What fixed it

**"4e: The Paladin's Engagement" (February 14, 2026).** https://press.invincible.ink/4e-the-paladins-engagement/

- The ending: after "an entire alternate mark system and an alternate class with a much simpler marking aura called the Cavalier", "the Paladin is one of the best and most interesting types of Defenders you can make." - The subject is pacing, not the mark. The Paladin's level 2 utilities are "overstuffed": "powerhouse utility powers Bless Weapon ... Virtue that can be literally the mainstay of your entire defense going forward, and Call of Challenge, an AOE mark and sanction power that's a burst three."

The direct upshot of this was that the Paladin had this really interesting play pattern that completely failed to do the Paladin's job of protecting the party." Plus: "most Paladin powers were a little weak and expected you to have a good Charisma, a good Strength, and a good Wisdom, and keep two weapons in working trim to use the two types of power. ## Part 5. Corrections that change a conclusion

1. **13th Age 2e kept the Paladin simple on purpose.** The Changes PDF ends its Paladin entry: "the paladin is still a very simple class." Report B's reading "2e answered the complaints" is right for Lay on Hands and Smite, but the "boring" complaint was not what they fixed.
2. **The "it can be played to spam Smite" defence of the 1e Paladin has no source** (not in the thread). The read evidence leans the other way: Ridley's Cohort's "after you have used your Smite, the combat might be little more than... a little grinding".
3. **"Rarely boring" is not evidence** (a poster unfamiliar with the game).
4. **"Divine Challenge two enemies" is a magic item**, not a feat or path. The real route to two marks was Divine Challenge plus Ardent Strike's Sanction, made easy by the errata that dropped the penalty round.
5. **The Cavalier's punishment**: Square Fireballs (a preview) calls it an "almost strict upgrade over regular paladin marks" with "unprecedented stickiness" and no crowd problem, weak only next to the Knight's. The "can't spike damage" line is not there. The Cavalier's Guide is the one that calls it the class's "one shortcoming" and names the dazed weakness. So the aura fixed reach and crowds; its only complaint is size.
6. **The Plight article is about pacing**: the Divine Power fix put so much at level 2 that later utilities could not compete. A lesson report B did not draw.
7. **Report E's Swordmage reception quotes are all unverified** (not on the opened pages). The conclusion survives on other, read quotes (G8), and gains two limits: the aegis fails once the swordmage is down, and multi-attack monsters get past the one interrupt.
8. **The PF2e Guardian "did its job" quote hides a negative report**: taunt was ignored, offense was too low to matter, and the player often did not attack. This is the read example closest to "a protector with no threat".
9. **Report E's "two-sided test" is solid**: it is the core passage of The Art of Defending, now quoted in full.
