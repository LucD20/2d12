# Helping another character's roll: how other games do it (raw report)

**Date:** 2026-11-19

**Lucas's words:** "When you help someone they gain support 1 boon. I planned to make tiered, like 1 bane / 1 boon / 2 boons but I think rolling to help is disruptive. At the same time, this means most rolls are simply going to hear 'I am helping, so add 1 boon', which, again, falls into the lameness. This likely requires a search of its own about Aid/Help action."

**What was read first:** `book/research/README.md`; Chapter 1 (Boons and Banes, Named Boons and Banes, Favored and Doomed, Checks); `book/roleplay-skills.md`. No note in `book/research/` covers helping: the only earlier mentions are one row of `raw/2026-11-19-skills-or-magic-first.md` (Draw Steel's Assist a Test, Fabula Ultima's Group Check) and a Pathfinder feat in the archer report.

**How far to trust it.** These are other games' rules and other people's opinions, never 2d12's. Nothing here designs the Action.

- **[read]** rules text on a page that opened. **[checked]** an opinion on a page that opened. **[lead]** a search summary, or a page that refused. **[memory]** Claude's knowledge, no page opened.
- Most pages were read as raw text, so the quotes are exact. Two were read through a summarizing tool (D&D 2024's glossary, Blades in the Dark's teamwork page): check those quotes on the page before repeating them.
- About a third of the games asked for are [memory] or [lead]; section 9 lists what did not open. No statement by a named designer about *why* their help rule is shaped as it is was opened (one by Ryan Macklin exists and could not be reached).

## 1. D&D and Pathfinder

- **D&D 3.5 / Pathfinder 1e, Aid Another.** In a fight: a standard action, "an attack roll against AC 10", then +2 to the friend's next attack or to their AC; "Multiple characters can aid the same friend, and similar bonuses stack." For skills: the same kind of check, 10 or higher gives +2, and "you can't aid another to grant a bonus to a task that your character couldn't achieve alone." [read, Pathfinder 1e's copy of the 3.5 text] https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat/ and https://www.d20pfsrd.com/skills/ (d20srd.org refused, 403)
- **D&D 4e.** A standard action, DC 10 for +2; a later revision raised the DC with level and made a failed attempt give -1. [memory; the fan wiki refused]
- **D&D 5e (2014).** No roll. Working Together: the leader "can make an ability check with advantage"; "A character can only provide help if the task is one that he or she could attempt alone", and only when working together "would actually be productive. Some tasks, such as threading a needle, are no easier with help." In combat the Help action spends the helper's action for advantage on one check or one attack against a creature within 5 feet of the helper. [read] https://5thsrd.org/rules/abilities/ability_checks/ and https://5thsrd.org/combat/actions_in_combat/
- **D&D 2024.** Help now asks the helper to "Choose one of your skill or tool proficiencies"; the ally gets Advantage on the next check "with the chosen skill or tool"; "The DM has final say on whether your assistance is possible." The attack version is unchanged. [read through a summarizing tool] https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary . Reception is split between "it constrains creative help" and "a welcome limit". [lead]
- **Pathfinder 2e, Aid (remaster).** A reaction, after you "prepare to help, usually by using an action during your turn. You must explain to the GM exactly how you're trying to help, and they determine whether you can Aid your ally." You roll: "The typical DC is 15". Critical success +2 (+3 for a master, +4 legendary), success +1, failure nothing, critical failure -1. Repeated Aid against the same creature: "the GM will usually increase the DC each time". [read] https://roll20.net/compendium/pf2/Rules:Basic%20Actions (Archives of Nethys needs a browser)
- **Follow the Expert** (exploration): a skilled ally leads; followers add their level even untrained, plus a bonus set by the expert's rank; the expert rolls nothing extra. A substitution, not a bonus by a roll. [memory]

**Reception, 5e.** The complaint is the reflex, and it is about the free version outside a fight. EN World's Reynard: "Help does not require a roll. There are no prerequisites for Helping on any given action" (a familiar gives it every turn). [checked] https://www.enworld.org/threads/is-the-help-action-broken.497755/ . In a fight the same thread treats it as fair because it eats the helper's action. A second thread asks for a downside; the best reply against one is Pauper's: "There's a certain class of player for whom, if a particular action has any chance of hindering rather than helping things, then that action will never be taken." Sunseeker adds that the benefit is too small to carry a risk. [checked] https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=7441735 . A third thread is titled "Mitigating players spamming Help, Guidance, Bardic Inspiration, and oh I'll roll too?"; on its fourth page a poster reads Working Together's limits onto Help ("Well, how are you helping your companion? Describe it to us.") [checked] https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=7497519

**Reception, Pathfinder 2e.** At the old DC 20, Blave: "DC 20 is pretty hard at level 1 and nearly an auto-success at level 10." Ubertron_X: "people who try to use Aid early on and fail often might easily never try to use it again. Negative conditioning if you will." Deriven Firelion on the price: "It costs an action and a reaction to provide a bonus to a single action or attack." [checked] https://paizo.com/threads/rzs43itb . After the remaster the opposite complaint: with a fixed DC 15 a trained character soon cannot fail, so some GMs scale the DC, and then a player reports failing Aid "again and again" against tough monsters and wanting to drop it "as a viable 3rd action". Squiggit's defence of the fixed DC: a scaled one "turns the character who's trained but not specialized at a higher level into much more of a potential liability." [checked] https://paizo.com/threads/rzs4s0c9 and https://paizo.com/threads/rzs65ox4 . So one rule is a trap at one end and automatic at the other; the roll decides something only in a narrow band of levels. [judgment]

## 2. The helper rolls, and can make it worse

- **Draw Steel, Assist a Test.** Allowed "provided you have a skill that applies to the test, the other creature isn't using that same skill on the test, and you can describe how your character helps to the Director's satisfaction." The helper makes a test: "≤11: You get in the way or make things worse. The creature takes a bane on their test."; 12-16 an edge; 17+ a double edge. [read] https://steelcompendium.io/compendium/main/Rules/Chapters/Tests/ . This is the three-tier shape Lucas first planned. With a total modifier of +4 the bad result is 21 in 100; with +2, 36 in 100. [judgment: my arithmetic on 2d10] A review names it as a disappointment: "Wait, for some reason helping in a Skill is very likely to make the outcome worse?" [checked] https://splitparty.substack.com/p/draw-steel-part-2 . In a montage test the book itself says a hero may do nothing if they "fear that their actions might make the situation worse". [read]
- **Savage Worlds (Adventure Edition), Support.** Supporters "roll whatever skill they feel is relevant (as an action if the game is in rounds). Success adds +1 to the lead character's applicable total, and a raise adds +2. A Critical Failure on the support roll subtracts 2". "The maximum bonus from all Support rolls is +4." Any skill with a story: "General encouragement, by the way, is a Persuasion roll." [read, the publisher's 2018 preview] https://peginc.com/savage-worlds-kickstarter-support-rules-preview/ . No reception found in one search.
- **Worlds Without Number.** The player "describes what sort of action they are taking"; if the GM agrees, they roll "a relevant skill check against the same difficulty as the original check"; success gives +1, and "the acting PC can't earn more than a +1 total bonus." [read as quoted on a blog that opened; the book was not opened] https://idraluna-archives.bearblog.dev/teamwork-makes-the-dream-work/ . The blogger calls this family "Perfectly serviceable" and underwhelming. [checked]
- **Dungeon World, Aid or Interfere.** "When you help or hinder someone you have a bond with, roll+Bond with them. On a 10+ they take +1 or -2, your choice. On a 7–9 you also expose yourself to danger, retribution, or cost." And "the target only gets the +1 or -2 once." [read] https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/moves/ . Apocalypse World's Help or Interfere is the parent (roll+Hx). [memory] Its reputation, a roll for +1, is poor. [memory]
- **Daggerheart, Group Action Roll.** A leader rolls; each other PC describes their part and makes a reaction roll: success +1, failure -1 to the leader. [read] https://callmepartario.github.io/og-dhsrd/
- **Fabula Ultima, Group Check.** Supporters roll against a low difficulty; each success adds +1, more with a Bond toward the leader. [memory]

## 3. A cost instead of a roll

- **Blades in the Dark, Assist.** "Take 1 stress and give them +1d to their roll." "Only one character may assist a given roll." The helper says how, and may be exposed to the consequences. [read through a summarizing tool] https://bladesinthedark.com/teamwork . An archived design thread from the game's development argues the price: its opening post wants "Take 2 stress" (the author's name did not come through; it reads as the designer's). Commenters: "1 stress to assist to give +1d would probably result in everyone always helping each other"; against that, "If the group can casually spend stress frequently to help each other, maybe they aren't getting pushed hard enough"; and "I would think sharing in the consequences of a failed or partially failed roll is a sufficient trade off for helping." [checked] https://bitd.gplusarchive.online/?p=142803 . Eric Faber on the finished rule: "Nothing innovative or interesting here." [checked] https://airborneham.substack.com/p/helping-hands-teamwork-in-ttrpgs
- **Daggerheart, Help an Ally (1 Hope).** "Describe how you help, roll your own advantage die, and apply the result as a modifier to their roll." Several helpers: only the highest die counts. The die is a bonus die, not a check: it cannot fail. Not allowed on reaction rolls. **Tag Team Roll (3 Hope):** two players describe a joint action, both roll, they choose one result; on a hit both roll damage and add it. [read, same page] Once a session for each player. [lead]
- **Year Zero games.** "This must be declared before you roll your dice. It must also make sense in the story—the individuals helping you must be physically present and have the capacity to support your action." "For each person helping you, you get a +1 modifier. No more than three people can help". [read] https://callmepartario.github.io/og-yzesrd/
- **Dragonbane:** a boon, one helper, and in a fight it uses the helper's action. **The One Ring 2e:** a Hope point for a die on a companion's roll. **7th Sea 2e:** a Hero Point for three dice. **Warhammer Fantasy 4e:** +10, one helper, who must have the skill. [all memory]
- **DC20:** Help costs 1 AP and gives a Help Die; one die a check. Fan pages disagree on its size. [lead]
- **Nimble, Shadow of the Weird Wizard, ICRPG, Mothership, Cortex, Call of Cthulhu:** not opened; nothing distinctive remembered with confidence. [memory]

## 4. Shared risk

- **Fate Core, Teamwork.** No roll by helpers: "Each other participant who has at least an Average (+1) in the same skill adds a +1 to the highest person's skill level, and then only the lead character rolls." Then: "If you fail a roll to combine skills, all of the participants share in the potential costs". The second way is to Create an Advantage (a real action with its own roll) and pass the free invocations. [read] https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/teamwork . The same page quotes co-author Ryan Macklin: "Some people are critical of the teamwork rules in Fate; depending on the day of the week, I'm one of them". [read] His article "Revising Teamwork in Fate" could not be opened.
- **Burning Wheel, Mouse Guard, Torchbearer.** A helper with a fitting skill hands over a die (+1D), says how, and is bound by the result. [memory; forum summaries agree: lead] In Torchbearer a failed test that gives a condition gives the helpers a lesser one: a table reports that the lesser condition makes "a second story of less interest", wants the same condition with +2D, and is told that at +2D "helping is just a given, because the odds of failing are so low." [checked] https://forums.burningwheel.com/t/giving-to-helpers-the-same-condition-as-to-tester-on-a-failed-test/15136 . The same Blades thread praises the gesture: "I love the immersion and engagement that handing over a physical dice as help invokes." [checked]

## 5. Substitution, and help that is its own action

- **Genesys / Star Wars (FFG).** Skilled assistance: the pool uses one character's characteristic and the other's skill; unskilled assistance adds one boost die. [lead: archived forum summaries]
- **Legend of the Five Rings 5e:** a skilled helper adds a skill die, an unskilled one a ring die. [memory]
- **Ironsworn: Starforged, Aid Your Ally.** "When you act in direct support of an ally, envision how you aid them. Then, Secure an Advantage or Gain Ground to take action. If you score a hit, they (instead of you) take the benefits of the move." Help is an ordinary action whose reward goes to someone else. [read] https://rsek.github.io/starforged-srd/moves/adventure
- **Blades, Set up:** a real action; whoever follows through "gets +1 effect level or improved position". **Protect:** "You suffer it instead of them." Faber calls Protect "a neat mechanic" for a moment other games need a roll for. [read through the tool; checked]
- **GUMSHOE.** Piggybacking: the lead's difficulty rises by 2 for each follower, and a follower spends 1 point to cancel it. Cooperation: the helper spends their own pool points into the roll, at a loss. [checked as The Alexandrian describes piggybacking, https://thealexandrian.net/?p=43860 ; Cooperation from memory]
- **13th Age:** no general help rule remembered; helping lives in class powers. [memory; see `paladins-other-games.md` for 4e and 13th Age ally powers]

## 6. Group checks

- **5e:** everyone rolls; "If at least half the group succeeds, the whole group succeeds." [read] **Draw Steel:** the same half rule, with a collective reward or consequence when half the group earned one, and "A hero who is participating in the group test can't assist another hero participating in the test." [read]
- **Blades, Lead a group action:** everyone rolls, the best result counts, the leader takes 1 stress for each roll of 1-3. [read through the tool] Faber: "that leading character cares an awful lot about how well the others do." A thread comment: "leaders often prefer to roll on their own". [checked]
- **The Alexandrian, "Art of Rulings 14: Group Actions".** Four kinds: separate actions; one success is enough (best result counts); one collective action (a point man with helpers); all must succeed together (piggybacking). "Everyone rolls and all must pass" turns five 70% chances into 17%. "Best result counts" skews the other way, and the cure is "consequences — or the risk of consequences — for participating." When players chose the method themselves they "would nevertheless rebel the minute the point character failed their test": the GM names the kind. [checked] https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/39430/roleplaying-games/art-of-rulings-14-group-actions

## 7. Helping in a fight

- Two games price help in action currency: 5e (the whole action for advantage on one attack) and Pathfinder 2e (an action, the reaction and a roll for +1). In the threads read, 5e's is called fair but is mostly handed to a familiar, and Pathfinder's is used as a third action when it is used at all. [checked, the threads above] Forum posters note Aid does not take the multiple attack penalty, which is why it competes only with a weak third attack. [lead]
- The free combat help complained about is the one with no positional cost: a familiar whose Help has "No action, no concentration, no save." [checked, EN World]
- Flanking is the usual "help with no declared action": the helper pays in position, not in a turn. No commentary on it was opened. [memory]
- 4e's and 13th Age's ally-enabling powers make the help the rider of an attack rather than its replacement. [memory; `paladins-other-games.md`, `tactical-combat.md`]

## 8. The design question

**The reflex.** DM David: "The GM tells a player to make a check, and then everyone at the table, and the pizza guy at the door, all start rolling to assist." His rules: ask "How do you assist?"; limit who can plausibly fit; the help must happen during the scene ("she cannot interrupt and declare that she assisted retroactively"); the helper's skill follows from what they do; and a helper with a sure contribution "might even assist without a roll". [checked] https://dmdavid.com/tag/making-assist-checks-more-than-a-way-to-wring-a-bonus-from-the-rules/

The Alexandrian gives the mechanism in "Guidance Sucks in Fifth Edition". Two principles: an option so good it is always taken has "broken the process of choosing", and "declaration gotcha mechanics aren't fun". A bonus that fits every check "must be cast for every single ability check", and the table gets "An endless, mindless drone which is both a tax you are obligated to pay and an action which is not only devoid of narrative worth". Because it stands for nothing in the world, "there's no true satisfaction or sense of accomplishment", and it "gets in the way of players creatively coming up with ways of assisting each other". [checked] https://thealexandrian.net/?p=46797 . In "Show the Help" he applies it to helping: once the helper vanishes from the narration, "the declaration of “I help!” at the game table has become a purely mechanical catechism that can rapidly degrade into a declaration gotcha." His fix is at the table: ask how ("It needs to be actionable in the fiction"), then show the helper's part in the outcome. [checked] https://thealexandrian.net/?p=51429

Faber: "If your character isn't doing anything, you may as well help, right?" and "Just tossing a small bonus of +1 at your friend doesn't require the kind of collaboration or communication that teamwork often entails." [checked]

**Is the roll worth it.** For: it lets the helper's skill matter and "feels like doing something" (Blades thread). [checked] Against: Pauper's point that any chance of harm stops some players helping at all; the Pathfinder record (taught not to try at low level, automatic later); the Draw Steel review. None of the three-tier help rolls read here (Draw Steel, Savage Worlds, Pathfinder 2e, Dungeon World) was praised for the roll itself. [judgment, from the pages above]

**Help that is not a number.** Found in rules: substitution (Follow the Expert, Genesys), taking the consequence (Protect), an ordinary action whose benefit is passed on (Starforged, Set up, Create an Advantage), changing who rolls (group actions), sharing the failure (Fate, Burning Wheel). Not found as a general help rule in any game read: "the helper makes the task possible at all" and "the helper softens the failure". [judgment]

**Satisfying against hollow.** Satisfying: handing over a die; a described act that shows in the outcome; a leader who cares how the others roll; two players combining turns (Tag Team). Hollow: a bonus announced after the roll is called; one that fits every roll; failing your own attempt to help; the helper absent from the narration. [checked, the sources above; "Tag Team is liked" is a lead]

## What seems to transfer

Other games' experience, sorted for Lucas to decide from. Not a proposal.

### (a) The shapes of helping

| Shape | Games | Cost at the table | Known weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free bonus | 5e Working Together; Year Zero (+1 each, three at most) | One sentence | The reflex: said on every roll, means nothing [checked] |
| Bonus for a cost | Blades (1 stress), Daggerheart (1 Hope), The One Ring, 7th Sea; 5e's and Dragonbane's action in a fight | A resource marked, no roll | Too cheap and it is automatic again (the Blades thread on 1 stress); in a fight the whole turn for a small bonus is rarely chosen |
| Bonus by a roll | 3.5 / Pathfinder 1e, Pathfinder 2e, Draw Steel, Savage Worlds, Worlds Without Number, Dungeon World | A second roll each helper | Doubles the rolls; a trap or automatic depending on the DC; a result that harms teaches players not to help |
| Shared risk | Fate, Burning Wheel family, Blades | A sentence, then the helper is in the consequence | Needs a consequence that fits the helper (Torchbearer's "second story") |
| Substitution | Follow the Expert, Genesys, Legend of the Five Rings | A lookup of two sheets | Only matters when the two differ; one expert covers the party |
| Widening the result | Blades' Set up and Protect; Daggerheart's Tag Team | A real action or a large spend | Is a second action, not an "I help" |
| Enabling | Starforged; Fate's Create an Advantage; 5e's "could attempt alone" used as a gate | The helper's own action and roll | Slower; the roll is the helper's turn, which is also why it is accepted |
| Group check | 5e, Draw Steel, Blades, GUMSHOE, Daggerheart | Everyone rolls once | Skewed odds unless the kind is named (The Alexandrian); leaders prefer to roll alone |

### (b) What stops "I help" from being automatic

| Device | Source | Its cost |
|---|---|---|
| Say how, and the GM must accept it | Pathfinder 2e, Draw Steel, Worlds Without Number, Year Zero, DM David, The Alexandrian | A judgment call each time: against "least ambiguity" unless the rule says what counts |
| Declared before the roll, during the scene | Year Zero; DM David | None; it only removes the afterthought |
| One helper, or a cap | Blades (one), Dragonbane (one), Worlds Without Number (+1 in all), Year Zero (three), Savage Worlds (+4) | The others at the table have nothing to add |
| The helper must be able: a skill, a proficiency, "could attempt alone" | 5e 2014 and 2024, Fate, Draw Steel, Warhammer | Needs written skills; split reception in 2024 [lead] |
| The helper must be placed | 5e's attack Help (within 5 feet of the enemy), flanking | Only applies on a map |
| A price in a resource or the turn | Blades, Daggerheart, 5e and Pathfinder in a fight | Must be priced: cheap is automatic, dear is unused |
| The helper shares the failure | Fate, Burning Wheel, Blades | The failure must reach the helper believably |
| A different skill from the roller's | Draw Steel; DM David | More GM judgment |
| A rising cost for repeating | Pathfinder 2e (the DC rises against the same creature) | Bookkeeping |
| Show the help in the outcome | The Alexandrian | A GM habit, not a rule |

### (c) Shapes that need no extra roll

Free bonus; bonus for a cost (Daggerheart's die is rolled, but with the main roll and cannot fail); shared risk (Fate, Burning Wheel, Blades); substitution; Protect; GUMSHOE's piggybacking. The shapes that always add a roll: bonus by a roll, enabling through one's own action, and every group check.
