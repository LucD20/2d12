# The paladin in other games: 4e, 13th Age, Pathfinder 2e (Champion, Guardian), Draw Steel (Censor), Nimble, Lancer, ICON

## 2026-11-14: for the class redesign (MASTER-PLAN item 5), before any Paladin card

**The question (Lucas):** the paladin and similar classes in 4e, Pathfinder 2e, Draw Steel, 13th Age, Lancer and others: what the rules give them and how players say they feel, compared with our rules.

### The games, one paragraph each

**D&D 4e Paladin** [read]. *The mark:* Divine Challenge, a minor action, within 5, one enemy: it takes -2 to attacks that don't include you, and **3 + Charisma radiant damage, no roll, no action**, the first time each round it attacks without you. The mark ends if you neither attacked it nor ended adjacent to it. *The heal:* Lay on Hands, a minor action, touch: **you spend your own healing surge, the ally heals as if they had spent one.** *At-wills that pay more against the enemy you marked* (Holy Strike, Enfeebling Strike). At launch it was called a weak defender. [lead, Giant in the Playground] The punishment "barely a deterrent" and on one target only. [checked, Talen Lee, Invincible Ink] Automatic, free damage "needs a drawback", and the one chosen let enemies beat it: plate is slow ("moving in 5s and your opponent is moving in 7s"), so "the correct course of action when an enemy does that is to give up on the Divine Challenge and pick someone else"; it felt like "picking one person and making their life a little inconvenient", and the Paladin became "the backup Defender". It also wanted Strength, Charisma and Wisdom and two weapons. The fix (Divine Power) [read]: **Divine Sanction**, the same punishment with a fixed duration and no upkeep, placed by an attack or by a burst. After it [checked]: "one of the best and most interesting types of Defenders you can make"; his lesson: make the class play "the way that people wanted it to play, rather than trying to make people play differently". A second lesson from the same author: the fix put three strong options at level 2, "and that set the bar": later options could not compete. The Essentials Cavalier [read] has the three "protect" shapes in one class: an aura 1 with -2 to attacks that don't include you, an automatic 3 + Charisma when an enemy in it ignores you, and once an encounter **Righteous Shield: take all of an ally's damage, then +2 to your attacks** [checked: the Cavalier's Guide calls it what redeems the class]. The automatic punishment was received well [checked, Square Fireballs]: an "almost strict upgrade over regular paladin marks" with "unprecedented stickiness"; its one complaint is its size. Its Holy Smite: an encounter power declared before the roll, the damage lands even on a miss, the daze only on a hit.

**13th Age Paladin** [read]. No powers: one feature and three talents. **Smite Evil:** once a battle plus Charisma times a day, declared before the roll, +1d12 and **half damage on a miss**; nothing requires an evil target. Talents: **Bastion** (once a battle, when a nearby ally is hit: you lose half the damage, the ally takes half); **Paladin's Challenge** (on a hit: until the battle ends you **both** take -4 against everyone else; it ends if you hit another enemy); **Lay on Hands** (twice a day, a quick action, your recovery heals the ally); **Way of Evil Bastards** (a smite that drops a non-mook is not used up). [checked, EN World, "Is the paladin underpowered?"] Reputation: "boring as opposed to underpowered"; "a pretty incredible 'defender,' but the design is still too simplistic"; after the smite "the combat might be little more than... a little grinding"; Paladin's Challenge "is great for a party that is competent at team work, but it might feel pretty meh to the player". One player's happy version: "I latch onto the biggest, meanest threat to the group, and trade punches with it... till it drops." The second edition [checked, Pelgrane's "Changes from 1e"] makes Lay on Hands a base feature and improves the smite, and keeps the class simple on purpose: "the paladin is still a very simple class".

**Pathfinder 2e Champion** [read]. One reaction carries the class. For the **four causes that protect**: trigger "An enemy damages your ally, and both are in your champion's aura" (15 feet; a level 6 feat makes it 30), payload: the ally resists 2 + level of that damage. **Each adds one rider:** a Strike if the enemy is in reach (Justice); the enemy chooses between no damage or resistance and being enfeebled (Redemption); the ally steps free (Liberation); the attacker is lit by revealing light, so no invisibility or concealment (Grandeur). The three unholy causes trigger when the champion himself is damaged (Obedience: kneel or take pain). The reaction grows instead of multiplying (level 9 adds lingering damage, level 11 spreads it to the aura). Lay on Hands: 1 action, an every-fight resource, 6 Hit Points and +2 AC for a round. The Remaster's Smite: 1 action, declare one enemy, +3 damage to your Strikes against it, **+4 if it is unholy**, and it extends itself while that enemy keeps harming you or your allies. [checked, Paizo threads] Praise: the catch-22 ("You can attack my allies and take a Champion's Reaction, or you can attack durable me and go through my Shield Block"); the party feels safe. Complaints: **the one reaction** ("one enemy can blow your reaction and then another can target a squishy without consequence"; "if their champion reaction never gets triggered, that's a comatose class feature"); the Justice cause is ranked last because its Strike "can miss", Redemption first because nothing in it can; the range (mostly about the older class). **Not found:** any complaint that the Champion has nothing to do on its own turn; a player says the opposite: "as a Champion player I actually feel like I don't have enough actions to do what I want". **Guardian** (2025) [relayed]: Taunt, 1 action, 30 feet, no roll: if the enemy acts against an ally and not you, -1 to it and it is off-guard. The playtest version (a save, and +2 for the enemy against you) was rejected ("Taunt is Bad") [checked]. A playtest report worth keeping [checked, VampByDay]: the taunt "seemed to work as intended... if the intent was the enemy ignored the taunt"; "piddly damage, which means there was not really a reason for the monsters to attack me"; "I often found myself not attacking". Against it, a Champion player on the Guardian: "waiting to use their reactions and hoping they trigger". Intercept Attack: an ally within 10 feet takes damage, you step to them and take it instead.

**Draw Steel Censor** [read]: the closest published match to the brief. Its text: "you're at your best against the strongest foes". The same Stamina as other front-liners and the most Recoveries (12): **tough across the day, not in the round.** *Judgment* (a maneuver, range 10, lasts the encounter): the judged enemy takes twice your Presence in holy damage, no roll, each time it uses a main action; on a kill it jumps to a new target for free. *Wrath* (its resource): +2 at the start of each turn, +1 the first time each round the judged enemy damages you, +1 the first time you damage it; all lost when the fight ends; a new fight starts with wrath equal to the party's Victories. **1 wrath buys a protection** against the judged enemy (a bane on its roll, it can't shift away from you, a taunt after your melee hit); **3, 5, 7 or 9 wrath buys the big ability**: so guarding delays the explosion. *My Life for Yours* (a triggered action, range 10, when an ally takes damage or starts its turn): **you spend one of your own Recoveries and the ally heals that much.** It never redirects the hit; the only true "take the hit" (Intercede) costs 9 wrath at level 6. Burst shapes worth noting: *Censored* (5 wrath: a non-boss brought to half by it drops to 0), the 7-wrath *Edicts* (a 2 aura for the rest of the fight that punishes nearby enemies, more against the judged one), 9-wrath strikes that are half support (the hit plus temporary Stamina to adjacent allies). [checked, two guides, 2026] "The Censor is the only class to get 12 [Recoveries], and this is incredible"; Judgment is "an uncleansable damage-over-time effect on a single enemy"; the loop: "lock a target down, force them to attack you, and get your Wrath back". Its weak points: **round 1** ("+2 Wrath. Not enough to do anything powerful"; another class does the same thing "an entire round earlier", and "doing the same action early in a fight is better than doing it later"); and My Life for Yours "doesn't generate any value. It doesn't end the encounter faster or prevent damage", and he "can run out of recoveries".

**Nimble Oathsworn** [read]: the best match for "explode". *Radiant Judgment:* "Whenever an enemy attacks you, if you have no Judgment Dice, roll your Judgment dice (2d6). On your next melee attack this encounter, if you hit, deal that much additional radiant damage. The dice are expended whether you hit or miss." **One charge, rolled in the open when you are attacked, never stacking.** *Interpose* (a reaction every hero has; free for the Oathsworn from level 4): become the target for an ally within 2. So **guarding loads the burst**. Options: spend the dice on every enemy in your aura once an encounter; hand a die to an ally when you are attacked with dice already loaded; once an encounter set an ally who would drop to 1 HP. A subclass maxes the dice when an ally is wounded. [checked, the designer, 2024] "They WANT to be hit, and they WANT to protect their friends." The move from stacking dice to one charge is confirmed [checked]. A player [checked]: "a cool revenge mechanic", "a mobile tank".

**Lancer** [read]. Defenders protect by position and penalty, not by soaking: adjacent allies use the mech as hard cover (the Guardian trait); the Saladin gives +1 difficulty to an attack on any ally in range once a round, at a cost in Heat. Burst is built then spent: the Sherman's gun gains a die for each Stabilize (up to 4) and resets when fired; Overcharge costs rising Heat (1, 1d3, 1d6, 1d6 + 4).

**ICON Stalwarts** [read]. *Vigilance:* a stock of dice (+1 a turn, up to 6) spent either to reduce damage to an ally within 2 or to hit a foe who leaves you. The Knave's Taunt gives Hatred: "Deal half damage to all foes other than foe X". The Demon Slayer upgrades its next attack if it does not attack this turn.

**Fabula Ultima Guardian** [read]: the opposite pole. *Protect*: take an ally's place against any attack, free, once a round, declared after the roll. A true sponge, by design.

### Takeaways

1. **"Can't take hits for you" is the common design, not the exception.** The Champion shaves the hit, the Censor heals after it, Lancer and ICON penalize or halve it. Three tested shapes of protection, all once a round or once a fight: **shave it** (Champion: resist 2 + level; Swordmage: minus 5 + Con), **halve it for both** (13th Age Bastion), **take all of it** (Cavalier's Righteous Shield, Guardian's Intercept).
   - *For 2d12:* Shield of Faith is "shave it" (2d6 + [Mod], worth 66 points a turn against a budget of about 15: it erases a Standard's whole hit and fires 82% of rounds). The shape is right; the size and the trigger are wrong. Bulwark of Faith (Guard equal to a Rally Point, then Slid 1) and Divine Intervention are the bigger, rarer shapes.
2. **One trigger, one payload, one rider is how the best-liked protector is written.** The Champion's four protecting causes differ by one clause.
   - *For 2d12:* this is our "a Reaction does one main thing and at most one rider" exactly. The two Hallmark Reactions can share a trigger and a payload and differ by the rider, as the causes do.
3. **The punishment must be certain, and not hang on upkeep the enemy controls.** 4e's flaw was the "stay adjacent" duty on its slowest class; the fix was a mark with a fixed short duration placed by an attack. The Champion's least-liked cause is the one whose punishment is a Strike that can miss; the 4e punishments with no roll (Divine Sanction, the Cavalier's) were the ones that worked.
   - *For 2d12:* our Paladin has Speed +0, heavy armor and, after the Advance rule, one unsplit move a turn. Don't write "while you are adjacent to it". Our Reactions reach 5 squares (25 feet, where the Champion's 15 is the top complaint): keep 5.
4. **A mark the player places is the spine of every modern paladin** (Judgment, Divine Challenge, Paladin's Challenge, the Remaster's Smite, Vow of Enmity). It makes the three roles one job: the marked enemy is the one you punish, protect against and explode on. Accepted marks are cheap (a maneuver or part of an attack), need no roll, and move for free on a kill.
   - *For 2d12:* the Archer already has it (Mark Quarry, 0 AP; the class's best-measured Feature, 26 to 39 points). The Paladin's Divine Power (a Bane on a Profane creature's Strikes) is worth about 0. A 0 AP "Judgment" in its place was the first audit's suggestion; the research backs it from five games. Our Taunted (1 Bane against anyone but you) is the same family as Draw Steel's taunt (a double bane) and ICON's Hatred (half damage): **ours is the mildest of the three** and the SIM prices it at 1 to 3.
5. **A mark that binds the Paladin too** (13th Age: you also take -4 against everyone else; hitting another enemy ends it) is a ready-made "soft": he guards against one enemy, not the room.
6. **Healing with the Paladin's own reserve** is in 4e, 13th Age and Draw Steel, and survived into 13th Age's second edition as a base feature: touch or short range, a minor action or a reaction, a few times a day. One poster calls it "retroactive defending".
   - *For 2d12:* Rally Points are the same rule as surges and Recoveries (a quarter of the Health Pool). Today Lay On Hands spends the **ally's**. A Paladin who spends **his own** for them "takes the hit for you" without taking the hit, and it makes his depth (Rally 6 against the Archer's 5) the tank part. See `frontline-protection.md`, takeaway 6.
7. **The burst is built in the fight and in the open.** Three loaders: time and the duel (Draw Steel's wrath), **being attacked or guarding** (Nimble's Judgment Dice, ICON's Vigilance), giving up a turn (ICON's Demon Slayer, Lancer's Sherman). Detail: `nova-burst.md`.
   - *For 2d12:* our Escalation is a clock that builds in the open and is never spent; the Paladin's hook already ties it to an ally becoming Bloodied or going Down. Nothing in the class today is loaded by protecting.
8. **When the support and the damage options compete for one slot, players get half a class.** 4e (Ardent Vow replaces Lay on Hands), 13th Age 1e (talents). Both games later moved the identity pieces into the base class.
   - *For 2d12:* this is our Hallmark Action choice (Smite **or** Lay On Hands) and the SIM's two Paladins (115 and 161 points at level 2). If the brief is one class with three softs, **the pieces that make it a Paladin (the mark, the Rally Point touch, the burst) belong in the Features every Paladin has, and the Hallmark choice picks a style.** A decision for Lucas.
9. **The simple paladin is called boring, not weak; and a protector with no threat is ignored.** 13th Age's complaint is the Paladin's own turn after the smite ("a little grinding"); the Guardian playtest's is that a soft taunt on a character with "piddly damage" gave monsters no reason to care. The Champion is **not** called dull on its own turn.
   - *For 2d12:* the redesign's first priority (utility) is aimed at the right thing: the Paladin's own turn needs jobs besides attacking. And our Taunted (1 Bane) on a Weapon +1 Paladin is the Guardian's failed taunt unless something real stands behind it: the certain punishment, or the burst.
10. **Split attack stats are remembered as the 4e Paladin's worst fault.**
    - *For 2d12:* "+2 and +1, you choose which is your Weapon Modifier" is a milder version of it. Part of the class numbers decision.

11. **One Reaction a round is the protector's whole budget, and enemies learn it.** [checked] "one enemy can blow your reaction and then another can target a squishy without consequence" (Paizo); in 5e "sometimes the threat of your reaction can be almost as powerful as the use of your reaction" (Tribality). A three-reaction map from a Champion player: "If the enemy attacks you, you shield block - If they attack your front row buddy, you use a champion reaction - If they try to get out of your aura you use reactive strike".
    - *For 2d12:* this is our Reaction AP exactly, and our Reactions are Turn (once on each creature's turn, **while he has AP for it**). AP he keeps from his turn pay for more Reactions: a Paladin who ends his turn with 1 AP unspent has two protections that round, and an unused one becomes Guard 2. That is already a "hold something back to protect" decision in our rules; the class can lean on it instead of a new resource.
12. **A fix placed at level 1 becomes the bar.** [checked, Invincible Ink] Divine Power's level 2 fixes were so good that later options were skipped for more of them.
    - *For 2d12:* the Features every Paladin has (the mark, the touch) must not outshine the picks, or the Hallmark choice stops mattering.
