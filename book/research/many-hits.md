# "Death by a thousand cuts": many small hits without many slow rolls

## 2026-11-16: for the Archer's redesign (its identity mechanic)

**The question (Lucas):** the Archer "deals damage by hitting a lot of times, not one big hit"; Double Shot (two separate attack rolls) was "quite boring" in his playtest. How do other games deliver many small hits without many rolls and without one repeated button? His own ideas: an "Arrows" pool of dice; several-AP Actions that roll once against several enemies; a common Field Boon for ranged.

**How far to trust this file.** One pass, by an agent; not re-checked. [read] = rules text read in full (Draw Steel, D&D 4e, 13th Age, ICON 1.5, Pathfinder 2e, Lancer). [quoted] = a rule seen only as a poster's or blogger's quotation (the Warhammer 40,000 RPGs, GURPS, Genesys, Savage Worlds, Nimble, Exalted). [checked] = an opinion read on its page. [lead] = a search summary only (Conan 2d20's Loads: its forum needs an account). Reddit, Giant in the Playground and RPG.net refuse readers and were left alone. No reception was found for Draw Steel's Two Shot, Lancer's Reliable, ICON's fray or 13th Age's even-roll attack. The full report: `raw/2026-11-16-thousand-cuts.md`.

### Takeaways: ten ways games do it

1. **The margin of one roll is the number of hits** [quoted: the 40k RPGs' bursts, GURPS, Genesys' Auto-fire]. One roll. Never called slow, always called too strong on one target: [checked, FFG forum, 2011] "It acts like a damage multiplier"; [checked, 2018] "pretty bananas [...] vs a single target. I think a simple fix is to only allow it to be used to hit multiple targets".
   - *For 2d12:* our three Tiers are a margin with a ceiling: "Tier 1: 1 arrow. Tier 2: 2 arrows. Tier 3: 3 arrows" can't run away as an open margin does.
2. **One roll applied to every target** [read]. Draw Steel's Two Shot: "When you fire two arrows back-to-back, both hit their mark. Ranged 12, Two creatures or objects. ≤11: 4 damage / 12-16: 6 damage / 17+: 8 damage". 4e's Split the Tree: "Make two attack rolls, take the higher result, and apply it to both targets." Fast ([checked, 2025] Draw Steel's roll "speeds up the game tremendously"), but it is many hits only against many enemies.
   - *For 2d12:* Lucas's second idea, and today's Twin Shot. It needs a single-target answer beside it.
3. **The die itself grants the second hit** [read]. 13th Age's Double Ranged Attack: "Your weapon damage die drops one notch [...] If your first attack is a natural even roll (hit or miss), you can make a second attack". ICON: "Deal 1 damage again to your target."
   - *For 2d12:* reading the d12s already on the table costs no roll (a die showing 9 or more: 56% of rolls).
4. **A floor: never a whiff** [read]. ICON: "On hit: 2[D] + fray. On miss: fray." Lancer's Reliable; 13th Age's miss damage. [checked, Jonathan Tweet, 2019] "A disadvantage of a single large attack compared to two small attacks is that the single attacks are more likely to produce an unlikely result." [checked, James Introcaso, 2025] not missing is "core to the experience".
   - *For 2d12:* consistency is the mechanical truth of many small hits. A Tier 1 that still lands one arrow says it.
5. **The hits are description; the damage is one number.** [checked, David Sirlin, 2018] "Moves only have 1 hit unless there is a reason for them to have more".
   - *For 2d12:* the cheapest way, and the one Lucas is moving away from: he wants the hits to exist.
6. **A flat rider on every hit; and its enemy, anything subtracted from every hit.** [checked, Paizo, 2012] "DR pretty much ruins any attempt at 'death of a thousand cuts' style characters". Pathfinder 2e adds the two shots' damage together before resistance [read] "so that they're not utterly boned by resistances" [checked, 2026].
   - *For 2d12:* **Resist N is flat here, taken once from each Action's damage of that subtype (Chapter 1).** So three separate Weapon Attacks lose N three times, and three arrows inside one Action lose it once: our own rule already favors "one Action, several hits" over "several Actions".
7. **A meter cashed out in one blow** [quoted: Exalted's withering and decisive attacks; read: Pathfinder 2e's swashbuckler]. [checked, 2025] "Their damage is tied to a single finisher strike that if it misses, their damage is terrible".
   - *For 2d12:* this is the nova, the opposite of the brief.
8. **A meter that is held, or spent in small amounts** [read: 13th Age's momentum, Draw Steel's surges, ICON's one Combo token]. What gets called fiddly is a token per hit: [checked, EN World, 2010] "constant recharges and expenditures on a per hit basis is a bit much"; keep "no more than 1-3".
   - *For 2d12:* if "Arrows" is a pool, it is small (three at most) and moves once a turn, not once a hit.
9. **Several real rolls, made cheap** [quoted]. Savage Worlds throws all the shots' dice at once; Nimble's damage die is the attack. [checked, The Alexandrian, 2021] "If you're saving just four seconds per roll, that adds up to 5 minutes per combat". [checked, Nimble's designer] "Extra Attack makes turns take longer and longer".
   - *For 2d12:* our roll is 2d12 and a Tier lookup: the expensive kind. Fewer rolls, more hits a roll.
10. **A falling return on repeating, and a different card for the follow-up** [read: Pathfinder 2e's penalty with the press and flourish traits; 13th Age's monk: opening, flow, finishing; ICON's Combo]. Cooldowns "enforce some variety" but "tracking it is a pain" [checked, 2026].
   - *For 2d12:* we have both already: the At-will Banes (the falling return) and Round Frequency (the different card). The Archer's turn is built from them.

**On "Arrows" as a pool of dice:** rolling a usage die for each shot is "more cumbersome than actually tracking individual arrows" for a multi-shooter [checked, 2025]; The Black Hack rolls it after the fight. The nearest published match, Conan 2d20's Loads (spend one for an extra die on the attack and on the damage), was not read.
