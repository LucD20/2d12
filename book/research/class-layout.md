# The class chapter: how published RPGs lay out a class, and what is easy or hard to use

## 2026-11-17: for the class documents' layout (the Paladin first)

**The question.** How do published RPGs lay out a class (archetype, playbook, calling) chapter, and what do designers and players say is easy or hard to use: for building a character, for levelling up, and for finding one's abilities at the table? Covered: the order of sections; the class or progression table and its columns; where choices are presented and how; grouping by level, by type or alphabetically; cross-references; sidebars; quick-build boxes; one page / one spread rules; class summary blocks. How a single ability block is drawn is in `card-layout.md`, `card-look-published-books.md` and `rpg-layout-craft.md`: not repeated here (Gavin Norman's one-spread rule is quoted there and only extended here).

**How far to trust this file.** One pass, by an agent (about 75 steps); not re-checked by a second. No raw report: everything found is here.
- [read] = read in full on its page, or a book page rendered and looked at: the SRD 5.2 (the Paladin, PDF pages 52-56, page 53 seen as an image; the "Class Overview" and "Standard Array by Class" tables of chapter 2); Archives of Nethys' "Reading Class Entries" (Player Core) and one class page (the Commander); the Steel Compendium's Draw Steel classes chapter (the Censor in full) and "Making a Hero"; the 13th Age SRD's Paladin; Old-School Essentials' Cleric spread (an image, looked at); Daggerheart's official *Character Sheets and Guides* (the Bard's guide seen as an image) and the Daggerheart SRD's Guardian; the Blades in the Dark SRD's playbook template; Massif's Lancer talent data; Pelgrane's *13A Changes from 1e*; Gavin Norman's two posts.
- [checked] = an opinion read on its page, with its date: EN World (Rob Wieland's 2024 PHB review, 2024; "PHB Book Layout" thread, 2009), Critical Hits (2008), the Paizo playtest forum (2018), Know Direction (2019), D&D Beyond's forum and article (2024-25), Gaming Trend (2025), Geek Native (2020), Zatu, Thaumavore and Drolleries on Daggerheart's cards (2025-26), Sly Flourish (Apr 2025), Rob Donoghue's 13th Age read-through (Aug 2013), Schwalb Entertainment on Weird Wizard's paths (Mar 2023), Pyrite on ICON (playtest).

## Q1. D&D 5e (2014) and the 2024 revision

**2014, the order** [lead: memory of the 2014 PHB; the SRD 5.1 keeps the same order without the flavor]: a full-page painting and a short scene; a few paragraphs of identity; a short intro sidebar or two; a **class table** ("The Paladin": Level, Proficiency Bonus, Features, then per-class columns such as spell slots); "Creating a [class]" with a **"Quick Build"** paragraph (which ability score is highest, which background, which spells); "Class Features" (Hit Points, Proficiencies, Equipment as three run-in blocks), then each feature under its own heading **without its level in the heading** (the level is in the first sentence: "Beginning at 2nd level..."); then the subclasses at the end of the class ("Sacred Oaths"), each with its own features, which the main table lists only as "Sacred Oath feature". Spells are in a separate chapter.

**2024, the order** [read: SRD 5.2, the Paladin, PDF pages 52-56, page 53 seen as an image]:
1. Class name, then a **"Core Paladin Traits" table** at the top: Primary Ability, Hit Point Die, Saving Throw Proficiencies, Skill Proficiencies ("*Choose 2:* Athletics, Insight..."), Weapon Proficiencies, Armor Training, Starting Equipment ("*Choose A or B:* (A)... or (B) 150 GP"). Two shaded columns, label left, value right; the word *Choose* in italic marks each choice inside the table.
2. **"Becoming a Paladin ..."**: two short bulleted lists, "As a Level 1 Character" and "As a Multiclass Character" (which traits of the table you get).
3. **"Paladin Class Features"**: one sentence pointing to the table, then every feature under a heading that **starts with its level**: "Level 1: Lay On Hands", "Level 1: Spellcasting", "Level 2: Fighting Style"...
4. **The "Paladin Features" table**, spanning both columns at the foot of the first page: Level, Proficiency Bonus, Class Features, Channel Divinity, Prepared Spells, Spell Slots per Spell Level (1-5). Every row names its features; empty rows show "—"; the subclass levels say "Subclass feature".
5. Then the class's spell list, then **"Paladin Subclass: Oath of Devotion"** with its features also headed "Level 3: ...", "Level 7: ...".
- Choices printed inside a feature: "Level 2: Fighting Style. You gain a Fighting Style feat of your choice (see "Feats") [...] Instead of choosing one of those feats, you can choose the option below", and a **recommendation** in the text: "Guidance and Sacred Flame are recommended" (the 2024 book recommends a pick in several features; the Epic Boon line: "Boon of Irresistible Offense is recommended" in the Monk) [read].
- **The reception of 2024's class chapter** [checked, EN World's official advance review by Rob Wieland, 7 Aug 2024]: "I think the Class section is the best part of the book. The Class section starts off with a short chart listing the complexities of each one which I found very useful when 13th Age did it, too [...] Between the complexity chart, the illustrations and the high level summaries it seems easier than ever to sit down with a player, let them flip through this chapter, point at a picture or subclass name and set to work building a character." Against: "I would have liked to see further discussion of this in each Class section as playing a Fighter Champion is less complex than playing a Fighter Battle Master"; and the table names "Ability Score Improvement" while the book calls it a feat: "This is probably the thing that vexed me the most during the reading because I was confused for a while about how Feats worked". On the whole book: "The thing I love most about the 2024 Handbook is the organizational clarity."
- **The level in the heading, missed when it was gone** [checked, D&D Beyond forum, bug threads, 2024-25, via a search summary first, then the thread on "Becoming a..." read]: digital readers asked that "Class and Subclass feature headers should be updated to show the level next to it, just like the Physical copy" [lead: search summary of the thread]; another thread [checked] points out that the digital "As a Level 1 Bard" against the printed "As a Level 1 Character" confuses a multiclass reader ("you ARE a level 1 Bard, but you are NOT a level 1 Character").
- **Before the classes, chapter 2 summarizes them** [read, SRD 5.2]: "Step 1: Choose Class [...] The Class Overview table summarizes the classes": Class, **Likes ...** (one word or two: "Battle", "Performing", "Gods", "Weapons", "Defense"), Primary Ability, **Complexity** (Low / Average / High; Fighter Low, Paladin Average, Bard High). A **"Standard Array by Class"** table gives each class's six scores ready-made ("The table puts the highest scores in a class's main abilities").
- **Feats moved out of the class** (Fighting Style is now a feat category) [checked, D&D Beyond's character-creation article, 2024]: "Previously, the Fighter's Fighting Styles were listed in their class features rather than being separated out into feats." The choice now sends the reader to another chapter.

## Q2. Pathfinder 2e (2019) and the Remaster (Player Core, 2023)

**The order, as the book defines it** [read: Archives of Nethys, "Reading Class Entries", Player Core]: an intro; **"Playing the Class"** (roleplaying sidebars); **Key Attribute**; **Hit Points**; **Initial Proficiencies**; the **Advancement Table** ("The first column of the class table indicates a level, and the second column lists each feature your character receives when they reach that level. The 1st-level entry includes a reminder to select your ancestry and background"); **Class Features** ("An ability gained at a higher level lists the required level next to the ability's name"), which includes reminders of the generic slots (Class Feats, Skill Feats, General Feats, Skill Increases, Attribute Boosts, Ancestry Feats, each a short paragraph); then **the class feats at the end of the entry**, "by level". "Your character's class entry also provides the information needed when they gain levels, so it will be a vital reference throughout the course of your campaign."
- **A class page on the reference site** [read: AoN, the Commander]: Key Attribute and Hit Points first, then a **"Key Terms"** sidebar (the class's trait words defined: Banner, Brandish, Flourish, Tactic), then "Roleplaying the [class]" in four modes (During Combat Encounters / Social Encounters / While Exploring / In Downtime), then "You Might..." and "Others Probably..." (three bullets each), then Initial Proficiencies, then the advancement table and features. The site splits the class into tabs: **Details | Feats | Sample Builds** (and class-specific tabs).
- **The advancement table repeats everything** [checked, Know Direction, "Randal Reads Pathfinder 2e: Ch 3 – Classes", Aug 2019]: "I still dislike the inclusion of every single thing in the advancement table (the ancestry feats, general feats, skill feats/increases, ability boosts are all included in each class' advancement table). I completely understand that it serves as a guide for new players and is meant to avoid needless flipping back and forth". He praises the sidebars: "From the list of feats by alpha (they are printed long form by level), to the key terms specific to the class [...] to the sample builds [...] they help break up the monotony of pages of crunch while helping me to avoid page flipping". The chapter opens with a two-page "how to read a class" section "with page numbers", "reminders of the items that get repeated in the class to avoid extra page flipping". Duplication's cost: "The feat Reach Spell is written up for each of the spell casting classes, it exists 5 times in this book."
- **Class feats apart from the class features** [checked, Paizo playtest forum, "Layout - How I use the book", Aug-Sep 2018]: "I liked the P1 layout of having all the feats together. If I'm building a character, I want all my feat options listed [...] Having the feats in the class section makes me flip through too many pages. I'm a fighter taking the rogue dedication? Now I must flip through separate sections for fighter feats, rogue feats, general feats... It's too much." Replies: "class feats aren't reeeally feats--they're like rage powers [...] class powers that you pick from by level to build your own class with"; "The real problem with Feats is that they are now calling everything a Feat."
- **Pathbuilder as evidence the book is hard to build from**: not verified (no page found that says so in so many words) [lead].

## Q3. D&D 4e (2008)

**The order** [lead: memory, consistent with a search summary of the 4e wiki]: class name and flavor; a **"Class Traits" box** (Role, Power Source, Key Abilities, Armor and Weapon Proficiencies, Implement, Bonus to Defense, Hit Points at 1st Level, per Level, Healing Surges, Trained Skills, **Build Options**, Class Features); "Creating a [class]" with **two named builds**, each a short block (Key Abilities, Suggested Feat, Suggested Skills, Suggested At-Will / Encounter / Daily Powers); "Class Features"; then the powers **grouped by level, then by frequency**: "Level 1 At-Will Prayers", "Level 1 Encounter Prayers", "Level 1 Daily Prayers", "Level 2 Utility Prayers", "Level 3 Encounter Prayers"..., then the paragon paths. The suggested builds prescribe only first-level choices [lead: search summary].
- **Reference vs reading** [checked, EN World, "PHB Book Layout - Good or No so Good", Jul 2009]: "the 4E PHB being good, IMO, for referencing things, but not good for getting the feel of the game just by reading through it [...] if you're looking for the feel of each class on an initial flip-through, the older set-up is better"; "Count me in as a fan of the PHB layout, a nice go-to book and easy to find stuff during the game"; "In-game, I just can't find fault with the PHB's reference power." Against: "particular powers are a pain in the ass to look up"; "A friggin index would have helped"; "All those descriptions of powers [...] feel like a card collector's album. The stuff is there, but why should you reference it, with all those power cards and item cards?" A suggestion: "provide the same information twice, in different layouts. The 'learning the rules layout' [...] and the 'quick reference layout'".
- **Building a character** [checked, Critical Hits, "Chatty's review", Jun 2008, and its comments]: "creating a character leads to a series of page hunting exercises where you dive in all over the book"; the fix asked for: "a series of tables with page-referenced Powers arranged by class and by level [...] followed by an alphabetical Powers index"; "having to remember that things like Hit Points and Healing Surges were found on the Class pages, rather than in a simple unified table". Others: "after a couple of characters the structure of the book feels very comfortable"; "I created a Tiefling Warlord and Tiefling Rogue tonight; both characters took about ten minutes". At the table: "What a lot of people do is print out some of the Power Cards already available online, slip them in plastic Sleeves [...] and keep track of used/unused ones with the cards."

## Q4. Draw Steel (MCDM, 2025)

**The order** [read: the Steel Compendium's text of *Draw Steel: Heroes*, chapter 5, the Censor in full]:
1. The chapter opens with the rules for reading abilities and the conditions, then **"Abilities in Class Tables"**: each advancement table has an Abilities column and a subclass-abilities column in which "Each ability is represented by a numeral noting the ability's Heroic Resource cost", e.g. "Signature, 3, 5, 7, 9" and "5, 9". Then **"Quick Build Gold Icons"**: "Abilities granted by your class that are quick build options are indicated by a gold icon to the left of their name."
2. Each class: a short intro in second person ("As a censor, you're at your best against the strongest foes"), an epigraph; **"Basics"** as labeled lines (Starting Characteristics with arrays to choose, Weak / Average / Strong Potency, Starting Stamina, Stamina per level, Recoveries, Skills "(Quick Build: Intimidate, Religion.)").
3. **"[Class] Advancement Table"**: Level, Features (named, including "7-Wrath Ability"), Abilities, Order Abilities.
4. **"1st-Level Features"**, then "2nd-Level Features", "2nd-Level Order Features", "2nd-Level Order Ability", "3rd-Level Features"..., to 10th. **Each level is a section; the subclass's features sit inside the level** (each split by subclass name), so a level-up is read in one place.
5. The subclass is chosen inside a level 1 feature, as a short list of one paragraph each: "Exorcist: You specialize in hunting [...] Oracle: [...] Paragon: [...]" and "(Quick Build: Paragon.)". Then "Your censor order is your subclass".
6. **Ability choices by cost**: "Censor Abilities": "Choose one signature ability from the following options. Signature abilities can be used at will. (Quick Build: Your Allies Cannot Save You!)", then "Choose one heroic ability from the following options, each of which costs 3 wrath", then 5, each followed by the two or three full ability blocks; later levels repeat the pattern ("7-Wrath Ability: Choose one...").
- **Quick Build, as a rule** [read, "Making a Hero"]: "Each option you can choose for your hero at 1st level includes a parenthetical selection labeled 'Quick Build.' This is for players who want to build a character faster without reading through all the available options, by choosing the most straightforward and archetypal option".
- **The reception** [checked, Gaming Trend's review, 2025]: "For someone completely new to Draw Steel, it's a marathon of decision making [...] All of this can easily take over an hour"; "many of these things not being on the same pages or even the same sections, so a whole lot of book flipping or pdf scrolling is required [...] the arrangement is likely about as optimized as it's possible to be"; "Now you pick your class. And once you have a class, then it's on to subclass, signature abilities, heroic abilities, and kits [...] it's a lot to throw at even veteran players"; one tab color for the whole classes third of the book "feels like a missed chance to make all the page flipping a little better". EN World (2026, in `rpg-layout-craft.md`): "the abilities are actually pretty well laid out in practice".

## Q5. Other games

**13th Age (Pelgrane, 2013; 2e 2024)** [read: the 13th Age SRD, the Paladin page]. Order: Ability Scores, Backgrounds (example list), Gear, Gold Pieces, an Armor table and Melee / Ranged Weapons tables (the class's own numbers), **"Level Progression"** (columns: Level, Total Hit Points, Total Feats by tier, Class Talents, Level-up Ability Bonuses, Damage Bonus From Ability Score), then a **"Stats" block** that computes the class's numbers as formulas (Initiative "Dex mod + Level"; AC "16 + middle mod of Con/Dex/Wis + Level"; PD; MD; hit points; Recoveries; Recovery Dice; Backgrounds; Icon Relationships; Talents "3"; Feats "1 per Level"), then **Basic Attacks** (the class's two attack blocks), **Class Features**, then **"Class Talents: Choose three of the following class talents"**, printed **alphabetically**, each with its **Adventurer / Champion / Epic feat upgrades printed under it**. Powers and spells (for classes that have them) follow by level ("1st Level Spells"...) [lead: memory]. The 1e class chapter orders the classes from simplest to most complex, with a note on how each plays [lead: two search summaries; the EN World 2024 review [checked] says the 2024 PHB's complexity chart was "very useful when 13th Age did it, too"]. The 2e changes note [read, Pelgrane's *13A Changes from 1e* PDF]: the paladin "is still a very simple class"; the 2e book adds "two pages of an annotated character sheet as character creation guidelines"; "As in 1E, the index is also the glossary."

**Old-School Essentials** [read: the Cleric spread, Classic Fantasy, an image from Necrotic Gnome reproduced on Geek Native, Feb 2020, looked at]. **Left page, text; right page, tables.** Left: the class name in a large display face; a **tinted summary box** (Requirements, Prime requisite, Hit Dice, Maximum level, Armour, Weapons, Languages: one bold label per line); a two-line description; then short headed sections (Combat, Divine Magic, Turning the Undead, After Reaching 9th Level), each made of **bold run-in items** ("Holy symbol:", "Deity disfavour:", "Magical research:", "Spell casting:"). Right: **"Cleric Level Progression"** (Level, XP, HD, THAC0, five saving throws, spells per day by spell level) with a one-line legend for the abbreviations, the class's own sub-table (Turning the Undead) and its result key, one piece of art. Nothing of the class continues on another spread; spells are in another book. Norman [checked, 2018 and 2019]: "all material relating to a topic should appear on the same page or spread. Thus, a very effective measure of the rules-complexity of a topic is simply: how much space does it take up when laid out?"; trimming to fit "forced me to consider two things: Which abilities can be simplified? [...] Which abilities are really the core of this class". The page size matters: "A spread of two Letter sized pages is a very different matter to a spread of two Digest sized pages". A GM [checked, Geek Native, Feb 2020]: "The physical book contains everything I need to run a thief when I open it to pages 34 and 35"; Forbidden Lands, for the same questions, sends him to "page 64 (which leads to page 125 which leads to page 63)".

**Daggerheart (Darrington Press, 2025)** [read: the official *Character Sheets and Guides* PDF, Aug 2026 version, the Bard's guide page seen as an image; the Daggerheart SRD's Guardian page]. The class in the book is short: an intro, then a summary block (Domains, Starting Evasion, Starting Hit Points, Class Items, **Suggested Traits, Suggested Primary, Suggested Armor**), the Hope Feature, the Class Feature, "Subclasses: Choose either the Stalwart or Vengeance subclass", Background Questions, Connections. **The abilities themselves are not in the class: they are domain cards**, chosen from the class's two domains by level, and subclass cards (Foundation, Specialization, Mastery). **Each class has its own sheet and a one-page "Character Guide"**: the suggested picks again, an inventory with "THEN CHOOSE BETWEEN: a Minor Health Potion OR a Minor Stamina Potion", description words to circle, the questions, and **the whole level-up as a menu of checkboxes in three tier columns** ("Choose two options from the list below and mark them"; "Take an upgraded subclass card. Then cross out the multiclass option for this tier"). The cards at the table [checked]: for (Zatu's review, 2025-26): "This is normally where I'd hit the rulebook panic spiral: Do I have anything that helps here? Is it a spell? A feature? What page?"; "you're often sharing a book [...] At Session Zero, we pulled the cards for our characters and built 'character sheets' using clear 9-card binder pages [...] So I could use my character without hunting" (Zatu's review, 2025-26); "two players who were new to not just Daggerheart, but tabletop rpgs in general [...] loved the cards. Because they were tactile and visual, it helped them remember their abilities and keep track of their options" (Drolleries, Aug 2025). Against (Thaumavore, Jun 2025): "spreading domain cards across a table and 'shopping' for abilities provides a tangible sense of character building that feels more accessible than poring through rulebooks", but "During combat, players flip through individual cards to verify ability details [...] This card-by-card checking is just slower than scanning a character sheet"; "Ten active cards per player can dominate table space"; "Checkboxes easily track which five domain abilities are currently active, while a complete character overview allows players to scan their entire capability set at once"; he moved his cards' text to a sheet: "this doesn't look as pretty, but it is WAY easier to manage at the table."

**Blades in the Dark (Forged in the Dark)** [read: the SRD's "Character Playbook" page, a template for designers]. A playbook's parts, in order: a description; **XP triggers**; **templates** ("If you want some guidance when you assign your four starting action dots and special ability, use one of these templates. Example 1. 4 Action Dots assigned plus a special ability" ×4: the quick build); friends and rivals (five); **"Choose seven special abilities for your playbook"**. Each special ability is written twice: **the short rule as it appears on the sheet**, then a longer paragraph of explanation and a question ("When you imbue yourself with arcane energy, how do you do it?"). The sheet is the menu: the abilities are printed on it with a box to mark [lead: memory of the sheets]. **Apocalypse World / PbtA playbooks** [lead: search summaries only]: "the first page generally runs you through character creation from top to bottom"; players "learn how Apocalypse World shapes the conversation during character creation".

**Lancer (Massif Press, 2019)** [read: Massif's own data files, `lancer-data/lib/talents.json`]: 29 pilot talents, alphabetical (Ace, Brawler, Crack Shot...), each with **a one-line "terse" summary** ("Improve your flight systems for rapid repositioning and extreme mobility") and **three named ranks** (Ace: Acrobatics, Afterburners, Supersonic), taken in order. Whether the printed book shows the terse line: not checked [lead]. COMP/CON, Massif's free app, marks "which weapons are affected by talents with little chevrons you can hover over" [lead: a build guide's search summary].

**Shadow of the Demon Lord / Shadow of the Weird Wizard (Schwalb)** [checked, Schwalb Entertainment, "A Closer Look at Paths", Mar 2023]: the path system "'chunks' the mechanical widgets that describe the ways characters improve over time"; a path is a short block whose benefits sit under headings by level ("Level 3 Druid", "Level 4 Druid", "Level 6 Druid", "Level 9 Druid"); one path is chosen at each tier (novice at 1, expert at 3, master at 7), "Paths have no requirements". "People just love being able to start as one thing, choose to become something else, and become something else once more." Whether each path fits one page in print: not checked [lead].

**ICON (Massif Press, playtest)** [checked, Pyrite's "First Impressions", playtest era]: "The player-facing sections are all readable, though the book's navigability is mixed at best"; "Color is used to differentiate elements in the text, which is an accessibility issue"; "No character sheets are provided"; the jargon's weight ("Action, attack, boon, effect, dash, phasing [...] finishing blow, slay"). The job page's structure was not seen [lead: a search summary says each class's jobs carry "reminders of the relevant rules for its abilities"].

**Not seen, from memory or search summaries only** [lead]: **Shadowdark** (each class on about one page: weapons, armor, hit points, bold run-in features, then a **2d6 table of talents rolled at odd levels**, so the choice is a roll, not a menu; a third-party variant lets "groups that require more choice" spend points instead); **Dragonbane** (each profession lists its key attribute, skills, Heroic Ability and gear to roll, with a name table; reviewers time creation from "15 minutes" to "around an hour for 4 players"); **Cypher System** (the character is a sentence, "I am an [adjective] [noun] who [verbs]", descriptor, type and focus chosen "in any order", type abilities picked from a list each tier); **Pathfinder Beginner Box** (classes to level 5 with fewer feats each); **Apocalypse World** playbooks (moves printed on the sheet with a box; "choose two" lists).

## Q6. Presenting choices: what the books do and what is said about it

- **Summaries before the full text.** At the chapter level: 2024 D&D's Class Overview (Likes, Primary Ability, Complexity) [read]; 13th Age's classes "listed in order of complexity with a note on how each one plays. Super practical, super useful" (Rob Donoghue, Aug 2013 [checked]); Wieland: the complexity chart cuts "down on players being dissatisfied with their characters if they know going in just how involved they'll be" [checked]. At the option level: Draw Steel's subclass list is one paragraph per order inside the feature [read]; Lancer's data gives each talent a one-line "terse" summary [read]; Pathfinder 2e's class feats sidebar lists the feats alphabetically with their level while the full text runs by level [checked, Know Direction]; the 2024 subclass "elevator pitch on why playing that character is fun or cool" [checked, Wieland]; Pathfinder's "You Might... / Others Probably..." and Daggerheart's background questions describe play, not mechanics [read].
- **The marked default (quick build).** Draw Steel puts "(Quick Build: ...)" in every level 1 choice and a gold icon on the quick-build ability: "for players who want to build a character faster without reading through all the available options, by choosing the most straightforward and archetypal option" [read]. 2024 D&D writes "X and Y are recommended" inside the feature and gives a Standard Array by Class [read]. Daggerheart prints "Suggested Traits / Primary / Armor" in the class and again on the class's guide page [read]. Blades gives four "templates" of starting dots and ability [read]. 4e gave two named builds per class with suggested powers for level 1 only [lead]. 2014 D&D had a "Quick Build" paragraph per class [lead: memory]. Sly Flourish's quick build for 2024 defaults a feat ("for a quick character build, default to the Tough feat") [checked, Apr 2025].
- **Choices on the sheet.** Daggerheart prints its level-up as a checkbox menu by tier on each class's guide page ("Choose two options from the list below and mark them"), with an either-or for gear ("a Minor Health Potion OR a Minor Stamina Potion") [read]. Blades prints the special abilities on the playbook to be marked [lead]. 13th Age 2e teaches creation with "two pages of an annotated character sheet as character creation guidelines" [read]. Donoghue on why playbooks can repeat everything: "if the character chapters were entirely self contained (in the style of *World playbooks [...]) then it would make a lot of sense to repeat information" [checked].
- **Where the choice sits.** Inside the level that grants it (2024 D&D's "Level 2: Fighting Style"; Draw Steel's "3rd-Level Features ... 7-Wrath Ability: Choose one heroic ability from the following options") [read], against apart from it (Pathfinder 2e's class feats at the end of the class; 2024's Fighting Style as feats in the Feats chapter) [read]. The complaint is always the second: "Having the feats in the class section makes me flip through too many pages" came from a reader who wanted all feats in one place, while Paizo kept class feats in the class [checked]; "I was confused for a while about how Feats worked" (Wieland on ASI against feats) [checked].
- **Too many decisions at once.** Draw Steel: "a marathon of decision making [...] easily take over an hour"; "it's a lot to throw at even veteran players" (Gaming Trend) [checked]. 4e: "a series of page hunting exercises" (Critical Hits, 2008) [checked]. Shadowdark's answer is to roll [lead]. A pick of one from two or three, as such, was not the subject of any complaint found; the complaints are about the number of separate steps and places.
- **No designer essay on side-by-side comparison of two options, on decision trees, or on "choose this if..." lines was found.** The nearest are the summary tables above and the quick-build marks.

## Q7. Finding one's abilities at the table

- **What players do instead of the book.** Power cards in sleeves (4e: "print out some of the Power Cards [...] slip them in plastic Sleeves [...] and keep track of used/unused ones" [checked, 2008]); Daggerheart's cards in a nine-pocket binder page (Zatu [checked]) or moved back onto a sheet (Thaumavore [checked]); the sheet as an index: "write down the page number of every notable character feature on your character sheet [...] Treat your character sheet like a custom index of the book. Looking stuff up in the book isn't a failure" (Sly Flourish [checked]); apps (COMP/CON, Pathbuilder, D&D Beyond) [lead]; a Paizo forum poster: "being able to make cards or cheat sheets will be a must" [lead: search summary].
- **By level against by type.** 4e grouped powers by level, then frequency ("Level 1 Encounter Prayers") [lead]; Draw Steel by level, with the level's abilities grouped by cost [read]; 2024 D&D by level, in the heading itself ("Level 1: Lay On Hands") [read]; 13th Age's talents alphabetical with their feats nested, its spells by level [read / lead]; Lancer's talents alphabetical [read]. Grouping by level serves the level-up; the alphabet or the type serves the lookup; the books that print the level in the heading (2024, PF2: "An ability gained at a higher level lists the required level next to the ability's name" [read]) carry both. The 2024 digital edition's missing level in the heading drew a request to restore it [lead: search summary of the D&D Beyond thread].
- **Indexes and lists.** 4e's missing power index was the most repeated complaint: "a series of tables with page-referenced Powers arranged by class and by level [...] followed by an alphabetical Powers index"; "A friggin index would have helped" [checked]. 13th Age: "the index is also the glossary" [read]. Pathfinder 2e: a two-page "how to read a class" with page numbers; key terms repeated in each class and at the back [checked].
- **One place for the whole class.** OSE's spread [read; checked]; "The physical book contains everything I need to run a thief when I open it to pages 34 and 35" [checked]; Pathfinder 2e repeats generic slots in every class table "to avoid needless flipping" [checked]. The price: "over 12 core classes, that is a couple of pages" (Know Direction) [checked]; Donoghue: repetition is "burning space that might be used for something else" [checked]. Draw Steel's one tab color for the whole classes third "feels like a missed chance" [checked].

## Q8. Summary blocks and progression tables

- **The summary block, by game** [read unless marked]: OSE (Requirements, Prime requisite, Hit Dice, Maximum level, Armour, Weapons, Languages); 2024 D&D "Core Traits" (Primary Ability, Hit Point Die, Saving Throws, Skills with "Choose 2", Weapons, Armor, Starting Equipment "Choose A or B"); Pathfinder 2e (Key Attribute, Hit Points, then Initial Proficiencies as a separate list); Draw Steel "Basics" (Starting Characteristics with arrays, Potencies, Stamina, Recoveries, Skills with Quick Build); 13th Age "Stats" (each number as a formula, after the table); Daggerheart (Domains, Starting Evasion, Starting Hit Points, Class Items, Suggested Traits / Primary / Armor); 4e "Class Traits" with Role, Power Source and Build Options [lead]. Common to all: **label left, value right, one line each, at the top of the class** (13th Age puts it after the table). Choices inside the block are marked by a word: *Choose 2*, *Choose A or B*, "(Quick Build: ...)".
- **The progression table's columns**: always Level and a Features (names) column; then the class's counting columns: 2024 D&D (Proficiency Bonus, Channel Divinity, Prepared Spells, spell slots), OSE (XP, HD, THAC0, five saves, spells per level), 13th Age (Total Hit Points, Total Feats, Class Talents, Level-up Ability Bonuses, Damage Bonus), Draw Steel (Features, Abilities as costs "Signature, 3, 5", subclass Abilities as costs), Pathfinder 2e (Your Level, Class Features, with every generic feat and boost listed in the row). Placement: the SRD 5.2 spans the table across both columns at the foot of the class's first page [read; the printed PHB not seen]; OSE on the right page, facing the text [read]; Draw Steel right after Basics [read].
- **Small conventions seen**: "—" in empty cells (2024, OSE) [read]; a legend for abbreviations under the table (OSE's "D: Death / poison; W: Wands") [read]; the subclass levels named "Subclass feature" in the main table (2024) [read] or given their own column (Draw Steel) [read]. No class-specific advice on table rules (vertical lines, shading) was found beyond what `rpg-layout-craft.md` already holds.

### Takeaways

1. **The recurring order is: identity (a paragraph) → summary block → progression table → features in level order → the choices → (subclasses).** 2024 D&D, Pathfinder 2e, Draw Steel and OSE all put the summary block and the table before any feature text; 13th Age puts its "Stats" after the table; 4e put "Class Traits" first and builds second.
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: the Paladin document already follows this order (intro, "at a glance", "by Level", then Level 1).
2. **The newest big books moved to level-first everywhere, with the level printed in each heading** ("Level 1: Lay On Hands"; "3rd-Level Features"; PF2's level beside the name). Draw Steel goes furthest: the subclass's features and the level's ability choices sit inside the level's section, so a level-up reads in one place.
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: the Paladin groups by kind (Arts, Repertoire, Escalation) after Level 1; these books group by level and name the kind in the heading.
3. **The most repeated complaint is having to go to another place to finish a choice** (PF2's class feats at the end and general feats in another chapter; 2024's Fighting Style as feats; 4e's powers without an index; Draw Steel's ancestry, culture and career across sections; Forbidden Lands' chain of page references). The opposite pole, OSE's spread, is praised in exactly those words ("everything I need [...] pages 34 and 35").
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: a class document that holds its own cards is the OSE case; the pointers it keeps (Escalation, Reactions, Stages) are the PF2 case.
4. **Repetition is the accepted price of no flipping, and reviewers name its cost** ("a couple of pages", "burning space"); Donoghue's line is that self-contained chapters (playbooks) justify it, half-contained ones do not.
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: an HTML document has no page budget, so the cost these sources name (pages) does not apply; length and scrolling would be its form.
5. **Every current book marks a default pick.** Draw Steel's "(Quick Build: ...)" on every level 1 choice plus a gold icon; 2024's "X is recommended" and Standard Array by Class; Daggerheart's Suggested Traits / Primary / Armor; Blades' four templates. No source found argues against marking a default.
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: the Paladin's selections (two Hallmark Actions, two Reactions, three Arts, two Escalation 2) carry no marked default today.
6. **A one-line summary per option exists in several games but no designer was found arguing for it in writing**: Lancer's "terse" line, Draw Steel's one-paragraph subclass list, PF2's alphabetical feat sidebar, 2024's Likes / Complexity table, 13th Age's complexity order (praised twice, 2013 and 2024).
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: the level list ("Binding Light or Lay On Hands") names the options without saying what each does.
7. **Players move their abilities out of the book to play**: sleeved cards (4e), binder pages (Daggerheart), a sheet that carries page numbers (Sly Flourish: "Treat your character sheet like a custom index of the book"), or an app. Cards help new players remember options; a sheet scans faster for experienced ones (Thaumavore).
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: the Foundry system is 2d12's app; the book's class document is mostly read at creation and level-up.
8. **The choice on the sheet (Daggerheart's tier checkboxes, PbtA playbooks) turns the level-up into marking boxes**; it works where the options are short enough to print whole.
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: the Paladin's options are full cards, too long for a sheet; a sheet could carry names and page or anchor references only.
9. **Summary blocks are the same everywhere: label left, value right, one fact a line, choices marked by a word** (*Choose 2*, *Choose A or B*, Quick Build). Progression tables always carry Level and Features, then counting columns; empty cells "—"; abbreviations get a legend under the table.
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: the "at a glance" box already has this shape.
10. **Size is a design limit, by one publisher's statement**: OSE's spread "serves as a limitation on class complexity" and forced the questions "Which abilities can be simplified? [...] Which abilities are really the core of this class".
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: the Paladin has 19 cards for levels 1-3; an OSE class holds a handful of run-in paragraphs and its tables on two pages.
