# Gear and hands in combat: drawing, stowing, free hands, swapping weapons

## 2026-11-14: for the Advance rule (Interact during an Advance; should drawing and stowing stay in it?)

**The question (Lucas):** with "you can use a single Interact Action during an Advance", should drawing and stowing stay part of Interact? How do other games handle gear and hands in a fight?

### Takeaways
1. **D&D 5e ties one free object interaction to your move or your action** (draw or sheathe one weapon, open a door). A second costs an action. Crawford's line: it is only an interaction when it takes "appreciable effort". Posters credit the cap with keeping weapon swaps cheap but not free every round; the 2024 rules made swapping easier still (draw or stow as part of an attack).
2. **Pathfinder 2e charges an action (Interact) for every draw**, and that is its most-cited friction: two-weapon fighters "pretty much need" Quick Draw (only some classes get it), Interact triggers Reactive Strike near an enemy, and posters ask for drawing to be a general free action.
3. **Draw Steel makes drawing a weapon a free maneuver** (with picking up an object or handing one to an ally): unlimited, stopped only by what stops maneuvers.
4. **The view across forums:** gear rules work best when they mostly stay out of the way, but they should still bite in an ambush or a mid-fight weapon swap ("swapping weapons mid-fight is where you can hurt yourself").
   - *For 2d12:* our Interact (1 AP) already covers draw, stow, swap, grab, open, help up, wake. Folding one Interact into the Advance is 5e's "interaction with your move", but still paid (1 AP), so it removes the friction of breaking the move without making gear free. Drawing and stowing stay inside it; Expert Mastery (draw, stow or regrip once a turn without Interact) keeps its value; Release (0 AP) and Regrip (no Handle) stay separate.
