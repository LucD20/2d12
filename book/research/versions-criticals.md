# Versions (one ability at several costs) and Critical lines on cards

## 2026-11-16: for the class redesign, asked by Lucas during the Archer

**The question (Lucas):** "Are we underutilizing the multiple actions here? Make a quick search about it, see if we need to give more versions to Actions. Same with critical, we are likely underutilizing it."

**How far to trust this file.** One quick pass, by an agent (about 24 steps); not re-checked. [read] = rules text read in full (Pathfinder 2e's Heal and Blazing Bolt, Draw Steel's critical hit, Lancer). [checked] = an opinion read on its page (EN World, Paizo's forum, Sly Flourish). Not found at source: 4e's augmentable powers and its "Critical:" lines, Pathfinder 2e's critical specializations. Reddit and Tapatalk refuse readers. The report: `raw/2026-11-16-versions-criticals.md`. Our own earlier note on Versions: `ability-scope.md`, takeaway 6.

### Versions

1. **Praised as an idea, rare in practice, and the cheap Version goes unused where the Versions only differ in size.** [checked, EN World, 2020] "the idea to make the choice between 1, 2 or 3 actions interesting is overall not accomplished... our wizard never casts anything but three-action magic missiles". [checked, Paizo, 2019] "I'm wondering how many people have ever actually used Magic Missile with only one action". 4e's augmentable powers, the same way [checked, EN World, 2010]: "very few psionic powers... worth augmenting with 6 PP when you can just spam the Level 1 powers at 2 PP augment".
   - *For 2d12:* this is what `ability-scope.md` found on our old Smite (1 / 2 / 3 AP: "biggest Smite I can afford, then attacks"). A Version that is only "more of the same" isn't a choice.
2. **The Versions that read as a choice differ in kind.** Pathfinder 2e's Heal [read]: 1 action touches, 2 actions reach 30 feet and heal 8 more, 3 actions heal everyone in an emanation. Posters name a different use for each.
   - *For 2d12:* our two Versions cards are of this kind (Judge: place the mark, or move it; Lay On Hands: Guard, or Guard and a Rally Point), and the Arts' **Modes are the same idea at one cost**: three different things, not three sizes.
3. **A second cost shared by every Version kills the cheap one** (the spell slot is spent whichever size is cast).
   - *For 2d12:* Frequency is that shared cost: a Round card's 1 AP Version spends the round's use as the 3 AP one does. An Encounter card with a cheap Version would almost never be used cheap.
4. **Why there are few, in players' words** [checked, Paizo, 2019]: a 3-action ability must be very good because "you cant do anything else during your turn"; the game "would have to account for the most powerful version"; an unpopular 3-action Cone of Cold was cut after the playtest. Other games use a rule of the turn (Lancer's Quick and Full actions) or one "Spend N:" line (Draw Steel) instead of three written Versions.
   - No rule of thumb for how many abilities should have Versions was found.

### Critical lines

5. **Games with rare criticals write the critical once, for everything, and make it big.** Draw Steel [read]: a natural 19 or 20 on 2d10 (3% of rolls) with an action "lets you immediately take an additional main action"; no ability has a critical line of its own. [checked, Mike Mearls, 2010, via Sly Flourish] on why a rare critical can carry a lot: "it only happens 1 in 20, it's random, players can't control it, let's throw a lot of effects there."
   - *For 2d12:* our Critical is 2.8% of rolls (Doubles on a result already Tier 3, at Weapon +2 against DC 13): Draw Steel's rate. We already write one global effect (Escalation +1, once an encounter). A line on each card would be read far more often than it happens.
6. **The excitement written on each ability sits on results that happen often:** Draw Steel's tier 3, Pathfinder 2e's degrees of success, 13th Age's "natural even" triggers.
   - *For 2d12:* our Tier 3 (about one roll in five) is that place, and the redesign's rule already says a Tier 3 must visibly beat a Tier 2.
7. **A rare critical rider is liked as a moment and discounted as value.** [checked, Pathfinder 2e playtest] "I keep forgetting about it... It's been cool when it has come up though." [checked, Paizo] "Critical feats are worth it if you (A) have a high crit chance AND (B) don't outright kill things on a crit."
8. **Rarity keeps a strong critical safe; trouble starts when the rate can be raised** (critical-fishing builds). Effects are liked more than bigger numbers.
