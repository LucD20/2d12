# A caster class without bigger numbers: Draw Steel's Elementalist

**Date:** 2026-11-18. **Asked by Lucas:** before the Magic design goes on, "look through our rules, then make sure to remember that my system grows horizontally, and these have to fit Talents and Classes. [...] Make any research or ask me anything about it before we proceed." Claude chose one question to research: **how a game whose shape 2d12 already borrows (three Tiers, a rising fight resource, abilities as cards) builds a caster class around elements, lasting spells and keywords.**

**What was read.** Small and partly second-hand.
- **[checked, through a summarizing reader]** Steel Compendium (Draw Steel's rules text): the Elementalist's Persistent Magic and Elemental Specialization features. The tool returned paraphrases with short quotes; the class's first-level page did not load its text.
- **[checked, through a summarizing reader]** One fan's class walk-through (altoholism.blogspot.com, "Draw Steel Class Deep Dive: Elementalist"): the essence numbers and the level list. A single fan's reading.

**Draw Steel's rules, not 2d12's. Nothing here is decided.**

## 1. Takeaways

1. **Elements are keywords on abilities, and the subclass is a passive keyed on the keyword.** The Elementalist chooses a specialization: earth, fire, green or void. At level 1 each gives one small standing benefit to abilities with that keyword: Earth: "whenever you use an Earth and Magic ability" your stability rises by 1; Fire: Fire and Magic abilities deal +1 damage; Green: damaging with a Green and Magic ability that costs essence gives someone temporary Stamina; Void: Magic, Ranged, Void abilities reach 2 squares further. **An ability is tagged with two words (its element, and Magic), and features read the pair.**
2. **The resource is the fight's own income.** Essence: 2 at the start of each turn (3 at level 7, 4 at level 10), and 1 more the first time each round that someone near takes typed damage. Abilities cost essence; there are no slots and no daily limit. (Draw Steel's every class works this way; the Elementalist's twist is the next point.)
3. **A lasting spell is paid from income, not from a duration.** "Persistent" abilities carry a value ("Persistent 1"). You choose to maintain one when you cast it; **maintaining lowers the essence you gain at the start of each turn by that value.** You can't maintain into negative income; you can drop one at any time for free. **All of them end if you take damage of at least 5 × your Reason in one turn**, and at the end of the encounter. A creature can't be under two copies of the same persistent ability. The fan's note: maintaining "is more efficient than casting it multiple turns in a row".
4. **Growth is partly horizontal, partly not.** New abilities at most levels (more options); the essence income also rises (2, 3, 4), and Fire's benefit is a flat damage bonus. Draw Steel is not a no-bloat game.
5. **Only four elements in the core book,** and the fan notes two of the classical four get most of the attention: even a professional team started narrow.

## 2. How it applies to 2d12

- **Takeaway 1 is Lucas's two-trait spell already in print.** "A [Fire, Spell] Action" read by a Feature or a Talent is how the Elementalist's subclass works, and it is the charter's rule 11 (a card that counts your Actions names a trait). It also shows what a keyed passive should be in 2d12's terms: Earth's and Void's are fine (a property, a reach); Fire's "+1 damage" is the silent bonus the charter forbids.
- **Takeaway 3 is a complete answer for "things that stand"** (decisions 9 and 10 in `book/magic-design.md`): no duration to count; a price paid every turn; a single, visible way to break it (one turn's damage at or above a threshold). 2d12 has no income to tax, but it has two things that work the same way: **AP** ("while you hold it, you regain 1 AP less": this is exactly what Slowed already does, so it could be written "you are Slowed 1 while you hold it") and **unspent AP becoming Guard**. The break threshold is the Focus rule proposed in `dominions-magic.md` 4.4, here shipped in a published game.
- **Takeaway 2** supports a caster limited by the fight's clock rather than by a pool: 2d12's clock is Escalation, which is never spent, so it can gate (a spell or a step "needs Escalation 3") but can't be paid.
- **Takeaway 4 is the warning:** the easy way to make a caster grow is income and flat bonuses. 2d12's version has to grow by what the levels unlock.
- **Takeaway 5** agrees with Lucas's choice: define all eight Paths, write cards for three or four first.
