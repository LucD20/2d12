# A front-liner who is neither a damage dealer nor a damage sponge: holding a line, protecting, keeping allies up

## 2026-11-14: for the class redesign (MASTER-PLAN item 5), before any Paladin card

**The question (Lucas):** what makes a front-liner who is neither a damage dealer nor a damage sponge feel good; what "they stay in front so the squishies can stay back" needs from the rules; "make sure you don't go down".

**How far to trust this file.** This is the thinnest of the five notes: the search budget ran out and only GitHub could be opened. [read] = rules text read in full (Draw Steel, Lancer, a 13th Age derivative). [lead] = an opinion seen only as a search summary. [memory] = stated from memory, unverified. [mine] = Claude's reading. The full report: `raw/2026-11-14-E-frontliner.md`. The defender material of `tactical-combat.md` (takeaways 3 and 4) is not repeated.

### Takeaways

1. **Tabletop has no aggro; seven families replace it.** [mine, from the rules read]

   | Family | Example | Who keeps the choice | Its known complaint |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Punishment mark | Draw Steel's Judgment [read]; 4e's Divine Challenge | the GM | dead if the GM plays around its one trigger |
   | Soft taunt (a penalty) | Draw Steel's Taunted: a double bane [read]; 4e's -2 | the GM, at a cost | a small penalty is ignored [lead] |
   | Hard taunt (must attack) | Pathfinder 1's Antagonize [memory] | nobody | "mind control"; the GM loses the monster [lead] |
   | Stickiness | 13th Age's disengage check [read]; Judgment's "can't shift" for 1 wrath [read] | the GM, but leaving is risky | the enemy must already be next to you |
   | Interception | 13th Age's intercept [read]; Lancer's Bonded [read] | the tank | the tank takes the full hit: it needs to be a sponge |
   | Mitigation for the ally | Draw Steel's Parry: half damage [read]; Lancer's Guardian cover [read] | the tank | invisible when it is passive |
   | Threat by being dangerous | "be scary enough to draw the heat" [lead] | the GM | needs steady damage |

   - *For 2d12:* the brief rules out two families (interception: "can't take hits for you"; threat: "isn't a consistent damage dealer") and Lucas's taste a third (a hard taunt takes the GM's choice; our Taunted is already the soft kind). **That leaves the mark, the soft taunt, stickiness and mitigation.** And the soft nova can carry the missing threat: if ignoring the Paladin loads his burst, ignoring him stops being the safe choice.
2. **What makes a front line real, strongest first.** [mine, from the rules read]
   1. **Movement simply ends next to the front-liner.** Lancer [read]: a character that becomes Engaged with a target of its size or larger "stop[s] moving immediately and lose[s] any unused movement"; Disengage is a Full action. 13th Age (a derivative) [read]: "If you move past someone who is not already engaged, they have the option to engage you and make you stop where they are"; one interception per enemy, and **an already engaged defender can't intercept** (numbers still beat the line).
   2. **Leaving costs the action economy** (Lancer's Full-action Disengage; 13th Age's failed disengage check loses the move).
   3. **Leaving costs damage** (the opportunity attack). The weakest: in Draw Steel [read] everyone has one and a 1-square shift avoids it; in 5e [lead] a smart enemy decides the tank "isn't worth it" and walks past.
   - *For 2d12:* **today nothing in our rules makes the Paladin's front line real.** There is no Opportunity Attack at Stage 0, no zone of control, an enemy's space is only Difficult Terrain, and Engaged only troubles [Ranged] Actions. "They stay in front so the squishies can stay back" is, in play, the GM's courtesy. The Paladin's low damage makes option 3 the wrong tool twice over. Options 1 and 2 fit, and our rules already hold the pieces: the defined moments "an enemy would enter a square adjacent to you or to an ally" and "an enemy moves closer to you" (Chapter 2), disrupting a Move Action, and Hindered ("the movement left is halved"). **With the Advance rule (one piece; the movement left is lost) stopping a move costs the enemy the rest of it**, and a movement Reaction fires once for the whole Move Action, the reactor choosing the square. A Paladin Reaction or Feature that ends or halves a move next to him is 13th Age's intercept in our words, limited the same way (one a round).
   - Also ours: **Frightened of you** makes every square that brings a creature closer to you Difficult Terrain. An enemy that fears the Paladin pays double to walk past him toward the back line. It is a line-holding condition we already have, on Rebuke today.
3. **"My best turn is the GM's turn": the three complaints of protector play, and their known answers.** [lead for the complaints, mine for the answers]
   - *Reactive:* give the protector a job on its own turn (placing the mark, taking a position), and let the reaction pay into its own resource.
   - *Invisible:* protection that **states a number or moves a token** is seen (Draw Steel's Parry shifts to the ally and halves the damage [read]; No Dying on My Watch moves to the ally, who spends a Recovery [read]). Flat defense bonuses and auras are not.
   - *Played around:* the enemy must lose something in both branches. [lead] 4e defenders are reported as most fun when sometimes ignored, because the punishment fires: "everyone who has played a 4e defender for long has seen their marks obeyed and disobeyed".
   - *For 2d12:* Beacon's Step (0R: step to the ally who is hit) and Bulwark of Faith's Slide move tokens: they are the visible kind and worth keeping as candidates. Beacon's +1 Defense Aura and Hallowed Stance's damage reduction are the invisible kind. And each protect Reaction could visibly load the burst (Nimble's pattern): then the round spent shielding shows on the table as a die waiting.
4. **How a soft front-liner stays up, best fit first.** [mine, from the rules read]
   1. It is paid when it protects (temporary Stamina, a resource on the trigger).
   2. It defends by reaction, not by a pool (one hit a round made smaller).
   3. **Depth instead of height:** the Censor has a front-liner's Stamina and the most Recoveries [read].
   4. It is not where the hit lands (4e's Swordmage [memory]): fits "can't take hits" but fights "stay in front".
   5. Thick armor and Health: the sponge.
   - *For 2d12:* the Paladin today is 20 / 10 Health (a Human: 35 against the Archer's 29, +21%), AD 14 against 13, Rally 6 against 5. A Standard's turn deals it 11.9 against the Archer's 13.3 (`stage0.md`). That is a modest height and a little depth. The unused AP's Guard (2 an AP) is our "pays itself for standing there". Part of the class numbers decision.
   - A warning [memory, the 5e Monk]: if self-defense and ally-defense draw on one scarce resource, the player keeps it for himself. Ours is the one Reaction AP: a Paladin Reaction "you or an ally" (Shield of Faith today) will be spent on himself. "An ally" only, as Lucas's draft already has it.
5. **Healing that players like better than plain healing.** [mine, from rules read and leads]
   - It doesn't eat the helper's turn (4e's minor action, Draw Steel's maneuver or triggered action).
   - **It is big enough to matter before 0.** [lead] 5e's yo-yo healing is blamed on a heal that is cheap, ranged and too small to matter until someone is down. A surge-sized heal (a quarter to a third of the ally's Health) makes healing the wounded ally correct.
   - It rides on something else (an attack, a mark, a move).
   - It spends a deep reserve (the ally's own, or the paladin's for them), so there is a daily cost.
   - It can fire on the hit (prevent the drop), not after the fall. In Draw Steel a dying hero can't Catch Breath alone [read]: someone else's trigger is the way back up.
   - *For 2d12:* Rally Points are that reserve and a Rally Point is that size (a quarter of the Pool: 9 for a Paladin, 8 for an Archer). Steel Yourself costs 2 AP and can't be used while Down; so **"the cheapest and fastest way in the party to let an ally use a Rally Point" is already a real support job**, and it needs no heal of the Paladin's own. This agrees with Lucas's target (healing through Rally Points) and his Lay On Hands draft.
6. **Whose reserve pays?** The Censor's My Life for Yours [read] and 4e's and 13th Age's Lay on Hands spend the **paladin's** own; our Lay On Hands spends the **ally's**.
   - *For 2d12:* a decision for Lucas. The ally's: support, and the party's Rally Points are the day's clock as today. The Paladin's own: sacrifice (he bleeds his depth for you), it works on an ally who has none left, and his Rally value becomes the tank stat. A card can also do one on the touch and the other on the Reaction.
