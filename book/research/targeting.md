# Targeting, and who a Reaction's target is

## 2026-11-15: for the class redesign, after Bulwark of Faith ("the target is the attacker, but everything it does is to the ally")

**The question (Lucas):** "check this whole targeting rule. Research it. See if we can make it less confusing. As I said, this is a can of worm in almost all systems."

**How far to trust this file.** One pass, by an agent; nothing re-checked by a second pass. [read] = rules text read in full from the game's data (4e's Compendium crawl, Pathfinder 2e on Archives of Nethys, Draw Steel's rules repository, the 5e SRD, Lancer's and ICON's data, 13th Age's SRD, Magic's rules). [checked] = a post read on its page (Paizo's forum, EN World). [lead] = seen only in a search summary. Reddit, Giant in the Playground and RPG.net refuse readers and were left alone. The full report with every quote: `raw/2026-11-15-targeting.md`.

### Takeaways

1. **The games with a Target line on every reaction have no recorded argument about it.** Draw Steel and 4e [read]. They choose the Target by where the card's main effect lands, and give the other creature a fixed role word.
   - *The Target is the protected creature, and the Trigger is written about "the target":* Draw Steel's Parry: "Melee 2 | Self or one ally. Trigger: A creature deals damage to the target. Effect: ... the target takes half the damage." Intercede: "Ranged 10 | One ally. Trigger: A creature makes a strike against the target." 4e's Interposing Shield: "Trigger: An ally adjacent to you is hit by an attack. Target: The triggering ally."
   - *The Target is the attacker, when the card is mainly a punishment:* 4e's Knightly Intercession: "Trigger: An enemy within 10 squares of you hits your ally ... Target: The triggering enemy. Effect: The triggering attack hits you instead of the ally." Draw Steel's No Dying on My Watch: "One enemy. Trigger: The target deals damage to an ally. Effect: You move ... toward the triggering ally ... The triggering ally can spend a Recovery ... the target is frightened of the triggering ally."
   - *The other creature is affected without being a target:* Draw Steel's Instant Retaliation halves the ally's damage (the target) and then makes "a power roll against the triggering creature".
   - Range and line of effect are owed to the Target only. Draw Steel: "To target a creature or object with an ability or effect ... you must have line of effect to that target."
   - *For 2d12:* this is the fix. Every Reaction gets a Target line chosen by its main effect; its Interrupt or Trigger says "the target"; the other creature is "the triggering creature" (already the book's word) or "the triggering ally".
2. **The game with no Target line on its reaction has the same argument every few years.** Pathfinder 2e's Champion [read]: "Trigger: An enemy damages your ally, and both are in your champion's aura." [checked, Paizo, 2020] "The ability does not say how it works, only the triggers (no targets specified for example)." One 2020 thread holds four readings: it needs sight, it needs awareness only, it needs line of effect only, it needs nothing. [checked, 2025, after the Remaster] "My GM ruled that you can't react while blind"; a reply: "Your Reaction does not target your ally, per se, and so it cannot fail."
   - *For 2d12:* a Reaction never goes without a Target line.
3. **No game read has a general rule that you must perceive what you react to.** Pathfinder 2e and 5e have it only for Ready ("observable by the character", "perceivable circumstance") [read], and players argue whether it extends: [checked] "That is a rule for Ready specifically." 4e writes sight on one reaction only, the opportunity attack: "you can see your enemy."
   - *For 2d12:* our perception rule (Lucas, 2026-11-15) is a rule the others lack, and it answers the question their forums ask. It is worth keeping, said once in the Reactions rule.
4. **Writing "a creature you can see" card by card is clear on each card and unclear across them.** 5e [read]: Protection: "When a creature you can see attacks a target other than you". [checked, EN World, 2024] "Half the spells say 'a target you can see' and the rest don't. It's sloppy." In Pathfinder a card that omits it is read as "then it isn't needed" [checked].
   - *For 2d12:* the requirement is general (in the rule), never repeated on some cards and not others.
5. **Two games state that being affected is not being targeted.** Magic, rule 115.10a [read]: "Just because an object or player is being affected by a spell or ability doesn't make that object or player a target." Gloomhaven: targeted only if it says so [read on a reference site].
   - *For 2d12:* the sentence to write next to "Targets of Effects": a creature a card names by a role word is affected, not targeted.
6. **A zone settles the range, not the sight.** Pathfinder's Remaster made it "both are in your champion's aura"; the 2025 "Reactions while blind" thread came after it.
