# Bringing back the dead: how other games do it, and where it goes wrong

**Date:** 2026-11-19. **Asked by Lucas** (magic record, decision 93): "I think bringing back the dead is necessary for the health of the game, but with extreme limits and likely just once. I like no revive spells but this is a big missed mechanic in games that do have it that I think is my job to fix."

**What was read, and how far to trust it.** One independent researcher; the full report with every source and link is `raw/2026-11-19-revival.md`. **This file is Claude's summary of that report; Claude did not reopen its pages.** Its marks are kept: [read] rules text on a page opened, [checked] an opinion on a page opened, [lead] a search summary, [memory] no page opened. Every page went through a reader that returns extracts, and four rules texts come from second-hand copies (Mercer's rules by Nerdist, Daggerheart by a fan site, Pathfinder 2e's Resurrect by a community copy, Warhammer by a blog): **recheck a quote on its page before it is printed.** About half the games are [memory] or [lead] only (AD&D, Old-School Essentials, D&D 3.5 and 4e, Blades in the Dark, Fabula Ultima, Burning Wheel, Savage Worlds, Mörk Borg, Forbidden Lands, Symbaroum, The One Ring, the video games); Shadow of the Demon Lord and Dragonbane were not found.

**Other games' rules and opinions, never 2d12's. Nothing here is decided.**

---

## 1. The pattern in D&D: the price moved from the character to the purse

| Edition | What a return cost | Trust |
|---|---|---|
| AD&D | A survival roll, 1 point of Constitution, and never more returns than your starting Constitution | [lead] |
| D&D 3.5, Pathfinder 1e | A 5,000 gp diamond and a lost level ("two permanent negative levels") | [memory] / [read] |
| D&D 4e | A ritual, 500 gp, a small penalty until three milestones | [lead] |
| D&D 5e | Revivify: a 300 gp diamond within a minute, no other cost. Raise Dead: 500 gp and a -4 that fades a point a long rest | [read] |

Each edition took the cost off the character and put it on gold and a few days of penalty, and the "revolving door" complaint grew as it did. DM David calls 5e's "D&D's most namby-pamby version of raise dead" [checked]. The Alexandrian notes that tables were already ignoring AD&D's limits [checked]: a limit too heavy is skipped, one too light means nothing.

## 2. The games that count, limit or refuse

- **13th Age counts on both sides** [read]: the cleric can resurrect "a limited number of times in your life". The first casting is one action; the second costs about half the caster's hit points and daily powers; the third must be a ritual; the fourth nearly kills the caster; "the fifth time you resurrect someone, that's the end of your story and you die." The one returned comes back weaker each time. **The closest printed model to "just once".**
- **Matt Mercer's rules** (Critical Role) [read, second-hand]: a ritual; up to three companions each contribute a check; the final DC is 10, lower for each contribution that works, **1 higher for each time the character has already returned**; "on a failed check, the soul does not return and the character is lost." Praised for the scene it makes; fans note nearly every ritual on the show succeeded.
- **Pathfinder 2e** [read, second-hand]: not a spell but an uncommon ritual: a day, gems by the target's level, two helpers, three checks; a week of penalties nothing shortens; a critical success leaves "a permanent mark from the afterlife"; a critical failure goes badly wrong.
- **Kept out of the spell list entirely:** Draw Steel (only "a special powerful item, such as a Scroll of Resurrection") [read]; Dungeon World (Last Breath: on a middling roll "Death himself will offer you a bargain"; its Resurrection always works but the GM names conditions) [read]; Daggerheart (the player picks a death move; avoiding death can cost a scar, a Hope slot crossed out for good) [read, second-hand]; Warhammer Fantasy (no resurrection: a small stock of Fate points spent to not die) [read, second-hand].
- **No return at all:** Pendragon (you play the heir), Ars Magica (outside the Limits of Magic), Mörk Borg, Forbidden Lands, Symbaroum [mostly memory].
- **Phoenix: Dawn Command** [checked]: death is how you advance, and "you only get to come back eight times".

## 3. Where it goes wrong

1. **The revolving door:** death stops meaning anything [checked, the Alexandrian].
2. **A tax in gold:** in 5e most characters "can afford the cost without borrowing" [checked].
3. **The dead character's player sits out,** worst when the return is a quest [checked, Gnome Stew].
4. **A penalty heavy enough to matter makes a new character the better deal.**
5. **The world stops making sense:** succession, inheritance, murder mysteries [checked].
6. **A die decides when a player stops playing a character:** the case for a return existing at all, and for giving the choice to the player [checked].
7. **What makes a return feel earned,** by these writers: the party takes part; it can fail; it leaves a mark that is a story and not a number (a character who "returned without his shadow"); it is scarce in the world; the next one is visibly harder.

**Across the list:** the returns writers praise combine a count, the party's part and a mark. The ones they mock are a short window, a fading penalty and a price in gold.

## 4. How it applies to 2d12 (observations, not rules)

Twelve shapes are in the raw report, each with its source and weakness. The ones that meet Lucas's words and hang on something 2d12 already has:

| Shape | From | Its weakness | What it could hang on in 2d12 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A count on the character** ("once") | AD&D, Mercer, Phoenix | "Once" needs a rule for the second death | **Wounds:** the Dying and Shattered Tables already add 1d6 for each Wound, a built-in "harder next time"; a Wound no Rest removes would be the mark |
| **A count on the one who revives, with a rising price** | 13th Age | A second reviver resets it unless the target is counted too | A Rally Point given up for good; the Paladin already unlocks other people's Rally Points |
| **A ritual the party performs, that can fail for good** | Mercer, Pathfinder 2e | Tables fudge the roll | **A Rest** (a day in a safe place), which already carries the Wound checks: the return happens in town |
| **A mark that is a trait, not a number** | Pathfinder 2e, Keith Baker | None named; the horizontal kind of cost | A Tag or a line on the sheet others can read |
| **A cost on the whole party** | 13th Age's campaign loss | It punishes those who didn't die | **Momentum**, which already drops when a PC goes Down |
| **Kept out of the spell list** | Draw Steel, Dungeon World | The GM's call unless the conditions are listed | "I like no revive spells": an item, a place or a rite, not a card |
| **Death chosen, for a price paid to the party** | Daggerheart, Fabula Ultima | Not a return | "Keep fighting" while Dying or Shattered is already a chosen risk |

**The two things every limited return needs written:** what happens at the second death, and what the dead character's player does until the return.
