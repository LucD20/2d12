# Getting better by use: how a weapon skill grows in other games

## 2026-11-16: for the Bow remake (how Mastery is gained)

**The question (Lucas):** Mastery is gained today on the first Tier 3 with a weapon of a group in an encounter, which comes to almost a point a fight. Should it be "you gain one Mastery when you win an encounter of X difficulty"? Does any other system do something like this?

**How far to trust this file.** One short pass by Claude: six searches, **no page opened**. Everything below is a [lead]: a search summary of the page linked, to open before it is quoted to anyone. Kingdom Death: Monster's weapon proficiency (from memory: a point when you wound with the weapon type, once a showdown, with ranks at 3 and 8) was searched twice and not found: it is the nearest match to today's rule and stays unverified.

### Takeaways

1. **No game found gives a weapon's own progress for winning a fight of a set difficulty.** The two halves exist apart: progress from use (takeaways 2 to 4) and progress from finishing a challenge (experience by encounter, milestones), which goes to the level, never to one weapon.
   - *For 2d12:* the proposal joins the two. Nothing to copy, and nothing found that warns against it.
2. **Progress by difficulty exists for single rolls.** Burning Wheel counts a skill's tests as routine, difficult or challenging and needs a set number of each to advance [lead]. Blades in the Dark marks 1 xp on a desperate action roll, in the attribute rolled [lead].
   - *For 2d12:* "only the hard ones count" is a known idea, at the size of a roll.
3. **What goes wrong there: players chase the roll that counts.** Burning Wheel's forum calls it testing for its own sake and answers that every test must matter and have a consequence; players also complain about the tracking, and about a test that arrives at the wrong difficulty being wasted [lead]. Of Blades: "a party could force desperate rolls to level up their characters much faster" [lead].
   - *For 2d12:* a party can't make a fight harder as easily as a player can make a roll desperate: the GM builds the encounter. The chase is weaker at the size of a fight.
4. **Progress by use is known for grinding, and the fixes named are a cap and a challenge.** Tim Cain and Raph Koster (video games): players repeat the cheapest use; skills used often outrun the others [lead]. Call of Cthulhu caps it: one check a skill, then a roll at the end of the scenario [lead]. An EN World thread describes the same cap for a tabletop house rule ("the first time a skill succeeds in an adventure") [lead]. Fixes listed across the sources: progress from failure or from challenge, a cap on repeats, checks made in downtime.
   - *For 2d12:* today's rule already has the cap (1 a group an encounter). "Win a fight of X difficulty" adds the challenge.
5. **Uneven progress between players is the complaint against use-based rules in a level game** ("very uneven advancement for players") [lead].
   - *For 2d12:* a point for the fight, not for the roll, gives every PC who fought the same pace.
