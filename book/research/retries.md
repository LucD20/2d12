# Retrying a failed Check: what games print

**Date:** 2026-11-19. **Asked by Lucas:** "You can just Force Open, Pick a Lock etc non stop until you succeed. We need to put something like: You can't try again if you fail at a task such as picking a lock, forcing open a door, repairing etc until the situation changes, you try something different or the GM decides. This of course doesn't apply to different ways of doing something: If you fail at picking a lock, you can still try to force it open. If you fail on repairing a bridge, you can still attempt to find other ways to cross it etc. I think Draw Steel does it. Feel free to research about it."

**What was read, and how far to trust it.** One independent researcher; the full report (about 4,000 words, every source) is `raw/2026-11-19-retries.md`. **This file is Claude's summary of it.** Draw Steel, the Year Zero Engine, Blades in the Dark and Pathfinder 2e were read in their rules text. Burning Wheel comes from two pages that quote it. Paizo's site, d20srd.org and dandwiki refused; the 2014 and 2024 D&D texts are forum quotes. **No rules text was found** for 13th Age, Savage Worlds, Torchbearer, Ironsworn, Cypher, GURPS, Warhammer 4e, Genesys, Shadow of the Demon Lord, Mothership or 7th Sea. Draw Steel's rule has almost no published reception and no design commentary was found.

**Other games' rules, never 2d12's. Nothing here is decided.**

---

## 1. Lucas's memory is right: Draw Steel has the rule

*Draw Steel: Heroes*, the Tests chapter, "Can I Try Again?" [read]: "In many cases when you fail a test, you can't attempt the test again unless the circumstances of the test change. [...] The Director decides when the circumstances have changed enough." Its examples (better lockpicks, an oiled lock, a demonstration) all improve **the same method**. **It doesn't say** whether a different method, or another hero, may try. Beside it: a paid reroll (a hero token), the Director's offer to turn a plain failure into a success at a cost, and a once-per-character limit on each skill in montage tests.

## 2. The closest printed text to Lucas's sentence

**The Year Zero Engine** [read, its SRD]: "you may not roll again to achieve the same goal. You need to try something different or wait until the circumstances have changed in a substantial way. **Or let another player character try.**"

Close behind:
- **Blades in the Dark's** Lost Opportunity [read]: "a new approach—usually a new form of action or a change in circumstances".
- **Burning Wheel's** Let It Ride [checked, quoted]: no new roll "until conditions legitimately and drastically change".

## 3. Pathfinder 2e confirms the complaint

Pick a Lock, Force Open and Repair have no line about trying again [read]. Their critical failures (a broken toolkit, a penalty on later attempts, damage to the item) are the only brake. Retry limits exist only action by action (Identify Magic, one day; Learn a Spell, after a level), and Recall Knowledge's "further attempts are fruitless" sits in the GM book, not on the action.

## 4. What the games agree and disagree on

- **Even the games with strong "fail forward" print a ban** (Draw Steel, Year Zero). It covers the plain failure with no consequence: the one result a player can roll again for free.
- **Whether another character may try, they disagree:** Year Zero, yes; Draw Steel's montages, once per character; Burning Wheel's own players, split.
- **No game defines "changed" beyond examples, and every one names the GM as the judge.**

## 5. How Lucas's wording compares (observations)

- His rule and Year Zero's are nearly the same: no second roll for the same goal by the same means; a different means is always open.
- **One difference:** his sentence lists "or the GM decides" as a third, separate exit. In Draw Steel the GM is the judge of whether the circumstances changed, not a separate way out.
- **Left open by his sentence, as by Draw Steel's:** whether another character may make the same attempt.
- **What 2d12 already has beside it:** Tier 1 is "no, or yes at a real cost" (decision 116), so many failures already change the situation; Routine (115) takes the roll away from what an expert does unpressed; "repeating an approach takes 1 Bane" in a Challenge (`roleplay-skills.md` item 9).
