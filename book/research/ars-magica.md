# Ars Magica: verb + noun magic, and what it teaches a Paths × Schools grid

**Date:** 2026-11-18. **Asked by Lucas:** after `dominions-magic.md`, he chose **Paths + Schools** as the direction for 2d12's magic, with a School being **the spell's form, with rules attached**. "Research Ars Magica. [...] I prefer of course Dominions' theme and how it is, but Ars Magica could give us ideas to make them work well in the system." So: not Ars Magica's setting or names, its machinery for two axes.

**What was read.**
- **[checked, through a summarizing reader]** Ars Magica Fifth Edition's open text (CC BY-SA 4.0 since 2024) on redcap.org: Chapter Seven, Hermetic Magic (the first 100,000 of 139,000 characters) and Chapter Nine, Spells (the first 100,000 of 358,000: the rules of spell design and the first guideline tables; the other tables were not read). The tool returns a summary of the opened page: the formulas below came back the same from both chapters where they overlap, but none was read by Claude on the page itself.
- **[checked]** Reception: an EN World thread ("Game Design Masterclass: Ars Magica") and an Atlas Games forum thread ("The use of spell guidelines").
- **[lead]** Reviews seen only as search summaries (RPGnet reviews of the Fourth Edition, the Not A Snark retrospective, an EN World "what's it like" thread). Marked [lead] where used.
- **Reception is thin:** two searches for complaints about speed at the table found little. What is below on that is two posters and the rules' own wording, not a consensus.
- **Not researched:** Mage: the Ascension's spheres, GURPS's syllabic magic and other verb + noun relatives.

**These are Ars Magica's rules, not 2d12's. Nothing here is decided.** The fit ratings are Claude's.

## 1. Ars Magica's magic in one page

1. **Fifteen Arts on two axes.** Five **Techniques**, the verbs: *Creo* (create, heal, make a thing a better example of its kind), *Intellego* (perceive), *Muto* (give a thing a property it can't naturally have), *Perdo* (destroy, make worse), *Rego* (control: move a thing to another state it can naturally have). Ten **Forms**, the nouns: Animal, Water, Air, Body, Plant, Fire, Image, Mind, Earth, and Vim (magic itself). Every spell is one verb and one noun: Creo Ignem makes fire.
2. **Two scores add.** Casting score = Technique + Form (+ Stamina + the place's aura). A magus has a number in each of the fifteen.
3. **A spell has a level; the roll is compared with it.** Total at or above the level: cast. **Up to 10 short: cast, and you lose a Fatigue level.** More than 10 short: it fails, and you still tire.
4. **How a level is built.** Each verb + noun cell has a table of **guidelines**: effects with a base level (Creo Animal: base 15, heal a light wound; base 20, a medium one). The base assumes the smallest shape: on yourself, for a moment, one target. Then **every step up a ladder adds one magnitude (5 levels)**: Range (Personal, Touch, Voice, Sight, Arcane Connection), Duration (Momentary, Concentration, Sun, Moon, Year), Target (Individual, Part, Group, Structure, Boundary). A spell's write-up ends with its **design line**: "(Base 10, +2 Voice, +1 Concentration)".
5. **Three ways to cast.** A **formulaic** spell is one you learned: fixed, reliable. **Spontaneous** magic is invented at the table from the guidelines: the total is **halved** (and it tires you) or **divided by five** (and it doesn't). A **ritual** is anything big or lasting (level 20 at least; Year duration and Boundary targets are ritual only): 15 minutes a magnitude and one pawn of **vis** a magnitude.
6. **Requisites: the merge rule.** A spell that needs a second Art names it as a requisite, and **you cast with the lower of the two scores**. Two kinds: a requisite the effect *needs* costs nothing (turning a clothed man into a bird needs Animal, and Plant for the clothes); a requisite that *adds* to the effect costs **+1 magnitude** or more. A purely cosmetic touch needs none.
7. **Vis.** Raw magic, a consumable, each pawn belonging to one Art. Rituals need it. On any other spell, each pawn is +2 to the casting score, **at most as many pawns as your score in that Art**, and each adds a botch die.
8. **Resistance.** Penetration = casting total − the spell's level (+ a Penetration skill): **a small spell cast by a master penetrates; a spell at your limit doesn't.** A magus resists with a trained shield plus **their own score in the spell's Form**.
9. **The body of the spell.** Words and gestures: none of either is -15 to cast, quiet and subtle -7, loud and exaggerated +2.
10. **Concentration.** A roll against a table: walking 3, jostled 9, knocked down 12, **damaged 15**; holding a second spell 12.
11. **Mastery, a spell at a time.** A score in one spell: fewer botch dice, and special abilities: cast several copies at once, cast it fast.
12. **Fast casting.** A spontaneous spell thrown as a defense against a spell you see coming: -10 to cast, and **you must know the Form** of what you answer.
13. **Botches and Warping.** A bad roll botches; botches give Warping Points; enough at once and the magus falls into Twilight. The long-term price of pushing.
14. **The Limits of Magic.** A written list of what no magic can do: touch the Divine, change a thing's essential nature (so Muto must be maintained), stop aging, create permanently without vis, affect time, the soul.
15. **The sigil.** Every magus's spells carry a personal mark (one's fire is always blue-white, another's smells of roses): no rule, pure identity.

**What players say** (thin, see above). The praise is constant: the system "with which I compare all others" (Fenris-77, EN World); "everyone in the group can have a speciality and a style for their magic" (the article); no spell points. The friction that could be found: the guidelines are a book section a player must have open (one storyguide skips them in play: "I don't want my players to have their noses stuck in the rule book", Erik Tyrrell, Atlas forum; another suggests flash cards of the Art combinations); the rules themselves hand the last word to the troupe again and again (the level of an odd combination, whether an effect is a ritual, sizes "agreed by the troupe"); point-buy allows "quite powerful one dimensional magi" [lead]; the game "is not for combat oriented groups" [lead].

## 2. What it teaches a Paths × Schools grid in 2d12

2d12's side, as it stands after 2026-11-18: Paths are Dominions' (Fire, Air, Water, Earth, Astral, Death, Nature, Glamour, Blood; Holy apart); Schools are forms with rules attached; spells are written cards (Power Actions), never free-form; health is not the main cost; Nonlethal damage is an optional extra fuel; no number bloat (the Power Modifier rolls, +4 at most).

| # | Ars Magica's piece | What it would be in 2d12 | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Each verb defined in one sentence, with a hard edge** (Rego: only states a thing can naturally have; Muto: only ones it can't) | Dominions' Schools overlap by its own admission. Keep Dominions' names and give each School **one sentence and a sorting rule**, so every spell has exactly one School and nobody chooses it by taste. See 3.1. | High: the main lesson |
| 2 | **Technique + Form add into the casting score** | Adding two levels to the roll is bloat. The two levels **gate** the spell instead. Three ways, see 3.2. | The decision to make |
| 3 | **Guidelines: a table of effects for each cell** | Not at the table. **A design-time table**: one line a cell saying what a Fire Evocation, a Fire Enchantment, a Water Alteration is. Claude and Lucas write cards from it; players read cards. | High, as a design tool |
| 4 | **Spontaneous magic** | The ambiguity engine: the rules themselves say the troupe decides. Against the system's purpose. | Low: leave |
| 5 | **Formulaic spells** | 2d12's cards. | Already there |
| 6 | **Range, Duration and Target as ladders; each step +1 magnitude** | **Overcast as ladders**: each level you have above the spell buys one step on a ladder the card allows: Distance (5 → 10 → 15), Area (one creature → Burst 1 → Burst 2), Duration (EoT → 1 round → while you Focus). The same three ladders on every spell: one rule learned once. See 3.3. | High |
| 7 | **The design line** "(Base 10, +2 Voice)" | The card's analysis already prices each line (`paladin-analyses.md`); a spell's analysis would show its level the same way. Not on the card. | Medium, for us |
| 8 | **A requisite: you cast at the lower of two Arts** | A two-Path spell uses **your lower level of the two**. Dominions says "the first Path listed"; Ars Magica's rule asks more of a merger and needs no ordering. | High |
| 9 | **A requisite that adds to the effect costs +1 magnitude** | The price of a merge: **adding a second Path's rider to a spell raises its level by 1.** A master of Fire can afford to lace it with Air; an apprentice can't. | High |
| 10 | **Up to 10 short of the level: it works, and you lose Fatigue** | Lucas's optional fuel, with a shape: **a spell one level above you can be cast by taking Strain** (Nonlethal Resolve damage). Never two above. Dominions' gem says the same (+1, never more). | High |
| 11 | **Penetration = total − level: small spells from a master land harder** | An Overcast step that gives 1 Boon on the roll. It is the one ladder that touches accuracy. | Low to Medium: probably leave |
| 12 | **Your score in a Form resists that Form** | Dominions has the same rule (half your Path level). Two games agree: **your level in a Path is your Resist against it**, or a Boon on those Saves. | High, small |
| 13 | **Vis: typed, required by rituals, capped by your Art, +risk** | Dominions' gems again, nearly rule for rule. Two games converge on: typed by Path, **you can't spend more than your level**, rituals need them. | Confirms gems |
| 14 | **Rituals: anything lasting or large is forced out of combat** | A clean border to write early: "an effect that lasts beyond the encounter, or covers more than X, is a Ritual". Then no battle card is ever asked to do those things. | High as a border; parked |
| 15 | **Words and gestures** (silent or still: a big penalty) | 2d12 has [Handle] (a free hand) and no rule for a voice. "A spell needs your voice and a free hand; casting it silent or with full hands costs a level each." It makes Grabbed, a gag and a shield matter to a caster, and gives stealth casting a price. | Medium |
| 16 | **Concentration: a roll against a table of situations** | A table of situations is a table of arguments. The threshold proposed in `dominions-magic.md` 4.4 (one Action's damage at or above X ends the Focus) stays the cleaner rule. Ars Magica's useful number: **holding a second spell is itself a test**, so "one Focus at a time" is the simple form. | Low for the table; keeps "one Focus" |
| 17 | **Spell Mastery** (a score in one spell: cast two copies, cast it fast) | A mirror of Weapon Mastery, which the 2026-10-29 decisions already wanted matched ("free growth matched"). Mastery of a spell could unlock one extra thing, as a weapon's steps do. | Medium to High, with Mastery |
| 18 | **Fast casting as a defense: you must know the Form** | **A counterspell with no judgment in it:** a Reaction against a spell, usable only if you have its Path, and only if your level reaches its level. Who can counter what is read off two cards. | High |
| 19 | **Botch, Warping, Twilight** | The long price of pushing. Dominions' horror marks are the same idea. Needs the Wound tables and a campaign rule. | Medium, later |
| 20 | **The Limits of Magic** | **Write 2d12's Limits before the first spell**: what no spell does (raise the dead? undo a Wound? act outside Line of Effect? create a permanent object in battle?). A short list that answers a hundred table questions in advance. | High: cheap and on purpose |
| 21 | **The sigil** | One line of the character: how your magic looks. No rule. It suits "Lucas doesn't write Flavor": the sigil colors every spell's flavor line at the table. | Medium, free |
| 22 | **Fifteen separate scores** | Too many. Dominions' mages have two or three Paths. A 2d12 caster: one to three Paths, one to three Schools, levels 1 to 3. | Low: keep few |
| 23 | **Point-buy specialists** ("one dimensional magi" [lead]) | A warning for the gate rule: if Path and School levels add, the best build is always one cell maxed. See 3.2. | A warning |
| 24 | **Players need the tables open; flash cards suggested** | 2d12 already answers it: the card is the flash card. The grid must never be needed at the table. | Already there |

## 3. Three things to settle, in this order

### 3.1 The Schools: Dominions' names, Ars Magica's discipline

Dominions' manual says its schools "don't categorize rigorously". For the School to carry rules, a spell's School can't be a matter of taste. A draft, to argue with:

| School | One sentence | The rules it carries | Ars Magica's nearest verb |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Evocation** | You throw the Path at a target. | [Strike]; rolls against a Defense; counts toward the 3 Strikes | Creo / Perdo, as an attack |
| **Alteration** | You change a creature or the ground for a short time. | Conditions, terrain, buffs; a Save or a willing target; ends EoT or in rounds | Muto |
| **Enchantment** | You make the Path stay. | [Focus]; lasts while you hold it; one at a time | (a Duration, in Ars Magica) |
| **Conjuration** | You bring something that wasn't there. | Summoned creatures and created objects; their own rules (not written) | Creo |
| **Thaumaturgy** | You reach the mind and the unseen. | Saves against Mind or Appeal; senses, knowledge, control | Intellego / Rego |

Construction (items) and Blood (as a school) are out of battle or a Path's business, and stay out of this table.

**The sorting rule** (so one spell has one School): a spell that leaves something on the map that needs you to hold it is an Enchantment; else one that brings a creature or an object is a Conjuration; else one that Strikes is an Evocation; else one that works on the mind or the senses is Thaumaturgy; else it is an Alteration. First match wins.

One thing Ars Magica shows by contrast: its fifth verb, *Intellego* (perceive), has almost no battle use, and Dominions has no school for it either. If 2d12 wants detection and knowledge as magic, it lives in Thaumaturgy and mostly in rituals.

### 3.2 How two levels gate one spell

A spell of Path P and School S. Three candidates:

| | The card says | Who can cast it | What it rewards | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Both written** (Dominions' way, twice) | "Fire 2 • Evocation 1" | Fire ≥ 2 and Evocation ≥ 1 | The designer tunes each spell | Two numbers a card; Overcast must pick which one it reads |
| **B. The sum** (Ars Magica's way, as a gate) | "Fire Evocation 4" | Fire + Evocation ≥ 4, at least 1 in each | Either specialist reaches it; Overcast = the excess, one number | The best build is one cell maxed (row 23) |
| **C. The lower** (the requisite rule) | "Fire Evocation 2" | The lower of Fire and Evocation ≥ 2 | Even builds; a third Path or School is cheap to make useful | A specialist feels nothing from their high number |

Claude's lean is **B with levels capped at 3**: one number on the card, Overcast is one subtraction, and a Fire 3 / Evocation 1 caster and a Fire 1 / Evocation 3 caster both throw the same Fireball while everything else they know differs, which is the "lots of possibilities" Lucas is after. The specialist problem is real but bounded: with a cap of 3 the best cell is 6, and a spell list that tops out at 5 or 6 leaves little excess to abuse.

### 3.3 Overcast as ladders, and the merge

- **Ladders (row 6).** Each level of excess buys one step on a ladder the card lists: "Overcast: Distance, Area". Ars Magica proves a small set of fixed ladders can describe a whole spell list; Dominions adds that each spell climbs different ones (Fireball: Distance and damage; Falling Fires: Area; Fire Darts: one more dart). Together: fixed ladders, and the card says which it may climb.
- **The merge (rows 8 and 9).** Each Path owns one rider. Adding a second Path's rider to a spell: you must have that Path, and **the spell counts 1 level higher**. So a merge spends the same excess Overcast does: more reach, or a second element, never both for free. A few written two-Path spells on top, cast at the lower of the two Paths.
- **The push (row 10).** One level short: take Strain and cast it anyway. This is the only place health pays for magic, and only when the player asks for it.
