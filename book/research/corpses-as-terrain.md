# Corpses as terrain (2026-11-26)

**The question (Lucas):** are dead bodies Difficult Terrain in 2d12, and is it worth keeping? "I think it is realistic but clutters the map."

**What 2d12 says today (Chapter 3, Creatures in your way):** an Unconscious creature and a dead one are Difficult Terrain, whichever side they are on. Foundry follows it (`move.mjs downTok`).

## Takeaways (a quick search; forum threads only, no rulebook text was found)

- **No official rule was found for D&D 5e or Pathfinder** that makes one body Difficult Terrain; forum posters say the rules leave it to the GM. Draw Steel: nothing found.
- **What tables do, three camps:**
  1. Remove the body when it drops, to keep the map clean (the most common answer on virtual tabletops; some move it to a background layer).
  2. Count bodies only when they pile up or are big: several Medium bodies in a square, or a Large or bigger corpse.
  3. Leave every body as a marker of what happened, with no rule attached.
- Nobody in the threads read argued for "every single body is Difficult Terrain" as a rule worth its bookkeeping.

## How it applies

- 2d12's rule is stricter than what any table in these threads does. With Minions (2 ticks, many of them), bodies appear fast and in the squares the fight is in.
- Options: (a) keep it; (b) only an Unconscious creature (a PC who is Down and out, an ally worth stepping around) is Difficult Terrain and a dead one is nothing; (c) only a Large or bigger dead creature; (d) nothing, bodies are scenery.
- Not checked: any printed rulebook; this is opinion from forums.
