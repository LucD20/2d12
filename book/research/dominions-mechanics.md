# Dominions 6: the rest of the manual, mechanic by mechanic, for 2d12

**Date:** 2026-11-20. **Asked by Lucas:** "Look at all the dominions research we have done. Using the same research instructions, search for dominions mechanics. [...] Check thoroughly, slow and carefully, separating mechanics piece by piece that you think it could fit the system. The ones you are unsure, still note down for me. I don't mind complexity, 2d12 is supposed to be a complex rpg, for veterans. It can be core mechanics, it can be flavor mechanics etc."

**What was read.**
- **[read]** The Dominions 6 manual (Illwinter, revision 2, 449 pages), extracted column by column and read page by page: **pages 5 to 117 in full** (the game, the map and provinces, forts, temples, labs, magic sites, unrest, scouting, the pretender god and its creation, the bless, dominion and scales, units and their abilities, experience, afflictions, recruiting, army setup and battle orders, movement, every order, the turn sequence, the whole combat chapter, morale and rout, sieges, weather, retreats, the magic chapter again, the Dominion chapter, the nations' origins, every magic item and path booster), and **pages 208 to 240 in full** (the descriptions of every global enchantment and every non-summoning ritual: the four earlier notes had their names only).
- **[scanned]** Pages 118 to 207 (battle spells, already in `raw/2026-11-18-dominions-spells.tsv`, and the summoning rituals) and 241 to 436 (the nations' unit lists): only for the names of unit abilities not met elsewhere. About twenty of those names are not explained anywhere in the manual (the game's tooltips and the modding manual explain them): they are listed in section 15 as names only.
- **Not read:** the modding manual, the in-game texts, forums, players' opinions. This note is the rules, not their reception.
- The reading log, page by page, with every number: `raw/2026-11-20-dominions-manual-notes.md`.

**These are Dominions' rules, not 2d12's. Nothing here is decided.** The fit ratings and the "For 2d12" lines are Claude's. Several rows touch pieces that are only proposals in 2d12 (the expedition loop, items and economy, base building, magic outside a fight); a row says so when it does.

**Reviewed and ranked (2026-11-20):** every High and Medium row was checked against the rulebook and the design notes in `dominions-ranked.md`, which orders the 101 that held from best fit down and adds a Complexity column. 15 rows did not hold and are marked here "after the review" (11 to Low, mostly because 2d12 already has the rule; 4 to Unsure); the reasons are in that note's section 1.

**How to read the tables.** Each row is one mechanic, split as finely as the manual splits it. **Fit:** **High** (it hangs on a rule 2d12 has or a piece being designed, with little new text); **Medium** (it needs a new word, a decision, or a piece not designed yet); **Low** (it works against a decision or a test of Lucas's; listed so it can be seen and refused on purpose); **Unsure** (Claude can't tell; noted because Lucas asked for these). **Kind:** Core (a rule of the round or of Health), Bestiary, Gear, Magic, Divine, Travel, Info (finding out and hiding), GM (a tool for running the game), Campaign (the layer between adventures), Flavor (no rule needed, or a rule that only colors).

## 2. Takeaways (the ones Claude would look at first)

1. **Fights end on nerve, and the rule says when** (section 3). Troops rout when every commander is dead or fled, an army routs at 75% of its total hit points lost, a squad checks morale on named triggers, at most once a turn. 2d12 has **no morale rule for NPCs**; the expedition loop's proposal "the fight is decided" (`book/expedition-loop.md` item 14) is the same idea, and Dominions gives it its missing pieces: what a leader's fall does, which creatures never break (the mindless) and what happens to them instead (they stop and crumble), and fear that routs **Minions only**, which removes bodies without taking a turn from a PC or a major enemy.
2. **The Wound tables can be filled from Dominions' afflictions** (section 4): a list by body part, a minor and a major tier by the size of the blow, and above all **wounds that matter outside a fight** (a limp slows the march and can worsen into a cripple on a forced one), each healed its own way (a healer cures a few, a ritual one, a cursed item's wound only once the item is gone).
3. **Gear can be damaged in two steps** (section 5): a shield is Damaged at one threshold and Broken at a higher one; acid rusts iron so that later hits damage it; magic gear repairs itself after the fight unless it was broken. It is the shape Lucas's Sunder question and the items note's Dulled and Repair are waiting for.
4. **Dominions writes every order down once**: the order of a melee attack (strikebacks, repel, the roll, wards, a cap, damage shared out, then damage) and a 63-step turn sequence where each step says what it guarantees ("gold is always sent, because everything that could stop it happens later"). 2d12 has the order of a turn's start as an open item (`decided.md` 5, item 14) and a sentence for reductions and Guard: this is the model for both.
5. **Information has a table** (section 9): a scout, a priest, a spy, a scrying and owning the land each reveal a fixed list; **what you learn depends on who you are** (a spy who is a mage also learns the magic, a priest the faith); a failed infiltration tells the enemy someone tried; a remote spell doesn't tell its caster whether it worked. Each is one sentence that settles a "what do I learn?" argument before it starts.
6. **A rank in a Path opens places, not numbers** (section 11): hidden magic sites can be found only by a caster whose rank in the site's Path reaches its difficulty; the unburied dead are a Death caster's resource that only a Death caster senses. Horizontal growth, and a reason to explore.
7. **The Bestiary gets a dozen creature rules with clear outs** (section 7): trample and displacing the small, swallow, a gaze that punishes whoever attacks, a heart piece whose fall crumbles the rest, a trinity sharing one power, a second form when wounded, berserk on being Bloodied, the mindless that stop without their master.
8. **The Paladin's god can be a small build** (section 8): Dominions' bless is bought with points from the god's Paths, each effect costing its requirements, some always on and some only when blessed in battle; one prophet at a time, with a wait after his death; faith as a value of a place, separate from who rules it. It meets Aveline's playtest Talent (the god's blessing on the weapon) and the open "deities".
9. **Revival has Dominions shapes the revival note doesn't** (section 8): a return **prepared in advance** (you come back where you prepared it, changed, and only if that place is still yours), a god called back by pooled effort to a threshold the players don't know exactly, a death that costs a Path level and can teach Death, and an Extra Life item (it fits the items note's Disruptive (Death) tag).
10. **Travel rules with seasons in them** (section 10): survival in one terrain as a whole skill family, rivers crossable only when frozen and passes only when warm, the party moving at its slowest, and magic travel with honest limits (not true teleport, a prepared gate, a path that may drift, a road through the dead with its own dangers).
11. **Items** (section 14): three trigger words (when it strikes, when it hits, when it gets through), weapons that deal almost nothing and exist for their rider, **cursed items as the strongest ones**, a free resource each fight, and group effects measured in size points.
12. **Battle orders** (section 12): an NPC or a summon follows a written order and a target priority (archers, fliers, the largest, the closest, the rearmost). A "Tactics" line on a stat block, and orders for a summon, take decisions off the GM and the table.

## 3. Morale, rout and the end of a fight

2d12 today: no NPC morale or rout rule (Chapters 2 and 14 have none); `book/expedition-loop.md` (a candidate proposal, nothing decided) has "the fight is decided" when the enemies lose half their CV or their leader is Down, then the runners flee and each PC takes one Parting Action. Lucas's worry there: players chase every runner, a morale rule falls in the same trap, the mop-up drags.

## 4. Wounds (Dominions' afflictions)

2d12 today: Wounds come from the Dying and Shattered Tables when you go Down; their entries are placeholders ("something minor", "something severe"); a Rest's Body or Mind Check removes 1 to 3 Wounds by Tier; magic removes no Wound ("spells that remove Wounds come with the Wounds tables").

## 16. The unsure ones, together (Lucas asked for these)

Each row above rated **Unsure**, in one list: **B1** a wound from any single big blow; **B12** a mark "lessened by dying"; **C1** the shield's middle band; **C18** wounds read by the attacker's reach; **D1** Mastery even for a fight you fled; **D3** revival only for the renowned; **F3** blessings that need the god awake; **F6** a place's Faith; **F9** the god's champion stronger at home; **F16** a gift that arrives later and stronger; **F19** judgment by deeds (stronger against killers); **K7** a curse whose price falls on someone close to the caster; **K10** a copy of yourself far away; **K11** helpers adding their ranks to a ritual; **M3** a mark whose severity the player doesn't know; **M9** the abilities known only by name.

**Added by the review** (`dominions-ranked.md` section 1): **G9** a place that seems other until entered and **G10** a place forgotten by those who leave (both past decision 128's "sight only; a face only"), **L4** tainted items (they need the horror-mark clock, M3), **F8** preaching capped by rank, inquisitors and heretics (they act on a place's Faith, F6).

**What decides most of them:** whether 2d12 wants a campaign layer that the GM tracks in secret (B12, F3, F6, M3), whether a cost may fall on someone else (K7, K11), and the Wound tables' shape (B1, C1, C18).
