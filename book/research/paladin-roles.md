# The Paladin's brief in players' words: soft tank, soft support, soft nova, a three-role class

## 2026-11-14: for the class redesign (MASTER-PLAN item 5), before any Paladin card

**The question (Lucas):** how his brief feels to play. "The paladin is a soft tank, soft support, soft nova class, focusing in divine action against profane forces. The paladin isn't a consistent damage dealer nor can take hits for you, but they can aid you, make sure you don't go down and when they use all they have against an enemy, they can explode before seeing it coming. They stay in front so the squishies can stay back." What do tactical TTRPG players mean by these words, and when does a class that is a bit of three roles feel great or diluted?

**How far to trust this file:** the cloud session could not open any forum page. Every poster's line below is a **lead**: text a search tool surfaced from the page, not checked against it. The full report with each excerpt and link: `raw/2026-11-14-A-role-vocabulary.md`. Tags: [lead] a search summary of a page not opened · [mine] Claude's reading.

### Takeaways

1. **"Soft tank", "soft support" and "soft nova" are not player terms.** [lead] The words in use are **off-tank / secondary defender**, **secondary or emergency healer / leader secondary** (4e's own label for its Paladin: "Defender primary, Leader secondary"), and **nova / burst** (the nearest phrase to "soft nova" is a 4e guide's "mini nova": a nova with no daily powers, action points or setup round).
   - *For 2d12:* the brief is readable to these players as "an off-tank and emergency healer with a capped nova". The book's intro can say it that way.
2. **A bruiser is not an off-tank.** [lead] A designer essay's three pillars of a tank: durability, control, protection; "without protection... they fill a Bruiser or Brawler role". Posters separate surviving from tanking: holding the front only means you don't need someone to tank for you. A tank must be "durable and sticky".
   - *For 2d12:* "can't take hits for you" is fine; "has no way to protect you" is not. The Paladin needs a real protection tool with a small count (one ally, one hit a round), not bigger Health.
3. **The worst defender feeling is being ignored; the best protector stands beside the target.** [lead] Defenders are "eternal losers when the monsters ignore them"; the 4e Paladin was called "strikers with some leader ability. Their so-called defense is all-too-easily ignored". And from a Pathfinder 2e thread, the most useful line for this brief: "the champion actually works best if you are not the one that's always attacked so that you can use your champion reaction".
   - *For 2d12:* a protector by Reaction is a second front-liner. It matches the party the CV assumes (two in the front) and our one Reaction AP a round.
4. **A secondary healer is for the emergency, not the upkeep, and must not cost the turn.** [lead] "A great emergency healer", not a sustained one; players split a Lay on Hands pool three ways (a revive, top-offs, one big heal); two "half healers" replace a healer. D&D 2024 moved Lay on Hands to a Bonus Action and that was received as the fix.
   - *For 2d12:* "make sure you don't go down" = stop the drop or pick someone up, once or twice a fight, for 1 AP or a Reaction. Our measured problem is the same from the other side: Lay On Hands 1 AP is worth less than the attack it replaces (`pc-budget.md` section 5: -11).
5. **A secondary role is "nice to have but not enough of a reason for the party to want you".** [lead] The class still needs one thing it does with depth.
   - *For 2d12:* three softs with no hard centre is the risk in the brief. See 7.
6. **Nova = "using all of your best and most effective class features", in one round.** [lead] Loved: the dice pile, holding it for "the biggest threat on the board". Hated: "blowing up the boss monster with 2-4 full-bore smite crits before anyone else... gets to do anything"; it is too strong or "barely worthwhile" depending on fights per day. The 2024 once-a-turn cap was mostly accepted; the complaint was its cost (a Bonus Action, a spell that can be countered). Posters' preferred shape: once per turn, like Sneak Attack.
   - *For 2d12:* "soft nova" = one round, a held resource, the player picks the target, a hard cap (once a turn or once a fight). Detail: `nova-burst.md`.
7. **The hybrid trap, and what posters say gets out of it.** [lead] John Hopson: if a hybrid matches a specialist, the specialist is pointless; if it falls short everywhere it is useless. Diluted: the 5e ranger ("Not as fightery as a fighter, not as druidy as a druid"; "fine mechanically... just feels bad to play"), the 3.5 bard, 4e hybrids ("bad at both"). Praised: the 5e paladin ("probably the best designed"). What posters say makes the difference:
   - depth in one role, breadth in the others;
   - **signature abilities of its own**, not borrowed ones (smite, Oaths);
   - **the parts feed each other** instead of competing for the same action;
   - doing two things in one Action;
   - separate pools per mode (the 5e paladin: a healing pool, slots for smite, an always-on aura), so no mode starves another;
   - contributing "more often than any other character".
   - *For 2d12:* the three softs should hang on **one spine** that is the Paladin's own (one chosen enemy; see `paladins-other-games.md`), and protecting should feed the burst, not compete with it. Note the conflict in the research: 5e separates the pools, Draw Steel uses one pool on purpose (`nova-burst.md`, takeaway 3). A decision for Lucas.
8. **The 5e paladin's three loved pieces and their flaws.** [lead] Smite decided after the hit (never wasted; but crit-fishing, and bosses deleted); the always-on aura ("absurdly good"; but forgotten at the table, 10 feet is too small, and it bunches the party into every area attack); the Lay on Hands pool. And a gap: 5e gives the paladin few ways "to actually protect people".
   - *For 2d12:* an aura of numbers is the wrong protection tool (our own note: always-on bonuses are invisible; `tactical-combat.md`, takeaway 5). Beacon's +1 Defense Aura is that kind.

### The brief, line by line [mine]

| The brief | Players' word | What they expect | What makes it feel bad |
|---|---|---|---|
| "soft tank... can't take hits for you" | off-tank, secondary defender | a real protection tool with a small count, visible when it fires; works standing beside the target | being walked past with no answer; protection that is easy to ignore; a passive nobody remembers |
| "soft support... make sure you don't go down" | emergency healer, leader secondary | stop the drop or pick someone up, without spending the whole turn | amounts too small to change who stands; healing that costs the attack |
| "soft nova... explode before seeing it coming" | nova, burst, "mini nova" | one round, a held resource, the choice of target, a pile of dice | stacking on every hit that deletes a boss before it acts; a nova that depends on the day's length |
| three roles at once | hybrid, jack of all trades | one role with depth, its own signature abilities, parts that feed each other | "not as X as an X" in all three; the roles competing for one Action |
