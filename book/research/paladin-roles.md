# The Paladin's brief in players' words: soft tank, soft support, soft nova, a three-role class

## 2026-11-14: for the class redesign (MASTER-PLAN item 5), before any Paladin card

**The question (Lucas):** how his brief feels to play. "The paladin is a soft tank, soft support, soft nova class, focusing in divine action against profane forces. The paladin isn't a consistent damage dealer nor can take hits for you, but they can aid you, make sure you don't go down and when they use all they have against an enemy, they can explode before seeing it coming. They stay in front so the squishies can stay back." What do tactical TTRPG players mean by these words, and when does a class that is a bit of three roles feel great or diluted?

**How far to trust this file.** Two passes the same day. The first could open no page (leads only); the second opened the pages and checked every line: 76 verified, 11 corrected, 26 not on their page (removed here), 17 on sites that refuse automated readers. **[checked]** = the sentence is on the page, with the poster and date in `raw/2026-11-14-A-verified.md`. **[lead]** = still a search summary of a page nobody opened (all of Giant in the Playground). **[mine]** = Claude's reading. The first report: `raw/2026-11-14-A-role-vocabulary.md`.

### Takeaways

1. **"Soft tank", "soft support" and "soft nova" are not player terms.** No page uses them. The words in use: **off-tank / secondary defender**; **secondary or emergency healer**, and 4e's label for its Paladin, "Defender primary, Leader secondary"; **nova / burst**.
   - A 2008 poster describes the 4e Paladin almost in the brief's words [checked, brislove, EN World]: "single target tank, he grabs a guy and duels that guy, however he has some party benefit abilities so he helps via heals/temp HP for his party while he holds one guy on himself."
   - *For 2d12:* the brief reads to these players as "an off-tank and emergency healer with a capped nova".
2. **A bruiser is not a tank: a tank needs control and protection.** [checked, the designer of PYRE, "Tanking in TTRPGs", 2025] "A real tank requires three pillars: Durability... Control, or the ability to influence enemy actions or positioning. Protection, or the ability to safeguard allies or redirect harm." "Without protection... Instead of being a tank, they fill a Bruiser or Brawler role." "Tanks need incentives for enemies to attack them, or consequences for ignoring them." His two tank types: the **Anchor** ("reward staying near allies, redirecting attacks, and punishing enemies that attempt to bypass them") and the Ram (impossible to ignore, retaliates).
   - [checked, The-Magic-Sword, Paizo, 2025] "A Tank doesn't actually have to take aggro, a tank has to reduce the incoming damage." [checked, Ryangwy, same thread] the Champion is a tank at level 1 "because it is the only class which can ensure that regardless of whether the enemy hits them or an ally, it's a bad choice".
   - *For 2d12:* "can't take hits for you" is fine; "has no way to protect you" is not. The brief's Paladin is the Anchor.
3. **A protector by reaction is a second front-liner, and the ally he protects stands near him.** [checked, nicholas storm, Paizo, 2020] "the champion actually works best if you are not the one that's always attacked so that you can use your champion reaction." [checked, Deriven Firelion, same thread] "Champions work best if someone else is in melee range or at least in that 15 foot range... Then the mob focuses on hitting the damage dealer, while the champion blocks the damage and harms them." [checked, SuperBidi] "If they don't take any hit and you don't have a shield to block then you lose your reactions every round."
   - A durable character the enemy can ignore is not a defender [checked, FadedC, EN World, 2008]: in 3e "there was nothing to keep the enemy from just ignoring him and eating somebody more threatening". [lead, Giant in the Playground] the 4e Paladin as "strikers with some leader ability. Their so-called defense is all-too-easily ignored".
   - *For 2d12:* **this pulls against one line of the brief.** "They stay in front so the squishies can stay back" puts the protected ally far from him; the posters say the Reaction is wasted unless an ally is close enough to be hit. Our Reactions reach 5 squares (25 feet against the Champion's 15), which covers an Archer standing a move behind him, and the CV assumes two PCs in the front. But the Paladin needs both: a Reaction for the ally who is hit, and a way to stop the enemy reaching the one who stayed back (`frontline-protection.md`, takeaway 2).
4. **A secondary healer is for the emergency, not the upkeep.** [checked, Paizo and EN World] two "half healers" can replace a healer; Lay on Hands heals one person well and is not enough for a party. [lead, Giant in the Playground] "a great emergency healer". D&D 2024 moved Lay on Hands to a Bonus Action [checked as a rule].
   - *For 2d12:* "make sure you don't go down" = stop the drop or pick someone up, once or twice a fight, for 1 AP or a Reaction. Our measured problem is the same from the other side: Lay On Hands 1 AP is worth less than the attack it replaces (`pc-budget.md` section 5: -11).
5. **Groups can reject a hybrid that only dilutes.** [checked, MGibster, EN World, on an MMO paladin built to tank and heal] "People laughed at me and refused to group with me until I fixed my talents." [checked, Fanaelialae] "The tank that can heal is significantly less useful if you have a dedicated healer in the party." In Pathfinder 2e [checked, Angwa, Paizo, 2026] defender and leader exist only as a secondary focus, "not really enough to make it a primary role".
   - *For 2d12:* with two classes written, the Paladin is the party's only protector and only healer, so "soft" in both is also the ceiling of what a party can have. Worth a thought when the third class comes.
6. **Nova: "using all of your best and most effective class features".** [checked, the definition of an EN World poll, 2023] In its 57 votes, "how often should you be able to go nova": once per long rest 15, **once per encounter 14**, once per day 10. One poster's origin for the word [checked, Umbran]: the Human Torch's "Nova blast": "one big blast, after which his flames faltered for a while... you don't do it all the time because it leaves you incredibly vulnerable afterwards."
   - Hated [checked, PsyrenXY, D&D Beyond, 2024]: "the Paladin winning initiative and blowing up the boss monster... with 2-4 full-bore smite crits before anyone else in the party or the DM even gets to do anything and ruining everyone's fun". [checked, Chaosmancer, EN World] a polearm paladin dealing "58 damage on average in a single turn at level 5... It was just too much."
   - Defended [checked, FrogReaver]: cap it and "you are causing them not to shine hardly anywhere ever". [checked, Blue] "The paladin smite uses up a limited resource... this is true, but also the point."
   - The 2024 cap [checked]: "One smite/turn: Fair"; "Smites needed limiting too once per turn, but BA for Divine Smite was not the way to go about it... Rogues do not use a BA to sneak attack". So the cap was accepted and its action cost was not.
   - *For 2d12:* "soft nova" = one Action, the player picks the moment and the target, a hard cap, **and a visible cost afterwards** (the word's own origin). Detail: `nova-burst.md`.
7. **The hybrid trap, and what gets out of it.** [checked, John Hopson, quoted on Isegoria] "if a hybrid can perform a given role as well as a specialist while also having other abilities the specialist can never have, playing a specialist becomes pointless"; "Conversely, if a hybrid is always inferior to a specialist in any given role, then it's always better to have a specialist fill that role." From EN World's "The 'Jack Of All Trades' is a cursed archetype" [checked]:
   - "a jack of all trades needs sufficient depth in **all** of their primary roles... A hybrid who lacks depth in their primary roles simply can't perform adequately at anything, and this is the most common mistake I see in hybrid designs" (Fanaelialae);
   - **two things at once:** "let them do their thing -and- something else at the same time. Maybe their secondary effect is weaker (... or use your reaction to apply it in response to someone getting hit) but by doing both at once you show the character is doing a whole bunch of stuff in the fight" (Steampunkette, a designer);
   - the versatile character "will often not be the best at a particular situation, but they won't be the worst, and they will be able to contribute more often than any other character" (TerraDave).
   - The 5e paladin as the success [checked, "Best designed classes in 5e"]: "There is a very good synergy between giving Concentration boosts, fighting, their paladin magic"; "Paladins smite feature is probably the best designed feature in the game! It has perfect flavor for the class." A dissent: "designed inconsistently with the rest of the game". The ranger as the failure: "so muddled in concept that there's no foundation for a good design."
   - [mine] The 5e paladin keeps a pool per mode (a healing pool, slots for the smite, an always-on aura). No poster was found saying so; it is an observation, set against Draw Steel's one pool in `nova-burst.md`, takeaway 3.
   - *For 2d12:* each of the three softs needs enough depth to matter by itself; the parts should feed each other; and "two things in one Action" is the hybrid's signature: a Strike that also helps, a Reaction that protects and punishes. That fits "one main thing and one rider" exactly.
8. **The always-on aura is strong, forgotten and resented.** [checked, EN World] "Forgetting to use the paladin's aura when it comes up"; "It's not the DM's job to remember or remind players... If they forget it, tough luck"; with the party inside it "they will all get hit by every AOE AND the squishy classes are within 20 feet of the front combat lines"; for the GM, "always on all the time... a real PITA".
   - *For 2d12:* an aura of numbers is the wrong protection tool (our own note: `tactical-combat.md`, takeaway 5). Beacon's +1 Defense Aura is that kind.

### The brief, line by line [mine]

| The brief | Players' word | What they expect | What makes it feel bad |
|---|---|---|---|
| "soft tank... can't take hits for you" | off-tank, the Anchor | a real protection tool with a small count; reduce the damage, make both of the enemy's choices bad | a durable body the enemy ignores; one Reaction spent and the next enemy free |
| "they stay in front so the squishies can stay back" | holding the line | a consequence for trying to bypass him | the protected ally too far for the Reaction; nothing that stops a walk-past |
| "soft support... make sure you don't go down" | emergency healer | stop the drop or pick someone up, without spending the whole turn | amounts too small to change who stands; healing that costs the attack |
| "soft nova... explode before seeing it coming" | nova, burst | one round, the choice of moment and target, a pile of dice, a cost afterwards | stacking on every hit that deletes a boss before it acts |
| three roles at once | hybrid, jack of all trades | depth in each, two things in one Action, parts that feed each other | "muddled in concept"; the specialist always preferred |
