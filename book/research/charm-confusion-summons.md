# Charm, confusion and summons: why they are trouble, and how games contain them

**Date:** 2026-11-18. **Asked by Lucas,** after the first pass of spell cards (`book/cards/magic-first-pass.md`) found no 2d12 words for sleep, confusion and charm, and no summons rules:
- "Confusion and charm is... weirdly placed. Make a search about those topics and you will know why."
- "Idea is to summon a similarly leveled creature, with stats similar to a Minion and it can grow as level goes up. Similar to SWADE, summoning can have its own 'build your summon', which we can make per preparation instead of per casting. Keeping in mind to limit summons because summons are famous for being fun killers, add to the research as well."

**What was read.**
- **[checked, through a summarizing reader]** Archives of Nethys, Pathfinder 2e's conditions (Confused, Controlled, Fascinated, Stunned, Unconscious, Fleeing); Arcane Eye on D&D 5e's charmed condition (quoting the Basic Rules); the RPGBot podcast episode on charm and domination; a fan wiki's copy of Savage Worlds' Summon Ally power.
- **[lead]** (search summaries, pages not opened): Pathfinder 2e's minion and summoned traits (Roll20's mirror and Paizo forum threads quoting the rule); the Pathfinder 2e Summoner's Act Together (Paizo forum, a cheat sheet); D&D 2024's conjure spells and Tasha's summon spells (Wargamer, EN World, D&D Beyond forums); EN World and Roll20 threads on summons and action economy; AD&D's "Enchantment/Charm" school.
- **[memory]** D&D 4e's dominated condition and its summons, from Claude's training.
- **Already in the project:** `conditions-and-senses.md` (losing a whole turn is the complaint, by that name); `dominions-rules.md` 1 and 6 (Dominions' control conditions and its summons); the Bestiary's Minion rank.

**Other games' rules and opinions, never 2d12's. Nothing here is decided.**

## Part 1: Charm and confusion

### 1.1 Why they are "weirdly placed"

1. **By school.** In D&D, charm, confusion, sleep, hold and dominate are all **Enchantment** spells: AD&D writes the school "Enchantment/Charm" [lead], and 5e's Dominate Person and Hold Person are Enchantment [lead]. In the 2d12 draft, **Enchantment means a magic that is held** ([Woven] or [Sustained]), and the mind belongs to Thaumaturgy. A player who knows D&D will look for Charm in the wrong School. (Dominions agrees with the draft: its charm and confusion are Thaumaturgy.)
2. **By what they are.** "Charm" is two different effects under one word, and most tables mix them up:
   - **Charmed, in 5e, is not control.** "A charmed creature can't attack the charmer or target the charmer with harmful abilities or magical effects", and the charmer has advantage on social checks [checked]. A guide's summary: "the charmed condition isn't mind control; it's mind influence". It is mostly a **social** effect: a podcast host notes charm "is gone once combat begins" [checked].
   - **Dominated or Controlled is control.** Pathfinder 2e: "the controller dictates your actions and can make you use any of them" [checked]. This is the effect people fear.
   - The podcast: charm and domination "feed into each other, and they are often confused"; at the host's tables charm debates "always devolve" into "No, no, that's not how charmed works" [checked].
3. **By who it is used on.** One host: charm "works well for monsters (harpies, succubi, vampires) used against players, but is less useful for players" [checked]. Against a monster it removes a piece from the GM's side, which nobody minds. Against a player it removes the player.

### 1.2 What goes wrong at the table

- **Agency.** Charmed "really really sucks for players" because it asks them to act against their own interests; turning a player against their friends frustrates [checked]. `conditions-and-senses.md` found the same about any turn lost outright.
- **Ambiguity.** "What would a charmed creature really do?" is a grey area; charm person's "friendly acquaintance" has no mechanics behind it, so tables disagree; whether the target knows it was charmed is the GM's call [checked].
- **Confusion is a different trouble: procedure.** Pathfinder 2e's Confused: you use **all** your actions to Strike, the GM picks your targets at random, you treat no one as an ally, and "each time you take damage from an attack or spell, you can attempt a DC 11 flat check to end the condition" [checked]. The whole turn is taken and replaced by dice.

### 1.3 How games contain them

| Device | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Split the word** | 5e (charmed against dominated) | "Can't harm the charmer" is one condition; control is another, rarer and higher |
| **Take a part, not the turn** | Pathfinder 2e's Stunned N (N actions lost); 4e's dominated: the target is dazed and its controller chooses its one action [memory] | The creature still plays; the effect is counted |
| **An exit the table controls** | Pathfinder 2e: Unconscious from an effect ends on damage, on healing, or when "shaken awake with an Interact action"; Confused: a flat check each time you are damaged; Fascinated "ends if a creature uses hostile actions against you or your allies" [checked] | Allies can do something about it |
| **A goal, not a script** | RPGBot's hosts: "give the player a goal rather than scripting their turn"; "change their motivations and let the player decide how their character acts" [checked] | The player keeps the pen |
| **Make the strong version hard on strong targets** | Pathfinder 2e's incapacitation trait [lead] | Bosses shrug off the turn-takers |
| **Warn the table** | The hosts: use domination sparingly, with consent [checked] | Not a rule |

### 1.4 How it applies to 2d12

- **Keep the two apart, with two words.** A 5e-style **Charmed by** a creature (you can't target it with a harmful Action) is the mirror of 2d12's **Taunted by** (your Actions against anyone else take a Bane): a condition with a source, which Chapter 8 already has a rule for. It takes no turn and needs no judgment.
- **Control as one Action, never a turn.** The bounded form is "the target uses one Action of your choice": the creature moves its Speed where you say, or makes one Weapon Attack against a creature you choose. It can be written as a spell's Effect with no condition at all, and the creature keeps its own turn.
- **Confusion without a table.** One sentence can carry the idea: "its next Strike must target the creature nearest to it". No random roll, no lost turn.
- **Sleep already has its exit** in the first pass (damage, or 1 AP from an adjacent creature): the same two exits Pathfinder 2e gives.
- **The name clash is real** and is one more reason the Schools' names may change later (Lucas: "we can also change names later on").
- **Whatever PCs can do, NPCs will do to PCs.** The test for each of these cards is whether it is still fun pointed at a player.

## Part 2: Summons

### 2.1 Why they are "fun killers"

1. **Action economy.** "A severely unbalanced action economy for one side (which is what happens when you have 20 minions) makes the game trivial"; spells that summon many creatures are considered stronger than single summons "because they really strain the action economy" [lead].
2. **Time.** One player takes several turns while the rest wait. D&D's 2024 revision changed the conjure spells "in an attempt to stop turns getting bogged down" [lead].
3. **Bodies as walls.** A Divinity: Original Sin 2 developer: summons are most valuable when they make the enemy waste actions on them [lead].
4. **Bookkeeping.** Extra stat blocks, extra Health to track, extra positions.

### 2.2 How games contain them

| Game | The rule | Trust |
|---|---|---|
| **Pathfinder 2e, summon spells** | The creature is a **minion**: only **2 actions** a turn (a character has 3), **no reactions**, and it acts only when its summoner spends **1 action to Sustain** the spell; unsustained, it only defends itself or flees. It acts at once when it arrives. It "can't summon other creatures". One creature a spell. | [lead: Roll20's mirror and forum quotes of the rule] |
| **Pathfinder 2e, the Summoner class** | The eidolon is half of one character: "Act Together" lets the two act for a shared cost; posters describe a shared health pool. "It's better to see the summoner and eidolon as the same PC, rather than a PC with a minion." | [lead: forums] |
| **D&D 5e, Tasha's summon spells** | One creature from a "build-a-X stat block" that scales with the spell's level, beside the older conjure spells that called many creatures from the monster book | [lead] |
| **D&D 2024** | The conjure spells no longer make creatures: they "summon a special effect that's themed to Fey, animals, or Elementals". Players split: a forum post calls the new Conjure Animals "reflavored Spirit Guardians" and others say a conjure spell "should still conjure a creature" | [lead] |
| **D&D 4e** | A summon has no turn of its own: its summoner spends their own actions for it to act | [memory] |
| **Savage Worlds, Summon Ally** | **A menu.** Four servants by the caster's rank: Attendant (Novice, 1 Power Point), Bodyguard (Seasoned, 3), Sentinel (Veteran, 5), Mirror Self (Heroic, 7). Duration 5 rounds. **Modifiers bought at casting:** a Combat Edge (+1, up to three), Flight (+1), one Trait a die higher (+1), Mind Rider (+1: sense through it), Additional Allies (half the cost each). "The ally acts on its creator's Action Card", "has no personality, creativity, or emotions", and "fades away with no trace when the power ends or when the ally is Incapacitated". With GM approval abilities can be swapped (a wolf-like attendant trades Shooting for Survival). | [checked: a fan wiki's copy] |
| **Dominions 6** | Battle summons last the battle; almost all cost a gem; they grow in size first, then in number; mindless ones dissolve without a leader of their kind | [read: `dominions-rules.md` 6] |
| **Fans' fixes for 5e** | Treat many summons as one swarm with one turn; make the caster spend their action to keep them; cap what can be summoned by the caster's level | [lead] |

**What the working versions share:** one creature (or one squad); it acts on the summoner's turn; the summoner pays actions for it; it is weaker than a character on purpose (fewer actions, no reactions); it can't chain (no summons from summons); its stat block is made for the spell and grows with the caster.

### 2.3 How it applies to 2d12

Lucas's idea (a creature of about your level with Minion-like stats, growing as you level; built from a menu at preparation, not at each casting; limited) lines up with what the working versions share. What 2d12 already has for it:

- **The Minion rank** (Chapter 14): **3 AP** a turn where others have 4, **no Reactions**, Health for "one or two PC hits", and tables written **by Stage**. "Grows as level goes up" is already true of a Minion: it uses its Stage's row.
- **[Sustained]** (decision 29): "Sustain: 1 AP" at the start of your turn or the spell ends; it also ends when you go Down or become Bloodied or Strained. This is Pathfinder 2e's summon rule in 2d12's words, and the first pass reached it without the research.
- **"Minions share turns"**: a Minion already acts in a shared slot, so a summon that acts on your turn, after you, adds no new place in the Initiative order.
- **Swapping on a Rest:** the build of a summon can be one of the choices made on a Rest, as Lucas wants (Savage Worlds lets the caster choose at each casting; "per preparation" is faster at the table).

**What a build menu could hold** (Savage Worlds' modifiers, in 2d12's terms; none decided): a movement (flying, climbing, burrowing), a damage subtype and the Path's rider, one condition it resists, a sense, Reach, a little Guard. A Path gives the shape its theme (Fire: it burns and lights; Earth: it is slow and hard; Death: a risen corpse; Glamour: an illusion that deals Nonlethal damage and vanishes when struck).

**The limits to decide** (each from a game above):
1. **How many.** One summon a caster (Pathfinder 2e, Savage Worlds' base, Tasha's). [Sustained] allows as many as you pay for; a summon would need its own "one at a time".
2. **Who pays for its actions.** Sustain 1 AP for its 3 AP (Pathfinder 2e's deal: about two for one); or it has no AP and you spend yours for it (4e: no gain in actions, only in position); or it shares your Health (the Summoner).
3. **What it can't do:** Reactions (a Minion has none); Flanking and Opportunity Attacks; summon; be healed; act when you can't.
4. **How it leaves:** when it goes Down (Savage Worlds: "when the ally is Incapacitated"), when you stop paying, when you go Down, when you become Bloodied or Strained.
5. **Whether an "effect" version exists beside it** (D&D 2024's answer: a zone with a theme, no body). 2d12's lasting areas already do this, so the two can live in different Schools: a body is a Conjuration, a zone is an Evocation or an Enchantment.
