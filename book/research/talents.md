# Talents, feats, edges and trees: how other games structure character picks

**Date:** 2026-11-19. **Asked by Lucas,** opening the design of Talents: "The talents is going to be the actual meat of the game [...] How a diplomatic paladin differs from a quiet, ranged paladin, with similar strengths and capabilities. I don't want to fall into the trap of skill feats from 2e, or skill tax from 1e and 3.5, or competing talents like edges from Swade (why nobody really gets social edges instead of combat edges)." ## 1. Lucas's three traps: their documented causes

### Permission (Pathfinder 2e's skill feats)

- **Pathfinder 2e's intent was "anyone can try at a penalty; the feat removes the penalty", but the sentence was never printed on the feats** [read, the Remaster adds "with no penalty" to some; Seifter's 2019 forum reply]. Players read each feat as "without this, you can't".
- **Permission comes from the size of the list:** every small option needs ground, and the ground is taken from the base rule. A forum poster: it "reduces the scope for basic skills, in order for Skill Feats to have something to do" [checked].
- **Draw Steel prints the escape hatch:** anyone can try a perk's mundane task with a test; the perk gives it "without a test" [read].
- **A pass, by the sources:** the Talent makes an attempt faster, cheaper, wider or better on a high result, or opens what is beyond a normal person.

### Tax (D&D 3.5, Pathfinder 1e, 4e)

The documented causes [read, checked]: a pick that restores a number the math assumes (4e's Expertise feats); a rung never used for itself, only to reach the next (Combat Expertise); a pick every character of a kind takes first (Power Attack); buying back a penalty; a swap against a universal number (5e 2014's feat against +2); a ladder whose order is forced. **13th Age's second edition removed the forced order even for feats attached to your own abilities.**

### Competition (Savage Worlds' Edges)

- **Why social Edges lose** [checked, a licensee's blog; read, the rules]: combat Edges "may make a difference between life and death"; with "ONE meaningful, relevant Social Conflict every six sessions, no player is going to go beyond Persuasion d8"; **the social Edges are flat numbers ("Charisma +2") while the combat Edges are new actions**; bonuses stack to near-certainty, so a second social pick is wasted.
- **What the publisher did** [read, Pinnacle's preview]: the Adventure Edition cut flat bonuses, toned down the bundled Professional Edges, and gave social skills work inside a fight (Support, Tests).
- **The one case where social picks won:** Legend of the Five Rings 5e's playtest; testers called its social techniques more needed than its combat ones, because they work in any scene and about half are new actions [checked, beta feedback only].
- **Separate slots end the competition but bring two failures** [read, checked]: combat leaks into the non-combat slot and becomes its best picks (Pathfinder 2e's Battle Medicine, Bon Mot: **actions used in a fight, not bonuses**); and a slot that must be filled breeds permission feats. **The best-liked Pathfinder 2e skill feats are "combat buffs in disguise"** [checked]: the wall on content is the part that matters.

**Why non-combat picks go untaken, a checklist from the edges report:** one budget and the fight is where you die; fights are frequent, social stakes rare; the non-combat pick is a number, the combat pick an action; the skill alone already does the job; the GM decides what a social success does while the rules decide what a hit does; the circumstance never comes up; prerequisites steer later picks to combat; the list holds more combat picks; one face is enough for a party; picks can't be taken back, so the safe one wins.

## 2. The structures, and what each cost

| Structure | Games | Solved | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **One currency or one list for everything** | GURPS, Savage Worlds, 3.5, Pathfinder 1e, Saga | Total freedom | Taxes, chains, traps; combat crowds out the rest; "a shopping list" |
| **Silos or typed slots, by kind** | Pathfinder 2e, Draw Steel, 4e's utility powers, Blades, The One Ring, Stars and Worlds Without Number | Non-combat stops competing | Each silo must be filled (permission); combat leaks in; more to track |
| **Picks attached to what you own** | 13th Age, metamagic, 4e's power feats | No orphan picks; a short list | Small picks; a ladder becomes a tax if forced |
| **A pick against a universal number** | 5e 2014 | Simple | The number wins; flavor picks die |
| **Fixed trees** | Fantasy Flight's Star Wars, old World of Warcraft, Borderlands | Theme, direction, a readable sheet | Filler between good boxes; a race to one node ("Dedication"); order taxes; heavy to write |
| **One shared graph** | Crucible (109 nodes, 370 talents, read from source), Path of Exile | Hybrids for free | Needs software; travel nodes |
| **A tier pyramid** | Genesys | Works for any setting; no box in your way | Sameness; a base-tier tax; lost theme: players rebuilt trees and the publisher later printed rules for them |
| **Three written ranks in one pick** | Lancer, AGE, Symbaroum, Ironsworn's assets | Depth against breadth is a real choice; each step is news | Each pick is three designs |
| **Paths at set levels** | Shadow of the Demon Lord, 5e subclasses, Daggerheart's subclass cards | No traps, fast | Few decisions |
| **Like-for-like rows** | World of Warcraft 2012, Guild Wars 2, Draw Steel's "one ability of each cost" | Every choice comparable; utility never competes with power | Fewer picks |
| **A loadout smaller than the collection** | Daggerheart (five live cards, the rest vaulted) | Narrow picks stay worth owning | Sorting at each rest |
| **A short list that is yours** | PbtA, Blades, Cypher | Easy choice; rule-bending picks | Uneven strength inside a list |
| **No picks** | Call of Cthulhu, Into the Odd, Mythras | Nothing to optimize | Difference only in history, gear, the moment |

**The video game designers, in their own words** [read]: Greg Street, 2011, removing World of Warcraft's trees: "fundamentally flawed and unfixable"; "bloated trees that have a ton of inconsequential talents that you have to slog through"; "we don't think it's humanly possible to have 40-50 fun, interesting and balanced talents in a tree"; "interesting choices are fun. The word 'interesting' is key". Blizzard, 2022, bringing trees back: they "communicate dependencies and magnitude intuitively, without a lot of added rules". **What survived both changes: free respec; utility kept apart from power; mandatory abilities given, not chosen.** ArenaNet, 2015: "separate stats from traits".

## 3. Ranks inside a pick

Three written steps, each a new rule (Lancer, Symbaroum, AGE), are praised; criticised only for uneven worth. **The repeatable +1** (Fantasy Flight's Grit, Warhammer 4e's +1 success level) is what gets called filler and the memory load: "Do you really notice that you now kill a creature in 2.9 GCDs instead of 3 GCDs?" [read, Street]. A correction to the 2026-11-09 proposal: **Lancer allows one reallocation at each level-up, not free re-spending** [lead, a fan reference].

## 4. Principles several designers state, with their counter-arguments

1. **An option is worth having when it is right in some situations and not others.** *Counter:* situational options go unused if the situation doesn't occur at this table.
2. **If everyone must take it, build it in** (Josh Sawyer: "If something is required for viable play, WHY make it an option"). *Counter:* the Alexandrian and Mark Rosewater: unequal options are what make choosing a skill.
3. **Fewer choices that can be compared beat many that can't.** *Counter:* overcorrection reads as stiff.
4. **A pick should be describable without numbers.** Street's example: a root against a snare, not 5% against 10% damage. *Counter:* numbers are the easiest to remember, price and stack; a game of only verbs gets wide.
5. **An option must not take from everyone else what the base rule gave** (permission). *Counter:* the supernatural, where gating is the point.
6. **Combat and non-combat picks should not come from one pool.** *Counter:* the wall leaks unless it is a wall on content; a silo with no rules under it is filler.
7. **Memory is the budget.** Rosewater calls bookkeeping what "could matter but often don't"; Daggerheart caps live cards at five; **conditional and off-turn triggers are the heaviest load.** *Counter:* a cap on live options is itself a rule to manage.
8. **You only learn which options are dead or dominant from play.** No counter found.

**Open disagreements he will have to decide:** balance of options or a slope; a hard wall between combat and non-combat, or options that serve both; reliability outside a fight; free swapping or weight; a hand or a catalogue; one size or minor and major; ranks or new verbs; numbers in Talents at all. One the record has already answered: options as the centre (Lucas: "the meat") against options as "the flourish" (5e, Mearls).

## 5. Loved non-combat options, and small picks that make an identity

- **Loved non-combat options share six traits** [the design-thinking report's reading]: they create a scene; they are reliable; they belong to the character alone; they are not a number; they sit just past what anyone could do; they are rationed. **Reliability collides with `roleplay-skills.md`'s "no certainty"**; Routine is one answer already taken.
- **Small picks that make an identity** (Cypher's focus, Ironsworn's assets) share four traits: a name that says who the character is; very few picks; a rule against duplicating another player's; a described trigger. Ironsworn prices deepening an asset at 2 experience against 3 for a new one.

## 6. Tests to hold a single Talent against (combined from the four reports)

- **Tax:** would a character who skips it fall below what the numbers assume? Is its main use to be a Prerequisite? Would everyone of a kind take it first? Does it buy back a penalty on the character's own kit? Must its upgrades come in a fixed order?
- **Permission:** would a GM have let anyone try this before the Talent existed? If yes, it must be written as faster, cheaper, wider, without a roll, or better on a high result. Did the base rule have to be narrowed to give it room? Does it ask the GM to remember a penalty for everyone without it?
- **Competition:** can a combat and a non-combat option fill the same slot? Can a number fill it? Can an effect usable in a fight fill a non-combat slot?
- **Must-have:** is there a build where it isn't the best in its slot? Can it be put on one scale with its neighbours? (Then one of them wins.)
- **Bare number:** can it be described without a number? Does it change a decision or a visible moment?
- **Forgettable:** is it a condition to remember on someone else's turn, or on a roll already made? Is it printed where it is used?
- **Parasitic:** how many things carry the tag it keys on, and will that grow?
- **Filler (for any tree or list):** does the structure need a fixed number of boxes? Are there more picks than good ideas?

## 7. Tests for the structure

Is any pick ever a choice between fight and not-fight? Can a non-combat pick be used in a fight? How many decisions at a level-up, and can the options in one slot be compared? **For a character at each Stage, count buttons, always-on rule changes and conditional or off-turn triggers: keep the third near zero.** How many picks must be written (slots × options × levels, against Street's ceiling)? What does a mistake cost to undo? Does the list eat the base rules? What happens when option N+1 is added?

## 8. How it applies to 2d12 (observations)

**What 2d12 already has, by the four reports:**

| What | Where | Matches |
|---|---|---|
| Typed slots (a Skill Talent at levels 1 and 3, a Talent at 2, a Stage Talent at 4) | Chapter 5 | Draw Steel, Pathfinder 2e, Stars Without Number: the fix for competition |
| "Levels give options; Stages change the numbers"; a Modifier never above +4 | Chapter 5 | Takes away the ground the math-fix tax and the pick-against-a-number stand on; Mutants & Masterminds' cap |
| Swapping on a Rest | Chapter 5 | The most generous retraining in the survey; one of the three things World of Warcraft kept both times |
| "A skill or Talent never grants permission for a mundane action: it makes you better at it" | `roleplay-skills.md`, proposal 1 | The sentence Pathfinder 2e didn't print |
| Talents that modify Actions by trait | the set-aside chapter; class charter rule 11 | 13th Age's attached feat, made general; Lancer's talents keyed on weapon types |
| The seeds: "the base rule carries the limit; a Talent lifts it" | magic record 5l | Fate's rule-exception stunt; Path of Exile's keystones; the one job a structure does when nothing grows vertically |
| "No one best button"; "a pair to choose from differs in shape, not in role" | class charter rules 18, 19 | The test against dominated options |
| "No silent bonuses, no tracking across turns" | class charter rules 6, 21 | The memory budget |

**What the reports flag as unsettled or conflicting in the project:**
- **The set-aside chapter is Savage Worlds' Edge structure almost item for item** (categories, a level, Prerequisites, chains): the design whose social picks nobody takes. Its example Heavy Blows is a number.
- **"Skill Talents never give combat numbers"** (the 2026-11-09 proposal) is narrower than the leak: what leaked in Pathfinder 2e was **actions used in a fight**.
- **Two retraining rules are on file:** any Rest (the book) against one swap at each level-up (the proposal).
- **Lucas's own note on skill direction 4 ("Talents can increase") reads "Talent tax".**
- **No precedent was found for a pick that always carries both a combat half and a non-combat half** (`roleplay-skills.md` option C); 5e's half-feat is the nearest.
- **Decision 117 (no help outside a fight; the best suited rolls) strengthens the "one face is enough for a party" reason** that any non-combat Talent has to answer.
