# Resources without spell slots, a resource for martials, and what recovery does to a cost

**Date:** 2026-11-18. **Asked by Lucas,** when the limiters of magic were reopened:
- "Nonlethal damage is removed after a quick rest, this isn't great and I already wasn't happy with it, since I think Take a Breather is just for it. Instead, Nonlethal is removed like normal damage, and removed completely after a Sleep, but then we are in the problem with: Martials don't need rest, casters do. Even if Overreaching is optional, I feel players might spam it, or
- A 'Stamina' mechanic that expands to Martials, differently named. Casters use Mana, Martials use 'Stamina'. This is an extra layer yes, but I have heard good things about Battlemaster and martial abilities that are similar to casting in the sense of they use a certain resource.
- These two above could make use of an extensive search."

**What was read, and how far to trust it.** Three independent researchers, each with its own brief, about 200 searches and page loads in all. Their full reports, with every source, are kept: `raw/2026-11-18-martial-resources.md`, `raw/2026-11-18-mana-and-stamina.md`, `raw/2026-11-18-recovery-and-rest.md`. **This file is Claude's summary of those three reports; Claude did not reopen their pages.** Their own marks are kept: [read] rules text on a page opened, [checked] an opinion on a page opened, [lead] a search summary or a page that refused, [memory] no page opened. Every page went through a reader that returns extracts, so **a quote here should be rechecked on its page before it is printed anywhere.** Weakest: D&D 4e's and Tome of Battle's rules (memory and reviews), DC20 (a beta; no official rules text opened; its Stamina refresh is disputed), the kineticist's reception (leads only), Shadowrun and Warhammer.

**Already in the project:** `nova-burst.md` (a resource built in the fight removes hoarding and makes round 1 slow; one pool or several), `caster-class-shape.md` (Draw Steel's caster), `dominions-magic.md` and `dominions-core.md` (fatigue). The class redesign's question 6 (2026-11-14): offered a pool for the Paladin, Lucas chose "nothing new to track" ("While I like the pool of points, I dont think the game needs more tracking").

**Other games' rules and opinions, never 2d12's. Nothing here is decided.**

## 1. A cost is priced by its recovery clock, not by its size

The clearest finding of the three reports.

| The clock | Examples | What players do |
|---|---|---|
| **A round** | Savage Worlds' Shaken; turns spent gathering power | Used every round; never causes a rest |
| **A fight** | 4e's encounter powers; Pathfinder 2e's focus points (10 minutes) | Spent every fight: it becomes the normal turn. No rest pressure |
| **10 minutes** | Starfinder's Stamina; Pathfinder 2e's Treat Wounds; **2d12's Breather** | **Free outside a fight**, unless the breather itself spends something slow. Tables hand-wave it |
| **Hours** | D&D 3.5 nonlethal (1 a level an hour); 5e's short rest (1 hour) | Long enough to be skipped; 5e's hour is the documented case |
| **A night** | 5e's slots; the Pathfinder 1e kineticist's burn | A daily budget: players nova and rest, or hoard and never use it |
| **A day or more** | Dungeon Crawl Classics' spellburn (1 point a day); Blades in the Dark's stress | Emergency use, or managed across sessions |

- **Paizo says it in its own rules** [read]: the Pathfinder 2e Stamina variant's breather costs a daily Resolve Point; the page offers a free version and warns that a feat must then be removed, "as it's too powerful if there's no cost to use it!".
- **Pathfinder 2e's Treat Wounds** (10 minutes, free) made healing "cheap and plentiful"; "many GMs just hand wave healing"; "it eats into table time" [checked, EN World]. A free 10-minute cost becomes bookkeeping that tables skip.
- **Starfinder's answer** [read]: the fast pool (Stamina) is refilled in 10 minutes **by spending the slow one** (a Resolve Point, back once a day). The breather is quick but not free.

**For 2d12:** Chapter 4's Breather removes all Nonlethal damage in 10 minutes at no cost. By this table, Overreach as decided (1d6! Nonlethal) is free between fights. Lucas saw it; Claude had called it "a real price".

## 2. Two clocks in one party

- **Jonathan Tweet** (a designer of D&D's third edition) [checked]: "Classes with lots of per-day power are too strong when there are one or two fights per day"; "Whatever per-day powers are balanced at one rate of fights per day are necessarily unbalanced at faster or slower rates."
- **D&D 5e is the documented case** of classes on different clocks (the fighter, monk and warlock recover in an hour; the wizard and cleric overnight): players "skip short rests very often in favour of long rests" [lead]; at the table, "We need a LONG REST / But you CAN'T HAVE ONE" [checked]. The 2024 revision didn't put everyone on one clock: it gave each side a little of the other's.
- **The five-minute workday**: resting after one or two fights "virtually eliminates the resource management aspect" [checked]; in a 2022 EN World poll about a third of respondents saw it regularly in 5e [checked].
- **The published ways out:** the same clocks for every class (4e; Blades in the Dark's stress track); count fights instead of hours (13th Age: a full heal-up after four battles, and resting early is "a campaign loss") [read]; assume no attrition between fights (Pathfinder 2e's hit points); **pay for pressing on** (Draw Steel's Victories; 4e's milestones).

**For 2d12:** this is exactly Lucas's worry ("Martials don't need rest, casters do"). Two things already in 2d12 matter here. **Everyone's Health is on the same slow clock** (only a Rest restores Rally Points), martials included: a front-liner needs the Rest more than anyone. And **Momentum already pays for pressing on** (+1 an encounter won, -1 a Sleep, cleared by a Rest): it is Draw Steel's Victories in 2d12's words.

## 3. Optional self-harm to cast harder: is it spammed?

| Game | The cost | It comes back | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pathfinder 1e kineticist, Burn** [read] | Nonlethal damage equal to your level, a point of burn | "can't be healed by any means other than getting a full night's rest" | **The closest precedent to Overreach on a Sleep clock.** Reported as "a disguised HP chassis modification"; players took a little early as a buff, then avoided it and spent turns "gathering power" to lower the cost; arguments over what a night's rest is [lead: forums refused]. **Its second-edition remake dropped self-damage entirely** |
| **Dungeon Crawl Classics, spellburn** [read] | Ability points, +1 to the casting roll each | 1 point a day, and not while you burn again | Saved for the big moment; 20 at once is a guaranteed best result |
| **Shadowrun, drain** [read: a fan summary] | Stun damage after every spell, resisted by a roll; Physical if you overcast | Stun by the hour, Physical by the day | The caster chooses the risk every spell; an extra roll every casting |
| **Blades in the Dark, pushing yourself** [read] | 2 stress | Only in downtime, by indulging a vice; a full track is a permanent trauma | Used constantly but watched; **every character has the same track, so nobody rests more than anyone else** |
| **GURPS** [read: an unofficial page] | Fatigue points, the same pool a fighter spends on extra effort | About 1 in 10 minutes | A few big spells, then a tired wizard; high skill makes small spells free |

**The pattern:** spam is decided by two things together: the clock, and whether a cheaper way to the same effect exists. A cost that returns in minutes is spammed; one that returns overnight is avoided or hoarded; one that every character shares causes no argument.

## 4. A resource for martials

**The Battle Master** (D&D 5e) [read]: four superiority dice (d8), back on a short rest; a maneuver is chosen **after the hit** and adds the die and a rider (trip, push, frighten).
- Liked: "You don't take maneuvers for extra damage but the rider" [checked].
- Disliked: too few dice ("only 4 Superiority die"); dependent on the table ("it's an issue of how many short rests your table takes"); the best maneuvers are taken first; and against any pool at all: "the fighter that can just 'go all day'" [checked]. The 2024 version gives a free d8 a turn at level 15, so it never runs dry.

**D&D 4e** (every class: at-will, encounter and daily powers) [memory; posters checked]:
- For: "It was so nice being able to play the character type I want with the level of mechanical interest I enjoy"; the martial and caster gap was solved [checked].
- Against, and this is the famous one: "The needless symmetry of all classes having the exact same number of powers, recharge, and format"; "why would you only get to trip a person 1/day?"; "nearly every class had a power that did the same thing" [checked]. One of its designers, relayed [checked]: "I wanted classes to have different power acquisition schedules"; "4e had a tendency to build matrices and try to fill them".
- **The complaint was the identical frame, more than martials having a resource.**

**Tome of Battle** (D&D 3.5) [reviews checked; the book not opened]: maneuvers readied, spent, and **recovered in the fight a different way for each class**: all at once by spending a turn on a plain attack; one at a time by a whole round doing nothing (hated); or dealt at random, a few a round, at no cost in actions (the best liked). "Many 3rd Edition players loved it"; twenty years on, its three recoveries are still praised.

**Level Up (Advanced 5e)** [read]: an exertion pool of twice your proficiency bonus (4 at level 1), back on a short rest; maneuvers cost 1 to 3. "Like getting Book of Nine Swords back." Its longest argument was the word: "You lose stamina. That's my peeve" (you build exertion, you spend stamina).

**13th Age** [read]: its first edition gave martials no pool, only triggers (the fighter chose a maneuver after seeing the die; the rogue's momentum was lost when hit). Both were disliked for taking control away, and **the second edition replaced both** with a resource the player builds and keeps.

**What the liked ones share:** a rider chosen at the moment of the hit; a refresh the player can count on every fight; a structure that differs from the caster's. **What the disliked ones share:** a daily limit the fiction can't explain; the same schedule for every class; too few uses; a turn spent only on recovering; control taken away.

## 5. A pool for casters, and "Mana and Stamina"

- **Every point pool read needs a second rule to stop a nova.** D&D 5e's spell points cap the top spells at one of each level a day; 3.5's psionics and DC20 cap the points spent on one casting; Shadow of the Demon Lord ties castings to each spell; Draw Steel starts the pool near empty. Slots are "a pool with a built-in nova cap": remove them and the cap must be written.
- **DC20 is Lucas's idea in print** [read: the publisher's page and a fan reference; a beta]: "Martials get Stamina Points and Attack Maneuvers"; "Spellcasters get Mana Points and Spell Enhancements". Stamina is tiny (about 2 at level 1), pays for maneuvers and comes back fast (sources disagree: a short rest, or the end of combat, or on class triggers in the fight). Mana is larger, daily, with a "Mana Spend Limit" a casting. **The two names carry two rhythms.** Reception is thin: one GM found the amount to track the hard part.
- **Two names, one mechanic** [checked, EN World on Draw Steel's nine named resources]: "These aren't different resources (you spend them in the same way too)"; "the Gygax Thesaurus problem of too many synonyms". Against: "the real difference is what it can be spent on", and the names "had 0 impact on how long it took players to learn".
- **Fabula Ultima** [read]: everyone, martial or caster, spends Mind Points on one formula; a second pool (Inventory Points) refuels the first as potions. No complaint found about the shared pool. Its fights are "designed to go no longer than about 3 or 4 rounds", and "a party can handle two conflict encounters before needing to rest".
- **Tracking is the standing objection.** Gygax on spell points: "more complication, more record keeping, more wasted time" [checked]. Savage Worlds prints an option that removes its pool and replaces it with a penalty on the casting roll [lead]. A designer of a one-pool game: "nothing slows a game down quite like players tracking a bunch of passive abilities". Small pools counted in tokens cost least; one writer suggests marking a resource only when it is **out**.
- **Built in the fight, or spent from full.** Built (Draw Steel): can't be hoarded, gives no five-minute day, "feels more heroic", and makes round 1 slow. Spent from full (every other pool; Dominions' fatigue): players hoard, or empty it and rest.
- **Is no resource at all liked?** Split [checked, a 2026 EN World thread]: without resources a game is "a prompt engine with randomizers", against "can do without this kind of overhead these days". One poster's wish: a single "stamina" for everything, with basic abilities still usable when "wiped out".

## 6. How it applies to 2d12

**What 2d12 already has that these games built pools to get:**

| Other games' device | 2d12's |
|---|---|
| A pool refilled every round (DC20's Action Points) | **AP**: 4 and a Reaction AP, for everyone |
| A resource built in the fight (Draw Steel, 13th Age's commander) | **Escalation**, which is never spent |
| A reward for pressing on (Victories, milestones) | **Momentum** |
| The day's clock, the same for every class (surges, Recoveries) | **Rally Points** and Health |
| A refresh every fight (encounter powers, focus points) | **Frequency**: Round and Encounter |

**The three roads, with what the research says of each:**

1. **Nonlethal on the Sleep clock** (Lucas's first idea). It makes Overreach a daily resource in all but name. The kineticist is the precedent: avoided, argued over, and dropped in its remake. Against that: in 2d12 Health is already everyone's daily clock, so a caster who spends Health by choice and a front-liner who loses it by standing in front are on **the same** clock, which is the thing 5e lacked. The real risk is the five-minute day; Momentum already pushes the other way.
2. **A breather that isn't free** (Starfinder's answer). The Breather stays quick but spends something slow that everyone holds. 2d12 has one: a Rally Point.
3. **A cost that lives inside the fight** (the remade kineticist; every "per round" cost). Tempo, a Bane, lost Guard, the Reaction AP. It never touches the rest question, and Smite already does it ("you can spend your Reaction AP to roll 1 more Smite die").
4. **Stamina and Mana.** The research's conditions for it to work: a nova rule written with it; two rhythms, or it is one pool with two names (and with one schedule for all, 4e's "samey" complaint, which is Lucas's own fear); small enough to count in tokens; refreshed every fight or built in it, never daily for one and not the other; free options beside it so a character at zero still has a real turn; and spent at the moment of the hit, for a rider, which is what everyone praises in the Battle Master.
