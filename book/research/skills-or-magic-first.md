# Skills first, magic first, or together: what the order of design did in other games

**Date:** 2026-11-19. **Asked by Lucas,** before designing magic outside a fight: "Should we do skills side by side? Should we do skill first? Or good to start with magic? Research before answering."

**What was read, and how far to trust it.** One independent researcher; the full report (about 3,250 words, every source and link) is `raw/2026-11-19-skills-or-magic-first.md`. **This file is Claude's summary of it.** **The evidence is thin, and the note says where:** three or four cases, no controlled comparison, and **designers themselves do not discuss this order in print** (no essay, column or diary on it was found; the only stated advice is the generic "core mechanic first"). The dates of D&D's history are solid; the reading of what they caused is historians' and bloggers'. The strongest evidence is four Paizo design blogs on Pathfinder 2e, read in full; their dates show publication order, not the order of work. Not opened: Jon Peterson's books, The Alexandrian, Kevin Crawford's posts, MCDM's design videos. D&D 4e's ritual rules are from memory. **No account of regret was found for any order,** and no case of a magic system rewritten once skills arrived.

**Other games, never 2d12's rules. Nothing here is decided.**

---

## 1. What followed each order

| Order | Games | What followed | How strong the evidence is |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Magic first, skills later, nothing fixed between them** | D&D 1974 (spells) then 1975 (the thief's skills, added when the spell list already held Knock and Invisibility); 3e and 5e carrying the old spell list into a new skill system | The spells kept the outcomes and were never re-cut, so the skill became the weaker road to the same place. Skills arriving without a stated baseline were read as a permission list | The order is fact; the cause is commentators' reading, repeated for decades. No designer's own account |
| **Skills first, then magic keyed to them** | D&D 4e (rituals resolved by skill checks); 13th Age (rituals resolved by a background check) | No overlap complaint. 4e's rituals went unused for other reasons (price, time) | Rules text for the dependency; no designer on the order |
| **A frame first, then both inside it** | Pathfinder 2e (proficiency ranks and four degrees of success stated first; rituals, skills and skill feats each cite them; the skill list came later); Blades in the Dark by construction | Magic and skills speak one language. Its reception problems (casters feel weak, feats read as permission) come from its stance, not its order | The strongest here: four designer blogs. One game |

**The honest summary:** the history supports "magic written with no skill frame fixed does not get corrected later" better than it supports "skills must be finished first".

## 2. What magic actually needs to know about skills

The researcher went through ritual and utility-magic texts and listed what they cite. **None cites the number of skills or their bonuses. They cite the frame.** Nine decisions:

| # | The decision | 2d12 has it? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What a check outside a fight is, and how many outcomes it has | **Yes, in the rulebook:** a Check is one Attribute Modifier against a DC (default 13), with the three Tiers |
| 2 | What a failure costs | Proposal only (`roleplay-skills.md` item 6); Alert is a ready price for loud magic (`book/ideas/expedition-loop.md`) |
| 3 | What "trained" means, in one sentence | Proposal only (items 1 and 2: a skill never grants permission, it makes you better) |
| 4 | Whether anything succeeds without a roll, and for whom | Proposal only (Routine, item 5; "no certainty", item 2) |
| 5 | What form help takes on someone else's check | **Yes, for the currency:** Boons and Banes with their named limits. Whether magic may give one outside a fight is the stance (step 1 of `magic-out-of-combat.md`) |
| 6 | How one character helps another, and how a group acts together | **Not found in the rulebook.** Proposals: Backgrounds as 1 Boon, Challenges |
| 7 | The units of time outside a fight | Partly (a Sleep, a Rest; long durations "as written") |
| 8 | The subjects skills cover, as categories of outcome (not the final list) | Proposal only, **and two project files disagree:** `roleplay-skills.md` says about 8 to 12 broad skills; `MASTER-PLAN.md` says 16, four for each Attribute |
| 9 | Which roll, if any, a casting makes | Open (step 6 of `magic-out-of-combat.md`) |

## 3. How it applies to 2d12 (observations)

- **2d12 is not in D&D's position.** The danger case is magic finished with no skill frame at all. 2d12 has the Check and its Tiers in the book, Boons and Banes as the one currency, and written proposals for the rest.
- **What is missing is small and is all frame:** decisions 2, 3, 4 and 6 above, plus the categories of 8. The skill list, the numbers and the Skill Talents are not on the list.
- **The stance of magic outside a fight and the frame of skills are one decision seen from two sides.** "Magic never does what a skill does" needs the categories of what skills do. "No certainty" has to be true of both or of neither. Routine (no roll for your own trade) is the non-magical side of the same balance.
- **A correction to an earlier note:** Fabula Ultima's rule that a ritual may never replicate "an existing spell or Skill" uses "Skill" for class abilities, not for a skill list [memory].
