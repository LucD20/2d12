# Cover from creatures: which line counts

**Asked 2026-11-19 (Lucas, playtest 6):** a corner line from Sylwen to the Ogre passed through Kaela, and Foundry gave no Cover. Should a
creature on any line give Cover, or only one on the center line (2d12 today)? **What was read:** search summaries only, with the pages not
opened, so everything below is a **[lead]** until checked. The playtest's record is `book/playtest-6.md` (R1).

## Takeaways

1. **Pathfinder 2e uses one line, center to center, for creatures** [lead: the Roll20 compendium's Cover entry, quoted on Paizo's forum]:
   "draw a line from the center of your space to the center of the target's space"; a creature on it gives **lesser cover** (+1 AC); terrain on
   it gives standard cover. A creature two or more sizes larger than both gives standard cover. Leaning: the GM can measure from an edge or a
   corner of your space. PF1 used "any point to any point"; PF2 changed that on purpose, and the forum threads found say the center line isn't too
   restrictive.
2. **4e used corner lines, and creatures in them gave cover against ranged attacks** [lead: EN World threads, no rules text read]. Posters read
   it as "your allies never grant cover to your enemies" and as cover only against ranged attacks. The corner method has a known quarrel, the
   "pingpong dilemma" (attacker and target get the same result whoever is behind what), which DMs override with common sense.
3. **5e: any creature gives half cover (+2 AC), whatever its size** (Jeremy Crawford's ruling, quoted in EN World threads [lead]). Posters say
   it is real but often unenforced: it slows play, and it is the reason Sharpshooter (which ignores it) matters so much; without it "Archery is
   so powerful".
4. **Cover from creatures is what makes a shooter move** (already in `archer.md`, takeaway 4: Pathfinder 2e, "one turn of setup pays
   dividends").

## How it applies to 2d12

- **Our rule is Pathfinder 2e's** (Chapter 3, Cover: "on the line from the center of your square to the center of the target's space"), with
  corner lines kept for walls and objects. It is the least ambiguous of the three: one line, drawn the same way at every table, and Foundry
  already draws it (the dashed line).
- **Any corner line** (4e and 5e style) would make Low cover almost constant for a shooter in a crowded fight. Every ally in front of a corner
  would give it, and the archers would pay most. It would also raise the worth of Clear Lines (the Archer's level 2 Passive), already priced on
  today's rule.
- **What looked wrong in playtest 6 was the picture, not the rule:** Foundry shows the four corner lines in the same green as the center line,
  so a corner line crossing Kaela looks like it should count. A clearer display (the center line strong, creatures it crosses marked; the corner
  lines only for walls) would answer the question on the map.
