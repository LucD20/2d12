# Divine against profane as a game mechanic

## 2026-11-14: for the class redesign (MASTER-PLAN item 5), before any Paladin card

**The question (Lucas):** the Paladin is "focusing in divine action against profane forces". How do games make holy against unholy a mechanic, and what goes wrong?

**How far to trust this file.** Two passes the same day; the second checked the first (verdicts with poster and date: `raw/2026-11-14-C-verified.md`). [read] = rules text read in full (4e, 13th Age, Draw Steel; **Pathfinder 2e from the official Foundry system's data**). [relayed] = rules text a search tool relayed, not re-read (5e, 3.5). [checked] = an opinion found on its page. [lead] = still unopened (Giant in the Playground refuses readers). [mine] = Claude's reading. The first report: `raw/2026-11-14-C-champion-holy.md` (sections 3 to 5).

### Takeaways

1. **A bonus gated on a creature type fails in both directions.**
   - *Does nothing:* D&D 3.5's Smite Evil [relayed]: "If the paladin accidentally smites a creature that is not evil, the smite has no effect, but the ability is still used up for that day." [lead] "Woefully underwhelming."
   - *Does too much:* Pathfinder 1's Smite Evil [checked, Paizo threads]: "solo BBEG battles are among the most climactic things you can have" and the paladin ends them; the GMs' counter: "change the BBEG to a BBNG... No evil, no smite evil." The designer answered "We are aware of some imbalance issues with this class ability" and cut it.
   - *The named version:* the ranger's Favored Enemy [checked, EN World]: "No class should dictate this much to the DM. Either Favored Enemy is worthless because it never comes up, it's too good because it comes up all the time". [checked, Paizo] "an ability that requires the DM building the game around one particular character"; Paizo's creative director: "Don't play a ranger if the GM isn't interested in running a game that gives you a chance to... use your favored enemy."
   - *Rare even in a game built around it:* in Pathfinder 2e's Monster Core [read] 33 of 492 creatures (about 7%) are weak to holy, 22 of 30 fiends, and **1 of 38 undead** (undead are unholy, but not weak to holy). [checked] "The 'Holy' and 'Unholy' traits - by themselves - do pretty much nothing."
   - *For 2d12:* **our Divine Power is this design** ("When you deal Holy damage to a creature with the Profane Tag, that creature takes 1 Bane on its [Strike] Actions"): a level 1 Feature that is 0 in a fight with no Profane creature, and the list of Tags isn't written (Chapter 7's open box).
2. **The settled pattern: it always works, and a little more against the profane.** 5e's Divine Smite [relayed]: full value on anything, +1d8 against fiends and undead. Pathfinder 2e's remastered Smite [read]: +3 damage against the enemy you declare, +4 if it is unholy. 4e [read]: the paladin's damage is radiant on everything, and most undead are vulnerable to radiant; the one loud type-only power (Turn Undead: a burst that damages, pushes 3 + Charisma squares and immobilizes undead) sits on the cleric.
   - *For 2d12:* Holy is already a damage subtype, and Resist and Weak exist. "Profane creatures are Weak to Holy" in the Bestiary gives the Paladin his edge with no line on any Paladin card; a card can add "more against a Profane creature" as its one rider.
3. **Don't let the anti-profane part be the class's budget.** [checked, Paizo, a Kingmaker GM's thread, 2024] "'Unholy' is a much smaller category than 'evil' was... a pretty unambiguous downgrade". A player's Champion from level 3 to 20: "her 9th-level Divine Smite ability to deal persistent good damage almost never mattered". The class survived because its reaction and its shield never depended on the enemy's type. **The GM's own fix was profane by behaviour:** "treat people who have specifically committed crimes against their kingdom as Unholy".
   - *For 2d12:* price every Paladin card against a non-Profane enemy; the Profane rider is a bonus on top, small (Mild or Moderate).
4. **The player declares the target of judgment.** Draw Steel's Judgment [read], 13th Age's Paladin's Challenge [read], 5e's Vow of Enmity and the 2024 ranger's Hunter's Mark [relayed]. The mark replaces the creature type: "against the profane" becomes "against the one I judged". [lead] The 2024 ranger's lesson: the mark must be cheap to place and must not fight the class's other buttons (Hunter's Mark needs concentration and a bonus action, and "DnD fans still dislike the Ranger").
   - *For 2d12:* a judged creature can simply **count as Profane for you**: one rule ties the mark and the Tag together, and every "against a Profane creature" line works in every campaign.
5. **Profane by behaviour has precedent, thinner than first reported.** A legacy Pathfinder 2e feat (Vengeful Oath) [read]: Lay on Hands can damage "a creature you witness harming an innocent or a ally as if it were undead". The Kingmaker GM's ruling above [checked]. The Champion's Liberating Step triggers on an ally being grabbed or restrained, not only damaged [read]. The Remaster's Smite extends itself while the enemy "takes a hostile action against you or one of your allies" [read]. No essay was found that proposes a list of behaviours.
   - *For 2d12* [mine]: behaviours our rules can already see: a creature that is Hidden or in Darkness, one that caused Frightened or a Lingering condition, a summoned creature, one that brought an ally Down or damaged a Down creature. "The creature that brought your ally Down counts as Profane for you" is a judgment with no button.
6. **4e's "radiant mafia": holy weakness as a status the party applies.** [lead] One character made targets vulnerable to radiant and everyone dealt radiant: campaign-proof, and it became the strongest party build in the game.
   - *For 2d12:* a caution more than a model. "The target is Weak to Holy" as a rider helps only the Paladin unless allies deal Holy; "Vulnerable 1" is our party-wide version and is priced (Mild, 3).
7. **Holy light as a tool is rare.** Pathfinder 2e's Flash of Grandeur [read]: the Champion's reaction lights the attacker (dazzled; no invisibility or concealment) until the end of the Champion's next turn; at level 9 its lingering damage can't be shaken off while the light lasts. A guide says it "punishes hidden tricks" [checked]; **no player's account of it in play was found.** A legacy feat, Light of Revelation [read]: a 30-foot bright light and +1 to find Hidden creatures. 5e's Sacred Weapon sheds bright light [relayed]. Draw Steel's Exorcist [read]: judged creatures can't Hide; later he sees through illusions. 3.5's Consecrate [relayed]: a zone where undead are weakened and can't be summoned.
   - *For 2d12:* this is where we are ahead: light is a real rule here (Bright and Dim, the same shapes as Areas, it matters where the target is, Hide, Darkness) and Smite already makes its target shed Bright light with a Bane on Hide. Today that is a line lost in a 133-word card. As the Paladin's rule-bender it is an identity: **creatures in your light can't Hide; your light ends magical Darkness; the creature you judged glows.**
8. **A type system can become a straitjacket.** [lead] 4e made Undead a keyword instead of a type.
   - *For 2d12:* Tags are already words with no rule of their own. Profane can stay one Tag the GM puts on what the world calls profane (undead, fiends, the cursed), with no sub-list.
