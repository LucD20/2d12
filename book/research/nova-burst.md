# Nova and burst: building it, capping it, making the big hit land

## 2026-11-14: for the class redesign (MASTER-PLAN item 5), before any Paladin card

**The question (Lucas):** nova and burst classes. "When they use all they have against an enemy, they can explode before seeing it coming", for a class that "isn't a consistent damage dealer".

**How far to trust this file.** Two passes the same day; the second opened the pages and checked the first's leads (52 verified, 13 corrected, 12 not on their page and removed here, 11 on sites that refuse readers; verdicts with author and date: `raw/2026-11-14-DF-verified.md`). [read] = rules text read in full (Draw Steel, 13th Age, Pathfinder 2e, Lancer, 4e, the 5e 2024 SRD). [checked] = found on its page. [lead] = still unopened. [memory] = from memory, unverified. [mine] = Claude's reading or arithmetic. The first report: `raw/2026-11-14-F-nova.md`. 13th Age's escalation die: `escalation-and-guard.md`.

### Takeaways

1. **What feels bad about a nova:**
   - *One character ends the climax while the table watches.* [checked, Mike Mearls, quoted by Wargamer, October 2025] 5e "was built around the assumption that characters would engage in about 20 rounds of combat between long rests"; "The gap between daily and at-will damage is far bigger in 5e than in 4e"; "A party that unloads with their best powers simply breaks the system"; "It's quite likely that a semi-optimized party can vaporize boss monsters in a round or even less." His fix is on the monster's side: "design boss monsters with a different hit point scale, one that assumes the party is alpha striking".
   - *The best turn comes first.* [checked, M_Natas, EN World, 2023] "the suspense curve is inverted... all the big cool stuff happens at the beginning, and if the fight drags on, it devolves into a slugfest... The dragon, for example, often gets killed by a normal melee attack". [checked, Tab Atkins-Bittner] "in wargames, action economy rules everything, and you want to minimize overkill, so it's almost always best to blow your strongest moves immediately". [checked, Perico, Square Fireballs, 2012] "Everybody starts off doing something truly awesome, then continues with more mundane movements, and ends up using their puniest attacks."
   - *Hoarding and crit-fishing.* [checked, EN World] "paladins just aren't bothered about their spells. They save them for boss fights"; the smite decided after the roll "creates a perversion of the gameplay loop where a player changes or waits to declare their action with a retcon after seeing the result"; "One never wastes a Smite."
   - *A fight that ends in a round.* [checked, Nine Sides] "A combat which lasts for one round or less often feels anticlimactic."
   - **What its defenders name** [checked]: the cost. "Yes, they can deal a lot of damage very quickly, but doing so leaves them pretty vulnerable later... it's their one schtick, offensively speaking" (Hawk Diesel). And "trading resources for the feeling of power and satisfaction is one of the fundamental processes that makes the game fun" (Nine Sides).
   - *For 2d12:* our normal fight is 3.5 rounds and an Elite has 130 Health. Ours can't be daily (Stage 0 has no Daily Pick), which removes the "length of the day" problem at once.
2. **A resource built in the fight and lost at its end removes hoarding, but it makes round 1 slow.** Draw Steel's Censor [read]: +2 wrath a turn, +1 when the judged enemy damages him, +1 when he damages it; all lost at the end of the encounter. The lead designer [checked, James Introcaso, 2025]: "every round you get some of your Heroic Resource, you can build it up and you can spend it all at once to do some big, cool things, or you can spend it a little bit at a time to boost your... regular stuff"; a reviewer's image: "like a limit break bar". **The cost** [checked, Aestus Guides, 2026]: on round 1 he has "+2 Wrath. Not enough to do anything powerful"; another class does its big thing "an entire round earlier"; and "doing the same action early in a fight is better than doing it later... this is a game where fights tend not to last that long". Objections to charge-up designs [checked, EN World]: it is only more cinematic "if the PCs then kill the enemy in their charged-up round and not two rounds after"; "Back loading PC damage will result in PC's taking more damage from monsters".
   - The 4e essay for late bursts [checked, Square Fireballs]: no big spend on round 1 (a check that is impossible on round 1 and automatic on round 4), no two encounter powers in a row, one daily a fight; "It's much more exciting... to end with a bang, rather than a whimper." Its author admits the price: "we are reducing character effectiveness... taking toys away".
   - *For 2d12:* **Escalation is this clock already** (1 to 6, +1 a round, +1 once for a Critical, +1 once for the Paladin's hook, Momentum from earlier fights), built in the open, never spent. A burst gated at Escalation 3 or 4 arrives in round 3 or 4 of a first fight (of 3.5 rounds: often the last one) and in round 1 or 2 with Momentum. **The research is split on whether that is good:** it is the drama the essays ask for, and it is the Censor's slow round 1. A burst with no gate that **grows** with Escalation gives the choice back to the player (now, small; later, big).
3. **One pool or several.** Draw Steel [read] pays the 1-wrath protections and the 9-wrath strike from one pool, so every round asks "protect now or save for the blow". The 5e paladin keeps a pool per mode (a healing pool, slots, an always-on aura) [mine; no poster was found saying so]. Nimble [read; the change checked] avoids a pool: one charge, loaded by being attacked, never stacking; its designer moved there from stacking dice.
   - *For 2d12:* we already have a shared pool (4 AP and 1 Reaction AP) and a clock (Escalation). **The cheapest "use all they have" in our rules is AP:** a burst that takes the whole turn (3 or 4 AP), or one that also spends the Reaction AP, is literally everything he has, and it leaves him exposed (no Reaction this round, no unused-AP Guard) with no new rule. A single charge (Nimble's shape) is the next cheapest. A decision for Lucas.
4. **How big.** The one designer's table found [checked, Rob Heinsoo, Pelgrane, 2017; 13th Age's damage targets]: at-will 10, once a battle 15, daily 20 at level 1, and the same **1 : 1.5 : 2** at every level. Several targets: two targets 80% each, three 70%, four 60%. Measured from other rules [mine]: the Censor's top strike is about 2.5 times his free strike's dice; the Pathfinder 2e Swashbuckler's finisher bonus about 3.5 times his normal bonus. Mearls [checked]: the gap between daily and at-will, not the average, is what kills bosses. How games cap stacking [read]: at most 3 surges on a Draw Steel damage roll; 13th Age's smite once a battle; Lancer's Overcharge costs more each time; 5e 2024's smite once a turn.
   - *For 2d12:* a Weapon Attack is 25 points. The PC budget prices an Encounter Action at **double** its AP: the top of Heinsoo's range (his per-battle attack is 1.5 times, his daily 2 times) and under the Censor's 2.5. Today's Holy Wrath 2 is 50 points, the same as Smite 2: **the Paladin has no nova at all today.** A real one sits between 1.5 and 2.5 times its AP's worth of Weapon Attacks; for a 2 AP Action, 75 to 125 points, a sixth to a third of an Elite's Health on Tier 2.
5. **Finisher beats opener, without forbidding the opener.** [read] 13th Age's Way of Evil Bastards: a smite that drops a non-mook is not used up. Draw Steel's Censored: a non-boss brought to half by it drops to 0 [checked: "extremely high value and very evocative of what the Censor is supposed to be"]. [checked, EN World] an idea from the same thread: "abilities that only come online when players are below 50% health".
   - *For 2d12:* Bloodied and Strained are our hooks, and every creature knows them. Chapter 4's tip says an enemy is Bloodied for about a round before it goes Down, so "when it becomes" is the usable trigger, or "against a Bloodied or Strained target" on the burst itself. A refund on a kill is one sentence.
6. **Making the big hit land** [read]: decide after the hit (5e: never wasted, but crit-fishing); **half on a miss** (13th Age's smite; Pathfinder 2e's Confident Finisher); the resource is kept on a miss (4e's Reliable); see the roll, then choose the attack (13th Age's flexible attacks); accuracy as the setup's payoff (13th Age's +4 feat; the 4e Avenger's two rolls); **no miss at all** (Draw Steel's three tiers). [checked, EN World] 13th Age's own tension: a daily is spent before the roll, so "you can use your best attack on turn 1, but probably want to wait until the Escalation Die gets up".
   - Draw Steel's tiers measured [mine]: on damage, **tier 1 is about half of tier 3 and tier 2 about 70%** (5 / 8 / 11; 10 / 14 / 20). A 9-wrath strike at tier 1 still beats a free strike at tier 3. The gamble sits in the riders, which are gated. Why [checked, a reviewer]: "missing as a player in D&D feels bad. Getting missed by a monster in D&D feels awesome"; early drafts let a triggered action negate damage fully, the final game only reduces it: "the intent is for every turn to move things forward". No complaint that a tier 1 "still feels like a miss" was found.
   - *For 2d12:* we have the three Tiers, and our cards already put damage on Tier 1 of the big Actions. The rule to take: **the burst's Tier 1 is about half its Tier 3, and its rider is Tier 2 or better.** Favored (2 Boons from Field, Pressure or Support, or the card) is our "accuracy as the payoff". And note Draw Steel's other choice: protection reduces a hit, it never erases it (Shield of Faith today erases a Standard's).
7. **The cost that suits a front-liner is "exposed", not "empty".** [mine, from rules read] Empty (5e's slots): the later turns are dull [checked: "you just say 'I attack for middling damage' the rest of the session"]. Exposed (Lancer's Heat, the Draw Steel Talent's strain, the Magus's recharge action): for a round the front line is thinner and the party covers its own tank. The word's own origin, as one poster tells it [checked]: the Human Torch's nova blast, after which "his flames faltered for a while".
   - *For 2d12:* see 3: a burst that eats the Reaction AP or the whole turn is "exposed" for free.
8. **The off-turns build the burst, and smiting must not be the only right choice.** [read] The Censor's normal turn is the mark and its upkeep [checked: "lock a target down, force them to attack you, and get your Wrath back... That's how this class plays"]. [checked, Jeremy Crawford, 2024, on why the smite changed] "the Paladin was too powerful, and that excess of power was largely because of how open-ended the use of Divine Smite was"; "we wanted Paladins to feel like they actually had a range of cool options and not be in the position of the right choice is always just to Divine Smite."
   - *For 2d12:* "isn't a consistent damage dealer" then means the Paladin's Round Hallmark is not the burst. Smite at 1 / 2 / 3 AP every round is a consistent damage dealer's card.
9. **"Before seeing it coming": in every game read, the build is visible to the table.** [mine] A pool, a die on the table, Heat. The surprise is the enemy's, in the fiction. No source supports a hidden or random trigger as good play.
   - *For 2d12:* read the brief's line as tempo: the enemy has spent its turn ignoring or hurting the Paladin's ally, and the answer comes at once and all in one Action.
