# Nova and burst: building it, capping it, making the big hit land

## 2026-11-14: for the class redesign (MASTER-PLAN item 5), before any Paladin card

**The question (Lucas):** nova and burst classes. "When they use all they have against an enemy, they can explode before seeing it coming", for a class that "isn't a consistent damage dealer".

**How far to trust this file.** [read] = rules text read in full from GitHub copies (Draw Steel, 13th Age, Pathfinder 2e, Lancer, 4e, the 5e 2024 SRD). [lead] = an opinion or a designer's line seen only as a search summary of a page nobody opened. [memory] = from memory, unverified. [mine] = Claude's reading or arithmetic. The full report: `raw/2026-11-14-F-nova.md`. 13th Age's escalation die: `escalation-and-guard.md`.

### Takeaways

1. **What feels bad about a nova** [lead]:
   - *One character ends the climax while the table watches.* Mike Mearls (2025, as reported): "a semi-optimized party can vaporize boss monsters in a round or even less"; 5e assumed about 20 rounds of combat a day and was "off by a factor of 5".
   - *The best turn comes first:* "the suspense curve is inverted"; fights open with the big moves and "devolve into a slugfest". The optimizer's logic: with no reason to wait, blow the strongest move at once.
   - *Hoarding:* "your only real decision is deciding whether this encounter is worth burning a daily power on"; paladins who stop casting to save slots for a crit.
   - *The big hit misses* (takeaway 6).
   - **What defenders of the nova name** [lead]: choosing the moment, and the swing (ending a threat, turning a losing fight). Nobody in the excerpts defends it as a routine opener.
   - *For 2d12:* our normal fight is 3.5 rounds and an Elite has 130 Health. A burst is a problem in proportion to its size over the target's Health; ours can't be daily (Stage 0 has no Daily Pick), which removes the "length of the day" problem at once.
2. **A resource built in the fight and lost at its end removes hoarding and moves the burst to the middle of the fight.** Draw Steel's Censor [read]: +2 wrath a turn, +1 when the judged enemy damages him, +1 when he damages it; "You lose any remaining wrath at the end of the encounter". By arithmetic [mine], with no Victories his 5-wrath ability comes on turn 2 and a 9-wrath one on turn 3; later fights of the day start higher. Others [read]: Lancer's Heat, the Pathfinder 2e Swashbuckler's panache, 4e's action point every second encounter.
   - *For 2d12:* **Escalation is this clock already** (1 to 6, +1 a round, +1 once for a Critical, +1 once for the Paladin's hook, Momentum from earlier fights), built in the open, never spent. What we lack is a burst that reads it before Escalation 6 and the Apex (level 4). A burst gated at Escalation 3 or 4 arrives in round 3 or 4 of a first fight and round 1 or 2 with Momentum: Draw Steel's curve, with no new counter.
3. **One pool or separate pools: the research disagrees.** Draw Steel [read] pays the 1-wrath protections and the 9-wrath strike from one pool, so every round asks "protect now or save for the blow", and "use all they have" is literal. The 5e paladin [lead] is praised for separate pools per mode (a healing pool, slots, an always-on aura), so no mode starves another. Nimble [read] avoids a pool altogether: one charge, loaded by being attacked, never stacking ("if you have no Judgment Dice"); a search summary says its designer moved there from a stacking pool.
   - *For 2d12:* we already have a shared pool (4 AP and 1 Reaction AP) and a clock (Escalation). A third counted number on the Paladin's sheet is a real cost at the table. **The cheapest "use all they have" in our rules is AP:** a burst that takes the whole turn (3 or 4 AP), or one that also spends the Reaction AP, is literally everything he has, and it leaves him exposed (no Reaction this round, no unused-AP Guard) with no new rule. A single charge (Nimble's shape) is the next cheapest. A decision for Lucas.
4. **Capped novas that players accept are about 2 to 3.5 times a normal hit, once a fight or once a build-up.** [mine, measured from rules read] The Censor's top strike about 2.5 times his free strike's dice; the Swashbuckler's finisher bonus about 3.5 times his normal bonus; 13th Age's smite roughly doubles one attack at level 1. The one that was nerfed (5e 2014: several smites in a turn, on a crit) could pass 4 to 6 times a turn [memory]. No source states a rule of thumb. How games cap stacking [read]: Draw Steel allows at most 3 surges on a damage roll; 13th Age's smite is once a battle; Lancer's Overcharge costs more each time; 5e 2024 made the smite once a turn.
   - *For 2d12:* a Weapon Attack is 25 points (11 damage on Tier 2 with a Greatsword). The PC budget prices an Encounter Action at double its AP (2 AP: 100 points, about 44 damage on Tier 2), which adds about 30 points to every turn of a normal fight. Today's Holy Wrath 2 is 50 points: the same as Smite 2. **The Paladin has no nova at all today**; a real one at the budget's price is about 4 Weapon Attacks' worth in one Action, inside the accepted band for a 2 AP Action and a fifth to a third of an Elite's Health.
5. **Finisher beats opener, without forbidding the opener.** [read] 13th Age's Way of Evil Bastards: a smite that drops a non-mook is not used up. Draw Steel's Censored: a non-boss brought to half by it drops to 0. The Fury gains resource the first time he is winded. [lead] A warning on comeback triggers: if a hurt state pays too well, players stay hurt.
   - *For 2d12:* Bloodied and Strained are our hooks, and every creature knows them. Chapter 4's tip says an enemy is Bloodied for about a round before it goes Down, so "when it becomes" is the usable trigger, or "against a Bloodied or Strained target" on the burst itself. A refund on a kill is one sentence.
6. **Making the big hit land** [read]: decide after the hit (5e: never wasted, but crit-fishing); **half on a miss** (13th Age's smite; Pathfinder 2e's Confident Finisher); the resource is kept on a miss (4e's Reliable); see the roll, then choose the attack (13th Age's flexible attacks); accuracy as the setup's payoff (13th Age's +4 feat; the 4e Avenger's two rolls); **no miss at all** (Draw Steel's three tiers).
   - Draw Steel's tiers measured [mine]: on damage, **tier 1 is about half of tier 3 and tier 2 about 70%** (5 / 8 / 11; 10 / 14 / 20). A 9-wrath strike at tier 1 still beats a free strike at tier 3. The gamble sits in the riders, which are gated, and the costlier the ability, the flatter its tiers.
   - *For 2d12:* we have the three Tiers, and our cards already put damage on Tier 1 of the big Actions (Smite 1: 1[W] on Tier 1; Holy Wrath: 1[W] + 1d8). The rule to take: **the burst's Tier 1 is about half its Tier 3, and its rider is Tier 2 or better.** Favored (2 Boons from Field, Pressure or Support, or the card) is our "accuracy as the payoff".
7. **The cost that suits a front-liner is "exposed", not "empty".** [mine, from rules read] Empty (5e's slots, Gloomhaven's burned cards): the later turns are dull. Exposed (Lancer's Heat, the Draw Steel Talent's strain, the Magus's recharge action): for a round the front line is thinner and the party covers its own tank.
   - *For 2d12:* see 3: a burst that eats the Reaction AP or the whole turn is "exposed" for free.
8. **The off-turns build the burst** [read]: the Censor's normal turn is the mark and its upkeep, and the mark is the generator; the Swashbuckler's setup action is itself useful. [lead] Jeremy Crawford's stated reason for the 2024 smite: the paladin was too often in a position where the right choice was simply to smite; they wanted "a range of cool options".
   - *For 2d12:* "isn't a consistent damage dealer" then means the Paladin's Round Hallmark is not the burst. Smite at 1 / 2 / 3 AP every round is a consistent damage dealer's card.
9. **"Before seeing it coming": in every game read, the build is visible to the table.** [mine] A pool, a die on the table, Heat. The surprise is the enemy's, in the fiction. No source supports a hidden or random trigger as good play.
   - *For 2d12:* read the brief's line as tempo: the enemy has spent its turn ignoring or hurting the Paladin's ally, and the answer comes at once and all in one Action.
