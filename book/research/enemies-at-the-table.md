# Enemies at the table: what players and GMs like and dislike

**Date:** 2026-11-25. **Asked by Lucas,** opening the Bestiary's rebuild: "Search how in ttrpgs people like about enemies, minions, enemies above and below level."

**What was read, and how far to trust it.** One independent researcher, one pass; the full report (about 7,200 words, every poster named and linked) is `raw/2026-11-25-enemies-what-tables-like.md`. **This file is Claude's summary of it.** Opinions come mostly from EN World threads (2008 to 2026), The Angry GM, Sly Flourish, Critical Hits, the Alexandrian and a few reviews: a forum's regulars, not a survey. One real count exists (an EN World poll on 4e minions, 2009). Marks: [checked] an opinion on a page opened, [read] rules text, [lead] a search summary, [memory]. **Reddit, RPG.net and Giant in the Playground were not read; Matt Colville's videos were not opened.** Where a claim is one voice, it says so.

**Opinions about other games, never 2d12's rules. Nothing here is decided.**

---

## 1. Enemies in general

- **Liked: an enemy that does something of its own that the players must answer.** A signature ability; a behaviour that follows from the block, so players can learn it (Keith Ammann); a threat beyond damage that changes where players stand (Sims's gas-spore minions: "players learned to keep their distance"); **a visible change during the fight** (The Angry GM: transformations "break up the fight and force the players to change up their tactics", and he prefers "a small number of very obvious changes"). Recurs in four or more sources [checked].
- **Disliked: an enemy that only absorbs hit points, takes a player's turn away, or cancels an ability with no cost and no warning.** One researcher's reading: every liked feature creates a decision for the players and every disliked one removes a decision; a stun on a PC and a free negation on a boss are the same complaint from two sides.
- **A fight that is decided and goes on** (4e's "grind": the PCs know they will win and it takes five more rounds) is the most repeated complaint about a whole system's monsters [checked].
- **GMs want a block that runs from one glance.** Complexity is counted for the encounter, not the monster: about 12 abilities across 5 types read as heavy (Justin Alexander), "about three stat blocks" a fight (Noah Grand); nobody gave a number of abilities that is too many for one enemy [checked, two voices].

## 2. Minions

- **The idea is loved more than any rule for it.** Praise recurs for four things: the heroes feel strong, area attacks matter, the GM tracks nothing, turns are fast (six or more voices) [checked].
- **The complaints pull in opposite directions:** "too fake, too free" and "my damage was wasted". The 2009 poll on 4e's minion [checked]:

| Complaint | Share |
|---|---:|
| Automatic damage kills a minion | 37.1% |
| A high-level monster with 1 hit point | 27.0% |
| Too little risk for the reward | 25.2% |
| A normal monster that becomes a minion later | 21.4% |
| "A missed attack never damages a minion" | 10.1% |

- **The top complaint is damage that needs no roll** (auras, zones, automatic hits): "Minions are supposed to be easy kills, but not free kills" (doctorhook). A miss dealing nothing bothered few.
- **Wasted damage** (a big hit on 1 hit point) is the reason given for the newer variants; **dying before they act** and **too little damage for their price** recur too.
- **The house rule tables converged on:** a weak hit bloodies, a strong hit or a critical kills, a second hit always kills. Its stated good: a low roll no longer kills and the striker's big hit is still rewarded. Its stated cost: a marker a minion, and low spread damage (a controller's) kills less. One GM tried a bloodied marker and dropped it because "players just like wiping out those minions" (one voice) [checked].
- **Overflow is praised where position is loose** (13th Age: "there are never four mooks each clinging to 1 HP") **and bounded or refused where it is exact:** The Angry GM refuses it on a grid; Draw Steel limits it to the nearest minions and never through an area. **No opened source complained about the absence of overflow as such.**
- **Fodder that most hits don't hurt stops being fodder:** a Savage Worlds fight dragged because the Extras' Toughness was 9 ("Mooks should go down fast") [checked, one thread].
- **Should players know?** Opinion leans to yes, by description; Draw Steel and 13th Age 2e print it.
- **How many?** 4e's four to a standard is called too few by several posters ("8 at least"; one halves their XP to double their number); Draw Steel has eight to a hero. Sims: minions fill the gap in a budget, arrive in waves, and are never the main challenge. GMs run them in groups of about four on one turn, with one roll a group [checked].
- **Draw Steel's squads:** turns are "lightning fast"; the complaint found is moving 20 to 30 tokens, not tracking the pool; a fan guide: "Prefer Horde Enemies over Minions" [checked].

## 3. Solo bosses

- **The diagnosis is nearly unanimous** [checked, many voices]: one creature against a party loses on turns ("The action economy just worked against the monsters"), is shut down by one condition, and then dies in a round ("goes down like a chump against the combined novas") or grinds, standing still under a dogpile.
- **The fixes, by how they are received:**

| Fix | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **More turns** (4e's later solos, Nimble's turn after each hero, Draw Steel's two turns, ICON) | Approved: "what we really need is not more protection, but more turns" (James Gasik); a lost turn is then a fraction of a round |
| **Paying to shake a condition** (Draw Steel's End Effect; Shea's "status shakers": swap one penalty for another, never simply remove) | Approved |
| **Control that works fully only under a hit point threshold** (13th Age; "I wish more spells worked more like Sleep") | Approved |
| **A scripted arc** (villain actions: one extra action a round in a set order) | Approved; Shea adds "Keep your hand on the HP dial" |
| **A boss plus others** | The commonest advice (six or more voices): "Bosses don't fight alone" |
| **Pathfinder 2e's Incapacitation** | "for the most part, is a good rule", and resented in effect (such spells "often" useless); one poster prefers a version tied to Bloodied |
| **Saves at the start of the turn** | Disputed: it "totally nerfs" the controller |
| **Legendary resistance** (a free negation, a few times) | **The most disliked rule in the whole sample:** "an UNFUN, band-aid mechanic" (Nimble's author); "wasting abilities, especially big abilities, feels bad" |

- **How long is a boss fight?** Shea: many expect about three rounds for a fight; a boss in phases "might run six or nine"; "a battle should end precisely when it means to" [checked].
- **The accepted anti-shutdown rules share three properties** [the researcher's inference]: the players' ability still does something, the boss visibly pays, and the players know before they commit.

## 4. Enemies above and below the party's level

- **Where numbers scale, a higher-level enemy is felt first as the PCs' abilities stopping working,** not as the enemy being strong: "you toss origami figures to papercut it to death" (payn, on Pathfinder 2e); "They're harder mostly because the player characters miss more often, and missing isn't fun" (on 4e). The defence exists: "Chipping down a terribly powerful foe is pretty much what defines a boss monster" [checked].
- **The preferred repair is the bosses' one:** a boss one or two levels up with others, not one creature three or four up.
- **A lower-level enemy is either trivial or the pay-off of growth,** depending on whether it still does anything: Pathfinder 2e "automookifies" them; "the wave of mooks crashes against the wall of my players with little to no effect", which one GM's players enjoyed [checked].
- **Bounded accuracy (5e) kept its promise for hordes and broke solos** [checked].
- **In flat games, what reads as "stronger"?** Thin evidence. "consider the ease of dispatch on a successful hit": high AC with low hit points was easy, lower AC with more hit points the real challenge (Ovinomancer). **More hit points alone is called boring;** more damage, more turns and abilities that must be answered are what posters ask for [checked, few voices].
- **Missing:** both camps agree a wasted turn hurts in proportion to the wait for the next one; being missed by a monster "feels awesome" (Dan Sz) [checked].

## 5. Elites, and the rank under a Standard

- **Little is said about elites, and that is the finding:** when noticed, it is for being longer, not different. The praised ones have a job in the group (commander, the boss's second, the wrecker) [checked, few voices].
- **Tables asked for a rank between the minion and the standard before games printed one,** and built it as a two-hit monster or a lower-level standard ("troopers", "adjuncts"); the printed versions (Draw Steel's horde, 13th Age's weakling) draw almost no comment. Where minions already take a variable number of hits, tables may not tell the two apart by durability alone: no source addresses it.
