## 1. The frame: a spell that finds things out is not the enemy of a mystery

- The Alexandrian [checked]: "the actual action of a mystery is not the withholding of knowledge but rather the discovery of knowledge." A divination "should be no more alarming than an FBI team taking fingerprints". He rejects blocking: "Nothing is more frustrating to a player than having their smart choices blocked".
- A spell breaks a mystery only if "(a) it trivializes all other methods of acquiring information and/or (b) short circuits a specific investigation" [checked].
- Paizo's own rules text names the stance [read, *Ultimate Intrigue*]: apply "the principle of 'yes, but. . .' rather than simply saying 'yes' or 'no'"; a divination should "provide clues more than answers".
- **The dissent matters for 2d12** [checked, a commenter]: leaving it to the scenario "makes designing mysteries harder than they already are" for a GM. **The burden falls on the GM unless the spell's own text carries the limit.**
- The line two writers draw [checked]: **repeatable and certain ends a mystery; once and partial gives a lead.**

## 2. The scene-breaking families, and the fixes on record

| Family | The play it endangers | Fixes on record |
|---|---|---|
| **Talking to the dead, beasts, plants** | Murder mysteries | A count of questions; the witness knows only what it knew and thinks its own way; a wait before asking again [read] |
| **Truth zones, mind reading** | Intrigue, interrogation | The target knows it is affected and can stay silent or evade; surface thoughts only; minutes; law and manners against casting on someone [read, checked] |
| **Locating, scrying, asking a power** | Hunts, hidden lairs | You must know or have seen the thing; range; lead blocks it; the power asked is not all-knowing; a scry can be noticed and answered [read] |
| **Charm, suggestion, domination** | Social play | A friend, not a puppet; "when the spell ends, the creature knows it was charmed by you"; a reasonable request only; bystanders can notice [read] |
| **Flight, teleport** | Terrain, travel, the dungeon as a route | Slow or vertical only (Levitation with rope and helpers is the praised form); you must know the place; guarded addresses and tokens [read, checked] |
| **Shelters** | Rest as a risk | A real cost: the complaint about 5e's Tiny Hut is that it is free [checked] |
| **Food, water, light** | Scarcity | None found in rules text; most tables drop scarcity [lead] |
| **Invisibility, disguise** | Infiltration | Sight only (sound, scent, tracks remain); appearance only (voice, gait and knowledge are the player's problem) [checked] |
| **Opening** | The lockpicker | It works, and it is loud: 5e's Knock is "audible from as far away as 300 feet" [read] |
| **Any of them** | The GM's preparation | A rarity tag: the GM hands the spell out, as a reward (Pathfinder 2e) [checked] |

## 3. What makes utility magic fun

The one essay found that states principles is Against the Wicked City [checked], from five years of weekly play. Its observation: players "regularly forget which magical weapons their PCs are carrying. What they never, ever forget is exactly who is wearing the Ring of Invisibility to Undead." Its test: "abilities that facilitate creative and intelligent play" against "abilities that short-circuit play without significant limitations".

1. **A component of a plan, never the plan.**
2. **It needs set-up or a helper.**
3. **It has failure points the players must guard** (an illusion nobody must touch).
4. **It makes a new situation, not a result** (weather, fog, heat).
5. **It opens routes** (breathing water connects places in new ways).
6. **It opens conversations.** "Let them talk to monsters. To animals. To rocks, plants, corpses, water, air... As long as whatever they're talking to is not guaranteed to be useful or co-operative."
7. **Narrow and absolute beats broad and partial** ("complete immunity to fire").
8. **The small version of a scene-breaker is good** (surface thoughts and emotions).

**A sorting the researcher draws from the sources** (not a quoted rule): **a solution spell names the obstacle it removes** (Knock, Goodberry, Tiny Hut, Zone of Truth); **a toolbox spell names a thing it does and leaves the obstacle to the player** (an image, a hand, a servant, a voice for a tree). The sources praise the second and tame the first with noise, time, knowledge or a count.

Also on record:
- **A small count of questions makes the table talk.** One group debated three questions "for the better part of half an hour" [checked].
- **No limit, no scene:** "a druid used speak with animals and a few pointed questions to uncover the adventure's surprise villain within the first ten minutes" [checked].
- **A menu of small effects invites rulings at its edges** (players argued from the fiction of each line): that is the fun and the ambiguity together [checked].
- **A cost from the pool that matters** gives ordinary encounters weight; the free version is where the complaints are [checked].

## 4. How magic marks its user (for decision 106)

| Kind | Game | The mark | How it was received |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cosmetic** | Ars Magica | A sigil on every spell | Liked; free [read; reception memory] |
| **Cosmetic** | Mage: the Awakening | A long-term "nimbus": small coincidences around the mage, by Path, with no roll | Not found [fan transcription] |
| **Cosmetic, growing** | Warhammer Fantasy | Marks by the wind of magic a wizard uses | Fondly remembered [memory] |
| **Others react** | Ars Magica | The Gift: people and animals distrust a mage (a penalty in every social scene) | Defining; it is why non-mages do the talking; often bought off [read; lead] |
| **Others react** | Symbaroum | A visible shadow, stigmas, witch-hunters | The game's central tension [checked; lead] |
| **A taboo** | Unknown Armies | Power is charged by obsession and emptied by breaking a taboo | Its best-loved idea [memory] |
| **An obligation** | D&D's warlock | A patron who asks things | Fun if the GM plays it, ignored if not [memory] |
| **Transformation** | Dungeon Crawl Classics, Shadow of the Demon Lord, Ars Magica's Warping | Corruption tables; a lifelong clock | Loved where it is the game; a tax elsewhere [read; memory] |
| **Temperament** | RuneQuest | One rating is both the magic and the personality; the player may act against it | Core to the game; "a little fluffy" [read; lead] |
| **Standing and law** | Ars Magica, Warhammer, Symbaroum | Orders, licences, hunters | Mostly setting [memory] |

**Across them** [the researcher's reading]: **marks that are free, visible and chosen are kept; marks that need a second person to remember are dropped; penalties are bought off when the game sells an exit.**

## 5. Magic and the rest of the party

- **A helper who can only make it worse is not invited.** Pathfinder 2e's ritual helpers each roll, and a failure costs more than a success gives; players there call them "a punitive requirement" [checked]. 13th Age's helpers only help.
- **A speaker for the mage:** Ars Magica's Gift gives every non-mage a job in town.
- **The corpse, the tree, the summoned thing are people** the whole table questions.
- **Familiars:** fun when voiced as a character; resented when the GM uses one to foil its player [lead].

## 6. Raw themes for each Path (other games' images; material only)

- **Fire:** light and warmth as hospitality and safety; the forge; a temper that shows.
- **Air:** weather read and nudged; voices carried and sounds thrown; the scout's high view.
- **Water:** routes opened (breathing water; rivers as doors); cleaning, cooling, preserving.
- **Earth:** shaping ground, slow tunnelling; talking to stone; lead and metal against magic.
- **Astral:** omens and fate; asking a power that is not all-knowing; knowing you are watched; true names.
- **Death:** the dead as witnesses with a few questions and a damaged voice; decay and rust; the unease of animals and strangers.
- **Nature:** beasts and plants as witnesses who think their own way; the land's general sense that something is wrong; food and care.
- **Glamour:** images guarded against a touch; a face that is only a face; an emotion scaled up, not a will replaced; dreams.

## 7. How it applies to 2d12 (observations)

- **"The limit is printed in the spell" is 2d12's least-ambiguity rule seen from outside.** The research's fixes are all things a card can say: a count, a wait, "the target knows", "you must have seen it", loud, slow.
- **Several fixes are already 2d12's decisions.** A casting is always noticed unless [Subtle] (97): the charm that is known, the truth zone the target is aware of. A hostile Action on an aware creature rolls Initiative (`items-economy.md` Part 2, item 1). Loud magic has a price ready: the site's Alert (`book/ideas/expedition-loop.md`). Magic never takes a whole turn and charm is "can't target it" (39).
- **Toolbox against solution is a test a card can be held to:** does its text name the obstacle it removes, or a thing it does?
- **"No certainty" is already the project's proposal for every out-of-combat ability** (`roleplay-skills.md`): the research's "yes, but" and "clues more than answers" are the same stance.
- **Decision 106 has printed relatives:** the chosen, free, visible mark (a sigil; the nimbus) and the rating that is also a temperament (RuneQuest). The warning: a mark that depends on the GM remembering it dies, and a penalty invites buying it off.
- **Helpers:** if a rite ever takes the party, they should only be able to help.
