# NPCs and their items: numbers that don't come from gear (2026-11-26)

**The question (Lucas):** "see if we already did a research on monsters not using items; I know strictly from a balance pov is best, but the game feel is what worries me, plus the suspension of disbelief."

**What 2d12 says today (Chapter 14):** "An NPC's items are flavor and loot: its Health, Defenses and damage never come from its weapons or armor." Losing its weapon is open (`book/bestiary-design.md`).

**Had we researched it?** No. `monster-building.md` and `enemies-at-the-table.md` say nothing on gear; `weapon-improvements.md` and `armor-and-shields.md` are the PC side.

**How good the sources are:** one official text (Pathfinder 2e's creature-building rules, partly through a forum quote); the rest is forum debate (EN World, Paizo) and a trope page. No designer statement was found for D&D 4e, Draw Steel, 13th Age or Lancer. Opinions, not facts.

## Takeaways

1. **Numbers from the monster, not the gear, is the common modern answer.** Pathfinder 2e builds a creature from "benchmark final numbers rather than combining each individual modifier". It says creatures have less treasure than PCs, so one that relies on a made weapon gets its damage raised to compensate (so it doesn't "shower the party with expensive gear"). D&D 4e posters describe the same: a monster's damage comes from its level and role.
2. **The feel problem is real and it is always the same three moments:**
   - **The swap:** "a hobgoblin that picks up a dagger should not hit as hard as with a greatsword." The answer given by the other side: then it is a different stat block.
   - **The loot:** if a monster's weapon visibly hits hard, players "would absolutely want to loot" it, and are let down when it is an ordinary sword in their hands. The trope has a name, Unusable Enemy Equipment.
   - **The disarm:** in 4e "monsters damage is based upon level and role etc. Not the items that they carry", so nobody could say what a disarmed monster does. 4e's one printed disarm (a named NPC's power) gives the Weakened condition and takes nothing away. Posters also feared a disarm that guts a solo or an elite.
3. **Pathfinder 2e keeps both:** the stat block lists the creature's items, its Strike names the weapon, PCs can Disarm it and loot it, but the numbers were set first and the gear is described to fit. Its NPC book has advice for an NPC that loses a crucial item (not read; only its existence was found).
4. **It is partly taste.** One poster: wanting everything in a stat block explained is "how we got 3e's monsters built like PCs problem"; another: shared rules "add to verisimilitude". Neither side convinces the other.
5. **A third tradition:** damage tied to the user, not the weapon (any class can use any weapon; the die is the wielder's). It removes the swap and the loot problems at once, for everyone.

## How it applies to 2d12

- The balance rule can stay (numbers from the rank) while the fiction is kept honest. What breaks disbelief is not the math, it is the three moments above having no answer at the table.
- **The loot:** what the party picks up is the item as Chapter 15 writes it (the orc's axe is an axe). The orc's own numbers were the orc's. One sentence in the book, for the GM to say out loud.
- **The swap and the disarm** are the open "losing its weapon" question: Pathfinder's way (the card names the weapon; without it that card is off) and 4e's way (a disarm is a condition on the creature, nothing leaves its hand) are the two models found. Claude's earlier proposal is the first.
- **A cheap aid to disbelief:** a stat block names what the creature carries, and its Actions are written around those things (2d12 already does: "A short blade in its hand", Shiv). Asymmetric numbers bother people less when the fiction on the card matches what is on the table.

## What players say (the feel)

- **Against a weapon that is only a name:** "If a hobgoblin can pick up a dagger and do the exact same damage as with a longsword or greatsword, something is wrong." And the trap on both sides: "Magical weapon tied to the monster? No good. Mundane weapon scaled to appropriate damage per CR? No good."
- **For it:** "How did the monster do X damage is not a question that any dm should have to answer." "The fact that a gribbly does 6d6 damage with a longsword does not mean that if a PC picks up the sword they will do 6d6." Monte Cook, a designer of 3e, on its symmetric NPCs: "I believe that NPCs should never work just like PCs ... those NPCs are going to last 10 minutes at the table." Jonathan Tweet: "both PCs and monsters are hand-crafted to fit their distinct roles at the table."
- **The loudest complaint found is gear you can see and take but not use:** BG3's Steel Watcher weapons ("Larian you Cowards").
- **The best-liked moments are real removals:** BG3 players disarming a god's avatar ("Aw dang it I dropped my thingy"); Final Fantasy Tactics players had a remaster patched so a boss's gear could be stolen again.
- **Symmetric games are loved for it and pay for it:** Mythras' special effects "transform combat from a meat-grinder into a play for advantage", and "the action economy can make solo monsters laughably weak."

## The patterns, and where 2d12 stands

1. **The weapon is a name, the numbers are the creature's** (5e, 4e, 13th Age, Daggerheart). **2d12 is here today.** Cheap and balanced; leaves the swap and the disarm with no answer.
2. **A reversion sentence for loot** (D&D 2024; Dark Souls does the same by its numbers). 2d12 doesn't say it.
3. **A written loss rule** (Pathfinder 2e: the closest attack at a penalty with the new weapon's dice, or a fist; a fallback attack guaranteed). 2d12 has a draft of this under an open box (Fists with its Weapon Modifier; "the GM adjusts").
4. **Disarm as a short condition, nothing leaves the hand** (Gloomhaven, DOS2, Owlcat, Battle Brothers, 4e). 2d12: not written.
5. **Disarm as a real removal with recovery built in** (Forbidden Lands: it lands within arm's reach, a quick action to take it back; BG3). 2d12: not written; Claude's proposal of this morning is this one.
6. **Immunity read off the creature** ("must be wielding a weapon"; a beast's natural weapon can't be disarmed; a boss lists its immunities). 2d12's proposal has the first half.
7. **Two layers: people use gear, beasts use their own attacks** (Forbidden Lands, Mythras, DCC, Fabula Ultima's Use Equipment trait). 2d12's four creatures already split this way in fiction (the orc's axe, the wisp's touch) but no rule says it.
8. **Loot held down by rule** (flagged drops, worn or shoddy gear, counted against treasure). 2d12: items and economy are not written yet (`book/items-economy.md`).
9. **Damage belongs to the wielder** (Dungeon World, The Black Hack). Not 2d12: a PC's damage is its weapon's die.
10. **Full symmetry** (3.5, Mythras). Lucas ruled it out for balance.

**What makes 2d12 different from most of these:** a PC's damage is built from the weapon ([W], [D], its properties), so players are taught that a weapon is its numbers. That is exactly the expectation that makes pattern 1 feel wrong at the loot and swap moments; games where it bothers people least either never gave weapons numbers (pattern 9) or say the reversion out loud (pattern 2). And 2d12 fights are short (over in round 2 or 3): a disarm that costs a whole enemy turn is worth far more here than in a six-round game.

## Ways forward (Claude's suggestions; nothing decided)

- **A. Say the loot sentence now (pattern 2).** "What a creature carries is the item Chapter 15 writes; in another's hands it uses that entry, never the creature's numbers." One sentence, no cost, and it answers the loudest complaint (visible, taken, disappointing) before it happens. Works with every option below.
- **B. The card names the weapon; without it that card is off (patterns 3, 5, 6).** The morning's proposal: a Requirement line on the Actions that use the weapon, Fists as the fallback, 1 AP to take it back when it is within Reach, a natural weapon can't be lost. Most believable; the price is one more line on some cards and a strong disarm in a two-round fight (it needs a cost or a rank limit when disarm is written).
- **C. Disarm is a condition, nothing leaves the hand (pattern 4).** "Disarmed (EoT): it can't use Actions that need its weapon." One marker, no item on the map, no loot exploit, the same on every rank; a boss can list it among its immunities. Least believable of the three at the table ("it is still holding the axe"), and the choice most video games made when they had to pick.
- **D. B for people, C for the big ones (patterns 6 and 8's rank gate).** A Minion, Grunt or Standard really drops it; an Elite or a Boss is only Disarmed (EoT) (it keeps its grip). The fiction holds where it is cheap and the fight holds where it matters.
- **E. Make the weapon's die show on the card (pattern 3's other half).** A creature's weapon attack is written from the weapon's own die plus what the creature adds, so the orc's axe and a PC's axe visibly share a die. The most believable and the most work: every weapon-using card is tied to Chapter 15, whose weapons are mostly placeholders.

**Claude's pick:** A now; B's card format for the four creatures; D's rank gate when disarm itself is written; E not before the weapon line is redone.
