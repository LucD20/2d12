# Moving diagonally between two creatures

**The question (Lucas, 2026-11-25, core rework batch 4, D3):** with enemies' squares blocking movement, should slipping diagonally between two
enemies that touch only at a corner be blocked too? "Most players assume it is like this, but no game actually runs like this I think." And should
Tumble allow it (you don't technically go through a creature)?

## Takeaways
1. **No major d20-family game blocks it by rule.** Pathfinder 1e says it outright: "You can't move diagonally past a corner (even by taking a 5-foot
   step). You can move diagonally past a creature, even an opponent" (the same in Starfinder, with "hard corners": building, starship, doorframe;
   plants and pits don't count). D&D 4e restricts the diagonal only at terrain that fills its square; forum readers agree creatures don't (they "aren't
   actually a solid unit"). Pathfinder 2e has no corner rule at all. Draw Steel: an enemy's space is Difficult Terrain (not blocking), an ally's is
   free; its corner rule names only walls and objects that fill a square.
2. **Players expect it anyway.** Every forum thread found splits on it, and the house rule "two creatures corner to corner block the diagonal" is
   common ("fair and realistic"); one GM allows it only for a creature small enough to squeeze, with the squeezing penalty. A forum poster remembers
   3.5 having "opposed diagonal corners" (unconfirmed).
3. **2d12 already has its own middle ground:** Chapter 3 makes the diagonal between two unwilling creatures Difficult Terrain (as passing through
   their squares). With D3 making an enemy's square block, keeping that diagonal as Difficult Terrain would leave every diagonal pair of enemies a
   gap anyone walks through at double cost: a "wall" would need enemies side by side.

## How it applies to 2d12
- Blocking the diagonal between two enemies is unusual by the rulebooks but matches what players expect, and it is what makes D3's blocking hold
  for a diagonal line of enemies.
- If it blocks, Tumble is the way through, as for a space; the open part is the Check: the two creatures' DCs (as crossing two spaces), one
  creature's (the higher, no Bane for the second), or a set DC (Lucas: "11?").
