# Movement and positioning: what a step, a free move or an unsplit move is worth

## 2026-11-14: for the Card Workbench analyses (Lay On Hands' granted Evade, Hallowed Stance's "Setup: Advance 3", the Advance rule change)

**The question:** how tactical games value movement that isn't the normal move (a safe step, a move bundled into an attack, movement given to an ally), and what changes when movement can't be split around an Action (Lucas's decision of 2026-11-14: an Advance is one piece; Interact can be used during it).

**Why it matters:** the SIM measured Evade on a melee PC at 0 because its policy never steps a melee PC anywhere useful. These notes are what the analyses use instead.

### Takeaways

1. **A safe step matters only where moving is dangerous.** In D&D 4e, leaving any threatened square provoked an Opportunity Attack, so the 1-square shift was the sanctioned way to reposition; a character with no legal square to shift into was "pinned". Posters credit this with making tactical movement a bigger part of 4e than 5e, where moving around an enemy is free.
   - *For 2d12:* there is no basic Opportunity Attack any more (Stage 0), so an Evade isn't about escaping one; it is about the **Reactions that answer movement** (Overwatch, Beacon's Step, an Elite's) and about **Engaged** (a [Ranged] Action while Engaged is Doomed). An Evade's worth goes up with every enemy Reaction that fires on movement.
2. **Movement given to an ally is a "quarterback" tool.** 4e's Warlord was designed so allies shift into flanking without spending their own movement (the designers' quarterback image; White Raven Onslaught). Its powers grant "an ally shifts 1 as a free action" as a rider on an attack. Forum posters rate free ally movement below a granted attack: a light rider unless it sets something up.
   - *For 2d12:* a granted Evade is worth most on the ally who needs it: the Archer stepping out of Engaged before its shot (the SIM: about 30 a turn), a melee ally stepping into Flanking (up to 2 Field Boons on its next attacks), anyone stepping out of a Zone or a hazard. It costs the ally nothing and fires no Reaction.
3. **Movement bundled into an Action is strong because it saves the move's action.** Pathfinder 2e's Sudden Charge (2 actions: Stride twice, then Strike) is one of the most valued martial feats: closing the distance normally costs the action you wanted for the attack. It can't be split (no Interact in the middle). 4e's Nimble Strike gave a shift before or after an attack.
   - *For 2d12:* "Setup: Advance 3" on an Action is that bundle: worth about what an Advance's AP is worth on the turns you had to move anyway, and nothing on the turns you didn't. With an Advance that can't be split, a second, separate movement is the only way to move, act and move again, so a bundled move gains value.
4. **Splitting movement is a design choice with known trade-offs.** 5e allows move-attack-move by default and frames it as an improvement (a ranged character can step out of cover, shoot and step back); older editions and Pathfinder 1 needed a feat for it (Spring Attack, Shot on the Run). Posters say not splitting "forces commitment" and may speed combat; it cuts mobile characters' options. Lancer lets movement be split around actions, but each piece counts as starting a movement for Overwatch.
   - *For 2d12:* after the rule change, "Evade, then attack" Actions (Rolling Shot, Double Shot 3), granted Evades and Setup moves are the only move-act-move left: they all gain value. Watch the Archer: its kiting depended on splitting.
5. **Movement that ignores Reactions is the cleaner one to price.** Draw Steel splits "move" (provokes) from "shift" (doesn't, can't enter difficult or damaging terrain); a rule that lets you shift can always be used as a move instead.
   - *For 2d12:* our Evade is that shift (no Reactions, no Difficult Terrain); an Advance granted by a card fires Reactions as usual. Price an Evade a little above the same squares of Advance when the enemies have movement Reactions, the same otherwise.
