# Helping another character's roll: the shapes, the reflex, and what stops it

**Date:** 2026-11-19. **Asked by Lucas,** of the help Basic Action he plans (Assist or Help, name open): "When you help someone they gain support 1 boon. I planned to make tiered, like 1 bane / 1 boon / 2 boons but I think rolling to help is disruptive. At the same time, this means most rolls are simply going to hear 'I am helping, so add 1 boon', which, again, falls into the lameness."

**What was read, and how far to trust it.** One independent researcher; the full report (about 4,000 words, every source and link) is `raw/2026-11-19-helping.md`. **This file is Claude's summary of it.** Marks: [read] rules text on a page opened, [checked] an opinion on a page opened, [lead] a search summary, [memory] no page opened. About a third of the games are [memory] or [lead] (Burning Wheel, Mouse Guard, Worlds Without Number, Genesys, Dragonbane, Warhammer, The One Ring, Pathfinder's Follow the Expert, 4e, 13th Age). Not opened: Archives of Nethys, d20srd.org, Reddit, RPG.net. The D&D 2024 glossary and the Blades teamwork page came through a summarizing reader: recheck their quotes.

**Other games' rules and opinions, never 2d12's. Nothing here is decided.**

## 1. Both of Lucas's instincts are on record

- **The free bonus becomes a reflex.** DM David [checked]: "The GM tells a player to make a check, and then everyone at the table, and the pizza guy at the door, all start rolling to assist." The Alexandrian [checked] gives the cause: an option so good it is always taken has "broken the process of choosing"; a bonus that fits every check "must be cast for every single ability check", and becomes "an endless, mindless drone which is both a tax you are obligated to pay and an action which is not only devoid of narrative worth". Because it stands for nothing in the world, "there's no true satisfaction".
- **Rolling to help has a poor record everywhere it was read.** Pathfinder 2e's Aid was a trap at its first DC and is automatic at the lowered one [checked]. **Draw Steel's assist is the 1 bane / 1 edge / 2 edges shape Lucas first planned,** and a review names "helping makes it worse" as a disappointment [checked]. Any chance of harm teaches some players not to help at all. No help roll read was praised for the roll itself.
- The 5e complaints are about the free version outside a fight; the Help that costs your action in a fight is called fair [checked].

## 2. The shapes of helping

| Shape | Games | A second roll? | Known weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A free bonus** | 5e's working together; Year Zero games (+1 each, three at most) | No | The reflex |
| **A bonus for a price** | Blades in the Dark (1 stress), Daggerheart (1 Hope); the action in a fight | No | Too cheap and it is automatic again; in a fight a whole turn for a small bonus is rarely chosen |
| **A bonus by a roll** | Pathfinder, Draw Steel, Savage Worlds, Dungeon World | Yes | Doubles the rolls; a trap or automatic depending on the DC |
| **Shared risk** | Fate, the Burning Wheel family, Blades | No | Needs a consequence that believably reaches the helper |
| **Substitution** | Follow the Expert, Genesys | No | Only matters when the two characters differ |
| **Taking the consequence** | Blades' Protect | No | It is a second act, not an "I help" |
| **Help as your own action,** whose benefit is passed on | Starforged, Fate's Create an Advantage, Blades' Set up | Yes (the helper's own) | Slower; accepted because the roll is the helper's turn |
| **A group check** | 5e, Draw Steel, Blades | Everyone, once | Skewed odds; leaders prefer to roll alone |

**Not found as a general rule in any game read:** help that makes a task possible at all, and help that softens the failure.

## 3. What stops "I help" from being automatic

| Device | Its cost |
|---|---|
| **Say how,** and it must plausibly help | A judgment each time, unless the rule says what counts |
| **Declared before the roll, during the scene** | None: it only removes the afterthought |
| **One helper, or a cap** | The others have nothing to add |
| **The helper must be able** (a skill, or "could attempt it alone") | Needs written skills |
| **A price** in a resource or the turn | Must be priced: cheap is automatic, dear is unused |
| **The helper shares the failure** | The failure must reach the helper |
| **Show the help in the outcome** (the Alexandrian) | A habit, not a rule |

**Satisfying, by the sources:** a described act that shows in the outcome; a leader who cares how the others do; two players combining turns. **Hollow:** a bonus announced after the roll is called; one that fits every roll; failing your own attempt to help; the helper absent from the narration.

## 4. How it applies to 2d12 (observations)

- **2d12 already has a cap with no new rule:** named Boons count twice at most, and Lucas names the help's Boon **Support**. A third helper adds nothing.
- **In a fight, the price exists:** an Action costs AP. The reflex is an out-of-combat problem.
- **Routine gives help a job that isn't "+1 to the roll"** (magic record, decision 115): if Boons count toward 11 + your modifier, a friend's help can turn a task that needed a roll into one that doesn't.
- **The stance of magic** (decision 114) already contains a kind of help: the caster makes something attemptable and someone else makes the Check.
- **Shared risk needs no roll and no new resource,** and decision 116's guide for a Tier 1 ("no, or yes at a real cost") is where the helper's share would land.
