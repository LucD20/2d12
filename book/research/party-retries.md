# When one character fails, may another try? The party retry

**Date:** 2026-11-19. **Asked by Lucas,** on the retry rule: "This requires extensive search to prevent the 'I attempt this, I failed' another player 'I will try as well'. With roleplay, it is easy to solve, with stuff like locked doors and such, it is hard: First, failed attempts need to mean something or prevent something small, a failed attempt can never stop story from moving [...] But a locked chest for loot usually is just extra, so a failed attempt means nothing meaning someone else can just try it again. [...] The easiest but a bit boring way is to just make everyone roll for it if Tier 1 just means failure. If tier 1 means failure plus can't be attempted again by anyone, it is also decent."

**What was read, and how far to trust it.** One independent researcher; the full report is `raw/2026-11-19-party-retries.md`. **This file is Claude's summary of it.** Read in rules text: Draw Steel, Old-School Essentials (a mirror; the official site errored), Year Zero, Cypher, Pathfinder 2e, Fate, Blades, Daggerheart. **Not verified:** the 2024 DMG's own text (forum quotes only); Mouse Guard's and Torchbearer's books; any words of Luke Crane; Savage Worlds, Ironsworn, Genesys, Mothership, 13th Age's core. Several quotes came through a reader that returns short fragments: the fragments are exact.

**Other games' rules, never 2d12's. Nothing here is decided.** Companion: `retries.md` (one character retrying).

---

## 1. What is printed

- **No game read says "a failed physical attempt is the party's, and nobody may repeat it."**
- **The closest:** Draw Steel's negotiation [read]: "If the heroes try to use the same argument [...] twice, the test automatically obtains a tier 1 outcome", and the NPC's patience is one budget the whole party spends. And Sly Flourish's advice [checked]: only the first attempt could work.
- **Per-character limits are the common printed form, and the others stay free to try:** Old-School Essentials (one try per character to listen or search; a thief gets one try per lock until he gains a level) [read]; Year Zero ("Or let another player character try") [read]; Cypher (a retry costs Effort) [read].
- **Pathfinder 2e allows the dogpile and raises the DC to pay for it** ("Group Attempts") [read].

## 2. The party-level answers found

| Shape | Games | Its cost |
|---|---|---|
| **One roll by the best-suited character; the others help or add nothing** | 5e, Fate, Daggerheart | The others watch |
| **Everyone rolls once; the result is read from all of them** (group check) | 5e, Draw Steel, Blades (stress for each failure) | A roll for each character; skewed odds |
| **One die result shared by everyone** | Bind | Unusual |
| **Every failure changes the situation,** so no retry is the same attempt | Fate ("Do not do this" to a failure that changes nothing), Daggerheart, Torchbearer's twist | The GM must find a change each time |
| **Time and exposure are the price of each attempt** | Old-School Essentials' turns and wandering monster checks; Torchbearer's grind; 5e 2024 ("just tell the player how long the task takes") | Needs a clock that bites |

## 3. Why the dogpile matters, by the commentators

- **The Alexandrian** [checked]: five tries turn a 70% chance into 96%.
- **Alphastream** [checked]: "Five people can't crowd around the lockpick."
- **DM David** [checked]: "the price of failure is often noise."
- **Burning Wheel's Let It Ride** binds one character; whether it binds the group is disputed on its own forum.

## 4. How it applies to 2d12 (observations)

- **Decision 117 already says who rolls** (the best suited), but not whether someone else may roll after a failure. That is the gap.
- **Routine covers the no-stakes chest only when the best character's number already meets the DC.**
- **Tier 1 "no, or yes at a real cost"** (116) is Fate's and Daggerheart's "every failure changes the situation", if the GM uses the "real cost" side.
- **Alert** (`book/ideas/expedition-loop.md`) has no step that time alone raises, so time spent retrying costs nothing in a site today.
- **Lucas's "Tier 1 means failure and nobody can try again"** has its nearest printed relative in Draw Steel's negotiation (the same argument twice is a Tier 1) and Sly Flourish.
