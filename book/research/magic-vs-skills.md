# Magic against skills: why casters outshine everyone outside a fight, and what games did

**Date:** 2026-11-19. **Asked by Lucas,** opening the design of magic outside a fight: "This is a huge can of worms, with the biggest problem being solving everything with magic, or martials feeling lame etc."

**What was read, and how far to trust it.** One independent researcher; the full report (about 4,350 words, every source and link) is `raw/2026-11-19-magic-vs-skills.md`. **This file is Claude's summary of it; Claude did not reopen its pages.** Marks: [read] rules text on a page opened, [checked] an opinion on a page opened, [lead] a search summary, [memory] no page opened. **Not verified:** Giant in the Playground, Reddit and archive.org would not open, so the 3.5 tier list is quoted from two copies and Kevin Crawford's forum comment is a lead; no D&D or Pathfinder designer was found stating the intent behind Knock in their own words; thirteen games are memory only (Shadowrun, Earthdawn, Exalted, The One Ring, Mythras and others). ## 1. The diagnosis

- **The gap is breadth outside the fight, not damage.** D&D 3.5's tier list ranks classes by how many kinds of problem they can answer: its top tier is "capable of doing absolutely everything, often better than classes that specialize in that thing", its fourth "capable of doing one thing quite well, but often useless when encounters require other areas of expertise" [checked, from copies].
- **"Quadratic" means new verbs, not bigger numbers.** Martials "mostly get more of the same but bigger"; casters "open up wildly new areas of power" [checked].
- **A niche protects an outcome, not a process.** Alexander Macris, designer of ACKS [checked]: "You have a chance to pick a lock? I just use knock. You have a chance to hide in shadows? I just cast invisibility." If one character alone can climb and everyone else can fly, the climber's niche is gone. He calls it "the bugbear of five generations of game designers".
- **Substitutability** [checked, Sable]: a caster can stand in for a martial across the usual challenges, and the reverse is not true. Seven areas are named: combat, social, logistics and survival, information, security, puzzles, the unique. Magic reaches all seven.
- **What it does to a player** [checked]: a choice between "stay true to my concept but mostly be a spectator" and dropping the concept. "Useless for an entire session, that feels really bad."

## 2. Why it happens: nine mechanisms

1. **The spell is certain where the skill rolls** (5e's Knock opens with no check; the lockpick can fail) [read, checked].
2. **One need a day.** "In a typical adventuring day you probably only encounter one need for stealth, or one need to climb": a once-a-day spell covers it and the at-will skill never gets its turn [checked].
3. **No opportunity cost.** The resource comes back before it is missed [checked].
4. **A toolkit changed daily.** Michael Sayre of Paizo [checked]: "if a character has the potential to do anything and a goal of your game is balance, it must be assumed that the character will do all those things."
5. **Magic is general by default.** "If the designers do not carefully think about what their magic can do, it will be capable of anything whatsoever" [checked]. Nobody has an intuition for what sorcery can't do, so its limits must be written [lead, Crawford].
6. **Magic is exclusive.** The non-magical can't sense it, analyze it or answer it, so a magical problem has one kind of solver [checked].
7. **Skills were written timidly, spells boldly** [checked].
8. **Casters have the skills as well** [checked].
9. **It drifts back.** Each new book adds spells, so a fixed game slides toward the gap again [checked].

## 3. What games did, and how it went

| Game | The fix | How it went |
|---|---|---|
| **D&D 4e** | Utility powers for all; non-combat magic moved into rituals anyone could learn; skill challenges | Credited with solving it [checked], at a price: rituals went unused, and "Martial Practices" (rituals for martials) read as permission slips |
| **Pathfinder 2e** | **Magic helps the skill:** Knock "grants a +4 status bonus" to the check and someone still rolls; scene-skipping spells tagged uncommon or rare; skills go superhuman at the top (hide with no cover at all) | Reported as dull; many threads say casters feel weak [checked, lead]; skill feats read as permission |
| **Worlds Without Number** | **Magic never does what a skill does:** "Spells should not substitute for other classes." And it rejects Pathfinder's fix: "Spells should not offer simple numeric bonuses... Magic is meant to create impossible effects and alter the ground rules of a situation. If it simply amplifies the existing abilities of the group, it changes nothing in actual play" [read] | Left to each GM to judge spell by spell |
| **Draw Steel** | Certainty for the non-magical too: perks "allow a hero to attempt a specific task without a test"; in a montage, magic is one contribution to a shared count | Lucas already finds its perks over the top (`roleplay-skills.md`) |
| **13th Age, Daggerheart** | Broad, player-written backgrounds for every class | Praised; hard to balance; the caster has them too |
| **Blades in the Dark** | Magic pays in the currencies everyone uses (stress, downtime), is learned as a long project, and stirs trouble | Praised; slow |
| **Fabula Ultima** | Rituals may never replicate "the mechanical benefits of an existing spell or Skill" | Players forget rituals |
| **Ars Magica** | Everyone plays a wizard sometimes | A different kind of campaign |

**Two of these contradict each other, and a designer has to pick:** Pathfinder 2e makes magic a bonus to the skill; Worlds Without Number forbids both the replacement and the bonus, and wants magic to change what is possible.

## 4. The opposite failures

- **Magic hedged until nobody uses it** (4e's rituals; Pathfinder 2e's casters).
- **Martials turned into casters** ("anime fighters"; fixes that solve it "by erasing martial classes") [checked].
- **Limits that punish** (the stolen spellbook, the lynch mob): they "directly target player fun" [checked].
- **Leaving it to the GM's spotlight:** most GMs "have neither the experience, nor the time, nor the inclination" [checked].

## 5. What feels good for the non-magical

From forum posts and rules text, not a survey:
- **Being good without spending a resource** [checked].
- **No roll for your own trade** (Worlds Without Number's rule; 5e's Reliable Talent) [read, memory].
- **Things only they do**: "qualitative differences are the most important and valuable weapon" for a niche [checked].
- **Strength that is theirs, not their gear's** [checked].
- **Simple and dependable reads as strong** [checked, Sayre].
- **"Equal impact, not equal power":** the aim is "to have all characters equally able to impact the course of fictional events" [checked].

## 6. A checklist to hold any out-of-combat magic rule against

1. Does it reach **an outcome a skill also reaches** (past the door, unseen, up the wall, the truth out of him)?
2. Is it **certain** where the skill rolls?
3. Is its bonus so large that the roll is decided?
4. Does it cover **the one need of the day**?
5. Is anything given up to cast it, or does the resource come back before it is missed?
6. Can the caster **change the toolkit** between problems?
7. Does it **remove the scene**, or change how the scene is played?
8. Can it fail, and does failing cost what a failed skill costs?
9. Can a non-caster perceive it, answer it, or help with it?
10. Does the caster have the same skills as well?
11. Does growth add **a new verb** to the caster and only a number to everyone else?
12. Will every later spell be checked against this list?

## 7. How it applies to 2d12 (observations)

**What 2d12 has already closed, by decisions taken for other reasons:**

| Mechanism | What closes it | Where |
|---|---|---|
| A bonus that decides the roll (3) | A rank is never a number on a roll or a DC | magic record, 18 |
| The long buff carried everywhere (Pass without Trace, Fly) | A spell with a duration is combat only | 87 |
| A lasting effect that leaks into fights | It changes a fight's circumstances, never its numbers: Crawford's sentence nearly word for word | `items-economy.md` Part 2, item 14 |
| No opportunity cost (5) | The Surge held outside a fight is small (0 to 4), doesn't return with a night's sleep, and is earned by any encounter overcome, skills included | 71, 105 |
| Exclusivity (6), in part | A casting is always noticed; a Magic Talent opens magic to any class | 97, 27 |
| A wide toolkit (4), in part | Few spells known | 19, 27 |

**What is still open:**
- **Certainty (2).** The project's proposal is "no certainty" for every out-of-combat ability. Whether magic outside a fight rolls is not decided.
- **The daily toolkit (6).** "Spells can be swapped on a Rest" is a small version of it.
- **The Limits of Magic** are a never-list, the tool Fabula Ultima and Crawford use; none of the three is about skills yet.
- **The non-magical side:** Skills aren't written. Routine (a Tier 2 with no roll when trained and unpressed) is the proposal nearest to "no roll for your own trade".
- **Drift (9):** only a written test that every later spell passes holds the line.
