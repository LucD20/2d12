# Magic outside a fight: rituals and free-form magic in other games

**Date:** 2026-11-19. **Asked by Lucas** (magic record, decision 87), after he made spells with a duration combat only: "Should we make these combat only and the 'out of combat' stuff we create per path or school?" The working idea: the Path says what the magic is made of, the School what it does, so the pair tells a player what they can attempt; higher levels open wider uses; the Surge held outside a fight pays for it.

**What was read, and how far to trust it.** One independent researcher, about 80 searches and page loads; the full report (about 5,900 words, with verbatim tables and every link) is `raw/2026-11-19-out-of-combat-magic.md`. **This file is Claude's summary of it; Claude did not reopen its pages.** Read directly from files: 13th Age's SRD, Knave, and Fabula Ultima's ritual rules (from the open Foundry system's transcription, not the book). Everything else came through a reader that returns extracts: **recheck a quote on its page before printing it.** [memory] only: D&D 4e's and 5e's ritual rules, Shadow of the Demon Lord, Savage Worlds, Genesys, Mage: the Ascension, GURPS Ritual Path Magic's mechanics, Draw Steel's projects. **Not found on any page:** the "one player dominates" complaint about Mage, an essay on "why didn't the wizard just", or a designer's word on why 4e's rituals went unused.

**Already in the project:** `ars-magica.md` (spontaneous magic, the five verbs with hard edges, the Limits), `dominions-magic.md` and `dominions-rules.md` (rituals), `crucible.md` (one written entry a substance).

**Other games' rules and opinions, never 2d12's. Nothing here is decided.**

## 1. Eight shapes, each with its known weakness

| # | The shape | Games | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A closed list of rituals**, each a written card | D&D 4e, Pathfinder 2e | Unambiguous, and under-used when the price competes with gear or the time idles the table |
| 2 | **A themed menu of small effects** | 5e's Prestidigitation, Druidcraft, Thaumaturgy; Crucible's rotes | The list reads as the limit |
| 3 | **A combat spell spent for a related effect** | 13th Age rituals | All GM in its plain form; its own designers later added a ten-line price list |
| 4 | **A domain sentence plus a price table** | **Fabula Ultima**; Blades in the Dark; Whitehack | The GM still judges the size; players forget it exists; level changes nothing |
| 5 | **Costs only, anything possible** | Dungeon World | Scope is "anything", and the conditions are fiat |
| 6 | **Verb + noun ranks** | Mage, Ars Magica, GURPS Ritual Path Magic | Negotiation and slow play; every such game ends up shipping a book of worked examples |
| 7 | **Negotiated once, then written down** | Blades in the Dark | Slow to acquire; needs downtime |
| 8 | **The combat ability, rationed outside** | Draw Steel; 5e's ritual tag | The prebuff road: the one decision 87 closed |

## 2. Fabula Ultima: the closest match to "a pair plus a pool" [read]

- **A discipline is a one-line domain:** Elementalism "Walk on water, shape rock, snuff out fires, cause a rainstorm or summon powerful cyclones"; Spiritism "Sense the presence and feelings of creatures, put someone to sleep or embolden their heart".
- **A never-list written first.** Ritual magic "should never be able to": cause direct damage; inflict or remove status effects; make characters lose or gain HP, MP or other points; replicate "the mechanical benefits of an existing spell or Skill (however, narrative effects such as teleportation or flight are okay)"; generate creatures or equipment or grant permanent abilities.
- **Two tables set the price.** Potency in four named steps with examples (Minor: "create a flash of light, block a passage, shatter a glass"; Medium: "create an illusion, treat an illness, locate someone or something"; Major: "sense thoughts, influence emotions, dispel a curse, temporarily alter the weather"; Extreme: "weaken a divine entity, prevent a catastrophe"), times an area in four steps. The cost is their product, from the same pool as combat magic.
- **The GM "has final say on whether a given effect can be accomplished, as well as which Ritual discipline it falls within"**; a check; on a failure the effect is "twisted in a catastrophic way".
- **Its known problem:** players forget rituals exist. The community's fix was a list of pre-written examples.

## 3. What the free-form games all end up adding

1. **A never-list before the always-list** (Fabula Ultima's five points; 13th Age: "none of these uses should be combat relevant or deal damage"; Ars Magica's Limits).
2. **Scope ladders of named things, not measures:** a room, a house, a village; a moment, minutes, an hour, a day, a week. Permanence is always a special step or forbidden.
3. **Fixed prices a player can compute before asking:** a product of two tables, a penalty a step, a DC change a dial.
4. **Priced worked examples.** Both the 4e and the Fabula Ultima communities asked for more than the books gave.
5. **Who rules on the borderline case, written down:** the GM (Fabula Ultima), the other players as a jury (13th Age's Expanded Rituals), settled once and recorded (Blades).
6. **Fit moves the price, not the permission:** a spell that fits the request well makes it cheaper (13th Age's "resonance": -5, 0, +5).
7. **A wall between the two modes.** One writer's advice [checked, Periapt Games]: a precise list for the fight and a priced table outside it, never mixed on one effect. Negotiated effects are "Mother May I"; a hybrid is the worst of both.

## 4. Two failures, not one

- **Abuse:** the caster solves every problem. The answers in rules text are never-lists and "once until the next win" (Draw Steel).
- **Disuse,** as common: 4e's rituals lost to gold cost and casting time; Fabula Ultima's get forgotten. A separate free-form subsystem is invisible to players used to lists.

## 5. What a two-word pair has been made to do elsewhere

- **Gate what may be attempted** (Mage, Ars Magica): the noun is the subject, the verb the operation.
- **Carry a one-line domain** (Fabula Ultima). With a pair, the sentence can be composed from one line a row and one a column, as Ars Magica defines its five verbs, each with a hard edge.
- **Unlock verbs by rank** (Mage: the Awakening's Practices): a higher level adds new kinds of doing, not a bigger number, and spare rank gives free steps on the scope ladders. **This is horizontal growth in print.**
- **Set the roll and the helpers.**
- **What no game found does: use the pair alone as the limit.** Wherever verb + noun is the gate, a separate ladder, price or never-list carries how big, how long and how many.

## 6. How it applies to 2d12 (observations)

- **Decision 87's idea is shape 6 gated by shape 4's supports.** The Path and School pair can say *what* may be attempted; by every game read, it can't also say *how much*. That needs its own ladder and price.
- **2d12 already has the pieces the others bolt on:** a never-list (the Limits of Magic, decisions 93 and 94; and decision 87 itself removes anything with a combat duration); a pool with a price (the Surge held outside a fight, equal to Momentum, back on a normal or harder win: Draw Steel's "once per win" in a finer grain); six verbs sorted by a fixed order (65); levels that could unlock wider uses (18: a level is an option).
- **The least-ambiguity test points at written examples.** Crucible and 5e write a few fixed uses for each substance; Fabula Ultima writes a domain sentence and priced examples. The games that left it to "the GM decides" are the ones blamed for negotiation.
- **Disuse is a real risk for a second subsystem.** What the communities reached for: a short list of ready-made uses on the sheet.
- **The Surge pool is small** (0 to 4 outside a fight), so prices would be 1 to 3, as in the fight.
- **Permanence** ("creating a bridge", Lucas's example when he dropped limit 6) is the step every game treats as special.
