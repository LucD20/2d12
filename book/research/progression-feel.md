# Does a nearly flat Health hurt the feeling of progression?

**Date:** 2026-11-18. **Asked by Lucas:** "do you think limiting HP so much is hurting the system's progression and 'feels good'? I don't want hp bloat but I am afraid of feeling too 'samey' and lame. Honest opinion here. And if it's a middle ground, feel free to say and suggest changes."

**What was read.** Thin: this is mostly opinion, and little of it is about tabletop games with a deliberately flat Health.
- **[checked, through a summarizing reader]** Scroll and Tome, "Vertical vs Horizontal Progression" (a tabletop designer's essay); Engadget, "MMO Mechanics: Comparing vertical and horizontal progression" (2014); Dunder & Dagar, "The hit points predicament" (a Dragonbane GM).
- **[lead]** (search summaries, pages not opened): Guild Wars 2's Ascended gear debate (PC Gamer; the official forum); a Forbidden Lands review (rpgbot) and EN World's Dragonbane and Forbidden Lands threads; D&D Beyond and The Alexandrian on bounded accuracy; 13th Age's second-edition change notes; MMORPG.com forum threads.
- **[memory]** Lancer's numbers (Grit) are from Claude's training, not a page opened today: two searches found nothing on how its leveling feels.
- **Already in the project:** rules-backlog.md, "[Q] HOW A PC'S DAMAGE GROWS" (2026-11-12): "numbers that only rise feel hollow, new abilities that change how you play are felt more"; 4e's 2[W] and 3[W] steps "remembered as sound scaling"; "bloat is Health growing faster than damage".

**Other people's opinions, never 2d12's rules. Nothing here is decided.**

---

## 1. Takeaways

1. **The two kinds of growth are a spectrum, and every game named mixes them.** Vertical: "characters grow stronger"; horizontal: they "become more capable rather than simply stronger" (Scroll and Tome) [checked]. Zelda adds hearts beside its tools; Guild Wars 2 kept levels.
2. **What vertical growth gives:** "clear, tangible goals", simple to grasp, easy to design for. **What it costs:** earlier content "becomes trivial or irrelevant"; the world shrinks (Scroll and Tome) [checked].
3. **What horizontal growth gives:** old content stays relevant, every enemy can still threaten, the player's skill counts more. **What it costs:** it is "harder to design for, since difficulty can't be raised simply by increasing damage"; rewards can "feel lackluster"; and "it's really hard to have horizontal progression and not constantly increase complexity" (a Guild Wars 2 forum poster) [checked for the essay; lead for the forum].
4. **The game that promised only horizontal growth added a little vertical.** Guild Wars 2's studio: "We do intend to focus on horizontal, but we will have vertical progression moving forward with [...] a very low power curve" [lead]. Players split: some felt betrayed, the game is still credited with having no gear treadmill.
5. **In tabletop games with a fixed Health, tables patch in a little growth.** Dragonbane: "If you have a 14 Constitution, you'll always have 14 hit points"; a suggested house rule: +1 HP or WP with each new heroic ability [lead]. Forbidden Lands: attributes never rise and are also the hit points, which a review calls a challenge [lead].
6. **Players of a grown-Health game argue the other way too.** Bounded accuracy in 5e is praised because "monster stat blocks stay relevant while characters still feel like they're improving relative to what they've fought before" [lead]: the feeling comes from **the comparison with an old enemy**, not from the size of the number.
7. **What makes Health matter is also how fast it comes back.** The Dragonbane GM misses the edition where "every Hit Point really counted": with fast healing, "PCs are almost immortal" until a big monster appears [checked]. A small pool that refills freely feels smaller than a small pool that doesn't.
8. **Even the best-known horizontal tabletop game moves a few numbers.** Lancer's license levels mostly unlock gear and talents, but every second level adds +1 to attack rolls, saves and hit points (Grit) [memory].
9. **13th Age goes the opposite way on purpose:** hit points and damage multiply across ten levels, and its second edition says the step from level 9 to 10 should feel as big as from 1 to 2 [lead].

## 2. 2d12's own numbers (for the comparison)

- **Health:** about 30 at level 1, 60 to 70 at level 10, at most ×2 over fifteen levels. It moves **only at levels 4, 8 and 12** (the Stages).
- **Class spread:** Paladin 20 + 10, Archer 16 + 8: with a Human's +5, **35 against 29**, a ratio of 1.2.
- **Modifiers:** +2 at level 1, +4 at most: without Doubles and explosions, a Tier 2 or better against DC 13 goes from about 69% to 81%, and a Tier 3 from about 19% to 31%.
- **Damage:** 1[W] + Modifier, with 2[W] at high Mastery; Empowered's die grows by Stage.
- **Fights:** tuned to about 3.5 rounds at every Stage; the Bestiary's tables are re-fitted by Stage.
- **Endurance:** Rally Points grow by 1 a Stage (Rally + 1 to Rally + 4), each worth a quarter of the pool.
- **A level gives:** a Talent every level; class cards at 1 to 3; the Apex at 4; Mastery by use.

## 3. How it applies (Claude's reading; the opinion Lucas asked for is in the message of 2026-11-18 and in rules-backlog.md)

- Takeaway 6 moves the question: **Health's size isn't what is felt; the change against a known enemy is.** With fights re-fitted to 3.5 rounds at every Stage, an on-level fight is the same length whatever the Health line is. What 2d12 lacks is a rule for what an outmatched enemy becomes.
- Takeaways 3 and 4 name 2d12's real risk: **all growth as new cards is growth in complexity.** A character with twelve Talents, a class and spells has more buttons every level. Some growth has to deepen what is already held (Mastery's steps, Overcast, ranks) or move a number.
- Takeaway 5 says a fully frozen stretch is what tables patch: 2d12's Health is frozen for three or four levels at a time.
- Takeaway 7 is on Lucas's side: his pool is small **and** slow to refill (Rally Points only on a Rest), which is why it "counts". It is also why Strain as the main cost of spells was rightly refused.
- The class spread (1.2) is narrow against the usual gap between a front-liner and a back-liner in other games, and is a second, separate source of "samey".
