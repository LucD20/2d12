# Weapons: a trick for each weapon, what "light" does, and short bow against long bow

## 2026-11-16: for the weapons pass (the Weapon Technique cards and their ladder of unlocks)

**The three questions.** (1) How do published games give each weapon, bows above all, its own trick, and what do players say works? (2) What does a "Light" property (or its equivalent) do in other games beyond "small"? (3) How are a short bow and a long bow told apart without one being strictly better?

**How far to trust this file.** One pass, by an agent (about 35 steps); not re-checked by a second. There is no raw report: everything found is here.
- [read] = rules text read in full: D&D 2024 (the SRD 5.2's Equipment chapter and Rules Glossary, in a markdown copy), D&D 2014's Light (Open5e's copy of the SRD), Pathfinder 2e (the remaster data: weapons, traits, critical specializations), Draw Steel (the Kits chapter and seven kits), Lancer (Skirmish, Barrage, the tags), 13th Age (the two-weapon rule, the ranger's weapon table).
- [quoted] = a rule seen as someone's quotation: D&D 4e's weapon properties (Wizards' 2008 excerpt, pasted on Paizo's forum); Baldur's Gate 3's weapon actions (a fan wiki; a video game).
- [checked] = an opinion read on its page (EN World, Paizo's forum, D&D Beyond's forum), with its date.
- [lead] = a search summary or memory only, to open before it is quoted to anyone: Tales of the Valiant's weapon options, Kingdom Death's weapon proficiency, the Rules Cyclopedia's Weapon Mastery, 4e's bow numbers, Pathfinder 2e's knife specialization, and the lines marked so below.
- Reddit, Giant in the Playground and RPG.net refuse readers and were left alone. `archer.md` (takeaways 5 and 8) and `gear-and-hands.md` already hold the Volley complaint in one line, reload, and the cost of drawing: not repeated here.

## Q1. A trick for each weapon

### The rules

**D&D 2024: one "mastery property" a weapon, unlocked by a class feature** [read]. "Each weapon has a mastery property, which is usable only by a character who has a feature, such as Weapon Mastery, that unlocks the property". The eight:

| Property | Text (shortened only where marked) | Roll? |
|---|---|---|
| Cleave | "If you hit a creature with a melee attack roll using this weapon, you can make a melee attack roll with the weapon against a second creature within 5 feet of the first that is also within your reach. On a hit, the second creature takes the weapon's damage, but don't add your ability modifier [...] only once per turn." | a second attack roll |
| Graze | "If your attack roll with this weapon misses a creature, you can deal damage to that creature equal to the ability modifier you used to make the attack roll." | none |
| Nick | "When you make the extra attack of the Light property, you can make it as part of the Attack action instead of as a Bonus Action. You can make this extra attack only once per turn." | none |
| Push | "If you hit a creature with this weapon, you can push the creature up to 10 feet straight away from yourself if it is Large or smaller." | none |
| Sap | "If you hit a creature with this weapon, that creature has Disadvantage on its next attack roll before the start of your next turn." | none |
| Slow | "If you hit a creature with this weapon and deal damage to it, you can reduce its Speed by 10 feet until the start of your next turn. If the creature is hit more than once by weapons that have this property, the Speed reduction doesn't exceed 10 feet." | none |
| Topple | "If you hit a creature with this weapon, you can force the creature to make a Constitution saving throw (DC 8 plus the ability modifier used to make the attack roll and your Proficiency Bonus). On a failed save, the creature has the Prone condition." | a save, each hit |
| Vex | "If you hit a creature with this weapon and deal damage to the creature, you have Advantage on your next attack roll against that creature before the end of your next turn." | none |

Who gets what [read, the weapon tables]: **Shortbow Vex; Longbow Slow; Light Crossbow Slow; Hand Crossbow Vex; Heavy Crossbow Push; Sling Slow; Dart Vex; Dagger Nick; Light Hammer Nick; Sickle Nick; Scimitar Nick; Shortsword Vex; Handaxe Vex; Rapier Vex; Club Slow.** No use limit: every hit, all day. The number of weapons a character has mastered is small (a search summary says two for rogues, rangers and paladins, three for a fighter [lead]).

**Pathfinder 2e: a trick on a critical hit, by weapon group, plus traits on the weapon** [read]. Critical specialization (unlocked by class): 
- Bow: "If the target of the critical hit is adjacent to a surface, it gets stuck to that surface by the missile. The target is Immobilized and must spend an Interact action to attempt a [DC 10 Athletics] check to pull the missile free".
- Sword: Off-Guard until the start of your next turn. Club: "knock the target up to 10 feet". Hammer and flail: Prone unless a save. Spear: Clumsy 1. Axe: damage to an adjacent second creature. Sling: Stunned 1 unless a save. Polearm: "moved 5 feet in a direction of your choice". Knife: persistent bleed [lead: from memory, its text was not in the file read].
- The bows' traits: Longbow 1d8, range 100 ft., "deadly d10, volley 30 ft."; Shortbow 1d6, range 60 ft., deadly d10; the composite versions add propulsive. **Volley:** "This ranged weapon is less effective at close distances. Your attacks against targets that are at a distance within the range listed take a -2 penalty." **Propulsive:** "You add half your Strength modifier (if positive) to damage rolls". **Deadly:** "On a critical hit, the weapon adds a weapon damage die of the listed size."
- Crossbows have no trait at all: Crossbow 1d8, 120 ft., reload 1; Heavy 1d10, reload 2; Hand 1d6, 60 ft., reload 1.

**Draw Steel: the trick is the kit's signature ability, not the weapon's** [read]. A kit names a category ("You wear no armor and wield a bow") and gives flat bonuses and one ability. The ranged ones:
- *Rapid-Fire* (light armor, a bow; Stamina +3 an echelon, Speed +1, Ranged Damage +2/+2/+2, Distance +7, Disengage +1): **Two Shot**, "Ranged 12, Two creatures or objects. ≤11: 4 damage / 12-16: 6 damage / 17+: 8 damage".
- *Sniper* (no armor, a bow; Speed +1, Ranged Damage +0/+0/+4, Distance +10, Disengage +1): **Patient Shot**, Ranged 15, 3 / 6 / 13 + M or A damage; "If you don't take a move action this turn, this strike deals extra damage equal to your Might or Agility score".
- *Cloak and Dagger* (light armor, "one or two light weapons"; Stamina +3, Speed +2, Melee and Ranged Damage +1/+1/+1, Distance +5, Disengage +1): **Fade**, "Melee 1 or ranged 10", 3 / 6 / 8 + M or A damage; "you can shift 1 square" / "up to 2 squares" / "up to 3 squares".
- *Ranger* (medium armor, a bow and a medium weapon): **Hamstring Shot**, Ranged 10, 3 / 5 / 7 + M or A; "A < WEAK, slowed (save ends)" and so on by tier.
- *Arcane Archer* (no armor, a bow; Ranged Damage +2/+2/+2, Distance +10): **Exploding Arrow**, Ranged 15, 5 / 7 / 10 fire; a second creature within 2 squares takes fire damage equal to the characteristic.
- "Bow" is one category: the Sniper's text says "a bow or crossbow". The game doesn't tell a short bow from a long one; the kit does (range +7 or +10, damage spread flat or all on tier 3).

**D&D 4e: properties on the weapon, and the trick comes from powers that name a weapon group** [quoted: Wizards' 2008 excerpt]. "It's easier if a designer can rely on a group keyword to say, 'This feat does X if you're wielding an axe,' or 'If you're wielding a light blade, this power also does X.'" Properties: **High Crit** ("maximum weapon damage and an extra 1[W] at 1st-10th levels"), **Load** ("load free [...] you draw and load ammunition as a free action, effectively part of the action used to attack"), **Off-Hand**, **Small**, **Versatile** (+1 damage in two hands). Bow-only powers are the ranger's (in `archer.md`).

**Lancer: tags on the weapon, no per-weapon trick** [read]. Accurate, Inaccurate, Arcing ("can be fired over obstacles [...] don't require line of sight"), Knockback X, Reliable X, Seeking, Loading, Ordnance ("can only be fired before the user moves [...] can't be used against targets in engagement"), Thrown X. A weapon is its size, its range and two or three tags.

**Per-weapon ladders and limited-use tricks found:**
- **Baldur's Gate 3's weapon actions** [quoted, bg3.wiki; a video game]: "Each weapon type has its own set of weapon actions [...] the wielder must be proficient in that weapon type [...] each weapon action can be used once per short rest. This limitation is character-specific, not weapon-specific." Bows: **Hamstring Shot** (shortbows and longbows; an Action; Hamstrung on a failed Constitution save, 2 turns) and **Brace** (longbows and heavy crossbows; costs 20 ft. of movement; "Reroll ranged damage rolls and take the higher value", 1 turn). Hand crossbows: **Mobile Shot** (a bonus-action attack after a Dash or Disengage). Crossbows: **Piercing Shot**. Light blades (scimitars, shortswords, rapiers): **Flourish**, a bonus action.
- **Tales of the Valiant's weapon options** [lead: memory and a search summary]: each weapon lists one or two options (Bash, Disarm, Hamstring, Pinning Shot, Pull, Ricochet Shot, Trip) used in place of an attack's damage. Not read; no reception found.
- **The Rules Cyclopedia's Weapon Mastery** (Basic, Skilled, Expert, Master, Grand Master, bought weapon by weapon, each rank changing damage, range and special effects) [lead]. **Kingdom Death: Monster's weapon proficiency** (a track for one weapon type a survivor: a specialization partway, a mastery at the end that the whole settlement then learns) [lead: memory; its wiki refused the reader]. **Pathfinder 2e's proficiency ranks** unlock the critical specialization by class level, not by use [read].

### Takeaways

1. **The liked part of 2024's masteries is that same-die weapons finally differ; the trick itself is small.** [checked, EN World, May 2023] "It at least distinguishes weapons a little and will help make different characters more unique. Before weapons were so boring other than the ridiculous rapier." [checked, Dec 2024] "it adds a bit of fun here and there, but I've never seen it bog anything down. Nor has it been a big revelation. It's fine."
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: a per-weapon card is judged first on whether two weapons of the same size feel different.
2. **What slows the table is the trick that asks for a second roll on every hit, not the trick itself.** [checked, EN World, Dec 2024] "only one that really slows down the game is topple as it requires extra rolls. rest are passive, once you learn them, they are just part of attack." "Topple might be a problem. Save on every hit slows things down." One poster's fix: "if you beat target AC by 5 or more, you knock the target prone. no extra rolls". Against: "I am not a huge fan. It makes combat longer on our table."
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: a rider read from the attack's own result (a Tier) is the "no extra rolls" shape these posters ask for.
3. **Automatic every-hit riders add things to remember, and beginners feel it.** [checked, EN World, Sep 2024, "Not loving weapon mastery with beginners"] "I like the idea of them, but in practice I find them to be stumbling blocks. They not only add decision-points and tactical analysis for the player, but some of them create an extra thing for the DM to remember or track." [checked, Dec 2024] "Downside is more complications for newer players."
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: who tracks the rider (the attacker's card or the target's sheet) is part of its cost.
4. **Several posters wanted a choice made on the attack instead of a rider fixed to the weapon.** [checked, EN World, Dec 2024] "Love the idea, hate the execution. mastery should have been 'battle master's maneuvers, light edition' [...] you can apply only one mastery per attack, you decide after attack is made." [checked] "I'd have rather had martial 'cantrips', some of which were restricted to weapon groups, damage types, and handedness".
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: this is the difference between a property printed on the weapon and a card the weapon gives.
5. **One trick a weapon plus a free draw on every attack produces the "golf bag".** The rule [read]: "You can either equip or unequip one weapon when you make an attack as part of this action." [checked, EN World, Sep 2024] "The whole juggling mechanic exists to support switching between weapon masteries." [checked, EN World, Feb 2026] "Nobody thinks back and gushes about the time they went through an entire golf bag of weapons [...] for the umpteenth time that session"; "the default rules don't present any difficulties carrying a golf bag of weapons & swapping between them has zero impact on action economy". A defender [checked, same thread]: "I can see the fictional fantasy of a hero smashing a foe with a big mace to knock them over, tossing the weapon aside and leaping atop the downed enemy to stab them with twin blades." A poster's fix [checked]: no mastery on an attack made with a weapon drawn as part of that attack, unless it is thrown. [lead, Reddit] the golf-bag fighter is "kind of the optimal way to play".
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: where swapping costs something, a per-weapon trick stays a choice of loadout; where it is free, it becomes a rotation.
6. **A trick that feeds the next attack is the one ranked highest and called too good; a trick that only slows is the one called useless.** [checked, EN World, May 2023, on the playtest] "Vex - this is too good IMO, since it creates a cascade, where each hit increases the chance of another hit." "Vex would be fine if it wasn't on the hand crossbow and rapier. Those are already the best". [checked, Dec 2024] "Only slow really sucks, I have not seen a usefull effect from this once"; another ranks Slow as "solid combat control to keep an enemy at range. Very good with a ranged weapon to shoot and retreat." Sap: "After about 3-4 levels the value of sap drops tremendously".
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: accuracy riders and control riders on two bows are not priced alike by players; Slow's worth depends on the map.
7. **A trick that only happens on a critical hit is liked when it lands and rarely planned around** (`versions-criticals.md`, takeaways 5 to 8, holds the reception). The bow's specialization also needs a wall: "adjacent to a surface".
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: a ladder step that lives on a rare result is a moment, not a tool.
8. **Limited uses are the other answer to "automatic on every hit".** Baldur's Gate 3 gives each weapon type two or three named attacks, each once a short rest for the character [quoted]. No tabletop reception was read for it or for Tales of the Valiant's version.
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: frequency is a second way to keep a weapon's trick from being a passive.
9. **Draw Steel puts the trick on the fighting style and leaves the weapon as description**: "One player using this kit could wear heavy layers of hide and wield a longspear, while another might [...] carry a halberd" [read]. A weapon outside the kit's categories is improvised.
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: the opposite pole from a card for each weapon; it removes the short bow against long bow question entirely.
10. **No per-weapon unlock ladder with read reception was found.** The three known (the Rules Cyclopedia, Kingdom Death, Pathfinder 2e's ranks) are leads or by level.

## Q2. What "Light" does

### The rules

- **D&D 2014** [read]: "A light weapon is small and easy to handle, making it ideal for use when fighting with two weapons." The property is only the key to the two-weapon rule. Light: club, dagger, handaxe, light hammer, sickle, scimitar, shortsword, hand crossbow. **Not light: rapier** (finesse only).
- **D&D 2024** [read]: "When you take the Attack action on your turn and attack with a Light weapon, you can make one extra attack as a Bonus Action later on the same turn. That extra attack must be made with a different Light weapon, and you don't add your ability modifier to the extra attack's damage unless that modifier is negative." Nick moves that attack into the Attack action. Same list of Light weapons; rapier still not. Drawing is the Attack action's "equip or unequip one weapon" (above), for every weapon, not a Light property.
- **Pathfinder 2e** [read] splits "light" into separate traits. **Agile:** "The multiple attack penalty you take with this weapon on the second attack on your turn is -4 instead of -5, and -8 instead of -10". **Finesse:** Dexterity on the attack roll, Strength still on damage. **Twin:** a damage bonus "if you have previously attacked with a different weapon of the same type this turn". **Concealable:** +2 to hide the weapon. **Free-Hand:** the hand can still hold and manipulate. **Parry:** one action for +1 AC. Who has what: dagger (agile, finesse, thrown 10, versatile S); shortsword (agile, finesse); hatchet (agile, sweep, thrown 10); light mace (agile, finesse, shove); sickle (agile, finesse, trip); light hammer (agile, thrown 20); main-gauche (agile, disarm, finesse, parry); **rapier (deadly d8, disarm, finesse: not agile); club (thrown 10: not agile)**. Quick Draw is a class feat (`gear-and-hands.md`).
- **D&D 4e** [quoted]: "Off-Hand: An off-hand weapon is light enough that you can hold it and attack effectively with it while holding a weapon in your main hand. You can't attack with both weapons in the same turn, unless you have a power that lets you do so". "Light blade" is a group that powers and the rogue's Sneak Attack name ("a crossbow, sling, or light blade"). "Small" lets a Small character use a two-handed weapon (the shortbow).
- **13th Age** [read]: three sizes, Small (1d4 dagger, 1d6 club in two hands, hand crossbow), Light or Simple (1d6 shortsword, hand axe, shortbow, light crossbow), Heavy or Martial (1d8 longsword, longbow). The size is only a damage die a class may or may not use well. Two weapons: "If your attack roll is a natural 2, you can reroll the attack but must use the reroll. You do not get an extra attack for fighting with two weapons."
- **Draw Steel** [read]: "Light weapons are one-handed melee weapons that can be used to make several strikes in rapid succession. Many such weapons can be thrown or used as an off-hand defensive weapon. Daggers, shortswords, rapiers, handaxes, and throwing hammers are typical light weapons. If your kit uses a light weapon, you can wield two light weapons at a time." **The rapier is light here; the club is medium.** The Dual Wielder kit pairs "a light weapon and a medium weapon"; its Double Strike hits two targets on one roll.
- **Lancer** [read]: an Auxiliary weapon rides along: "In addition to your primary attack, you may also attack with a different AUXILIARY weapon on the same mount. That weapon doesn't deal bonus damage."
- **Baldur's Gate 3** [quoted]: off-hand weapons grant no weapon actions.

### Takeaways

11. **In five of the seven games "light" means "may be the second weapon"; only how the second weapon pays out differs.** An extra attack with no modifier (5e), a smaller penalty on later attacks (Pathfinder 2e), permission for powers (4e), a free rider with no bonus damage (Lancer), a reroll of a natural 2 (13th Age).
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: "light" is a pairing rule in most games, and almost nowhere a rule about the weapon alone.
12. **Paying for the off-hand attack with the turn's small action was the old complaint; moving it into the main action is liked and brings its own trouble.** [lead, EN World 2019 threads, summary] the 2014 rule "uses up the bonus action", which the same classes need. [checked, EN World, 2019] "While I would love to get rid of the bonus action, that would imbalance the Rogue and Monk." After Nick [checked, EN World, Sep 2024]: "the more I work with it, the more I love the improvements to dual wielding. It's a very compelling style now"; "a clever way to elevate light weapons for classes other than rogues". [checked, Dec 2024] "the most effective is nick [...] If you cheese the sword and board with light weapons it would be way OP."
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: what the second weapon's attack costs decides whether dual wielding is a style or a tax.
13. **Nick tied to a short list of weapons forces odd pairs.** [checked, EN World, Dec 2024] "the only 2 weapon combo I see is Shortsword + Scimitar which is a damn weird combo if you ignore mechanics and just run off the visual let alone history"; a poster replaces Nick so "people can do Rapier/Dagger right away".
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: the classic pair players ask for is a non-light main weapon with a light off-hand (rapier and dagger), which most Light rules forbid.
14. **The rapier is the test case of where "light" stops.** Not light in 5e and not agile in Pathfinder 2e (it keeps finesse and the bigger die); light in Draw Steel. The club is light in 5e, not agile in Pathfinder 2e, medium in Draw Steel.
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: games separate "uses agility" (finesse) from "pairs well" (light or agile); the rapier gets the first and usually not the second.
15. **Free draw is nobody's Light property.** Where drawing is cheap it is cheap for every weapon (5e 2024, Draw Steel's free maneuver), and that is what produced takeaway 5. Pathfinder 2e sells it as a feat. Concealment is the one "small" benefit written as a rule (Pathfinder 2e's Concealable +2; a 5e poster notes there is no such rule there: "I'm not aware of any RAW anywhere saying two ranged weapons of the same size category have different Sleight of Hand DCs to conceal" [checked, D&D Beyond, 2021]).
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: a quick draw or a hidden weapon as the meaning of "light" would be new ground; no reception exists to borrow.
16. **Thrown rides with light in every list** (dagger, handaxe or hatchet, light hammer), and 2024's playtest saw it exploited with the free draw: "clever use of Nick, involving swapping weapons during a turn, stepping back, and throwing daggers with Nick, for enormous average damage" [checked, EN World, May 2023].
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: light plus thrown plus cheap draw is the combination that was abused.

## Q3. Short bow against long bow

### The rules, side by side

| Game | Short bow | Long bow | What tells them apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| D&D 2014 [read; Heavy from memory] | 1d6, 80/320, simple | 1d8, 150/600, martial, Heavy | proficiency, and Heavy (Small creatures) |
| D&D 2024 [read] | 1d6, 80/320, simple, **Vex** | 1d8, 150/600, martial, Heavy, **Slow** | the mastery; Heavy now "Disadvantage [...] if it's a Ranged weapon and your Dexterity score isn't at least 13" |
| Pathfinder 2e [read] | 1d6, 60 ft., deadly d10, Bulk 1, 3 gp | 1d8, 100 ft., deadly d10, **volley 30 ft.**, Bulk 2, 6 gp; "can't be used while mounted" | -2 inside 30 ft.; the mount; Bulk. Composite versions add half Strength to damage |
| D&D 4e [lead: a fan table] | 1d8, 15/30, Small | 1d10, 20/40 | size (a halfling's bow) and one die step |
| 13th Age [read] | 1d6, Light or Simple | 1d8, Heavy or Martial | which classes use the bigger category without a penalty |
| Draw Steel [read] | one "bow" category | | the kit: Rapid-Fire (+2/+2/+2, +7 range) or Sniper (+0/+0/+4, +10 range, pays for not moving) |
| Baldur's Gate 3 [quoted] | Hamstring Shot | Hamstring Shot and Brace (give up movement, reroll damage) | the long bow has the extra, stand-still action |

### Takeaways

17. **A bigger die and more range alone make the short bow a bow for those not allowed the long one.** [checked, Paizo, Aug 2019] "in PF1, as shortbows were just dumbed down longbows"; "I've always thought the idea of wandering a close quarter dungeon with a longbow as being the optimal range option in prior editions was very weird." The 2014 short bow's defence is proficiency and weight, not play: "the same magic item support as the longbow [...] without the martial proficiency requirement or heavy property" [checked, D&D Beyond, 2021].
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: range beyond the map and one die step don't make two starting bows a choice.
18. **Pathfinder 2e's Volley works as balance and is hated as fiction.** Against [checked, Paizo, Aug 2018, the playtest, when it was 50 ft.]: "Why does the volley weapon trait exist? I am aware of no physics that makes firing a longbow on a flat trajectory more difficult"; "It makes no sense, nerfs longbows for no reason"; "Volley bothered them [a new group] it it bothers me too." For [checked, same thread]: "I actually like the volley trait on the longbow [...] Giving it a bit of a nerf helps make it less of an all around, anytime superior option and gives it more of a specialized niche"; "If you are going to be making your way through tighter spaces - dungeon crawling, or hunting in the woods, it just makes sense to carry a shortbow". After release [checked, Paizo, a thread begun Aug 2019]: "You have a real choice between longbows and shortbows"; "I'm glad that longbows aren't just the unopposed champion of ranged weapons anymore"; "If the longbow didn't have volley, it'd be the best ranged weapon in the game." The shipped rule cut the distance to 30 ft.
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: a penalty at short range does split the two bows, and is the most complained-about way to do it (see `archer.md`, takeaway 5).
19. **The feat that removes Volley is read as the real reason for it.** [checked, Paizo, 2018] "It creates design space. The Fighter gets Point-Blank Shot, which nullifies the Volley penalty"; "Only fighters can use longbows properly."
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: a drawback that a later unlock erases is felt as a tax, not as the weapon's character.
20. **The alternatives players proposed give the short bow something instead of taking from the long bow.** "I go with removing volley and adding agile to a shorbow, sounds way more fun"; "give Longbows a Reload 1, since it does take more time and effort to draw". The warnings [checked, Paizo, 2019]: an agile, deadly short bow "would be a murder machine [...] Welcome to crit-landia"; a long bow with reload 1 instead of volley would be the worst martial weapon, in one poster's words (the line was cut off in the copy read); "I am for giving ranged weapons beneficial traits instead of negative [...] but the common solution of give shortbow agile and remove volley requires ALSO rebalancing all other ranged weapons".
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: "fast bow against strong bow" is the split players reach for; each half was said to break something in a game with many attacks a turn.
21. **A different rider on each bow can turn the smaller bow into the damage pick.** In 2024 the short bow's Vex (advantage on the next attack) is weighed against the long bow's Slow, which doesn't stack. [checked, D&D Beyond] "Fast forward to 2024...there is a new reason: vex property [...] If playing a rogue, shortbow would be an excellent". [checked, EN World, May 2025] "With a Vex weapon (shortbow) you would rarely need to use your bonus action for Steady Aim." [lead, D&D Beyond, summary] the short bow comes out about 1 damage a round ahead against AC 14 to 19; the long bow keeps range, control and the Heavy feats.
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: two bows with two different tricks are told apart by the trick, and the die becomes the smaller question.
22. **Mobility and a mount are the fiction players accept for the short bow.** Pathfinder 2e's long bow "can't be used while mounted" and has twice the Bulk; its short bow is "favored by skirmishers and cavalry" [read]. Baldur's Gate 3 gives the long bow the action that costs movement [quoted]. Draw Steel's two archer kits split the same way: the one that pays for standing still and the one that sprays two targets [read].
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: "the long bow wants you still, the short bow lets you move" is the split with the least objection found, and no direct reception either.
23. **A strength requirement exists in two forms:** a gate (5e 2024's Heavy: disadvantage under Dexterity 13; Pathfinder 2e firearms' Kickback: -2 under Strength +2) or a bonus (Propulsive: half Strength to damage). On propulsive [checked, Paizo thread title, 2020s]: "Is +1 composite shortbow better than +1 shortbow in every way?" (for a positive Strength, yes: it is an upgrade bought with gold, not a different bow).
   - *For 2d12:* a note only: a strength bonus on a bow reads as a better bow; a gate reads as a class or build restriction.
