# Dominions 6: the rules around its magic

**Date:** 2026-11-18. **Asked by Lucas:** with the pass on the Paths (`dominions-paths.md`): "You should even see adjacent rules as long as they seem important for my system. If after the search you think you need to read more about the rules, do it as well, as extensive as the Paths."

This note holds what the spells stand on: the conditions they inflict, the lingering clouds, the kinds of creature, how protections override each other, the rules for things that stay, summons, the rhythm and cost of casting, linked casters, and the dice. `dominions-magic.md` (the first pass) gave the overview of several of these; this one is the detail, read on the pages, with the corrections.

**What was read.** **[read]** by Claude as the pages' own text: the wiki's pages (illwiki.com) on magic, communion, fatigue, encumbrance, empowerment, gems, precision, the DRN, Magic and Drain scales, stun, false damage, luck, twisted fate, sight, curse, regeneration, awe, ethereal, horror mark, fear, morale (first half), innate spellcaster, elemental resistance, natural protection, sacred, mindless, magic being, undead, inanimate, the spell-table notation; the manual's pages on special damage and clouds (66 to 68) and its Magic chapter. **Read only as a tool's summary** (first pass): magic resistance, afflictions, blood magic. **Not read:** the combat chapter's attack and armor rules, morale and rout beyond the first half, items, sieges, the strategic game.

**Dominions' rules, not 2d12's. Nothing here is decided.** Lines marked **For 2d12** are Claude's.

## 1. The conditions spells inflict

Dominions has no single list of conditions; these are collected from the spell tables and the pages above. **How it ends** is given for each, because that is where a tabletop rule usually gets argued.

| Condition | What it does | Resisted by | How it ends | Paths |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Stunned** | Helpless, no action | Size and Strength (wind); Magic Resistance (lights); a chance by damage (shock: 5% + half the share of hit points lost) | After 1 round | Air, Glamour; Earth (hammers, quake), Holy (on the sacred) |
| **Paralyzed** | Helpless | Magic Resistance | After a number of rounds set by the "paralysis damage" and the target's size | Astral |
| **Asleep** | "Useless until harmed" | Magic Resistance; the mindless, undead and Inanimate immune | When it takes damage | Glamour; Nature (Torpor) |
| **Confused / Bewildered** | Half its actions are random | Magic Resistance; mindless immune | Bewildered: 1 turn; Confused: not stated | Glamour; Nature (beasts) |
| **Enraged** | Half its actions are random melee | Magic Resistance; mindless immune | Not stated | Fire |
| **Berserk** | Charges and can't be routed or frightened; stronger | (a buff or a curse by context) | The battle | Nature; Fire's Fury is the mild form (+2 attack, +2 morale) |
| **Charmed / Enslaved** | Changes sides | Magic Resistance (Charm Animal: easily; Soul Transaction: morale too) | Charm: for good; battle enslavement: the wiki doesn't say | Glamour (Charm), Astral (Enslave Mind; Control: Magic Beings), Death (the undead), Nature (animals), Blood |
| **Frightened** (Fright) | Morale down, at most 5 below base; no rout check | Nothing (it just applies) | Fades; priests remove it | Death (Frighten), Blood (Blood Rain) |
| **Afraid** (Fear) | Morale down by the spell's value and **a rout check for the squad**; the one struck may flee alone | The rout check is morale; some spells need a first morale check | Fades | Death (Terror), Glamour (Despair), Nature (Panic), Blood (Agony) |
| **Dread aura** | As a Fear aura, but **True Sight ignores it** | True Sight, Spirit Sight, blindness | The battle | Glamour + Death |
| **Awe** (on the defender) | Who would attack it in melee checks morale against 10 + the value; on a failure **the attack is lost** | Morale; the mindless and the blind immune | The battle | Glamour + Fire |
| **Entrapped** | Can't move or act until free | **Each Path its own check**: Strength against 22 or 23 (Earth's grip, Nature's web), Strength against about 19 (vines), morale against 21 (Fire's bonds; 3 fire on leaving), Magic Resistance against 20 (Glamour's false fetters); flyers and floaters ignore Earth's; the ethereal slip most | When the check is won | Earth, Nature, Fire, Glamour; Water underwater |
| **Slowed / Slimed** | -2 attack, -2 defence, **acts half as often** | Magic Resistance (Slime: easily) | Slimed: a resistance check each round against 22 | Water |
| **Quickened** | +2 attack, +2 defence, **acts twice as often** | (a buff) | The battle | Water |
| **Frozen** | Slowed, and 2d6 fatigue every round | Cold Resistance 10 or more can't freeze | Each round 25% + Cold Resistance to thaw | Water |
| **Burning** | Damage every round: a die of the target's size, halved | Fire Resistance 10 or more, the misty and the ethereal can't burn | Each round 25% + Fire Resistance to go out; rain puts it out | Fire; Earth + Fire (magma) |
| **Poisoned** | The damage is taken over the next rounds: **10% of what remains each round**; attack and defence reduced while much remains | Poison Resistance lowers the amount at the start, never the duration | When it has run its course | Nature |
| **Bleeding** | 5% of hit points and 10 fatigue every round | The Inanimate and undead immune | Each round 10% + Regeneration to stop | Blood; Nature's Smite |
| **Decaying** | Ages 5 years every round (the old collect injuries and die) | Magic Resistance | Not stated | Death |
| **Diseased** | -10% hit points every turn of the game; no natural healing | The undead and Inanimate immune | Only a healer, between battles | Death, Nature + Fire |
| **Weakened** | Strength -2 or -3 (the cloud's version is permanent) | Magic Resistance | The battle (or never) | Death |
| **Desiccated** | +2d8 fatigue every round | Magic Resistance; undead and Inanimate immune | Not stated | Water |
| **Blinded** | -9 attack, -9 defence, -9 precision | Fire's version: nothing; Glamour's: Magic Resistance | A lasting injury (it stays after the battle) | Fire, Glamour |
| **Cursed** | +15% chance that any damage leaves a lasting injury; Horror Marks count double | Nothing | **Never** ("not even death removes it"; only the Wish ritual) | Astral + Glamour, Death, Astral |
| **Horror-marked** | Horrors come for you, likelier with each mark; never removed while alive | The mindless can't be marked | Never | Astral, Blood |
| **Feebleminded** | Can't cast; **every enchantment it holds ends**; -5 Magic Resistance | Magic Resistance | A lasting injury | Astral + Death (nether bolts), Astral |
| **Petrified** | Helpless, as stone (at least 20 armor) | The incorporeal and stone immune | With the battle | Earth |
| **Shrunk / Enlarged** | Size, hit points (30%), Strength (3) down or up | Magic Resistance (Shrink) | Shrunk: for good; Enlarged: the battle | Nature |
| **Transformed** | Becomes a frog, a boar | Magic Resistance; mindless, undead, Inanimate immune | For good | Nature |
| **Fatigued** (a number) | Every 10: -1 defence; every 20: -1 attack; 50: armor-defeating hits three times likelier; **100: unconscious** | Elemental resistance counts double against fatigue damage | -1 a round; -5 while unconscious | Air (Steal Breath), Astral (Stellar Cascades), Death (Ghost Grip, Rigor Mortis), Water (cold), Nature (Torpor), Glamour (Elf Shot) |

**What the list shows.**
- **Three families of "out of the fight":** helpless for a round (Stunned), helpless until something happens (Asleep until harmed; Entrapped until a check is won; Paralyzed for a count), and acting wrongly (Confused, Enraged, Charmed). Dominions gives each a different way out, and that way out is most of the difference between them.
- **Many durations are simply "the battle"**, which a computer can afford and which is also the easiest duration at a table (2d12's default: a condition with no duration lasts until the end of the encounter).
- **Several effects are deliberately permanent** (Cursed, Horror-marked, Blinded, Shrunk, Feebleminded): Dominions' campaign layer. In 2d12 these are the Wound tables' job, not a condition's.
- **Damage over time comes in three shapes:** a fixed amount each round until a roll ends it (Burning, Bleeding), a total decided at the start and paid out in shrinking parts (Poison), and a cost in tiredness rather than health (Frozen, Desiccated). 2d12's Lingering is the first shape.

**For 2d12, the nearest words** (where one exists): Stunned → Dazed (a value) or Staggered; Slowed and Quickened → the same names; Entrapped → Immobilized (with Break Free's Attribute set by the Path); Burning, Bleeding → Lingering; Poisoned → Lingering with a falling value (new); Afraid → Frightened; Awe → the mirror of Taunted (new); Blur → Pressed on the attackers; Blinded → Blinded or Half-Blinded; Fatigued → Exhausted is far too heavy for it, so there is **no light "tired" condition** in 2d12; Asleep, Confused, Charmed → no 2d12 word (`conditions-and-senses.md` argues against turns lost outright).

## 2. False damage

Glamour's own damage type. A creature dies when **false damage + real damage** reaches its hit points ("their mind or soul assumes they are dead"). It causes no lasting injury. Healing, regeneration and drain don't remove it; effects that react to real damage (Blood's reversals and bonds) ignore it. **It fades by 2 a round once no enemy Glamour mage remains**, and a Glamour 3 mage sheds 1 a round regardless. The mindless are immune. All of it is armor-negating.

**For 2d12:** this is "damage that exists while its caster stands". 2d12 already tracks two kinds of damage on one pool (Lethal and Nonlethal) with rules for what happens when each is dominant; false damage could be **Nonlethal Resolve damage that is removed when the caster goes Down**. No new track.

## 3. Clouds: areas that linger and fade

"Certain beings, spells and attacks create battlefield clouds." [manual, read]

- A cloud has a **type** and a **level from 1 to 7**.
- **It weakens by about one level a round** until it is gone ("will dissipate quite fast if not replenished").
- **Clouds of one type don't overlap**: a second one spreads the cloud further, or raises a square's level by one at most.
- **All cloud damage is armor-negating.**
- Monsters that trail clouds make level 1 (so at most level 2); spells make higher ones (Fireball leaves heat clouds of level 1; Fire Cloud, level 4; Sulphur Haze, level 5).
- **Heat and frost clouds cancel each other.** Rain makes clouds fade 25% faster; Mist stops them fading.

| Cloud | Effect by level |
|---|---|
| Heat, Frost | 1: 2 fatigue; 2: 3 fatigue; 3: 4 fatigue and 1 damage; 4: 5 and 2; 5+: 6+ and 3+ |
| Poison | level = poison damage |
| Death | level = damage (resisted) |
| Sulphur | fire fatigue and poison, both rising |
| Disease | Diseased (easily resisted at 1 and 2) |
| Rust | armor rusts |
| Shimmering Light | false damage (resisted) |
| Leeching | Weakness, and 1 damage from level 2 (resisted) |
| Sleep | Asleep (easily resisted at 1) |

**For 2d12:** a zone with **a level that drops by 1 at the end of each round and is gone at 0** is a duration nobody has to remember: the level is the clock, and it is written on the map. "Two zones of one kind don't stack: the higher level stays" is 2d12's Stacking rule already. Heat against frost is a ready-made interaction between two casters.

## 4. Darkness and sight

- **Darkness** has a value. Caves, deep seas and night: -3 attack, -3 defence, -3 precision. The Darkness spell: -6. Twilight: -2 precision only.
- **Darkvision** 50 or 100 removes that share of the penalty. **True Sight** sees through Blur, Displacement, Invisibility, Dread and Twilight (not Mirror Image; badly through Bewilderment). **Spirit Sight** is both. The blind-born ignore darkness.
- Astral's Solar Brilliance sets darkness to -1 (bad for those who draw strength from the dark) and blinds.

**For 2d12:** Chapter 3 has Light rules and the backlog has an open idea for a Darkness zone ("Light never makes a square darker" needs a rule). Dominions' answer is a single battlefield value that spells set, and senses that ignore a share of it.

## 5. Kinds of creature

The kinds spells ask about. A creature can have several.

| Kind | What it is | What it is immune to | What only it suffers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mindless** | No will of its own (many constructs, zombies, vine men) | Sleep, confusion, rage, charm, false damage, fear, Horror Marks, mind damage; hard to interrupt when casting | Dissolves if its leader is lost |
| **Undead** | Raised or from the Underworld | Much of Death, Glamour, Nature and Blood (anything "Undead negates"); disease's harm; cold and poison in part | Banishment, Solar Rays, Dust to Dust, the swarm, Control the Dead |
| **Demon** | From the hells | Sleep; whatever the undead are immune to | Smite Demon, Banish Demon, Cleansing Water |
| **Inanimate** | Not alive (skeletons, zombies, statues, constructs, elementals, illusions) | Luck, bleeding, regeneration, thirst, sleep, disease, rage; all healing and all fatigue removal; drain; changes of shape | Shatter |
| **Magic Being** | Alive by magic (elementals, angels, vine men, void beings) | Nothing by itself | Arcane Bolt, Hidden Flame, Control, Opposition, Unraveling; needs a leader with Magic Leadership |
| **Animal** | | Fire's Fury | Nature's beast spells (buffs and charms), Animal Awe |
| **Sacred** | Can be blessed | | Holy Word, the unholy weapons |
| **Ethereal** | Barely here | Ordinary hits land only 25% of the time; no burning, freezing, bleeding from them; slips most entrapment; walks through walls | Magic hits it normally |
| **Spiritform** | Fully incorporeal (ghosts) | As ethereal, and no petrifying, melting, transforming, and **no skin buffs** (Barkskin, Stoneskin, Ironskin, Mossbody, Liquid Body, Enlarge) | |
| **Illusion** | A false body: 1 hit point, deals false damage | As a spiritform | Can be disbelieved; dissolves without a Glamour mage |

**For 2d12:** Chapter 7 says "the list of Tags (Profane, Beast, Undead...) and what each one covers is not written yet". This is a worked list with what each means for magic. `divine-profane.md`'s finding stands: a Tag should add to a spell, never switch it off, except where the fiction demands it (a thing with no mind can't be charmed).

## 6. Summons and bodies on the field

- **Battle summons last the battle.** "Any summons gained through battle magic exist only as long as the battle lasts" [manual]. Ritual summons are permanent troops.
- **They cost gems.** Almost every battle summon is 100 fatigue: a gem for one elemental, two wisps, ten asps. The cheap exceptions are Death's raised dead (20 to 40 fatigue for one to ten bodies) and Glamour's illusions (10 fatigue for one).
- **They scale by number or by size.** "Fire Elemental ×1 (Size 4)" at Fire 1; "(Size 7)" at Fire 3; "×4 (+1 a level)" at Fire 4. Raise Dead: ten, +2 a level.
- **They need a leader of their kind**: Magic Leadership for Magic Beings (given by Path levels: 10 a level, 20 for Astral), Undead Leadership for the undead and demons (50 a level from Death). Without it the mindless ones **dissolve**.
- **Illusions need a Glamour mage** on their side or they dissolve in a few rounds.
- **Where they appear:** "closest square", within 5 squares, or "enters from the side" of the field.
- Dominions sorts the three ways to get a body into three Schools: **called from elsewhere** (Conjuration: elementals, phantasms), **something already here made to move** (Enchantment: the dead; Alteration: trees), **a false body** (Evocation: illusions).

**For 2d12:** a tabletop summon costs the table time, which Dominions never pays. What transfers: summons **end with the encounter**; **they end when their summoner goes Down** (the leadership rule, simplified); one summon grows in size before it grows in number; and the three-way sorting gives each of Death, Glamour and the Elements a different kind of companion.

## 7. Protections, and how they override each other

Dominions never adds two protections of one family. Its rules, as written on the spells:

- **Skins (natural armor):** Barkskin (+7, up to 10; -5 Fire Resistance) < Stoneskin (+10, up to 15; -5 Cold Resistance) < Ironskin (+13, up to 20; -5 Shock Resistance). "Stoneskin overrides Barkskin"; "Ironskin overrides Barkskin, overrides Stoneskin". Each has a **cap** and gives only a small bonus to a creature already above it. **Each costs a weakness.** Death's Invulnerability "substitutes for natural protection, if it's higher", and magic passes it.
- **Being missed:** Blur (-2 to attackers) < Displacement (-10 on the first swing, -5 after; "overrides Blur") < Invisibility ("overrides Blur, Displacement, Mirror Image"). True Sight negates each.
- **Counted wards:** Twist Fate cancels **the next wound** and is gone (not hits of 0 or 1; not instant deaths). Mirror Image: with n images, n in n+1 hits are lost and each pops one. Mistform: each hit reduced by 25 or to 1, **ended by a hit of 25 or more or any magic hit**. Luck: a blow that would kill misses 75% of the time, for the battle; "instant kills leave no room for luck".
- **Chance wards:** Air Shield (80% of missiles); Protective Force (50% chance of +20 armor on each hit).
- **Elemental resistance:** N points remove N damage, then 2% × N of the rest; 50 is immunity; it counts double against fatigue damage. A vulnerability is negative resistance. Wards give +5 (one creature or many) or +10 (the caster).
- **Regeneration:** 10% of hit points a round; not for the undead or the Inanimate.
- **Retaliation:** Fire Shield, Astral Shield, Charge Body, Shroud of Bewilderment, Soul Vortex, Damage Reversal, Holy Avenger: the attacker pays, by the Path.

**For 2d12:** this is a stacking rule that needs no table: **protections of one family don't add; the stronger replaces the weaker, and the card names its family.** 2d12's Guard already works this way ("keep the higher value"). Three ideas are new to 2d12 and cheap: a buff with **a cap** ("up to"), a buff with **a price** (a Weak to one subtype), and a ward that is **a count** ("the next instance of damage") instead of a duration.

## 8. Things that stay

- **A battlefield enchantment** lasts the battle and "stops having an effect when the caster leaves the battlefield or gets Feebleminded" [wiki]; the manual adds that every enchantment ends at sunset in a very long battle.
- Each is marked for **whom it touches**: (Neutral) everyone, (Friendly) the caster's army, (Enemy) the other, (Caster) everyone but the caster. **Most are Neutral**: weather, darkness, fire storms hit both sides, and the caster's side prepares for it (fire resistance before a Fire Storm).
- Many work by **striking a random share of the field many times a round** ("just under 24 times a round, a random 3%"): the whole field is dangerous, nobody is certainly hit.
- Enchantments answer each other by name: Heat from Hell and Grip of Winter cancel out; Rain raises the cost of Fire spells and douses the burning; Storm doubles Wrathful Skies and lets Storm Power work; Nightfall needs Twilight and falls with it.
- By Path (battle only): Water 7, Glamour 6, Astral 5, Nature 5, Air 4, Death 4, Fire 3, Earth 2.
- World enchantments (rituals; the slots and the contest) are in `dominions-magic.md`.

**For 2d12:** Lucas decided that anything that stands is an Enchantment. Dominions adds three usable rules: **who it touches is one word on the card** (everyone, allies, enemies, all but you); **a weather-like enchantment touches everyone**, which makes it a decision and not a free bonus; and **enchantments name the ones they cancel**.

## 9. Casting: rhythm, cost, and what breaks it

Confirmed on the pages; the overview is in `dominions-magic.md`.

- **Rhythm.** A spell of "100% casting time" takes half a round to prepare and half to recover. Evocations are typically 75% ("snappier"); enchantments on the field and gem spells 125% to 225%. So a mage casts about one spell a round, and a little more than one if they throw bolts.
- **Interruption.** Only during the preparation, only by a hit that takes at least 1 hit point: the share of hit points lost + 25%, halved for a Combat Caster and for the mindless. **Innate casters** (idols, creatures with several minds) cast extra spells with no preparation and can't be interrupted.
- **Cost.** The spell's fatigue ÷ (1 + levels above its first Path's minimum), **then** the caster's own encumbrance is added (their base + twice their armor's and shield's): **armor is a flat tax on every spell that mastery doesn't reduce.** A human's base is 3.
- **At 100 fatigue a mage can't cast** and is unconscious; past 200 the excess becomes damage (every 50 = 1).
- **Recovery** is 1 a round (5 while unconscious): within a battle fatigue only goes up, unless something removes it (Earthpower: 4 a round; Nature's Relief; Blood's Reinvigoration; drain).
- **Gems.** One closes a one-level gap in the first Path (never two); extra gems each count as a level **for cost only**; at most as many a spell as your level in the Path; a spell of 100 fatigue or more needs one for each full 100 whatever your level.
- **Penetration.** 11 (7 easy, 15 hard) + a roll, +1 for every second level above the minimum; the target adds half its own level in that Path to its Magic Resistance.
- **The land.** Each point of Magic scale: spells cost 10% less and everyone resists a little worse; Drain the reverse.
- **Self-raising.** Every Path but Death has a battle spell that gives +1 level in itself for the fight (`dominions-paths.md` 3.2), usually the first thing a mage casts.
- **Empowerment** (permanent): 50 gems for a first level in a Path, then 15 × the new level.

**For 2d12:**
- The arithmetic of a fight: fatigue 20 a spell against a limit of 100 and a recovery of 1 means **about four or five spells at your own level before you are useless**, and twice as many one level below your mastery. Dominions' fights are long; 2d12's are 3.5 rounds. A caster with a budget that lasts "about one fight" needs no other limit.
- **"A spell to raise your own level, cast first"** is Dominions' version of a caster's first turn. 2d12 has Escalation for the whole party; a caster's Path level rising with Escalation would do the same job with no spell.
- The armor tax is flat and unarguable: "in armor, each spell costs N more".

## 10. Linked casters (the communion), in detail

Corrections and additions to `dominions-magic.md` 4.2, from the wiki's page [read]:

- **Three kinds**, and an army can run one of each: the Astral Communion (cheap to enter, 20 fatigue; the master casts 25% slower), the Blood Sabbath (a blood slave to enter; **the master takes half the fatigue, the slaves 25% more**), and a national Chorus (singers leave safely).
- **The boost: +1 level in every Path the master has with 2 slaves, +2 with 4, +3 with 8** (2 to the power of the boost). It is the same for every master in the link.
- **The cost is split evenly** among the casting master and all slaves (other masters don't share), the master's encumbrance included; then each slave multiplies its share by how it compares with the master in the spell's first Path: **master at least double the slave: ×4; above: ×2; equal: ×1; below: ×½.** A master who raises their own level with gems makes it worse for the slaves, not better.
- **A spell that targets only the master also targets every slave.** (A master's Reinvigoration clears everyone.)
- **Slaves do nothing else.** They can't leave; the link ends when all masters or all slaves are dead or gone, and then each slave takes 3 to 150 fatigue and loses a round.
- **A slave pushed past 200 fatigue takes 1 damage "inside their head"**, more past 250.
- Items let a non-Astral mage join.
- The **Grand Communion** is a different thing: outside battle, the levels of everyone helping are added to a world ritual's strength.

**For 2d12:** the level comparison is the interesting rule for players: **helping a caster stronger than you costs you more.** A helper of equal skill shares the cost fairly; an apprentice feeding a master burns out. That is a price that needs no extra number.

## 11. The dice

Dominions rolls **2d6, open-ended**: a 6 counts as 5 and is rolled again and added, without limit ("the DRN"). Almost everything is one such roll against another, and ties have a named winner. The designers' reason [wiki, quoting the manual]: "very few things are actually impossible": a militiaman can always hit the ethereal monster, rarely.

**For 2d12:** 2d12 also has exploding dice (damage) and a fixed target instead of an opposed roll. The parallel worth noting is the intent: Dominions lets spells be resisted by "impossible" rolls so that no spell is certain; 2d12's three Tiers do it with one roll.
