## What Foundry draws today (from system.css, theme.css, build-theme.mjs and the approved mockups; Foundry itself not run)

- **Two stylesheets:** `system.css` (about 500 KB, grown version by version) and `theme.css`, generated by `tools/build-theme.mjs` from it: dark by default, light as the parchment set, chosen by the system's own setting. Shared colors are the `--s-*` tokens (page, band, rule, hairline, ink, mute, gold, the stamp, the Tier colors, the pool colors, five frequency colors, four rank colors).
- **Type:** Signika for everything read; Cinzel still declared about 26 times (big titles, the character's name, some older rules).
- **Color carries meaning almost everywhere:** the frequency (cost pills, the edges of Basic Actions, the chat card's pill), the trait group (filled chips with the group's name under them), the Tier (red, yellow-green, green, blue for a Critical: rows, the ladder, the big Tier stamp), the pools (Vigor red, Resolve blue), AP blue and Guard blue in text, the Attributes' colors, the rank's color, the subtypes' colors and icons.
- **The Action card still has the old card structure:** a column a cost with "As 1 AP", an "Effects" row, "Vs. AD", trait chips with group labels; the book's cards have the Structure of 2026-11-16 (blocks a Version, labeled lines, the roll line, Tier lines). Its Foundry pass is in `foundry-backlog.md` ("THE PALADIN'S REDESIGN IN FOUNDRY").

## What the research says for a VTT (vtt-style.md; one pass; players' own verdicts thin: Reddit, the Foundry forum and Discord unreadable)

1. **The book look shows up in reference views, not working ones** (Draw Steel's journal and monster stat blocks, pf2e's maroon trait boxes); chat, roll windows and play sheets stay plainer in every system looked at. None sets its chat text in a book serif.
2. **Chat cards draw the most "too big" fixes** (modules that cut margins, portraits, buttons, repeated tags); the systems' answers: collapse older cards, summary cards, one message updated in place.
3. **Sheet speed means the per-turn controls always visible** (favourites, mini sheets, a Play / Edit toggle).
4. **Light cards in a dark frame is dnd5e's documented look** ("Interface Dark, Applications Light"); parchment is what dark modules remove first, and dark mode was Roll20's most requested feature.
5. **Foundry V13/V14 has its own color-scheme settings, two of them** (applications and interface), a per-sheet override (13.334), and CSS layers (a system's CSS sits in the `system` layer); popped-out chat is forced light (13.340). dnd5e and Draw Steel scope their colors under Foundry's `.theme-dark` / `.theme-light`.
6. **Accessibility shipped by others:** high-contrast modes, blue in the place of green for success, a legibility-first font (Draw Steel: Atkinson Hyperlegible), rem sizes that follow Foundry's font slider; WCAG asks 3:1 for controls.

## The honest answer (Claude, 2026-11-17)

1. **"Fit the book" should mean the same identity, not the same page.** The research found no VTT that puts its book look on its working surfaces; they put it on reference views. A VTT needs things a book doesn't: results at a glance (the Tier), buttons to click, a narrow dark chat beside a map. So: the **Action card and every preview of a card** take the book's look (it is the book's card on a screen); **chat cards, sheets, bars and windows** keep the table look and take the book's type, accent and restraint.
2. **The biggest gap is not style.** The Action card shows the old card structure. Restyling it before the Structure's Foundry pass would be done twice. That card's look goes with that pass.
3. **Much is already close:** the accent (gold against bronze), the pool colors, square corners, one stamp for the thing clicked. The type is the one global swap that brings everything nearer at once.
4. **Color is where Foundry and the book now disagree most,** and where the research is clearest: color for a meaning, never alone, few at once. Some Foundry colors earn their place on a working surface (the Tier, the pools, maybe the frequency on a usable button); others are the decoration the book removed (AP blue and Guard blue in text, the Attributes' colors, filled trait chips with group labels, the double frame).
5. **Two theme switches is one too many.** Foundry now has its own; the system's setting duplicates it and fights it in places (popped-out chat). Following Foundry's would be the convention dnd5e and Draw Steel use. The default (dark or light) is then the user's Foundry setting.
6. **Cost:** the last style pass was approved and built six days ago and Lucas hasn't tested v0.0.386 yet. A full second pass of mockups for every surface would repeat much of it. A short list of global changes, mocked on the existing pages (they are generated), costs a fraction.

## The proposed steps (each mocked first, each waiting for Lucas; nothing applied until all are approved, as before)

1. **The principle:** which surfaces take the book's look (the Action card and the card previews) and which keep the table look with the book's type and accent (chat, sheets, HUD, bars, windows). One decision.
2. **Type:** Source Sans 3 in the place of Signika, Source Serif 4 in the place of Cinzel (titles) and for card Flavor; both shipped with the system (the book's files). Mocked on the existing pages by the generator.
3. **Color, one meaning at a time:** a table of every color Foundry uses, keep or drop, with its reason (the research's rule). Proposed to keep: Tier, pools, the stamp, the accent; to drop or quiet: AP and Guard blue in text, Attribute colors, the trait chips' fills and group labels, the double frame. The frequency: Lucas's call.
4. **The theme switch:** follow Foundry's own color scheme (applications) in the place of the system's setting, or keep the setting.
5. **The Action card and the previews, in the book's look:** with the Structure's Foundry pass (the data and the look together), mocked from the book's own cards.
6. **The chat cards' size:** the research's main complaint; a review of what each card shows, still a style change (collapse what can be collapsed).
7. **Then the build,** one pack, and Lucas's test checklist.
