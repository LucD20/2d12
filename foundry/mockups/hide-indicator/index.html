Hide: to whom is it Hidden or Unsensed?

A mockup, three options; nothing is built. The scene is the same in every picture: Kaela used Hide behind a wall. She is Unsensed to the Goblin Sneak (north of the wall), Hidden to the Orc Warrior (it sees her over the wall's end) and Clear to the Goblin Archer, which has her in plain sight. Sylwen is her ally. The data is the flag on Kaela: one entry for each creature Hide worked against.

Unsensed an empty dashed ring: it can't target her Hidden a half-filled disc: its rolls against her are Doomed Clear no mark

The two marks are the same in all three options, in ink, told apart by shape. Wherever there is room the word is written beside the mark.

A. Seals on the hider, lines to the observers Proposed

A mark on the map. At rest, one small seal for each creature she is hiding from. Select or hover her, and each of those creatures is joined to her by a line and named Hidden or Unsensed.

Who sees itThe GM and Kaela's player. (Could be the whole party; the foes' players, if any, never.)
At a glanceAt rest: "she is hiding, from two". On her: who, and which of the two, without reading a list or looking for names.
Trade-offsThe most to build (seals, lines and plates on the canvas, redrawn when a token moves or the flag changes). With six observers the lines fan out; they show only while she is selected or hovered, so the map stays clean. The seals sit on the left edge, since Judge's seals use the right.

B. A line of text: the Alt condition line and the HUD

No new mark on the map. Hide's state is written where conditions are already written: under the token while Alt is held, and in the HUD of the selected token.

Who sees itThe Alt line: the GM and Kaela's player (Hide's entry is left out for everyone else; the other conditions stay as today). The HUD: whoever controls the selected token.
At a glanceExact and unambiguous once read ("Hidden to Orc Warrior"), but it is reading: with two Goblin Sneaks the names don't say which one, and nothing shows until Alt or a selection.
Trade-offsThe least to build: two existing surfaces, one new line each. The Alt line gets long with many observers (it wraps). The HUD is one new group on an existing bar, so it waits on the HUD's own pass.

C. From the observer's side: the hider as that creature perceives it

Select a creature, and every token hiding from it is redrawn: a dashed empty outline if Unsensed, faded with a solid outline if Hidden. It answers "can this one attack her?" at the moment the GM picks its target.

Who sees itThe GM, for any creature selected. A player, for their own PC, only for tokens Foundry already shows them.
At a glanceThe strongest answer to the GM's question on an NPC's turn. It doesn't answer Kaela's player's question ("from whom am I hidden?") without clicking each foe, and with no token selected nothing shows that she is hiding at all.
Trade-offsIt changes how a token is drawn (its alpha and outline) by selection, so it must not be mistaken for Foundry's own hidden-token fade: the plate under her says which it is. Several tokens selected: the worst for her of the selected ones, or none (to decide).

Claude's pick: A. The question was "to whom", and that is a question about places on the map: A answers it where the tokens are, for the GM and for Kaela's player alike, and it is the only one that shows she is hiding before anything is clicked. B's Alt line is a cheap companion (the exact words, for the day two Goblin Sneaks stand side by side) and could ship first. C is worth adding later for the GM's turn; alone it leaves the hider's player without an answer.

Not tried in Foundry. Open: whether the party's other players see A; what C shows with several tokens selected.
