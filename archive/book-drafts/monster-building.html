Chapter 11: Monster Building

Monsters are built from roles and picks. The role sets the numbers. The picks decide how those numbers are distributed. Two monsters of the same role can play completely differently — a Standard brute and a Standard caster share a role but not a silhouette.

Every monster in this chapter is built against one ruler: the Standard PC. A level-1 PC has about 30 Health, deals about 15 damage per round, rolls attacks at +2, and defends at DC 13.

Scope

This chapter covers level 1, the only level currently written. When higher levels are added, revise every table here in the same pass as the PC benchmarks they depend on.

Roles

Every monster is one of four roles. Worth is what the monster contributes to an encounter budget: four Minions fill one Standard's slot, two Grunts fill one, one Elite fills one and a half.

RoleWorthHealthDPRAttackDefense DC
Minion0.25~9~5+112
Grunt0.5~21~7+213
Standard1.0~38~15+213
Elite1.5~52~22+214

A Standard matches a PC on paper, but a PC has Rally, Talents, and more options. A Standard loses a fair 1v1 against a PC. A Standard plus a Minion wins. An Elite beats a single PC and loses to two.

Step 1: Attributes

Each role has a fixed array of four values. Distribute them across Body, Mind, Instinct, and Appeal. The highest value usually goes to the attribute the monster attacks with, but nothing forces it.

RoleArraySum
Minion−1, 0, 0, 10
Grunt0, 0, 1, 12
Standard0, 1, 1, 24
Elite0, 1, 2, 25

The Minion's −1 is the only negative value in the system at level 1. It can go on any attribute, but it hurts HP the most when it lands on Body or Mind — see the formula below.

Example

A Standard Brute takes Body 2, Mind 0, Instinct 1, Appeal 1. Its attack attribute is Body. A Standard Caster takes Body 0, Mind 2, Instinct 1, Appeal 1. Same array, opposite silhouette.

Step 2: Body and Mind thresholds

Monsters have two damage pools, exactly like PCs. Body damage and Mind damage are tracked separately, each with its own threshold. When Body damage reaches the Body Damage Threshold (BDT), the monster is Bloodied. When Mind damage reaches the Mind Damage Threshold (MDT), the monster is Strained.

Each threshold is: Base + 2 × own attribute + 1 × other attribute.

Formula

BDT = BaseBody + 2 × Body + 1 × Mind
MDT = BaseMind + 2 × Mind + 1 × Body
Total HP = BaseBody + BaseMind + 3 × (Body + Mind)

Bloodied and Strained apply

Monsters use the same conditions as PCs. Bloodied gives −1 bane on [Strike] and [Weapon] Actions, and on Body and Instinct Saves. Strained gives −1 bane on [Magic] Actions, and on Mind and Appeal Saves. Both at once gives −1 bane on everything. This is the payoff of the two-pool system: a body-heavy monster has a tiny MDT but doesn't care about being Strained; a mind-heavy monster has a tiny BDT but doesn't care about being Bloodied. Attacks targeting the wrong pool still work — they just don't trigger anything useful.

Step 3: Speed

Base Speed is 5 for every role. Speed can be adjusted by spending or refunding points from the damage pool.

SpeedCostNotes
3Refund 1Takes 3 turns to close 8 squares. Real tactical loss.
40Comfortable.
50Default.
6Cost 1Arrives at turn 2 with 4 squares to spare.
7Cost 2Arrives at turn 2 with 6 to spare. Dangerous.

Below Speed 3 is unplayable. Above Speed 7 warps the map. The refund from Speed 3 can only be spent on boons or Minor signature picks — never on damage.

Step 4: Damage

Each action a monster makes has an AP tier — 1, 2, or 3. Tier damage is picked from the tables below. Add the monster's attack attribute to Tier 2 and Tier 3 damage. Tier 1 stays as written.

Not every monster needs all three tiers. A monster built around one big attack has 3 AP only. A monster built around pokes has 1 AP only. Unused tiers are just absent from the sheet.

Averages, not dice

Every value in these tables is an average. 1d6 is written as 3.5. The monster's damage roll at the table still uses dice — the tables exist so you can see the math. When building a monster, choose the nearest dice expression. A Tier 2 of 4 could be 1d6+1 (average 4.5) or 2d4-1 (average 4). Close enough.

The damage pool

Each role gets a budget of points to spend on damage. Low costs 0, Med costs 1, High costs 2. Unlocking a 2 AP tier costs 1 point. Unlocking a 3 AP tier costs 2. The 1 AP tier is free to exist.

RoleDamage pointsTypical build
Minion1Med 1 AP, or two Low tiers
Grunt2Med 1 AP + Low 2 AP, or 2 Med tiers
Standard3Med 1 AP + Med 2 AP, or Med across all tiers
Elite4High 2 AP + Med 1 AP, or Med across all tiers

Leftover points (from Speed 3 refund or unfinished budgets) buy +1 boon on the first attack each turn (capped at +1) or a Minor signature pick. They never buy damage picks.

Baseline, not ceiling

The DPR target is a baseline. A monster with a 2 AP Med attack and a 1 AP Med attack will land around 165% of the target — that's an intentional optimized build. It trades signature picks and flexibility for raw output. The target exists so a GM knows when a monster is off by an order of magnitude, not to enforce uniformity.

Step 5: Signature picks

Signatures are optional traits that give a monster its flavor. Each role gets a specific number of slots. Each slot picks from a fixed menu. Picks are suggestions — skip any or all of them if the monster doesn't need them.

RoleSlotsAllowed categories
Minion1Minor only
Grunt2Minor, Reaction, Passive
Standard2Minor, Reaction, Passive
Elite3Minor, Reaction, Passive, Encounter, Aura. Max 1 Encounter or Aura.

Menu

Minor (any role)

  • Nighteyes — sees in Darkness as Bright.
  • Unshakable — cannot be knocked Prone.
  • Death Throes — collapses into a 1-square hazard (1d4 damage, one round).
  • Sure-Footed — ignores Difficult Terrain.
  • Hollow Endurance — at 0 HP, 25% chance to remain at 1 HP (once per encounter).
  • Tidal Born — swims at full Speed, breathes water.

Reaction (any role)

  • Reaping Strike (1R) — attack a creature that leaves your Reach.
  • Bitter End (1R) — when hit by a melee attack, deal your Attribute back as damage.
  • Scatterstep (1R) — when a creature ends a move within 1, Evade 2.
  • Shelter (1R) — when an adjacent ally is hit, redirect the attack to yourself at half damage.
  • Warding Blow (1R) — impose 1 bane on an incoming attack.

Passive (any role)

  • Bloodlust — while Bloodied, gain 1 boon on all attacks.
  • Fever Focus — while Strained, gain 1 boon on all saves.
  • Momentum — Charge grants 2 boons instead of 1.
  • Hardened — resist one damage subtype.
  • Toxic Blood — melee attackers take 1d4 Poison damage.
  • Pack Hunting — gain 1 boon when attacking a target adjacent to an ally.

Encounter (Elite recommended)

  • Chieftain's Roar — allies within 3 squares each remove HP ÷ 4 damage.
  • Sundering Strike — one attack deals double damage and Prone on hit.
  • Terrifying Roar — enemies within 3 squares save vs. Fear.
  • Linebreaker — Advance 8 and attack all enemies in a line.
  • Gathering Horde — 2 Minions appear.
  • Fade Step — become Incorporeal for 1 round.

Aura (Elite recommended)

  • Dread Aura — enemies within 2 squares take 1 bane on attacks.
  • Healing Radiance — allies within 2 squares heal 1 HP per round.
  • Miasma — enemies within 2 squares take 1 bane on saves.
  • Steady Presence — allies within 3 squares gain 1 boon on saves.
  • Grasping Ground — squares adjacent count as Difficult Terrain.

Minor Enemies

Minions and Grunts are Minor Enemies. This is a free property — it doesn't consume a signature slot.

A Minor Enemy dies instantly when Forced Movement pushes it into a Deadly Hazard. Standards and Elites are Imperiled instead.

The complete build

  1. Pick a role. Minion, Grunt, Standard, or Elite.
  2. Distribute attributes. Use the role's array. Highest usually goes to the attack attribute.
  3. Pick thresholds. Choose Low, Med, or High for Body and for Mind.
  4. Set Speed. Base 5. Buy up or refund for a low Speed.
  5. Spend damage points. Pick Low / Med / High per AP tier, plus unlock costs.
  6. Spend remaining points. Speed 3 refund and leftover damage points buy boons or Minor signatures.
  7. Fill signature slots. Pick from the menu, or leave them empty.
  8. Check DPR. Three 1 AP attacks should be within 10% of the role's target.

Designer's notes

Baseline, not ceiling

A monster built to the role target is a default. Nothing stops a GM from building monsters above or below the line. What matters is that the GM knows which direction they're moving.

The Bloodied / Strained trigger

Body-heavy monsters are the ones a physical party Bloodies. Mind-heavy monsters are the ones a psychic party Straineds. A monster with two values the same has two thresholds at the same place — every attacker is the right attacker. Mixed attribute picks are strong defensively.

Deadly Hazard interaction

A Minion's −1 attribute gives it an obvious weak pool. That same Minion dies instantly in a Deadly Hazard, so terrain becomes a second front. A GM who places a chasm and a Push effect turns three Minions into one Standard's worth of work.

Why not more attribute range?

Flat DC 13 means the tier distribution only tolerates a two-step band of attribute values. A +3 attack would succeed 78% of the time; a +0 attack would succeed 58%. Both are playable, but the gap makes "+2 monsters" feel categorically different from "+1 monsters." Capping at +2 keeps the game's math flat across roles.

Appendix: Freeform building

The point-buy system is the recommended path. If a GM wants to eyeball a monster instead, use the same tables but skip the budget. Pick any array, any threshold value, any damage values. The DPR check at the table catches outliers.

Every table in this chapter is a reference, not a constraint. A GM who wants a Standard with High thresholds and Low damage — a tanky grinder — can build it. The DPR target will show it's dealing about half the normal Standard output, and the GM can decide whether that's the intent.
