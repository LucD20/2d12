# Divine against profane as a game mechanic

## 2026-11-14: for the class redesign (MASTER-PLAN item 5), before any Paladin card

**The question (Lucas):** the Paladin is "focusing in divine action against profane forces". How do games make holy against unholy a mechanic, and what goes wrong?

**How far to trust this file.** [read] = rules text read in full from a GitHub copy (4e, 13th Age, Draw Steel). [relayed] = rules text a search tool relayed from a page nobody opened (Pathfinder 2e, 5e, 3.5). [lead] = an opinion seen only as a search summary. [mine] = Claude's reading. The full report: `raw/2026-11-14-C-champion-holy.md` (sections 3 to 5).

### Takeaways

1. **A bonus gated on a creature type fails in both directions.**
   - *Does nothing:* D&D 3.5's Smite Evil [relayed]: "If the paladin accidentally smites a creature that is not evil, the smite has no effect, but the ability is still used up for that day." [lead] "Woefully underwhelming."
   - *Does too much:* Pathfinder 1's Smite Evil [lead]: "Smite ruined a lot of climatic fights"; the GM's usual counter was to make the villain not evil.
   - *The named version:* the ranger's Favored Enemy [lead]: "worthless because it never comes up, it's too good because it comes up all the time"; poor design because it needs the GM to build the game around one character.
   - *For 2d12:* **our Divine Power is this design** ("When you deal Holy damage to a creature with the Profane Tag, that creature takes 1 Bane on its [Strike] Actions"): a level 1 Feature that is 0 in a fight with no Profane creature, and the list of Tags isn't written (Chapter 7's open box).
2. **The settled pattern: it always works, and a little more against the profane.** 5e's Divine Smite [relayed]: full value on anything, +1d8 against fiends and undead. Pathfinder 2e's remastered Smite [relayed]: +3 damage against the enemy you declare, +4 if it is unholy. 4e [read]: the paladin's damage is radiant on everything, and most undead are vulnerable to radiant; the one loud type-only power (Turn Undead: a burst that damages, pushes 3 + Charisma squares and immobilizes undead) sits on the cleric.
   - *For 2d12:* Holy is already a damage subtype, and Resist and Weak exist. "Profane creatures are Weak to Holy" in the Bestiary gives the Paladin his edge with no line on any Paladin card; a card can add "more against a Profane creature" as its one rider.
3. **Don't let the anti-profane part be the class's budget.** [lead] A GM's thread on a campaign with almost no unholy enemies: the Champion's anti-evil feats were lackluster and "the value came from the Champion's Reaction and Shield Block". The class survived because its core never depended on them.
   - *For 2d12:* price every Paladin card against a non-Profane enemy; the Profane rider is a bonus on top, small (Mild or Moderate).
4. **The player declares the target of judgment.** Draw Steel's Judgment [read], 13th Age's Paladin's Challenge [read], 5e's Vow of Enmity and the 2024 ranger's Hunter's Mark [relayed]. The mark replaces the creature type: "against the profane" becomes "against the one I judged". [lead] The 2024 ranger's lesson: the mark must be cheap to place and must not fight the class's other buttons (Hunter's Mark needs concentration and a bonus action, and "DnD fans still dislike the Ranger").
   - *For 2d12:* a judged creature can simply **count as Profane for you**: one rule ties the mark and the Tag together, and every "against a Profane creature" line works in every campaign.
5. **Profane by behaviour has printed precedent.** Pathfinder 2e's Lay on Hands FAQ [relayed]: you can use it to damage "a creature you witness harming an innocent or a ally as if it were undead". 3.5's Gray Guard [lead]: smite those "aiding and abetting evil". The Champion's Liberating Step triggers on an ally being grabbed or restrained, not only damaged [relayed]. No essay was found that proposes a list of behaviours.
   - *For 2d12* [mine]: behaviours our rules can already see: a creature that is Hidden or in Darkness, one that caused Frightened or a Lingering condition, a summoned creature, one that brought an ally Down or damaged a Down creature. "The creature that brought your ally Down counts as Profane for you" is a judgment with no button.
6. **4e's "radiant mafia": holy weakness as a status the party applies.** [lead] One character made targets vulnerable to radiant and everyone dealt radiant: campaign-proof, and it became the strongest party build in the game.
   - *For 2d12:* a caution more than a model. "The target is Weak to Holy" as a rider helps only the Paladin unless allies deal Holy; "Vulnerable 1" is our party-wide version and is priced (Mild, 3).
7. **Holy light as a tool is rare and liked.** Pathfinder 2e's Flash of Grandeur [relayed]: the Champion's reaction lights the attacker (dazzled; no invisibility or concealment) until the end of the Champion's next turn. 5e's Sacred Weapon sheds bright light [relayed]. Draw Steel's Exorcist [read]: judged creatures can't Hide; later he sees through illusions. 3.5's Consecrate [relayed]: a zone where undead are weakened and can't be summoned.
   - *For 2d12:* this is where we are ahead: light is a real rule here (Bright and Dim, the same shapes as Areas, it matters where the target is, Hide, Darkness) and Smite already makes its target shed Bright light with a Bane on Hide. Today that is a line lost in a 133-word card. As the Paladin's rule-bender it is an identity: **creatures in your light can't Hide; your light ends magical Darkness; the creature you judged glows.**
8. **A type system can become a straitjacket.** [lead] 4e made Undead a keyword instead of a type.
   - *For 2d12:* Tags are already words with no rule of their own. Profane can stay one Tag the GM puts on what the world calls profane (undead, fiends, the cursed), with no sub-list.
