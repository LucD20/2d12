# Tactical combat: what makes it engaging and cinematic

## 2026-11-13: for the Paladin's Stage 0 audit

**The question (Lucas):** opinions in tactical TTRPGs on forced movement, attacks with extra effects, battlefield movement, cinematic Actions and effects, what makes combat look cool and feel engaging. Asked to find the Paladin's lame or underused features and ways to spice them up (MASTER-PLAN item 5, the card sessions).

**What it was used for:** the Paladin audit page (https://claude.ai/artifact/4VnSmKyfFvuye6ZeErNHhN, private to Lucas): every Stage 0 Paladin card ranked against `book/ruler/pc-budget.md`, with suggestions. Nothing was applied to the book.

### Takeaways

1. **Forced movement needs a payoff.** Draw Steel makes Push, Pull and Slide a core verb because of what the creature hits: walls, objects (which can break) and other creatures (both take damage for the squares left over), with Stability to resist it. Some players find it over the top ("WWE"), and you can always stop the movement short.
   - *For 2d12:* the SIM agrees from the other side: a PC's Push aimed at nothing is worth about 0 (`pc-budget.md` section 9). A card's Forced Movement should have somewhere to send the creature: into a wall (Collision), toward an ally's reach, into a light or a zone. A Pull toward you or the target heaps enemies for the party; a Push away scatters them.
2. **When "just hit it" wins, the other options die.** Forum posters describe maneuvers and riders going unused because the math favors plain damage. A rider has to change the board (where creatures stand, what they can do this turn), not add a small number.
   - *For 2d12:* Smite's light (a Bane on Hide) and its 3 AP Push are flavor in practice; the Encounter Picks are bigger Smites with no moment of their own.
3. **A defender punishes being ignored.** 4e's Divine Challenge, homebrew 4e marks (Sacred Ward: the mark protects an ally, but needs a Reaction to limit abuse) and MMO tanks (Neverwinter: a mark that makes the target weaker and lets the tank force attention). The enemy may still hit someone else, but it pays for it.
   - *For 2d12:* the Paladin has no such tool; the Archer has Marked Quarry (worth 26 to 39). The suggestion was a "Judgment" mark in Divine Power's slot.
4. **Let the enemy choose its punishment.** Pathfinder 2e's Glimpse of Redemption: the attacker picks between the ally resisting the damage or being Enfeebled afterward. The Reaction is useful whatever it picks, works on mindless creatures (they take the default), and the choice is a moment at the table. Forum posters rate the Champion's Reactions as strong as the Fighter's Reactive Strike.
   - *For 2d12:* a shape for Shield of Faith (today about 4 times its budget).
5. **Always-on bonuses are invisible.** Posters prefer passive options in polls, but designers warn they crowd out active choices and that small flat bonuses mean little. Passives that change what you do, or fire at a dramatic moment, read as cinematic.
   - *For 2d12:* three Paladin Passives are a small damage bonus each (Radiant Zeal, Searing Blows, Reckoning); the Escalation 6 rung is seldom reached, so a one-off moment when you reach it is worth more than an ongoing bonus.
6. **Meaningful choice needs real alternatives and some risk.** Positioning and the action economy are where the decisions are; a choice with one obvious answer is not a choice (a Passive that is right for one Hallmark and useless for the other, such as Font of Mercy).
