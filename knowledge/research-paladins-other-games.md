# The paladin in other games: 4e, 13th Age, Pathfinder 2e (Champion, Guardian), Draw Steel (Censor), Nimble, Lancer, ICON

## 2026-11-14: for the class redesign (MASTER-PLAN item 5), before any Paladin card

**The question (Lucas):** the paladin and similar classes in 4e, Pathfinder 2e, Draw Steel, 13th Age, Lancer and others: what the rules give them and how players say they feel, compared with our rules.

**How far to trust this file.** [read] = rules text read in full from a GitHub copy (Draw Steel, Lancer, ICON, Nimble, Fabula Ultima, the 13th Age 1e SRD, a crawl of the 4e Compendium with its final errata). [relayed] = rules text a search tool relayed from a page nobody opened (all of Pathfinder 2e and 5e). [lead] = a poster's or reviewer's opinion seen only as a search summary. [mine] = Claude's reading. The full reports with every text and link: `raw/2026-11-14-B-4e-13th-age.md`, `-C-champion-holy.md`, `-D-censor-lancer-icon-nimble.md`.

### The games, one paragraph each

**D&D 4e Paladin** [read]. *The mark:* Divine Challenge, a minor action, within 5, one enemy: it takes -2 to attacks that don't include you, and **3 + Charisma radiant damage, no roll, no action**, the first time each round it attacks without you. The mark ends if you neither attacked it nor ended adjacent to it. *The heal:* Lay on Hands, a minor action, touch: **you spend your own healing surge, the ally heals as if they had spent one.** *At-wills that pay more against the enemy you marked* (Holy Strike, Enfeebling Strike). [lead] At launch it was called a weak defender: the punishment "barely a deterrent" and on one target only; the engagement duty on the slowest class let enemies walk the mark off ("moving in 5s and your opponent is moving in 7s"); half its attacks used Strength and half Charisma, remembered as its worst fault. The fix (Divine Power) [read]: **Divine Sanction**, the same punishment with a fixed duration and no upkeep, placed by an attack or by a burst; one attack stat a build. After it: "not particularly sticky, but... impossible for an enemy marked by a paladin to avoid punishment". The Essentials Cavalier [read] has the three "protect" shapes in one class: an aura 1 with -2 to attacks that don't include you, an automatic 3 + Charisma when an enemy in it ignores you, and once an encounter **Righteous Shield: take all of an ally's damage, then +2 to your attacks** (the power players praise by name [lead]). Its Holy Smite: an encounter power declared before the roll, the damage lands even on a miss, the daze only on a hit.

**13th Age Paladin** [read]. No powers: one feature and three talents. **Smite Evil:** once a battle plus Charisma times a day, declared before the roll, +1d12 and **half damage on a miss**; nothing requires an evil target. Talents: **Bastion** (once a battle, when a nearby ally is hit: you lose half the damage, the ally takes half); **Paladin's Challenge** (on a hit: until the battle ends you **both** take -4 against everyone else; it ends if you hit another enemy); **Lay on Hands** (twice a day, a quick action, your recovery heals the ally); **Way of Evil Bastards** (a smite that drops a non-mook is not used up). [lead] Reputation: "boring as opposed to underpowered"; the strongest tank and the least offensive character; the complaints are identical turns and identity pieces that cost talents, never the smite. The second edition reportedly makes Smite and Lay on Hands base features and scales the smite by level.

**Pathfinder 2e Champion** [relayed]. One reaction carries the class. Trigger: "An enemy damages your ally, and both are in your champion's aura" (15 feet). Payload: the ally resists 2 + level of that damage. **Each cause adds one rider:** a Strike if the enemy is in reach (Justice); the enemy chooses between no damage or resistance and being enfeebled (Redemption); the ally steps free (Liberation); the attacker is lit by revealing light, so no invisibility or concealment (Grandeur); kneel or take pain (Obedience). The reaction grows instead of multiplying (level 9 adds lingering damage, level 11 spreads it to the aura). Lay on Hands: 1 action, an every-fight resource, 6 Hit Points and +2 AC for a round. The Remaster's Smite: 1 action, declare one enemy, +3 damage to your Strikes against it, **+4 if it is unholy**, and it extends itself while that enemy keeps harming you or your allies. [lead] Praise: the catch-22 ("You can attack my allies and take a Champion's Reaction, or you can attack durable me and go through my Shield Block"); the party feels safe. Complaints: 15 feet is too short and never grows; Justice's second gate (reach) makes it the least reliable and Redemption the most; "a reaction that never triggers is dead weight"; "reactive rather than proactive"; players "may feel like all they do is attack and raise their shield". **Guardian** (2025) [relayed]: Taunt, 1 action, 30 feet, no roll: if the enemy acts against an ally and not you, -1 to it and it is off-guard. The playtest version (a save, and +2 for the enemy against you) was rejected ("Taunt is Bad"); the final one is accepted but called an action tax. Intercept Attack: an ally within 10 feet takes damage, you step to them and take it instead.

**Draw Steel Censor** [read]: the closest published match to the brief. Its text: "you're at your best against the strongest foes". The same Stamina as other front-liners and the most Recoveries (12): **tough across the day, not in the round.** *Judgment* (a maneuver, range 10, lasts the encounter): the judged enemy takes twice your Presence in holy damage, no roll, each time it uses a main action; on a kill it jumps to a new target for free. *Wrath* (its resource): +2 at the start of each turn, +1 the first time each round the judged enemy damages you, +1 the first time you damage it; all lost when the fight ends; a new fight starts with wrath equal to the party's Victories. **1 wrath buys a protection** against the judged enemy (a bane on its roll, it can't shift away from you, a taunt after your melee hit); **3, 5, 7 or 9 wrath buys the big ability**: so guarding delays the explosion. *My Life for Yours* (a triggered action, range 10, when an ally takes damage or starts its turn): **you spend one of your own Recoveries and the ally heals that much.** It never redirects the hit; the only true "take the hit" (Intercede) costs 9 wrath at level 6. Burst shapes worth noting: *Censored* (5 wrath: a non-boss brought to half by it drops to 0), the 7-wrath *Edicts* (a 2 aura for the rest of the fight that punishes nearby enemies, more against the judged one), 9-wrath strikes that are half support (the hit plus temporary Stamina to adjacent allies). [lead] Guides call its round 1 slow and praise its Recoveries and boss-killing.

**Nimble Oathsworn** [read]: the best match for "explode". *Radiant Judgment:* "Whenever an enemy attacks you, if you have no Judgment Dice, roll your Judgment dice (2d6). On your next melee attack this encounter, if you hit, deal that much additional radiant damage. The dice are expended whether you hit or miss." **One charge, rolled in the open when you are attacked, never stacking.** *Interpose* (a reaction every hero has; free for the Oathsworn from level 4): become the target for an ally within 2. So **guarding loads the burst**. Options: spend the dice on every enemy in your aura once an encounter; hand a die to an ally when you are attacked with dice already loaded; once an encounter set an ally who would drop to 1 HP. A subclass maxes the dice when an ally is wounded. [lead] "They want to be hit."

**Lancer** [read]. Defenders protect by position and penalty, not by soaking: adjacent allies use the mech as hard cover (the Guardian trait); the Saladin gives +1 difficulty to an attack on any ally in range once a round, at a cost in Heat. Burst is built then spent: the Sherman's gun gains a die for each Stabilize (up to 4) and resets when fired; Overcharge costs rising Heat (1, 1d3, 1d6, 1d6 + 4).

**ICON Stalwarts** [read]. *Vigilance:* a stock of dice (+1 a turn, up to 6) spent either to reduce damage to an ally within 2 or to hit a foe who leaves you. The Knave's Taunt gives Hatred: "Deal half damage to all foes other than foe X". The Demon Slayer upgrades its next attack if it does not attack this turn.

**Fabula Ultima Guardian** [read]: the opposite pole. *Protect*: take an ally's place against any attack, free, once a round, declared after the roll. A true sponge, by design.

### Takeaways

1. **"Can't take hits for you" is the common design, not the exception.** The Champion shaves the hit, the Censor heals after it, Lancer and ICON penalize or halve it. Three tested shapes of protection, all once a round or once a fight: **shave it** (Champion: resist 2 + level; Swordmage: minus 5 + Con), **halve it for both** (13th Age Bastion), **take all of it** (Cavalier's Righteous Shield, Guardian's Intercept).
   - *For 2d12:* Shield of Faith is "shave it" (2d6 + [Mod], worth 66 points a turn against a budget of about 15: it erases a Standard's whole hit and fires 82% of rounds). The shape is right; the size and the trigger are wrong. Bulwark of Faith (Guard equal to a Rally Point, then Slid 1) and Divine Intervention are the bigger, rarer shapes.
2. **One trigger, one payload, one rider is how the best-liked protector is written.** The Champion's seven causes differ by one clause.
   - *For 2d12:* this is our "a Reaction does one main thing and at most one rider" exactly. The two Hallmark Reactions can share a trigger and a payload and differ by the rider, as the causes do.
3. **The punishment must be certain, and not hang on upkeep the enemy controls.** 4e's flaw was the "stay adjacent" duty on its slowest class; the fix was a mark with a fixed short duration placed by an attack. The Champion's flaw is a second gate (reach) and a fixed short range.
   - *For 2d12:* our Paladin has Speed +0, heavy armor and, after the Advance rule, one unsplit move a turn. Don't write "while you are adjacent to it". Our Reactions reach 5 squares (25 feet, where the Champion's 15 is the top complaint): keep 5.
4. **A mark the player places is the spine of every modern paladin** (Judgment, Divine Challenge, Paladin's Challenge, the Remaster's Smite, Vow of Enmity). It makes the three roles one job: the marked enemy is the one you punish, protect against and explode on. Accepted marks are cheap (a maneuver or part of an attack), need no roll, and move for free on a kill.
   - *For 2d12:* the Archer already has it (Mark Quarry, 0 AP; the class's best-measured Feature, 26 to 39 points). The Paladin's Divine Power (a Bane on a Profane creature's Strikes) is worth about 0. A 0 AP "Judgment" in its place was the first audit's suggestion; the research backs it from five games. Our Taunted (1 Bane against anyone but you) is the same family as Draw Steel's taunt (a double bane) and ICON's Hatred (half damage): **ours is the mildest of the three** and the SIM prices it at 1 to 3.
5. **A mark that binds the Paladin too** (13th Age: you also take -4 against everyone else; hitting another enemy ends it) is a ready-made "soft": he guards against one enemy, not the room.
6. **Healing with the Paladin's own reserve** is in 4e, 13th Age and Draw Steel, and survived into 13th Age's second edition as a base feature: touch or short range, a minor action or a reaction, a few times a day. One poster calls it "retroactive defending".
   - *For 2d12:* Rally Points are the same rule as surges and Recoveries (a quarter of the Health Pool). Today Lay On Hands spends the **ally's**. A Paladin who spends **his own** for them "takes the hit for you" without taking the hit, and it makes his depth (Rally 6 against the Archer's 5) the tank part. See `frontline-protection.md`, takeaway 6.
7. **The burst is built in the fight and in the open.** Three loaders: time and the duel (Draw Steel's wrath), **being attacked or guarding** (Nimble's Judgment Dice, ICON's Vigilance), giving up a turn (ICON's Demon Slayer, Lancer's Sherman). Detail: `nova-burst.md`.
   - *For 2d12:* our Escalation is a clock that builds in the open and is never spent; the Paladin's hook already ties it to an ally becoming Bloodied or going Down. Nothing in the class today is loaded by protecting.
8. **When the support and the damage options compete for one slot, players get half a class.** 4e (Ardent Vow replaces Lay on Hands), 13th Age 1e (talents). Both games later moved the identity pieces into the base class.
   - *For 2d12:* this is our Hallmark Action choice (Smite **or** Lay On Hands) and the SIM's two Paladins (115 and 161 points at level 2). If the brief is one class with three softs, **the pieces that make it a Paladin (the mark, the Rally Point touch, the burst) belong in the Features every Paladin has, and the Hallmark choice picks a style.** A decision for Lucas.
9. **The simple paladin is called boring, not weak.** 13th Age's and the Champion's complaints are about the Paladin's own turn ("attack and raise their shield").
   - *For 2d12:* the redesign's first priority (utility) is aimed at the right thing. The Paladin's own turn needs a job besides attacking: placing the mark, the touch, a position to take.
10. **Split attack stats are remembered as the 4e Paladin's worst fault.**
    - *For 2d12:* "+2 and +1, you choose which is your Weapon Modifier" is a milder version of it. Part of the class numbers decision.
