# The archer: ranged martial classes in other games

## 2026-11-16: for the class redesign (MASTER-PLAN item 5), before any Archer card

**The question:** what makes a ranged martial boring or fun at a tactical table, how games make the map matter for a shooter, what a shooter does besides damage, and how range is paid for. Asked before Lucas gave the Archer's brief (no quarry; many small hits; very mobile; a quick melee strike; fragile, in the back), so the "for 2d12" lines below were written against the brief afterwards.

**How far to trust this file.** One pass, by an agent; not re-checked by a second. [read] = rules text read in full (Draw Steel, Pathfinder 2e, D&D 4e, 13th Age, Lancer, ICON 1.5). [checked] = an opinion read on its page (Paizo's forum, EN World, DM David, RPGBot). [lead] = seen only in a search summary (eight, tagged in the raw file). Reddit, Giant in the Playground and RPG.net refuse readers and were left alone. No players' reception was found for Lancer, Draw Steel's kits or ICON. The full report: `raw/2026-11-16-archer.md`.

### Takeaways

1. **What bores is one best button, repeated.** [checked, EN World, 2009] on 4e's ranger: "Every. Single. Ranger. Uses. Twin-Strike. Almost. Every. Round. [...] They're so boring. There's no variety." [checked, Paizo, 2024] on Pathfinder 2e's: "very boring and rotation dependent [...] Gravity Weapon > Hunt Prey > Hunted Shot [...] Repeat until there are no more enemies". [checked, Paizo, 2024] "flurry rangers aren't fun or good unless you only want to stand in one spot and shoot arrows".
   - *For 2d12:* **this is the brief's main risk.** "Hitting a lot of times" is Twin Strike's and the flurry ranger's shape. The many hits must come from several different cards, each with its own reason, and no single card may be the best answer every round.
2. **The same posters liked the shots that move.** [checked, EN World] "I like Hit And Run/Nimble Strike. They are basic attacks, yes, but they also let you move. Very helpful for 'get the hell out of dodge' or, 'Hit this guy and then run over there to threaten that one'." The texts [read]: 4e's Nimble Strike ("Shift 1 square before or after you attack"), Fading Strike ("you shift 2 squares to a square that is not adjacent to the target"); Pathfinder 2e's Skirmish Strike ("Either Step and then Strike, or Strike and then Step"); Draw Steel's Fade (melee 1 or ranged 10; shift 1, 2 or 3 squares by tier); Lancer's Skirmisher ("Before or after you Skirmish [...] you may move 2 spaces").
   - *For 2d12:* "shooting fast and precise, then moving away" is the liked pattern, and "before or after" is how the others write it. Draw Steel's Fade is one card for both the bow and the blade, which is the brief's "quick strike then back".
3. **Range is paid for in one of three ways: less damage, danger when an enemy closes, or things in the line of fire.** [checked, DM David] 5e sold feats that erased all three: "the game's two most broken feats dismantle all these disadvantages". [checked, EN World] "The game severely, nay critically, undercosts the quality of range".
   - *For 2d12:* ours are in place: a ranged weapon deals less (Chapter 15: "A melee weapon deals more than a ranged one, which strikes from safety"), and Cover is a rule. No Archer card should erase either for free.
4. **Less damage alone doesn't make a shooter interesting; the map does.** [checked, Paizo] players ask "Is being an archer a trap?" and agree the terrain decides. What makes shooters move in the rules read: cover from creatures in the line (Pathfinder 2e: "one turn of setup pays dividends"), high ground, an exact best distance (ICON's "exactly range 3"), 4e's Prime Shot (a bonus when no ally is nearer the target than you).
   - *For 2d12:* a reason on the line of fire moves a shooter better than a rule that orders it. Cards that pay for a flank, an open line or a changed position fit "moving side to side".
5. **A penalty for shooting up close is accepted as danger and resented as an accuracy rule.** [checked, Paizo] on Pathfinder 2e's volley penalty: "It makes no sense"; on having no penalty for firing into melee: "Excellent! No feat-tax!"
   - *For 2d12:* the Archer in melee should be in danger (fragile) and have something good to do there (the quick strike), not a Bane on his bow.
6. **Standing still can be a priced choice instead of the default.** [read] Draw Steel's Patient Shot: "If you don't take a move action this turn, this strike deals extra damage". Lancer's Crack Shot: "you become Immobilized [...] but gain +1 Accuracy". No reception found.
   - *For 2d12:* the opposite fits this brief: a card that pays for having moved.
7. **The non-damage tools players praise are riders used every turn, with a choice.** [checked] 4e's Clever Shot (slide 2, prone or slowed) is "The star of the show". The cautions: an archer that is only control (4e's Seeker: "way too full of 'soft' control") and trick arrows rationed to a few a day ("nowhere near often enough").
   - *For 2d12:* the Arts are that place (a Round card with three Modes). The brief has no control side, so a rider is small and the damage stays the point.
8. **Reload is judged by what its action buys.** Alone it is "a reloading tax" [checked]; bundled with a move or with taking cover (Pathfinder 2e's Running Reload) it is liked.
   - *For 2d12:* for the crossbow's Reload property, with the weapons pass.
9. **On the mark** (the quarry leaves the Archer for a later ranger; kept for that class): a mark that costs an action for each dead quarry is "a very cumbersome mechanic" [checked, Paizo, 2020] and every game refunds it; players take the damage and accuracy marks and leave the defensive ones ("Outwit is infamously bad"); the mark pulls the ranger away from the party's focus fire.
