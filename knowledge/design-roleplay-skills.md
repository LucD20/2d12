# Roleplay and Skills (proposals, from 2026-11-07; nothing decided, nothing in rules-src)

Lucas (2026-11-07): "it is a Roleplaying game, so this is a big part of the system." Skills aren't written yet; they will be raised mainly by Talents. His dislikes: 4e's reputation for no roleplay, Draw Steel's over-the-top out-of-combat features (Resist the Unnatural, Gum Up the Works), PF2e's skill feats. **Everything below is a proposal (Claude, from the research) for him to review in depth later.**

## The research (2026-11-07; mostly forum and blog opinion)
- **4e, "we can't roleplay":** the skill challenge was read as reducing non-combat play to skill checks; its defenders say it works when run loosely, and that the problem was GMs locking onto the listed skills (Tribality, "In Defense of Skill Challenges"; OldeGreybeard, "The Skill Challenge Paradox"; EN World, "The We Can't Roleplay in 4E Argument").
- **PF2e skill feats, "permission":** so many permissions that it feels you can't do something without the feat; some chains feel like a tax (Medicine). Defense: they are new uses or improvements, and they keep high-level tricks for specialists (EN World, "Simplified PF2e?"; Paizo, "Feat tax and 2e").
- **Draw Steel perks:** meant as "heroic moments outside of combat", and the Director can always allow a test for the mundane task a perk mentions (Steel Compendium, Perks); skills are "tools for character expression", not needed for success (Drolleries). Lucas's experience: many perks are over the top (certainties, near-supernatural feats). No online discussion of the two perks he named was found.
- **Draw Steel negotiation:** praised as mapping negotiation better than D&D or PF; criticized for obtuse tiers (show them to the table), running out of justifiable skills by the third roll, and one bad move ending it at low Interest (EN World, "What gets me playing Draw Steel and not Pathfinder 2e?").
- **The sheet as a cage:** players hunt their sheet for a power and miss in-world solutions (EN World, "Stop Looking At Your Character Sheet"); social rolls should reward the quality of the idea, not acting or a score (EN World, "The Answer is not always on your Character Sheet").
- **Skill lists:** they make players think they can only act through the listed skills (Primeval Patterns); the middle ground: "you need skills, just not that many" (Hephaistos); a too-universal list erases differences between characters.
- **Ribbons (5e):** flavor abilities given beside the real ones: they don't compete with combat, but are often forgettable.
- **Lancer's split:** narrative and mech combat are separate on purpose; critics find the switch jarring and the story without weight in the fight (Wikipedia; EN World, "Is Lancer any good?"; Geeknative).
- **Praised:** 13th Age Backgrounds and Daggerheart Experiences (player-written, a bonus when the backstory applies; hard to balance when free-form); Burning Wheel's Beliefs kept alive by rewards (a freeform reward invites power-gaming: tight triggers); tiered outcomes out of combat (Draw Steel, PbtA's mixed success).

**The six failure modes:** permission (PF2e skill feats), the menu (4e's listed skills), certainty (over-the-top perks), budget competition (feat taxes, forgettable ribbons), the roll replacing play (social subsystems), two games (Lancer).

**What 2d12 already has:** tiers on every Check (Tier 2 can be "yes, but"); four broad Attributes, none king, each with an economy job; a bridge between the story and combat (the preparation bonus, Surprise from Hidden, Momentum, Retreat, Supplies and Rough Days, Load, Light, towns); no skills yet, so every failure can be avoided from the start.

## Proposals

### Writing rules for every out-of-combat ability
1. **Anyone can attempt anything plausible** with an Attribute Check. A skill or Talent never grants permission for a mundane action: it makes you better at it. Only extraordinary feats (magic, the supernatural) may need an ability.
2. **No certainty:** an out-of-combat Talent improves odds, saves time or cost, adds something on a Tier 3, or allows Routine (item 5). It never auto-solves a scene, and never gives a martial character a supernatural sense.
3. **Separate budgets:** out-of-combat growth doesn't compete with combat Talents in one pick. Options: **A** a Skill pick every level beside the Talent (the proposal: simplest, matches "a Talent every level"); **B** every other level; **C** two-sided Talents (a combat effect and an out-of-combat one).
4. **The idea sets the roll, not the acting:** the approach, argument or leverage sets the DC or gives a Boon or Bane; acting is welcome, never required.
5. **Routine:** trained in a skill, with no pressure or danger, you may take a **Tier 2 without rolling** (expertise as reliability).
6. **GM tier guidance:** Tier 1 no, or yes at a real cost; Tier 2 yes, but (a complication, more time, a resource); Tier 3 yes, plus something; failure moves the story forward.

### Skills (the structure; numbers later)
7. **About 8 to 12 broad skills, two or three per Attribute**, raised by Talents. An example list: Body: Athletics, Endurance; Mind: Lore, Craft, Medicine; Appeal: Persuasion, Deception, Performance; Instinct: Perception, Survival, Stealth.
8. **Backgrounds:** 1 or 2 short player-written Backgrounds per PC ("former smuggler"), each **1 Boon** on a Check where it plausibly applies; one per Check, no stacking, the GM confirms it fits.

### Structured scenes, for big moments only
9. **Challenges** (montages, chases, heists) and **Negotiations**, with visible progress (successes before failures): no fixed skill list (any approach that fits); **repeating an approach takes 1 Bane** (as At-will Actions do); the tiers and the progress shown to the table; one bad roll never ends a scene alone. Most roleplay stays free-form.

### Rewarding roleplay
10. **Drives and Sparks (to test):** each PC writes 2 Drives. Once per session, when a Drive makes you choose something that **costs you**, you gain a Spark: spend it for Favored on a Check, or to start an encounter at +1 Escalation.

### Classes
11. **One grounded out-of-combat Feature per class, free** (a ribbon that matters), following items 1 and 2.

### The bridge
12. **Out-of-combat success changes fights** (a preparation bonus, fewer enemies, an ally, terrain, Surprise), and fights change the story (Retreat, Rough Days, a lost purse).

### To avoid
Skill feats that unlock basic uses; abilities that remove the roll or the scene; printed "use these skills here" lists; one shared budget for combat and non-combat picks; free-form backgrounds with no limits; a social system where Appeal alone wins.

## Decisions for Lucas
1. Skill growth: A, B or C. 2. About 8 to 12 broad skills. 3. Backgrounds as 1 Boon (1 or 2 per PC). 4. Routine. 5. Challenges and Negotiations for big moments, "repeat an approach: 1 Bane". 6. Drives and Sparks: in, out or later. 7. One grounded out-of-combat Feature per class.
