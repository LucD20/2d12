# Base Building (proposals, from 2026-11-07; for much later; nothing decided, nothing in rules-src)

Lucas (2026-11-07): the next fantasy after the economy: building a place, a base of operations. "My economy is more realistic than most RPGs." Logged as proposals for a session much later. Ties: book/items-economy.md (Upkeep, Dulled, Repair, Supplies, Load, the town stat block, Retreat, crafting).

## The research (2026-11-07; mostly reviews, forum and blog opinion; three pages blocked by the session's network: thealexandrian.net, gamesradar.com, a Substack essay on strongholds)

**The complaints, across every system:**
1. **Paperwork instead of play:** D&D 2024 Bastions "like completing forms", "a thing we processed" (Behind the Screen, a two-year review); PF2e's kingdom rules "a ton of bookkeeping that rapidly becomes tedious", community rewrites remove whole subsystems (Domainkeeper), a group dropped them and "really tightened up our play"; Kingmaker's video game "tedious", later scaled back; OSR domain play: players "realize strongholds are a game of logistics and nope out".
2. **It doesn't touch the characters:** PF2e kingdoms "completely non-interactive with the player characters"; Darkest Dungeon critique: you care about characters by playing them, not by shuffling them between buildings.
3. **It solves no problem:** the known Bastion critique (the Alexandrian): extra perks in a game that already has plenty.
4. **Too late, or never:** domain play as an end-game players rarely reach (arguments to start it at level 1); Bastion reviewers want it friendlier to low levels ("why not a tavern between adventures?").
5. **Power and money leaks:** Strongholds & Followers' enhanced class features are its most controversial part, a stronghold "can unbalance the game"; Bastions gave gold "outside my control", while some income is "a pittance".
6. **Random events that wear thin:** Forbidden Lands' stronghold events (players soon roll 61-66 almost every time).

**What people love:**
1. **People, more than bricks:** S&F's followers and retainers (its most praised part); Draw Steel's followers capped by Renown, artisans and sages helping projects; attachment comes from recurring named NPCs who like the PCs, have a hook and provide a service.
2. **Something you can lose:** Blades in the Dark's crew ("something it can actually lose"; lair upgrades "flavorful and mechanically meaningful"); Forbidden Lands' sieges ("awesome"), risk rising with Reputation. Threats energize players only after attachment, occasional and telegraphed, with player agency; no extreme changes without buy-in.
3. **Visible progress:** the two-year Bastion review's fix (a rescued herbalist unlocks the herbalist's facility: "closer to progression than paperwork"); the video-game hubs (Darkest Dungeon's hamlet, XCOM's base): come home, spend rewards, see it grow.
4. **A real benefit:** Forbidden Lands' stronghold as a safe place to rest and a base for exploring.
5. **Abstraction on purpose:** Forbidden Lands' stronghold "a bit abstract", deliberately; S&F's thin construction, which most tables don't want deeper.
6. **Time and generations:** Pendragon's winter phase (manor, household, children) shows a campaign's years passing (its manor rules "raw").

**The fantasy:** "A place that is ours, full of people who know us, that grows because of what we did out there, that we'd fight to protect, and that makes the next trip out possible." Not a spreadsheet that pays dividends.

## Why 2d12 is well placed
The realistic economy is full of problems a home can solve: Rest needs a safe place; Load limits carrying (a stash); Supplies cost every Sleep; Upkeep and Repair need crafters; a town's Purse limits selling; Wounds heal at a Rest; Retreat needs somewhere to fall back to; crafting and Mastery need time and a place. A base doesn't invent perks: it makes the existing economy easier and becomes part of the story.

## Proposals (Claude)
1. **Solves the economy's real problems, never with combat numbers** (no Boons, Defense or Health, like items); at most a preparation bonus when defending your own home (Momentum or +1 Escalation, as Actions Out of Combat allow when you know what you'll face).
2. **People first:** every facility comes with a named person (steward, smith, healer) with a need and a hook.
3. **Progress through the story, not only silver:** a facility needs SC, downtime days and often someone you met (the herbalist you rescued, the smith who owes you).
4. **One sheet, one moment:** a Holding sheet (facilities, people, Upkeep, a Threat track); everything happens when you come home: pay what's owed, one roll if you were away a week or more, use the services. Nothing between visits (unlike Bastion turns).
5. **Reuse the economy's vocabulary:** Upkeep per facility paid at a Rest; unpaid, a facility is Dulled (closed) until paid or Repaired; **the base grows into a town with its own town stat block** (Level, Tags, Purse, Services): investing raises its Level, adds Tags and Services, grows its Purse (you build the market you sell to).
6. **Occasional, telegraphed stakes:** a Threat track (0-3) rising with fame and enemies; a watchtower or steward warns ahead; threats come from named factions; Retreat sends you home.
7. **Modest income, inside the treasure table:** it saves money (cheaper Supplies, free storage, free Repair) and at most earns small rents counted within the treasure table's "rewards" share (the fitted economy stays true).
8. **Early and optional:** a rented room at Stage 1 (stash, safe Rest), a house around Stage 2, a hall or estate at Stage 3, then a settlement.
9. **Party-owned by default,** personal rooms allowed (with the Supplies shared-purse option).

**Example facilities:** Stash (a caretaker; storage that ignores Load, lots wait for a better market); Workshop (a smith; moves enchantments and Repairs at home, at a discount); Infirmary or chapel (a healer or priest; a Rest there removes 1 more Wound and counts as Safe even when the town isn't); Larder (a cook; cheaper Supplies, holds foraged stock); Stable (a groom; mules, their Upkeep halved, porters); Library (a sage; identifies rare items, research: Mind's job); Training yard (a master-at-arms; a little Mastery with downtime, capped so it never replaces Tier 3s in fights); Warehouse or stall (a factor; sells loot at home with the base's own Purse); Watchtower (a sentry; warns of threats: the Threat track); Hall of trophies (a herald; reputation, social doors: Appeal's job).

**Watch-outs:** abstract construction (facilities, not rooms: PF1e's rooms and teams and PF2e's kingdom economy are the cautionary tales); no weekly random event; no class features from the base; threats only after attachment, never a surprise wipe without buy-in; every facility answers "what does it save us or open for us?" ("+1 to something" is a perk: out).
