# Conditions and senses: "stunned", and two creatures that can't see each other

## 2026-11-15: two quick checks while naming a new condition (Lucas)

**How far to trust this file:** two web searches; forum lines are search summaries of pages not opened ([lead]). Our own rules were read.

### Is "Stunned" hated?

**The question (Lucas):** rename our Dazed (it takes AP) to Stunned, to free the name Dazzled? "Stunned is usually so severe people hate it."

1. **Losing a whole turn is the complaint, by that name.** [lead] EN World has threads titled "Stun: The Fun-Killer" and "Is Losing your Turn The Worst That Can Happen"; a poster: Stunned and Dazed are bad because the player doesn't get to play for the next 5 to 50 minutes; another: if you still have options, you're still playing. Lancer GMs are reported replacing stun on players with a weaker effect that leaves one quick action.
2. **The games split the word in two.** [lead] Pathfinder 2e uses "stunned" for a counted, weaker thing: Stunned 1 means you lose 1 of your 3 actions. 4e's dazed leaves one action and its stunned none [memory, unverified].
   - *For 2d12:* our Dazed N (lose N AP now) is Pathfinder 2e's Stunned N in all but name, and at 1 it is what the genre calls dazed. "Stunned 1" would be accurate and would carry the word's bad name; "Dazed 1" promises a partial loss and delivers it.

### Two creatures that can't see each other

**The question (Lucas):** our sentence "you gain no Favored or Boon from a creature's poor perception of you if you perceive that creature as Hidden" is confusing; how do others write it?

1. **5e's version cancels, and players find it counterintuitive.** [lead] In darkness both are blinded: disadvantage to attack and advantage for being unseen cancel, so two blind creatures attack each other normally. The common house rule: both attack with disadvantage. Others limit the unseen attacker's bonus to melee, or leave it to the GM.
   - *For 2d12:* our rule already is that house rule for Hidden (both Doomed). For Obscured the Bane and the Boon still cancel (two creatures in Dim light fight normally): the same complaint in small.
2. **No game found writes it as a table**; the cleanest fix seen is a single principle (your own blindness comes first).
   - *For 2d12:* a small table (how you perceive it, against how it perceives you) removes the sentence altogether.
